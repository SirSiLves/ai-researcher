- **The Protocol That Ate the Enterprise: Why MCP Is the Most Important Thing Your Leadership Team Hasn't Discussed** — Sudha Sreejith, 2026-05-04. - **The Agent Internet Has a Cryptography Problem — and It's Worse Than the Scores Suggest** — Dr. Mark Tehrani (CyberSeQ), 2026-05-06. Post-quantum scan across MCP, A2A, ACP, ANP, AITP shows two failure modes: protocols either don't define their crypto (ANP, A2A — "TLS unspecified" dominates) or define weak crypto (MCP runs 82/83 instances on RSA-2048; ACP and AITP still ship deprecated SSL). Zero meet NSA CNSA 2.0; zero use ML-DSA or ML-KEM. _Why interesting:_ "harvest now, decrypt later" applies to every signed agent message today; spec maintainers have a narrow window to mandate ML-DSA-65 / ML-KEM-768 before billions of agents inherit the weakness. https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agent-internet-has-cryptography-problem-its-worse-than-tehrani-xivre

- **EU postpones obligations for high risk AI systems** — Adrian Boris Brandauer (Baker McKenzie), 2026-05-08. Political agreement on the Digital Omnibus shifts Annex III high-risk obligations from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027, and Annex I from 2 Aug 2027 to 2 Aug 2028; bans on CSAM-generating and "nudifier" apps explicitly confirmed; SME privileges extended to small mid-caps; data-processing legal basis added for bias detection. _Why interesting:_ authoritative legal recap of what changed — "breathing space, not a free pass." Other AI Act timelines are unchanged. https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-postpones-obligations-high-risk-ai-systems-adrian-boris-brandauer-bkgfe

- **The RAG era is ending. Next is the knowledge layer** — Jakob Reiter (Mimirio), 2026-05-05. Argument that document-retrieval-at-runtime breaks for agentic workloads (inconsistent answers, token cost, weak traceability). Calls for a continuously curated, governed knowledge layer ingesting CRM/email/wiki/SharePoint/tickets with provenance, freshness scores, and access control — outputs are next-best-actions, not document lists. _Why interesting:_ Vendor-leaning but a clean articulation of the post-RAG agentic stack from a European data-sovereignty angle (on-prem / private cloud, GDPR + AI Act). https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/rag-era-ending-next-knowledge-layer-jakob-reiter-jldbf

- **The runtime behind production deep agents** — LangChain (Sydney Runkle, Vivek Trivedy), 2026-05-05. Splits "harness" (prompts, tools, skills) from "runtime" (durable execution, memory, multi-tenancy, HITL, observability, sandboxes, cron). Maps each production requirement to LangSmith Deployment / Agent Server primitives; deepagents deploy is MIT-licensed, exposes MCP and A2A endpoints automatically, persists memory in your own PostgreSQL. _Why interesting:_ a fully open-stack take on the agent runtime that explicitly avoids server-side compaction lock-in; covers double-texting strategies, time-travel debugging, and sandbox auth-proxy patterns rarely written up elsewhere. https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/runtime-behind-production-deep-agents-langchain-dkhqe

- **Top Six AI Act Exposure Scenarios (You're Probably in Scope)** — Aleksandr Tiulkanov (CIPP/E, ISO 42001 implementer), 2026-05-05. Walks through how using a vendor API can still make you a provider under Art. 25 (rebranding, substantial modification, intended-purpose change) — e.g. asking a generic chatbot to evaluate job candidates makes you a high-risk AI provider. Recaps fines (€15M / 3% turnover), product liability under Directive 2024/2853, and GDPR overlay. _Why interesting:_ practical role-classification primer; pairs well with the Brandauer postponement piece since most non-Annex-III obligations were untouched. https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/top-six-ai-act-exposure-scenarios-youre-probably-aleksandr-mfowc
