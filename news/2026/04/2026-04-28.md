## Major announcements & releases

**OpenAI lands on AWS Bedrock — frontier models, Codex and Managed Agents (limited preview)** — At AWS's What's Next event on 28 April, AWS and OpenAI announced three new Bedrock offerings, all in limited preview: GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.4 available through the standard Bedrock model-access / fine-tuning / orchestration flow; Codex on Bedrock (via Codex CLI, desktop app and VS Code extension) bringing OpenAI's coding agent into AWS environments; and Amazon Bedrock Managed Agents powered by OpenAI, packaging the OpenAI agent harness for "production-ready" deployment on AWS. Sam Altman appeared by recorded video; the keynote was led by AWS CEO Matt Garman, SVP Colleen Aubrey and CMO Julia White. _Why it matters:_ This is the first day post-Microsoft-exclusivity (which lapsed on 27 April), and the first hyperscaler-grade distribution of OpenAI frontier models outside Azure since the partnership formed. Bedrock's enterprise-procurement footprint is the unlock — every AWS customer can now buy GPT-5.5 the same way they buy Claude or Llama. **AWS What's Next: Amazon Quick (work AI assistant), Bedrock AgentCore CLI, plus partnership roll-ups** — AWS used the same event to launch Amazon Quick — an AI assistant for work that connects across systems, learns the user's context and takes action — alongside the Bedrock AgentCore CLI (mentioned in AWS's 27 April weekly roundup) and a clutch of partnership updates (Meta-Graviton5 follow-on, Anthropic Cowork in Bedrock GA, Snowflake-Bedrock joint connector work). AWS framed the day as "the largest cluster of AI product announcements since re:Invent 2025." _Why it matters:_ Amazon Quick is AWS's most visible response to Microsoft 365 Copilot — built around Bedrock model choice (Claude, OpenAI, Llama, Mistral) rather than a single first-party model. Combined with the OpenAI launch, the day's announcements reposition Bedrock from "model catalogue" to "agent operating environment." [AWS Blog — top announcements](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/top-announcements-of-the-whats-next-with-aws-2026/) · [AWS What's Next landing page](https://aws.amazon.com/events/whats-next-with-aws/) · [AWS Weekly Roundup (May 4) tying it together](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/aws-weekly-roundup-whats-next-with-aws-2026-amazon-quick-openai-partnership-and-more-may-4-2026/)

**EU AI Act Omnibus trilogue collapses after ~12 hours; 2 August 2026 high-risk deadline back in play** — The second political trilogue on the Digital Omnibus on AI between the European Parliament, the Council of the EU and the Commission ended on 28 April without political agreement after roughly twelve hours of negotiation. The Cypriot Council Presidency confirmed: "It was not possible to reach an agreement with the European Parliament." The package would have deferred the AI Act's high-risk obligations, narrowed Annex III, and integrated AI obligations with sectoral product-safety law — none of it passed. The breakdown centred on the conformity-assessment architecture for AI in regulated products under Annex I (machinery, medical devices). Crucially, GPAI obligations (Articles 50–55) were not in dispute and remain on schedule. A follow-up trilogue is set for ~13 May. _Why it matters:_ Without an Omnibus deal, Regulation 2024/1689's original applicability dates remain legally in force, including 2 August 2026 for high-risk system obligations. The Cypriot presidency has 88 days and a single trilogue left before its term ends 30 June 2026. For enterprises with Annex III workflows in scope, today's collapse is the cue to stop assuming a deferral and start budgeting against the original deadline. **IBM launches IBM Bob — AI-assisted software development assistant** — IBM publicly launched IBM Bob on 28 April, a watsonx-anchored coding assistant positioned ahead of the broader IBM Think 2026 launch (5 May). Bob targets enterprise dev workflows with policy-aware code generation, package allow-lists, and built-in evidence collection mapped against IBM Concert governance. _Why it matters:_ IBM is staking a claim in the enterprise-coding-agent lane defined by Claude Code, Codex and Cursor — but with governance-first packaging tuned to regulated buyers. Bob also seeds the Think 2026 narrative on the "governance-first AI operating model."
