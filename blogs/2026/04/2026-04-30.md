## Long-form picks

- **Our evaluation of OpenAI's GPT-5.5 cyber capabilities** — Simon Willison (on UK AISI). _Takeaway:_ The UK's AI Security Institute (which earlier evaluated Claude Mythos) finds GPT-5.5's vulnerability-finding capabilities comparable to Mythos — but, unlike Mythos, generally available to the public. Significant policy data point on cyber-capable models out in the wild. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/30/gpt-55-cyber-capabilities/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/30/gpt-55-cyber-capabilities/)

- **The Zig project's rationale for their firm anti-AI contribution policy** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Zig has one of the most stringent anti-LLM policies of any major OSS project — no LLMs for issues, PRs, or comments. Simon walks through the maintainers' rationale; counter-data point to the "AI-assisted contributions are an unalloyed good" narrative. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/30/zig-anti-ai/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/30/zig-anti-ai/)

- **A quote from Andrew Kelley** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Companion to the Zig post — Kelley (Zig's BDFL) on why "we can't tell who is using LLM and who is not" is a misconception, and how they have in fact been catching LLM-assisted PRs. Useful operational counterpoint to the assumption that AI-tool detection is hopeless. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/30/andrew-kelley/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/30/andrew-kelley/)

- **We need RSS for sharing abundant vibe-coded apps** — Simon Willison (riffing on Matt Webb). _Takeaway:_ Matt Webb's idea — an RSS feed for the explosion of one-off vibe-coded apps with an "Install" button per item. Simon thinks through the "install to where?" problem, which lands on the same agent-runtime/sandbox question that drove pip 26.1's cooldown feature this week. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/30/rss-vibe-coded-apps/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/30/rss-vibe-coded-apps/)

- **The Pulse: AI load breaks GitHub – why not other vendors?** — Gergely Orosz (Pragmatic Engineer). _Takeaway:_ Dispatch on GitHub's repeated outages under AI-coding load, plus Anthropic's "speed run to break devs' goodwill," big Copilot price increases, and Mitchell Hashimoto's "building block economy" framing. The state-of-developer-tooling read for late April. [https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-github-breaks](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-github-breaks)

- **Amazon Earnings, Trainium and Commodity Markets, Additional Amazon Notes** — Ben Thompson (Stratechery). _Takeaway:_ Argues Amazon's bet on Trainium is paying off precisely because the workload mix is shifting from training to inference and agents; pairs nicely with the "Inference Inflection" thread on Latent Space the same day. [https://stratechery.com/2026/amazon-earnings-trainium-and-commodity-markets-additional-amazon-notes/](https://stratechery.com/2026/amazon-earnings-trainium-and-commodity-markets-additional-amazon-notes/)

- **[AINews] The Inference Inflection** — Latent.Space (smol AI). _Takeaway:_ Frames the day's news around a thesis the wider analyst community has been converging on this week — that compute economics are visibly inflecting from training-dominated to inference-dominated, with downstream consequences for AWS/Trainium, Nvidia, and the hyperscaler P&Ls. [https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-the-inference-inflection](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-the-inference-inflection)
