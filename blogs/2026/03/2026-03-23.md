## Long-form picks

- **The machine didn't take your craft. You gave it up.** — David Abram, via Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Argues the hardest parts of software — understanding systems, debugging, architecture, knowing what should exist and why — are precisely what LLMs cannot do. Choosing is still yours. Anti-deskilling framing for engineers worried about agents. [https://www.davidabram.dev/musings/the-machine-didnt-take-your-craft/](https://www.davidabram.dev/musings/the-machine-didnt-take-your-craft/)
- **Slop is something that takes more human effort to consume than it took to produce** — Neurotica (@schwarzgerat.bsky.social), via Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ One-paragraph definition of slop that quickly traveled the dev/AI corner of Bluesky: "When my coworker sends me raw Gemini output he's not expressing his freedom to create, he's disrespecting the value of my time." [https://bsky.app/profile/schwarzgerat.bsky.social/post/3mhqu5dogos2v](https://bsky.app/profile/schwarzgerat.bsky.social/post/3mhqu5dogos2v)

- **Import AI 450: China's electronic warfare model; traumatized LLMs; and a scaling law for cyberattacks** — Jack Clark. _Takeaway:_ Monday issue with three sharp angles: a Chinese EW model, evidence that long RL training can leave models psychologically "traumatized" in detectable ways, and a scaling law showing cyberattack capability rising roughly with model size. [https://importai.substack.com/p/importai-450-chinas-electronic-warfare-model](https://importai.substack.com/p/importai-450-chinas-electronic-warfare-model)

- **Research: Starlette 1.0 skill** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Companion to the Mar 22 Starlette write-up — a complete skill markdown teaching Claude every Starlette 1.0 feature. A reusable template for "skills as just-in-time training data" for libraries that broke compatibility after the model's cutoff. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/23/starlette-1-skill/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Mar/23/starlette-1-skill/)
