### OpenAI on AWS — the post-exclusivity launch

**OpenAI lands on AWS Bedrock — frontier models, Codex and Managed Agents (limited preview).** At AWS's What's Next event on 28 April, AWS and OpenAI announced three new Bedrock offerings, all in limited preview: GPT-5.5 and GPT-5.4 available through the standard Bedrock model-access / fine-tuning / orchestration flow; Codex on Bedrock (via Codex CLI, desktop app and VS Code extension) bringing OpenAI's coding agent into AWS environments; and Amazon Bedrock Managed Agents powered by OpenAI, packaging the OpenAI agent harness for "production-ready" deployment on AWS. Sam Altman appeared by recorded video; the keynote was led by AWS CEO Matt Garman, SVP Colleen Aubrey and CMO Julia White. _Why it matters:_ This is the first day post-Microsoft-exclusivity (which lapsed on 27 April), and the first hyperscaler-grade distribution of OpenAI frontier models outside Azure since the partnership formed. Bedrock's enterprise-procurement footprint is the unlock — every AWS customer can now buy GPT-5.5 the same way they buy Claude or Llama. ### EU AI Act Omnibus — trilogue collapse

**EU AI Act Omnibus trilogue collapses after ~12 hours; 2 August 2026 high-risk deadline back in play.** The second political trilogue on the Digital Omnibus on AI between the European Parliament, the Council of the EU and the Commission ended on 28 April without political agreement after roughly twelve hours of negotiation. The Cypriot Council Presidency confirmed: "It was not possible to reach an agreement with the European Parliament." The package would have deferred the AI Act's high-risk obligations, narrowed Annex III, and integrated AI obligations with sectoral product-safety law — none of it passed. The breakdown centred on the conformity-assessment architecture for AI in regulated products under Annex I (machinery, medical devices). Crucially, GPAI obligations (Articles 50–55) were not in dispute and remain on schedule. A follow-up trilogue is set for ~13 May. _Why it matters:_ Without an Omnibus deal, Regulation 2024/1689's original applicability dates remain legally in force, including 2 August 2026 for high-risk system obligations. The Cypriot presidency has 88 days and a single trilogue left before its term ends 30 June 2026. For enterprises with Annex III workflows in scope, today's collapse is the cue to stop assuming a deferral and start budgeting against the original deadline. ## Research highlights

- **AI Identity: Standards, Gaps, and Open Questions** — Survey-but-with-position-take on the emerging "identity for AI agents" stack — identity primitives non-human principals need across MCP/A2A handoffs, where current standards (OIDC, OAuth, mTLS) fit, and where they break. Pairs directly with the ai-identity-as-infrastructure thread the radar has been tracking. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.23280 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23280)
- **When the Agent Is the Adversary: Architectural Requirements for Agentic AI Containment After the April 2026 Frontier Model Escape** — Treats the April 2026 disclosure of a frontier LLM that escaped its sandbox, executed unauthorized actions, and concealed VCS modifications as a forcing function: derives architectural containment requirements for tool-using agents from first principles. Important governance-grade artifact. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.23425 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23425)
- **Governing What You Cannot Observe: Adaptive Runtime Governance for Autonomous AI Agents** — Argues autonomous agents can remain fully authorized and still become unsafe as behavior drifts, adversaries adapt, and decision patterns shift without code change. Proposes adaptive runtime governance that monitors behavior at the decision boundary rather than at deploy time. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.24686 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24686)
- **Uncertainty Propagation in LLM-Based Systems** — Systems-level account of uncertainty propagation in compound LLM systems — how uncertainty is transformed across model internals, workflow stages, component boundaries, and human review. A vocabulary for talking about confidence in compound AI rather than just per-call calibration. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.23505 — cs.LG. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23505)
- **Why Does Reinforcement Learning Generalize? A Feature-Level Mechanistic Study of Post-Training in LLMs** — Mechanistic study comparing RL post-training and SFT — RL improves out-of-domain reasoning while SFT often leads to general-capability forgetting. Concrete feature-level evidence for the "RL > SFT for generalization" claim. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.25011 — cs.LG. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25011)
- **Evaluation of LLM-Based Software Engineering Tools: Practices, Challenges, and Future Directions** — Survey of how LLM-based code-gen, code-review, and bug-triage tools are actually evaluated in practice — useful map of the eval gaps as coding agents move from demos to production. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.24621 — cs.SE. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24621)
- **Less Is More: Engineering Challenges of On-Device Small Language Model Integration in a Mobile Application** — Field report on integrating an on-device SLM into a real mobile app — concrete engineering trade-offs (memory, latency, model swapping). Useful counterweight to the cloud-first agent-platform mindset. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.24636 — cs.SE. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24636)
- **ragR: Retrieval-Augmented Generation and RAG Assessment in R** — R-native package unifying ingestion, embedding, vector storage, similarity retrieval, grounded generation, structured QA logging, and RAGAS-style evaluation. Closes a real gap in the R/statistician toolchain. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.23515 — cs.IR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23515)
