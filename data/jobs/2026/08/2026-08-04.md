## Notes

- **The specialisation thesis just acquired its opposite, and both halves landed on the same day.** For four runs this file has tracked AI hiring becoming *more* specialised — FDE ladders, neuro-symbolic hard requirements, post-training titles. Today produced a clean natural experiment. **Siemens** wrote the maximalist version: one Zürich JD carrying **eight** slice tags, demanding LLM applications, RAG, agentic workflows, prompt engineering, embeddings, evaluation pipelines *and* responsible-AI/model-risk awareness from a single hire. **LGT**, a private bank three tram stops away, wrote the minimalist version for a post on its **named GenAI team**: "**Deep AI expertise is not required**", with LLM, prompt engineering and RAG demoted to *Nice-to-Have* and the hard requirements being Java/Python, TypeScript, Kubernetes and CI/CD at 3+ years. Both are Zürich, both are August 2026, both are banded at **CHF 100–120K**. The honest reading is that Swiss GenAI work is bifurcating rather than uniformly specialising: employers building AI capability hire specialists, while employers *operating* AI capability increasingly hire ordinary product engineers to wrap managed model APIs. LGT's own phrasing — "applying **managed AI services and APIs** responsibly in a regulated environment" — is the tell.
- **MCP has entered a Swiss job description as a delivery standard.** The LGT post asks the engineer to "support other teams in designing and exposing APIs for AI-enabled use cases, including through standards such as the **Model Context Protocol (MCP)**". This is the first time this collector has recorded MCP named as a required interface standard in a Swiss JD rather than as a stack keyword on a board tag. Coming from a private bank's platform team rather than an AI-native startup, it is a meaningful adoption signal — MCP is being written into enterprise integration requirements in a regulated industry.
- **Swiss banking now has an AI org chart, and it is being staffed in the SAFe idiom rather than the FDE idiom.** The 08-01 → 08-03 thread traced Forward Deployed Engineering acquiring management grades. Today the same structural story appears in banking with completely different vocabulary. **Julius Baer** is hiring a Senior System Architect "**within the ML & AI ART**" who works "in collaboration with **the Lead Architect**" and aligns with "**other System Architects**" — meaning a bank has a standing AI Agile Release Train with a lead architect and a multi-architect bench, delivering a named "**AI automation orchestration layer**", governed by **Architecture Decision Records** approved at **architecture boards**. Put beside **Vector8**'s AI Governance Lead – Banking, **SIX**'s Senior Privacy & AI Governance Specialist, **LGT**'s four posts including a **FinOps & AI Cost Management** specialist, and **Pictet**'s AI Security Engineer, Swiss finance is no longer hiring AI individuals; it is filling out an AI operating model with architecture, governance, security and cost-control seats.

- **The EU AI Act's high-risk regime begins this month — and the specialist governance board carries zero Swiss roles and two European ones.** This is the substantive governance finding of the run, and it sharpens yesterday's negative result with a date. `ai-governance-jobs.com`'s EU AI Act page was fetched in full and states the phasing explicitly: the Act "entered into force on **August 1, 2024**", with "prohibitions and AI-literacy duties from February 2025, obligations for general-purpose AI models from August 2025, and **the bulk of the high-risk regime from August 2026 into 2027**" — i.e. the heaviest obligations start **now**, with extraterritorial reach ("any provider or deployer whose AI output is used in the EU is in scope, wherever they sit") and penalties to "**35 million euros or 7 percent of global annual turnover**".
