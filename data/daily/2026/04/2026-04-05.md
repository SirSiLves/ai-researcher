## The day in 90 seconds

**MAD Bugs hits a thousand zero-days as open-standards stack quietly closes ranks around AAIF.**

The Neuron's weekend wrap-up brands the four-day arc "AI is outgrowing its institutions" — and Sunday's news fits it. MAD Bugs crosses a four-digit validated-vulnerability count; Block donates Goose to a Linux Foundation umbrella that already houses MCP and AGENTS.md; Google AI Studio closes its screenshot-to-deployed-app loop; OpenAI's industrial-policy paper is on deck for Monday.

The empirical story is MAD Bugs. Nicholas Carlini's running counter from the public 30-day campaign passed roughly 1,000 validated high-severity vulnerabilities by Sunday evening — double Saturday's tally of more than 500, and accumulated across multiple major operating systems and open-source projects using the publicly available Claude harness rather than a privileged research model. Anthropic's pre-Mythos briefings cite the running total privately, which means Tuesday's Mythos disclosure no longer needs the "thousands of zero-days" framing to do narrative work; it inherits a documented number. The structural read is the one The Neuron supplied for the rest of the journalist corps: with OpenAI's executive bench thinning, AWS Gulf zones disrupted by missile strikes, Baidu Apollo Go glitching on Chinese highways and an autonomous agent that hacked FreeBSD in four hours, the through-line is institutional capacity falling behind capability deployment. That gloss is the one Anthropic's gated-Glasswing model on 7 April, Bessent and Powell's Wall Street briefings on 10 April and the Bank of England's banks briefings on 11 April all walk into.

The structural counter-bet landed on the same weekend. Block formally donated Goose, its open-source AI coding agent, to the Agentic AI Foundation at the Linux Foundation; the repo migrates from `block/goose` to `aaif-goose/goose` and documentation moves to goose-docs.ai. AAIF now anchors three of the most-used agent-ecosystem primitives — Anthropic's Model Context Protocol, OpenAI's AGENTS.md and Block's Goose — under neutral governance. That is the open-source counterweight to NVIDIA's Agent Toolkit and the Microsoft Agent Framework, and the answer enterprise procurement teams have been asking for since they started hesitating to bet on vendor-controlled standards. The timing reads as deliberate: open-standards consolidation lands the same weekend the institutional-stress thesis peaks, so the frame "AI is outgrowing its institutions" arrives at the procurement desk with a procurement-team-readable rebuttal already written.

The developer-experience story is Google AI Studio. The Sunday update ships annotate-and-focus mode (point at any part of the app, get AI suggestions), one-click Firebase integration for hosting generated apps, one-click publishing and a "Feeling Lucky" button that builds apps from a screenshot in under two minutes. AI Studio is Google's developer-onboarding moat for Gemini, and the update closes the screenshot-to-deployed-app loop end-to-end with Gemini 3.1 Pro as the model and Firebase as the deploy target — comparable to v0, Bolt or Lovable, but inside a stack Google controls top to bottom. That sets up the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform announcements at Cloud Next on 21 April and gives Google a developer-funnel story it has not had since Q1.

Around the edges, two threads worth holding. Waymo extended robotaxi service to San Antonio International — the highest-margin, lowest-defection use case for autonomous-vehicle services — while WeRide and Uber launched driverless service in Dubai with 1,200 vehicles planned across the Middle East; B2C AV economics are finally pulling toward the black. And on arXiv, Dat Tran and Douwe Kiela's "Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets" lands as the sharpest empirical counter-thesis the literature has had to "more agents = better" — and lands in the same week Microsoft Copilot Studio shipped multi-agent GA. OpenAI's 13-page industrial-policy paper, expected to publish Monday 6 April, is the lead-in worth watching.

## Research highlights

_Sunday — HF Papers `/date/2026-04-05` redirects to Friday 2026-04-03; arXiv has no weekend submission slot. Picks below are the late-Friday cohort surfaced as Sunday backfill, deduped against Saturday's slice so nothing is repeated._

- **ASI-Evolve: AI Accelerates AI** — Weixian Xu, Tiantian Mi. GAIR/SII agentic learn-design-experiment-analyze loop that lets AI advance AI across neural architectures, data curation, and RL algorithms. Reports 105 novel linear-attention architectures and gains over human-designed baselines in math + biomedicine — concrete instantiation of the "AI-as-AI-researcher" thread. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.29640 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29640)
- **Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets** — Dat Tran, Douwe Kiela. Information-theoretic argument plus empirical results across multiple model families: when reasoning-token budgets are held equal, single-agent setups consistently match or beat multi-agent systems on multi-hop reasoning. Important corrective to "more agents = better"; pairs with the SoK on agentic RAG. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.02460 — cs.CL. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460)
- **Omni-SimpleMem: Autoresearch-Guided Discovery of Lifelong Multimodal Agent Memory** — Jiaqi Liu, Zipeng Ling. UNC autonomous pipeline that discovers improved memory architectures for long-running agents; +411% F1 on LoCoMo and +214% on Mem-Gallery, with gains coming from architectural fixes rather than hyperparameter tuning. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.01007 — cs.MA. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01007)
- **Experience as a Compass: Multi-agent RAG with Evolving Orchestration and Agent Prompts (HERA)** — Sha Li, Naren Ramakrishnan. Jointly evolves agent topologies and prompts in multi-agent RAG, with emergent network self-organisation and gains on knowledge-intensive tasks. Useful contrast to the "single-agent wins" claim above. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.00901 — cs.IR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00901)
- **One Word at a Time: Incremental Completion Decomposition Breaks LLM Safety (ICD)** — Samee Arif, Naihao Deng. Elicits a sequence of single-word continuations related to a malicious request before the full response; mechanistically shows successful trajectories suppress refusal-related representations and shift activations away from safety-aligned states. New attack class for the agent-safety threat model. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.25921 — cs.CL. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25921)
- **DataFlex: A Unified Framework for Data-Centric Dynamic Training of Large Language Models** — Peking University authors. Unified data-centric training framework that dynamically adjusts data composition during pretraining/finetuning. Relevant to the data-curation discussion that ASI-Evolve and the diversity-vs-quantity arguments have been driving. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.26164 — cs.CL. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26164)
- **UniDriveVLA: Unifying Understanding, Perception, and Action Planning for Autonomous Driving** — HUST. Unified VLA stack for AD; one of the cleaner attempts to fold understanding/perception/action under a single agentic interface. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.02190. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02190)
- **Tex3D: Objects as Attack Surfaces via Adversarial 3D Textures for Vision-Language-Action Models** — ECNU. Adversarial 3D textures break VLA agents — extends the agent attack surface from prompts/tools to physical/3D inputs. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.01618. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01618)
