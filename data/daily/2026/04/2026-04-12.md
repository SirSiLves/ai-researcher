## The day in 90 seconds

**Open-weights fragment into three licence camps as MiniMax ships a CLI built for other agents to call.**

Sunday closed the most consequential week in AI policy and capability disclosure since GPT-5, and it closed on a single observation: the open-weight ecosystem is no longer one thing. A Chinese lab now sits at the top of Hugging Face under a restrictive licence, a Bloomberg Opinion preview anointed Anthropic's gated-release template as the new governance standard, and the bridge between those two worlds shipped as a Node.js command line.

The structural release of the day was MiniMax's MMX-CLI, a terminal-native command-line interface that gives AI agents direct access to image, video, speech, music, vision and search on the MiniMax platform. It is built to be invoked from human terminals and, more pointedly, from agents running inside Cursor, Claude Code and OpenCode. MiniMax shipped it the day after dropping M2.7 weights, and the pairing is the clearest example yet of a lab packaging a model plus a tool server explicitly for invocation by other agents rather than waiting for harnesses to wrap an API. By Sunday evening M2.7 was trending to the top of Hugging Face alongside Gemma 4, and the contrast between MiniMax's modified-MIT licence (commercial use requires prior written authorisation) and Gemma 4's Apache-2.0 had become the load-bearing debate in the open-weight community. Two of April's three most-downloaded weight families are now under restrictive commercial licences, with DeepSeek's preview about to be the third. The phrase "open weights" is splintering into Apache-2.0, OpenRAIL and commercial-restricted, and the genuinely permissive bucket is now the minority.

Underneath the licence fight, the Spring 2026 State of Open-Source AI report from Hugging Face quantified what the week's narrative was already pointing at. Chinese-developed models pulled 41 percent of Hugging Face download volume in Q1 2026, and robotics datasets grew 23x year-over-year, from 1,145 to 26,991. AGIBOT World 2026 open-sourced its embodied-AI dataset the same weekend, adding to the slope. Read against Meta's Muse Spark closed-weights pivot four days earlier, the gap between U.S. frontier-lab open-weight commitment and Chinese open-weight share is widening at exactly the moment enterprise procurement is consolidating around governed agent stacks. Nathan Lambert's open-model-consortium argument from Saturday is the policy proposal that now has a number to point at.

The third thread is governance closing the bracket. A Bloomberg Opinion preview that runs formally on Monday frames Project Glasswing — capability disclosure, access gating, and an 11-partner defender consortium — as the operating template policymakers should cite for future capability-gated frontier rollouts. That places the week between two emerging standards: Glasswing on the governance side, Chinese open-weights plus agent-CLIs on the distribution side. On the research desk, Hugging Face Papers redirected to Friday's cohort over the weekend, but the Friday slate matters: SkillClaw stakes out a third corner of the agent-skills design space between in-weight skill internalisation and inference-time retrieval; MolmoWeb releases an open-weight visual web-agent family on screenshots alone, putting direct counter-pressure on closed CUA stacks; and KnowU-Bench shows that mobile agents which excel at explicit tasks fall below 50 percent on vague instructions that require user-preference inference. Long-form blogs were quiet — Simon Willison's QuickJS async-sandbox investigation and a uv-run recipe for Gemma 4 audio transcription, neither a real essay — so Medium carried the day, led by Suresh Kumar Ariya Gowder's "Prompt Engineering Is Dead. Long Live Context Engineering," the cleanest statement yet of the vocabulary shift the week had been pushing toward.

## Research highlights

_Sunday — HF Papers `/date/2026-04-12` redirects to Friday 2026-04-10; arXiv has no weekend submission slot. Picks below are the Friday cohort surfaced as Sunday backfill, deduped against Saturday's `2026-04-11.md`._

- **SkillClaw: Let Skills Evolve Collectively with Agentic Evolver** — Ziyu Ma, Shidong Yang. Aggregates experience from many users so an autonomous evolver can identify behavioral patterns and refine/extend a shared skill repository. Third corner of the agent-skills design space — between SKILL0 (in-weight skill internalization) and Graph-of-Skills (inference-time retrieval). _Category:_ arXiv:2604.08377 — cs.MA. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08377)
- **MolmoWeb: Open Visual Web Agent and Open Data for the Open Web** — Tanmay Gupta, Piper Wolters. Open-weight family of multimodal web agents trained on diverse browser-task data, navigating from screenshots without HTML. State-of-the-art on web benchmarks among open-weight models; releases data alongside models — direct counter-pressure to closed CUA stacks. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.08516 — cs.MA. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08516)
- **KnowU-Bench: Towards Interactive, Proactive, and Personalized Mobile Agent Evaluation** — Tongbo Chen, Zhengxi Lu. Mobile-agent benchmark testing preference inference and proactive assistance, not just task execution. Agents that excel at explicit task execution drop below 50% on vague instructions requiring user-preference inference. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.08455 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08455)
- **Beyond Stochastic Exploration: What Makes Training Data Valuable for Agentic Search** — Chuzhan Hao, Wenfeng Feng. Hierarchical Experience (HiExp) transforms raw reasoning trajectories into clustered, contrastive experience knowledge so RL-trained search agents explore by experience rather than by random sampling. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.08124 — cs.IR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08124)
- **Rethinking Generalization in Reasoning SFT: A Conditional Analysis on Optimization, Data, and Model Capability** — AI45Research. Conditional analysis of why reasoning SFT generalizes (or doesn't): isolates contributions from optimization, data composition, and base-model capability. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.06628 — cs.CL. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06628)
- **A Survey on the Security of Long-Term Memory in LLM Agents: Toward Mnemonic Sovereignty** — Zehao Lin, Chunyu Li. Surveys persistent-memory threats (poisoning, unauthorized access, cross-session corruption) and proposes "mnemonic sovereignty" as a verifiable governance frame. Pairs with STALE on the agent-memory governance line. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.16548 — cs.CR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16548)
