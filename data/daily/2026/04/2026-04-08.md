## The day in 90 seconds

**Meta pulls its biggest model off the open shelf, and the U.S. open-weight anchor goes with it.**

Wednesday was the day the open-weight thesis lost its largest American sponsor. Meta unveiled Muse Spark — the first model from Meta Superintelligence Labs and the first major release since Alexandr Wang took the CAIO seat nine months ago — and shipped it without weights. In Washington, Anthropic's Mythos disclosure crossed from lab announcement to governance template in 24 hours.

Muse Spark, internally codenamed "Avocado," scored 52 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.0 — fourth place behind Gemini 3.1 Pro and GPT-5.4 at 57 and Claude Opus 4.6 at 53. It is the model Meta built after paying $14.3 billion for 49 percent of Scale AI to bring Wang in-house, and Mark Zuckerberg is keeping it closed. The model powers a rebuilt Meta AI assistant on the web and lands in WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, Messenger, and Ray-Ban Meta over the following weeks; a private API preview opens to selected partners. The company says it "hopes to open-source future versions," language that Simon Willison and Artificial Intelligence News read as a permanent pivot. Llama 4 was the open-source baseline for roughly 40 percent of Hugging Face download volume going into Q1; Muse Spark sitting behind a private API instead of public weights pulls Meta out of that role. With DeepSeek V4 lining up for an Apache-modified release on 24 April, the structural read is that the Chinese-lab share of open-weight downloads — already 41 percent per Hugging Face's Spring 2026 report — is set to climb without a U.S. counterweight.

The compute commitment landed in the same news cycle. Reporting on Wednesday set up Thursday's formal announcement that Meta is expanding its CoreWeave deal by roughly $21 billion on top of the existing $14.2 billion, with capacity running 2027 through 2032 and the first deployments of NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform. Meta's 2026 CapEx is now guided to $115-135 billion, nearly double 2025. Read in sequence — Wang to CAIO, Muse Spark closed, CoreWeave to roughly $35 billion of backlog on Rubin silicon — the Wang-led superintelligence stack is complete and public-facing in a single 24-hour window. The strategic message is that Meta now believes the moat lives in the harness and the compute, not the weights.

The governance picture moved in parallel. The day after Anthropic's Project Glasswing disclosure, the World Economic Forum, the Internet Governance Project, and the IT-security analyst shop ITECS each published framing pieces: WEF's "Mythos moment" essay on frontier AI redefining cybersecurity, IGP on the changing institutional economics of bugs, ITECS on the end of secure-by-obscurity for enterprises. Twenty-four hours moved Glasswing from "lab announcement" to working governance template. In the same cycle, OpenAI's "Industrial Policy for the Intelligence Age" paper picked up Bloomberg and TechPolicy.Press coverage; the latter's "policymercial" critique went viral on policy Twitter. With the Treasury and Fed banks briefing scheduled for Friday, the Industrial Policy paper and the Mythos coverage are now joined-up reading material for officials walking into financial-services CEO conversations.

Research kept pace. Claw-Eval introduced trajectory-aware grading with both Pass@k and Pass^k to punish stochastic flakiness; Beyond Accuracy proposed Prefill Token Equivalents as a hardware-aware latency metric that correlates with wall-clock cost far better than raw token counts; and "How Well Do Agentic Skills Work in the Wild" produced the first hard counter-evidence to Tuesday's optimistic SkillX framing, showing skill utilization degrades sharply when retrieved from a library instead of handcrafted in-context. The week's two biggest arguments about self-evolving skills are now on the table at once.

## Research highlights

- **Claw-Eval: Toward Trustworthy Evaluation of Autonomous Agents** — anonymous. Multi-modal agent benchmark with trajectory-aware grading (not just final-state checking) + safety/robustness assessments. Uses Pass@k AND Pass^k (success on k consecutive runs) to penalize stochastic flakiness. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.06132 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06132)
- **Learning to Retrieve from Agent Trajectories** — anonymous. Retrieval models for agentic search trained directly on agent interaction trajectories, with weighted optimization respecting "relevance intensity." Moves beyond NQ-style labels for agent contexts. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.04949 — cs.IR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04949)
- **Beyond Accuracy: Unveiling Inefficiency Patterns in Tool-Integrated Reasoning** — anonymous. Introduces PTE (Prefill Token Equivalents), a hardware-aware metric accounting for KV-cache eviction and long tool responses — correlates with actual wall-clock latency far better than raw token counts. Concrete instrumentation for agent-observability stacks. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.05404 — cs.CL. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05404)
- **How Well Do Agentic Skills Work in the Wild** — anonymous. Skill utilization degrades sharply when skills must be retrieved and refined from a library rather than handcrafted in-context. Targeted query-specific refinement partially restores performance on Terminal-Bench 2.0. Counter-evidence for the optimistic SkillX-style framing from yesterday. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.04323 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04323)
- **In-Place Test-Time Training** — anonymous. Modifies the final projection matrix in MLP blocks via an efficient context-parallel update with a task-aligned next-token-prediction objective at inference. Light-weight TTT inside an attention forward pass. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.06169 — cs.LG. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06169)
- **ClawsBench: Evaluating Capability and Safety of LLM Productivity Agents in Simulated Workspaces** — anonymous. Productivity-agent benchmark with high-fidelity mock services, deterministic snapshot/restore, per-step unsafe-action scoring; probes multi-step sandbox escalation and silent contract modification. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.05172 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05172)
- **MegaTrain: Full-Precision Training of 100B+ Parameter LLMs on a Single GPU** — anonymous. Pushes 100B+ full-precision training onto a single GPU via host-memory parameter streaming, gradient offloading, pipelined double-buffered execution with persistent autograd graphs. Concrete answer to "what's after DeepSpeed ZeRO-3" for cluster-less researchers. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.05091 — cs.LG. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05091)
- **DARE: Diffusion LLM Alignment and Reinforcement Executor** — anonymous. Unified post-training stack (SFT + RL + preference optimization + PEFT) for the increasingly fragmented diffusion-LLM landscape. Addresses the "no equivalent of TRL/Axolotl for dLLMs" gap. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.04215 — cs.LG. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04215)
