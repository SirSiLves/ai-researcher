## The day in 90 seconds

**Bank of England joins the Mythos response and MiniMax tests what "open" still means in Chinese weights.**

Two parallel arcs closed the Glasswing week. UK regulators formally folded into the U.S. and Canadian convening on Anthropic's Mythos, completing a 96-hour transatlantic playbook with no public precedent. Hours earlier, MiniMax pushed a 230-billion-parameter sparse mixture-of-experts model to Hugging Face under a license that allows free non-commercial use but requires written authorisation for anything commercial — reigniting the argument over what an open-weight release actually grants.

The regulator move came first. Bloomberg broke on Saturday that the Bank of England will discuss Mythos with UK financial institutions at the next two meetings of its Cross-Market Operational Resilience Group and the CMORG AI Taskforce, both scheduled within a fortnight, with HM Treasury, the Financial Conduct Authority and the National Cyber Security Centre expected to attend. Anthropic is preparing to bring the model directly to UK banks the following week. Pair that with Friday's Treasury convening, where Scott Bessent and Jerome Powell briefed Wall Street CEOs on the same model, and the Bank of Canada's earlier coordination, and the four-day arc adds up to the most-coordinated regulator-to-regulator response to a single AI capability disclosure on record. The Bank of England's published letter to the Treasury Select Committee names the shared FCA, NCSC and HMT workstream, which is unusually specific for an early-stage regulator response.

The second arc cut against the closed-weights direction Meta set earlier in the week with Muse Spark's invite-only API. MiniMax M2.7 went live on Hugging Face — 230 billion parameters sparse, 10 billion active, 256 experts across 62 layers, a 200K context window, and pricing at thirty cents per million input tokens and sixty per million output on the MiniMax API. The model targets self-evolving agent benchmarks via self-play and reportedly competes with models three times its size on agentic tasks. But the licence is the news. The "modified MIT" clause turns commercial use into a permission request, and a Hugging Face discussion thread already disputes whether the release qualifies as open-source at all. It lands one day before MMX-CLI on 12 April and exactly as Nathan Lambert publishes the cleanest argument yet that the for-profit funding structures behind Llama and Mistral will not survive another two years of frontier-cost inflation — and that a multi-company consortium is the only stable replacement.

The third thread is mostly about how the Mythos story now lives outside the financial press. CP24 in Toronto, Progressive Robot, and several syndicated outlets ran "too dangerous to release" explainers for general readers on Saturday; Via Satellite picked up the defense and space critical-infrastructure angle. Once a frontier-capability disclosure crosses into mainstream consumer press within 96 hours, the politics of future capability gating shift permanently. Anthropic, and any lab following, will stage the next disclosure with that consumer-press reflex already priced in. Meta's parallel choice to keep Muse Spark in a hand-picked private preview with no waitlist, no documented endpoint, and no third-party benchmarks visible by mid-April reads as a different answer to the same problem — control the distribution surface rather than the press cycle. Underneath both, the security literature is finally catching up to deployment: MCP-DPT publishes the first layer-aligned defense-placement taxonomy across MCP's six layers, Securing Retrieval-Augmented Generation maps six workflow surfaces against four attack surfaces, and Silencing the Guardrails demonstrates representation-level inference-time jailbreaks — all the same week MiniMax's agentic positioning pushes MCP further into the production stack.

## Research highlights

_Saturday — arXiv has no Saturday submission slot; HF Papers `/date/2026-04-11` redirects to Friday 2026-04-10. Picks below are the strongest items from that Friday cohort and late-week security/MCP submissions, surfaced as Saturday backfill. - **MCP-DPT: A Defense-Placement Taxonomy and Coverage Analysis for Model Context Protocol Security** — Mehrdad Rostamzadeh, Sidhant Narula. Layer-aligned taxonomy that organizes MCP attacks by which architectural component is responsible for enforcement; finds defenses unevenly distributed across MCP's six layers. First defense-placement view rather than the more common attack-catalog framing. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.07551 — cs.CR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07551)
- **Externalization in LLM Agents: A Unified Review of Memory, Skills, Protocols and Harness Engineering** — Chenyu Zhou, Huacan Chai. Frames the shift from "model recovers internally" to "memory stores + reusable skills + interaction protocols + harness" as cognitive externalization. Cleanest taxonomy for the agent-platform stack. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.08224 — cs.MA. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08224)
- **Securing Retrieval-Augmented Generation: A Taxonomy of Attacks, Defenses, and Future Directions** — Yuming Xu, Mingtao Zhang. Six-stage workflow surfaces, four primary attack surfaces, and a pivot from reactive patching toward layered, boundary-aware governance. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.08304 — cs.CR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08304)
- **Graph of Skills: Dependency-Aware Structural Retrieval for Massive Agent Skills** — Dawei Liu, Zongxia Li. Executable skill graph offline + retrieves bounded dependency-aware skill bundle at inference via semantic-lexical matching + PageRank. Sits between SKILL0 (in-weight) and SkillClaw (collective-evolution). _Category:_ arXiv:2604.05333 — cs.IR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05333)
- **Act Wisely: Cultivating Meta-Cognitive Tool Use in Agentic Multimodal Models** — Shilin Yan, Jintao Tong. HDPO separates tool-efficiency optimization from accuracy goals; orders-of-magnitude reduction in tool invocations while improving reasoning. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.08545 — cs.MA. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08545)
- **Cross-Tokenizer LLM Distillation through a Byte-Level Interface** — Avyav Kumar Singh, Yen-Chen Wu. Byte-Level Distillation (BLD) transfers knowledge between LLMs with different tokenizers via byte-level probabilities. Boring-but-useful infrastructure win as the model zoo fragments by tokenizer. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.07466 — cs.CL. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07466)
- **ClawBench: Can AI Agents Complete Everyday Online Tasks?** — Yuxuan Zhang, Yubo Wang. 153 tasks across 144 live websites; even the best frontier model lands at ~33% success. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.08523 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08523)
- **Silencing the Guardrails: Inference-Time Jailbreaking via Dynamic Contextual Representation Ablation** — Wenpeng Xing, Moran Fang. Identifies and suppresses refusal-inducing activation patterns at inference; demonstrates current alignment defenses are vulnerable to representation-level interventions. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.07835 — cs.CR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07835)
