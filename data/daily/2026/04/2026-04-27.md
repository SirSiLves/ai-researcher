## The day in 90 seconds

**Microsoft and OpenAI quietly end the Azure exclusivity era as Meta books millions of Amazon CPUs.**

The legal architecture of the LLM hyperscaler economy moved on a single Monday. Microsoft and OpenAI restructured their partnership into something the two sides will spend the rest of the year reinterpreting. Meta committed to tens of millions of Arm cores from AWS to run agents on CPUs. Anthropic opened its first office south of the equator. Read together, the AWS-Anthropic stack widened on exactly the day the Microsoft-OpenAI stack loosened.

The headline move is the amended Microsoft-OpenAI deal. Azure remains OpenAI's primary cloud, and OpenAI products still ship there first unless Microsoft cannot or chooses not to support a given capability, but Microsoft's IP license is now non-exclusive through 2032, the revenue-share to Microsoft runs only through 2030 and is for the first time subject to a total cap, and the AGI clause that would have voided Microsoft's commercial rights if AGI was declared has been quietly retired. Simon Willison's same-day history-tracker is already the canonical reference for what that clause was and what its disappearance means. Microsoft keeps its equity stake and trades a distribution moat for capex relief; OpenAI trades dependency for optionality. The CNBC angle that the revenue-share cap is what investors are reading first looks correct, and the practical consequence — a cleared legal path to multi-cloud — sets up tomorrow's AWS What's Next event, where the OpenAI-on-Bedrock launch is now widely expected to land.

The second thread is Meta's multi-billion-dollar agreement with AWS to deploy tens of millions of 192-core Graviton5 chips for agentic workloads — real-time reasoning, code generation, search, and multi-step task orchestration. AWS claims a 25 percent performance lift and 33 percent lower inter-core latency over Graviton4, and the deal makes Meta one of the five largest Graviton customers worldwide. The operative line from the announcement, that "no single chip architecture can efficiently serve every workload," is the clearest public signal yet that agent orchestration is CPU-bound, not GPU-bound, and that Meta's diversification away from a single GPU vendor is structural rather than opportunistic. Tom's Hardware reframes the year's infra story around this — GPU scarcity gave way to GPU surplus only to surface a CPU shortage at the orchestration layer.

The third thread is Anthropic, which spent the same day widening the AWS side of the stack. The Sydney office opened formally with Theo Hourmouzis, who ran Snowflake's ANZ and ASEAN business, as general manager — Anthropic's first APAC outpost beyond Tokyo, with Canva, Xero, and four Australian universities named as launch customers and partners. Hours later, AWS's weekly roundup confirmed that Claude Cowork, the desktop agent that summarises meetings, drafts documents, and executes multi-step tasks, is now generally available in Amazon Bedrock alongside a new Bedrock AgentCore CLI. Hiring a Snowflake regional chief and shipping Cowork into Bedrock the same day signals an enterprise GTM playbook converging on data-cloud-style account coverage — and lands precisely as Microsoft loosens OpenAI's collar.

Around the edges, the research layer was unusually crisp on a single theme: MCP is now the most-attacked surface in agent research. "Tool Attention Is All You Need" measures the MCP tax at 10,000 to 60,000 tokens per turn from eager schema injection and proposes dynamic tool gating with lazy schema loading; "Breaking MCP with Function Hijacking" defines a new attack class for tool-selection manipulation. Latent Agents on the same day distills multi-agent debate into a single fine-tuned model that matches or exceeds explicit debate at 93 percent fewer tokens — a direct cost-performance hit on the "is multi-agent worth it" question that has run since GPT-5.5. The radar shows the OpenAI-governance topic transitioning to "emerging" with positive momentum for the first time in the month, while the lab-by-consultancy GTM thread sits at the highest breadth of the day.

## Major news & releases

### Microsoft–OpenAI partnership restructure

**Microsoft–OpenAI restructure their partnership: non-exclusive license, revenue-share cap, Azure-first through 2032.** Microsoft and OpenAI announced an amended deal that simplifies their relationship. Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner and OpenAI products ship first on Azure (unless Microsoft cannot or chooses not to support a capability), but Microsoft's IP license is now non-exclusive through 2032, and the revenue-share to Microsoft continues only through 2030 and is now subject to a total cap. Microsoft retains its equity stake. The reset clears the legal path for OpenAI to multi-cloud — including the AWS Bedrock launch that lands the next morning. _Why it matters:_ This is the formal end of the Azure exclusivity that defined LLM hyperscaler economics since 2023. Microsoft trades a wider distribution moat for capex relief; OpenAI trades dependency for optionality. The cap on revenue-share payments is the most-cited change among investors. ### Meta–AWS Graviton5 agentic CPU deal

**Meta signs multi-billion-dollar AWS Graviton5 deal to run agentic AI on CPUs.** Meta announced an agreement with AWS to deploy tens of millions of Graviton5 Arm cores for CPU-intensive agentic AI workloads — real-time reasoning, code generation, search, and multi-step task orchestration. AWS's 3nm Graviton5 has 192 cores per chip with a cache 5× the previous generation, and AWS claims a 25% performance lift and 33% lower inter-core latency. The deal makes Meta one of the five largest Graviton customers worldwide. _Why it matters:_ This reframes the AI-infra story from GPU scarcity to CPU bottleneck for the inference/agent layer. "No single chip architecture can efficiently serve every workload" is the operative quote; the deal is the clearest public signal that agentic workloads are CPU-bound on the orchestration side — and that Meta's CapEx diversification away from a single GPU vendor is structural, not opportunistic. ## Research highlights

- **Latent Agents: A Post-Training Procedure for Internalized Multi-Agent Debate** — Distills multi-agent debate into a single LLM via two-stage fine-tuning; internalized models match or exceed explicit multi-agent debate using up to 93% fewer tokens. Direct cost/perf payoff for the "is multi-agent worth it?" debate. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.24881 — cs.CL. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24881)
- **Tool Attention Is All You Need: Dynamic Tool Gating and Lazy Schema Loading for Eliminating the MCP/Tools Tax** — Quantifies the "MCP tax": 10k-60k tokens per turn from stateless eager schema injection in typical multi-server deployments. Proposes dynamic tool gating + lazy schema loading. Practical infra fix for MCP-heavy agent stacks. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.21816 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21816)
- **Breaking MCP with Function Hijacking Attacks: Novel Threats for Function Calling and Agentic Models** — Defines a function-hijacking attack that manipulates the tool-selection process of agentic models to force invocation of a specific attacker-chosen function. Important data point for the MCP threat-model thread. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.20994 — cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20994)
- **Thinking Without Words: Efficient Latent Reasoning with Abstract Chain-of-Thought** — Post-training mechanism that produces a short sequence of tokens from a reserved discrete latent vocabulary in lieu of natural-language CoT before the response — a concrete "latent reasoning" alternative to explicit chains. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.22709 — cs.LG. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22709)
- **Self-Aware Vector Embeddings for RAG: A Neuroscience-Inspired Framework for Temporal, Confidence-Weighted, and Relational Knowledge** — SmartVector augments dense embeddings with temporal awareness, confidence decay, and relational awareness, plus a five-stage lifecycle modeled on hippocampal–neocortical consolidation. Pushes back on the "static, context-free embedding" assumption in production RAG. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.20598 — cs.IR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20598)
- **HARBOR: Automated Harness Optimization** — Automated optimization of agent harnesses — the layer between the model and the tools. A move toward learning the scaffolding rather than hand-tuning it; pairs naturally with Anthropic's 23 April harness-postmortem framing. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.20938 — cs.LG. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20938)
- **Outcome Rewards Do Not Guarantee Verifiable or Causally Important Reasoning** — Argues outcome-only RL rewards optimize for getting answers right without ensuring the underlying chain is verifiable or causally responsible — a structural critique of current RLHF/RLVR practice for reasoning models. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.22074 — cs.LG. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22074)
- **Structure Guided Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Factual Queries** — Existing RAG predominantly relies on vector similarity, which is prone to semantic noise and fails to guarantee responses satisfying complex conditions of factual queries; this work injects structural constraints into the retrieval step. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.22843 — cs.IR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22843)
