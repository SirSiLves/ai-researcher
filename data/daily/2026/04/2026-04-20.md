## The day in 90 seconds

**Anthropic puts $100 billion on AWS silicon as Google quietly splits its chip stack.**

Week 17 opened with a compute-policy doubleheader. Anthropic and Amazon expanded their pact to up to five gigawatts of AWS capacity, with Anthropic pledging more than $100 billion over ten years on Trainium and Graviton silicon. Hours later, reporting surfaced that Marvell is co-designing two new AI chips for Google — including a novel memory processing unit — pulling Broadcom's stock down on diversification fear and reframing the inference economics conversation. Underneath both, Foreign Policy gave the Anthropic gated-frontier playbook a foreign-affairs venue for the first time.

The structural move was Anthropic-Amazon. The terms are unusually concrete: $100 billion-plus from Anthropic over a decade on AWS technologies — Trainium2, Trainium3, Trainium4 and successors, plus tens of millions of Graviton CPU cores — in return for $5 billion fresh equity from Amazon at a $350 billion pre-money valuation, with up to $20 billion more tied to commercial milestones on top of $8 billion in prior staged investments. Total Amazon exposure runs toward $33 billion if every milestone hits. Capacity targets are equally hard-edged: up to five gigawatts in aggregate, with nearly one gigawatt of Trainium2 and Trainium3 online by end-2026. It is the deepest single-vendor multi-cloud commitment any frontier lab has made, and it is the supply-side answer to two months of Anthropic signalling that Claude demand is effectively uncapped. It also pre-positions AWS's Q1 narrative and re-prices the Google compute deal expected later this month.

The Google-Marvell story is more architectural than financial. Marvell is co-developing two chips for Google's stack: a memory processing unit that handles part of the computation rather than only storing data, and a new inference-optimised TPU. Manufacturing pilots target roughly two million MPUs within a year. Marvell closed up about six percent at $147.84; Broadcom dropped as the deal made Marvell Google's third chip-design partner alongside Broadcom on training TPUs and MediaTek on the Zebrafish inference part. The MPU concept matters more than the headline pop — compute-in-memory at the device level has been the unsolved variable in AI inference unit economics, and Google is putting a real bet on it. SiliconANGLE's preview the same day named eighth-generation TPUs split into 8t Sunfish (Broadcom, training) and 8i Zebrafish (MediaTek, inference), Workspace agent integrations, and a Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform rebrand of Vertex AI as the Cloud Next themes Pichai will anchor on Wednesday — confirming the supplier picture had already been baked into Google's keynote planning.

The third thread is governance and open-weights pressure, moving on parallel tracks. Foreign Policy ran a long-form on Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview, written from security leadership, arguing that capability-gated rollout through Project Glasswing is the only responsible path when a frontier model can find thousands of zero-days. The piece lands inside the EU AI Act trilogue and the GP-AI Code of Practice motion, and it is the first time the withhold-and-coordinate playbook gets flagship foreign-affairs framing rather than tech-press framing. Underneath, the cost story keeps moving: Moonshot's Kimi K2.6 landed on OpenRouter at $0.60 per million input and $2.50 per million output, a trillion-parameter MoE pitched at twelve-hour autonomous coding stamina; Simon Willison shipped llm-openrouter 0.6 with a refresh command the same day. Willison also quantified Anthropic's "Opus 4.7 uses 1.0 to 1.35x more tokens per input" claim — measured 1.46x on Opus 4.7's own system prompt versus 4.6, 1.08x on a 30-page PDF — yielding roughly a 40 percent effective price bump for text-heavy workloads at unchanged sticker pricing. By end of day Ewan Mak's Medium review was claiming 88 percent coding-cost savings versus Opus 4.7 on Kimi K2.6.

**Read this first:** [Anthropic — Amazon expand collaboration up to 5GW of compute](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute) — the single announcement that quantifies the rest of the week's compute, capital and pricing arcs.

### Google's chip-supplier diversification

**Marvell co-developing two AI chips with Google — MPU + new inference TPU — stock pops 7%.** Reports surfaced on 20 April that Google and Marvell Technology are co-developing two new AI chips for Google's stack: a **memory processing unit (MPU)** that handles part of the computation rather than just storing data (complementary to Google's TPUs), and a new **inference-optimised TPU**. Marvell shares closed up ~6% at $147.84; production target is ~2M MPUs in pilot manufacturing within a year. Broadcom stock dropped on the news as the deal makes Marvell Google's third chip design partner alongside Broadcom (training TPUs) and MediaTek (Zebrafish inference TPU). _Why it matters:_ This is the cleanest public signal that the inference side of the AI stack is becoming as architecturally specialised as training — and that Google is deliberately distributing chip design among multiple partners to avoid Broadcom dependency. The MPU concept (compute-in-memory at the device level) is novel enough to reshape the memory-bandwidth conversation that has dogged AI inference economics. ### Governance — Mythos crosses to Foreign Policy

**Anthropic's Foreign Policy op-ed: "Claude Mythos Preview changes the cyber calculus."** Foreign Policy on 20 April ran a long-form on the Mythos Preview disclosure, framed by Anthropic security leadership. The piece argues that capability-gated rollout via Project Glasswing is the only responsible path when a frontier model can find thousands of zero-days; that defenders' time-to-patch is now the bottleneck; and that nation-state actors are presumed to be building similar capabilities without consortium constraints. _Why it matters:_ Foreign Policy is a flagship venue for the "AI cybersecurity governance" narrative. The op-ed both legitimises Anthropic's withhold-and-coordinate choice and signals to policymakers that the technical capability is real, not theoretical — feeding into both the EU AI Act trilogue and the GP-AI Code of Practice motion. ### Cloud Next preview — agent control plane

**SiliconANGLE preview: Google Cloud Next 2026 — "the real story isn't AI, it's the control plane."** On 20 April SiliconANGLE published the Cloud Next 2026 preview: Google's pitch will recast the entire cloud stack around the agent control plane, not the model. The preview names the new **Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform** (formerly Vertex AI), **eighth-generation TPUs split into training (8t/Sunfish/Broadcom) and inference (8i/Zebrafish/MediaTek)**, and Workspace agent integrations as the headline themes. _Why it matters:_ SiliconANGLE's framing — "the real story isn't AI, it's the control plane" — captures how Google positions itself differently than OpenAI/Anthropic. Where the labs sell models, Google sells the substrate (TPUs, agent runtime, identity, observability) and lets customers mix in any model via Model Garden. The preview lands two days before the Pichai keynote.
