## The day in 90 seconds

**Anthropic's $965B raise overtakes OpenAI as AI security shifts from finding bugs to fixing them**

The richest private company in AI today is Anthropic, not OpenAI — and the second-biggest story of the day is that frontier models have started finding software vulnerabilities faster than anyone can patch them. Underneath both: a clear tilt toward cheap, fast, agentic models as the default, and the first genuinely loud note of AI fatigue on the front page.

The day's structural shift was in security. Anthropic's Project Glasswing surfaced more than 10,000 high and critical vulnerabilities across 1,000-plus open-source projects in about 30 days, including a 27-year-old OpenBSD remote-crash bug and a 16-year FFmpeg flaw. The punchline is that models can now out-discover human remediation capacity, so the bottleneck has moved from finding bugs to fixing them — and the enterprise platform layer answered at frontier-lab scale. IBM and Red Hat committed $5B to Project Lightwell, a clearinghouse backed by 20,000-plus engineers, with Bank of America, Citi, Goldman Sachs, JPMorganChase, Morgan Stanley and Visa among its early adopters. It explicitly builds on Glasswing and OpenAI's cyber programs.

Beneath the headlines, the releases pointed one direction: cheap and fast. Google shipped Gemini 3.5 Flash to general availability with frontier agentic scores (76.2% Terminal-Bench 2.1, 83.6% MCP Atlas) at one-half to one-third the cost of comparable models, betting the agent era on low-cost defaults rather than flagship-benchmark chasing. Mistral pushed into simulation with Physics AI and a unified "Vibe" work agent. The counter-current was tonal: an unusually large skeptical cluster trended on Hacker News at once — AI-fatigue, cost-rationing ("Corporate America is rationing AI as cost skyrockets"), and AI-job grief — a measurable shift from the build-everything optimism of recent days, even as "Domain expertise has always been the real moat" topped the board at 779 points.

## Research highlights

- **[DeepWeb-Bench (2605.21482)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21482)** — Evaluates nine frontier models on open-web deep research; retrieval is not the bottleneck (12–14% of errors) while derivation and calibration failures account for 70%+. The diagnostic the agentic-search field needs.
- **[RL for LLM Multi-Agent Systems through Orchestration Traces (2605.02801)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02801)** — RL over orchestration traces (spawn, delegate, communicate, tool-use, aggregate, stop) with eight reward families and five sub-decisions — a structured taxonomy for training orchestrators.
- **[LatentRAG (2605.06285)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06285)** — Shifts both reasoning and retrieval into continuous latent space, producing latent thought/subquery tokens in a single forward pass and aligning the LLM with a dense retriever in latent space.
- **[LEMON (2605.14483)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14483)** — An LLM orchestrator emits an executable orchestration spec (roles, duties, capacity, dependency structure), trained with counterfactual RL into a single deployable multi-agent system.
- **[MultiBreak (2605.01687)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01687)** — 10,389 multi-turn adversarial prompts across 2,665 distinct harmful intents — a large benchmark for safety under conversational, multi-turn attack pressure.
- **[Structure Retention in Embedding Spaces (2605.22202)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22202)** — Across 25 embedding models on five MTEB tasks, high performers organize their embedding space consistently — structure retention predicts downstream benchmark performance.
- **[AgenticRAG (2605.05538)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05538)** — A practical agentic harness over enterprise KBs that lifts the fixed-candidate-set constraint of standard RAG by letting the model drive grounding.
