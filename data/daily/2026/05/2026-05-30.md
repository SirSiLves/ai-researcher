## The day in 90 seconds

**Agentic coding leaves the cloud for the desktop, and gets its first big enterprise scoreboard**

The story today is agentic coding growing up on two fronts at once: it moved onto the developer's own machine, and it finally produced a hard enterprise number. OpenAI shipped the proof of reach; Salesforce shipped the proof of return. Underneath, a noisy Hacker News fight over whether the MCP protocol is "dead" showed the plumbing layer is still very much being argued over — while the day's quieter governance news drifted away from model policy and toward power grids and malware.

OpenAI released Codex v2.5 for Windows on May 29, bringing its "Computer Use" mode — previously Mac-only — to Windows 11. The agent now sees the screen, clicks, types, and drives local apps and terminals to test software and hunt bugs under supervision, and can be steered remotely from the ChatGPT mobile app. It runs foreground-only and needs a Copilot+ class NPU rated above 40 TOPS. The shift matters because it pulls agentic coding off the cloud sandbox and onto the developer's own desktop, edging toward agents that operate a whole machine rather than just a repository — and raising the obvious supervision and trust questions that come with letting a model touch local tools.

The demand-side counterweight came from Salesforce, which moved its entire engineering org to Claude Code on unlimited tokens and published April-2026 results: 79% more pull requests per developer, roughly 5% fewer incidents, and a 50.8% year-over-year rise in work items completed per developer. The headline anecdote is a 33-endpoint API migration estimated at 231 person-days that finished in 13 — about 18 times faster. It is one of the largest first-party data points yet on agentic-coding ROI inside a major software vendor, and the kind of number that will get cited in every "does this actually work" meeting for months. The figures are Salesforce's own and have not been independently audited, so read them as a vendor's best case rather than a neutral benchmark.

The third thread is whether the connective tissue under all these agents is consolidating or fracturing. A 353-point Hacker News thread titled "MCP is dead?" turned into a live referendum on the Model Context Protocol, with OpenAI's own MCP and App-Store lead jumping in to rebut the post, and a commenter pointing out that its benchmarks predate the November-2025 deferred tool-loading change — making the underlying data roughly seven months stale. As often happens, the thread is more informative than the article. Meanwhile governance kept moving into the physical world: FERC is heading toward a June deadline to rewrite how large loads above 20 MW connect to the grid, with a 100%-participant-funding model that would make data-center customers pay the full cost of the upgrades they trigger — a sharp break from socialized-cost norms, and possibly the binding constraint on AI buildout. And Push Security documented "LLMShare," a maturing attack that weaponizes shared ChatGPT and Claude conversation links to deliver malware from trusted domains that security tools don't flag.

**Read this first:** [Salesforce's engineering org goes "agentic" on Claude Code](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/how-engineering-became-agentic/) — the single biggest enterprise number on agentic-coding ROI to date, and the one item to open before your next "does this actually pay off" conversation.

## Major news & releases

**OpenAI ships Codex v2.5 for Windows with autonomous "Computer Use"** — Released May 29, Codex on Windows 11 gains the Computer Use mode previously limited to Mac: the agent sees the screen, clicks, types, and operates local apps, terminals and dev tools to test apps and hunt bugs under user supervision, plus remote control from the ChatGPT mobile app. It runs foreground-only on the active desktop and needs a Copilot+ class NPU (40+ TOPS). _Why it matters:_ agentic coding moves off the cloud onto the developer's own machine — a step toward agents that drive a full desktop, not just a repo. ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/codex-for-almost-everything/) · The Decoder, Neowin)

**Salesforce reports its engineering org has gone "agentic" on Claude Code** — Salesforce moved its entire dev org to Claude Code with unlimited tokens and published April-2026 results: 79% more pull requests per developer, ~5% fewer incidents, and a 50.8% YoY rise in work items completed per developer; the flagship anecdote is a 33-endpoint API migration estimated at 231 person-days finished in 13 days (~18×). _Why it matters:_ one of the largest first-party data points yet on agentic-coding ROI inside a major software vendor, and a notable Claude Code proof point. ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/how-engineering-became-agentic/) · The Decoder)

**Attackers weaponize ChatGPT and Claude shared-chat links to spread malware ("LLMShare")** — Push Security documented a maturing pattern: adversaries craft public shared conversations that mimic outage notices or install guides, surface them via paid search ads, and abuse code-rendering to build fake error pages or laced "Apple support" walkthroughs with malicious Terminal commands. Because links sit on trusted openai.com / claude.ai domains, security tools don't flag them; now seen across ChatGPT, Claude and Grok. _Why it matters:_ public chat-sharing has become live malware-delivery infrastructure on trusted AI domains — a new abuse surface vendors and security teams must defend. ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/attackers-abuse-shared-chatgpt-and-claude-chats-to-spread-malware/) · BleepingComputer, Push Security)

**FERC heads toward a June deadline to rewrite large-load grid rules for AI data centers** — FERC is preparing a June proposal governing how large loads (>20 MW, including AI data centers) connect to the interstate transmission system, built on a 14-principle directive. A core feature: a 100% participant-funding model requiring data-center customers to pay the full cost of network upgrades they trigger — a sharp break from socialized-cost norms. OpenAI, Microsoft and Google have all filed. _Why it matters:_ power interconnection is the binding constraint on AI buildout. ([POWER Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/ferc-sets-june-deadline-to-rewrite-large-load-grid-rules-for-ai-era-power-demand/) · Politico, E&E News)

**Genesis AI releases Genesis World 1.0, a physics platform for robotics-model evaluation** — Announced May 27, the four-part stack (physics engine, real-time path-traced renderer Nyx, Python-to-GPU compiler Quadrants, simulation interface) targets the robotics dev bottleneck: evaluations that took ~a week of real-robot testing now run in ~30 minutes in sim, with a claimed 89% correlation to on-hardware rollouts and a 45%-smaller reality gap, using zero sim training data. _Why it matters:_ reframes the robotics "sim-to-real" gap as a compute/eval problem — useful infra for robotics foundation models. ([Genesis](https://www.genesis.ai/blog/the-role-of-simulation-in-scalable-robotics-genesis-world-10-and-the-path-forward) · MarkTechPost)

**Aleph Alpha and Cohere in merger talks for a "sovereign AI" alliance with Canada** — Handelsblatt reports Germany's Aleph Alpha (Heidelberg) and Canada's Cohere are discussing a tie-up framed around joint Germany–Canada sovereign-AI ambitions. _Why it matters:_ consolidation among the Western non-US-frontier labs — a signal that "sovereign AI" is moving from policy rhetoric toward corporate combination. ([Handelsblatt](https://www.handelsblatt.com/politik/deutschland/kuenstliche-intelligenz-allianz-mit-kanada-aleph-alpha-und-cohere-sprechen-ueber-fusion/100211624.html))

**Qdrant 1.18 ships TurboQuant, Google Research's training-free vector quantization** — TurboQuant (ICLR 2026) applies a fast Hadamard rotation before compression and needs no per-dataset training, calibration set, or codebooks, claiming SQ-level recall at 2× less memory; the 2-bit/1-bit variants match binary-quant storage budgets at higher recall. _Why it matters:_ cheaper, calibration-free quantization makes large vector indexes meaningfully more memory-efficient for RAG at scale. ([Qdrant](https://qdrant.tech/articles/turboquant-quantization/) · Google Research, Towards Data Science)

## Research highlights

**[OmniRetrieval: Unified Retrieval across Heterogeneous Knowledge Sources](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29250)** — Baek, Jeong, Park, Yeo et al. A single retriever spanning unstructured text, relational tables, knowledge graphs and property graphs — addresses RAG fragmentation behind fixed query languages. _(2605.29250)_

**[AgentDoG 1.5: A Lightweight, Scalable Alignment Framework for AI Agent Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29801)** — Liu, Li, Yang, Wang et al. Open-world agents introduce broad new safety risks as frontier models lower attack barriers; proposes a lightweight alignment framework for real-world deployment. _(2605.29801)_

**[Alignment Tampering: How RLHF Is Exploited to Optimize Misaligned Biases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27355)** — Hahm, Hadfield-Menell, Lee. A model under alignment can influence its own preference dataset, causing RLHF to amplify undesired behaviors. _(2605.27355)_

**[AsyncTool: Evaluating Asynchronous Function Calling under Multi-Task Scenarios](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27995)** — Shi, Zhang, Huang, Nie et al. Benchmarks tool-use under realistic tool-response latency and concurrent multi-task settings — a dimension prior function-calling evals overlook. _(2605.27995)_

**[When Cloud Agents Meet Device Agents: Lessons from Hybrid Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30102)** — Rainone, Belli, Major, Behboodi. Cost/performance trade-offs of pairing cloud frontier LLMs with on-device small models in hybrid multi-agent inference. _(2605.30102)_

**[LaRA: Layer-wise Representation Analysis for Detecting Data Contamination in RL Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29888)** — Gwak, Kwak, Lee, Son et al. First focused look at contamination during RL post-training, with a layer-wise detector to protect generalization and eval reliability. _(2605.29888)_

**[Towards Verifiable Multimodal Deep Research: A Multi-Agent Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29861)** — Zhang, Dong, Liu, Zhao et al. Moves agents from deep search to deep research — synthesizing scattered multimodal evidence into verifiable long-form reports. _(2605.29861)_

**[How LoRA Remembers? A Parametric Memory Law for LLM Finetuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30260)** — Xu, Hong, Yu, Cui et al. Quantifies the capacity limits of LoRA-based knowledge updates, moving beyond qualitative downstream evals. _(2605.30260)_
