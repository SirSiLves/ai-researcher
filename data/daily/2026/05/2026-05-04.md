## The day in 90 seconds

**Anthropic and OpenAI both go after the consulting industry on the same Monday the Pentagon freezes Anthropic out.**

Two of the three frontier labs spent Monday building services firms, not models. Anthropic, Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs launched a $1.5 billion standalone enterprise-AI firm aimed at mid-market companies; OpenAI is reportedly structuring a near-identical vehicle with TPG and Bain. Three days earlier, the Department of Defense signed AI contracts with everyone except Anthropic — and Anthropic is now suing the government.

The biggest story was the standalone firm. Anthropic, Blackstone, H&F, and Goldman put $1.5 billion behind a new enterprise-AI services company, joined by General Atlantic, Leonard Green, Apollo, GIC, and Sequoia. The pitch is not consulting in the McKinsey sense — Anthropic engineers embed directly into mid-market customers' core operations under an "embedded engineering" model that bills against outcomes rather than slide decks. TechCrunch confirmed within hours that OpenAI is structuring a near-identical vehicle with TPG and Bain. Read together, that is the frontier labs conceding what they have hinted at all year: at the Fortune 5000, the model layer is not the product. Implementation is the product, and the labs intend to own that revenue rather than hand it to McKinsey, Accenture, and BCG — who, by Monday afternoon, were the only large incumbents in the consulting industry without a frontier-lab joint venture behind them.

The counterweight came from the Pentagon. On May 1, DoD signed AI contracts with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, AWS, Nvidia, SpaceX, and Reflection AI — and explicitly excluded Anthropic, citing supply-chain risk after Anthropic refused to grant "all lawful" use of Claude over concerns about domestic surveillance and autonomous weapons. Anthropic is contesting the designation in court. This is the first visible commercial penalty for a frontier lab taking a principled use-policy stance, and it lands in the same week the same lab is announcing a $1.5 billion private-sector services firm with three of Wall Street's largest balance sheets. The signal to every other lab CEO is unambiguous: the federal contract premium now comes with a binding policy concession, and the private-sector premium does not. Anthropic appears to have chosen — and the Goldman/Blackstone deal looks, in that light, less like a separate announcement and more like a hedge written the same week the federal door closed.

Underneath the deals, the production stack also moved. Mistral shipped Medium 3.5 on April 29 — a 128-billion-parameter dense model with a 256K context window under a modified MIT license, hitting 77.6% on SWE-Bench Verified and 91.4 on tau-three-Telecom while beating Devstral 2 and Qwen3.5 397B on coding. Its companion product, Remote Agents in Vibe, runs in isolated cloud sandboxes and opens GitHub pull requests autonomously when work is done. Cloudflare used Agents Week to ship the Rust-based Infire engine across multiple GPUs with RDMA for direct GPU-to-GPU context transfer, splitting prefill and decode onto separate hardware, lifting KV-cache hit rates from 60% to 80%, and claiming a three-times speedup on Kimi K2.5 over their prior stack — a Llama 4 Scout now fits on two H200s with 56 gibibytes of headroom for KV cache. Google Cloud Next 2026 closed the loop on the platform side with managed MCP servers, the A2A protocol now production-grade, and Project Mariner clearing 83.5% on WebVoyager — and explicitly inviting Anthropic into its 200-plus-model catalog while the Pentagon was shutting Anthropic out. Salesforce, separately, has now exposed its entire platform via headless APIs so agents can act on data and workflows without going through the UI, conceding that "agent-native" is the destination and the UI is no longer the dependency. Amazon, in regulated healthcare, quietly launched a free 24/7 AI health agent for Prime members through One Medical. The pattern is consistent: the labs are racing to monetize implementation while the platform vendors race to make implementation cheap.
