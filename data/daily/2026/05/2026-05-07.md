## The day in 90 seconds

**Five frontier labs ship on the same day; Anthropic books 300 MW of Colossus compute; EU regulators end their trilogue suspense.**

The second-half-of-2026 capacity story lined up in a single working day. OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and Mistral each pushed a flagship release between dawn and dusk; Brussels closed the open question hanging over the EU AI Act; and MCP quietly stopped being an experiment in three of the largest cloud estates. The day reads less like five stories and more like one structural moment told from five angles.

The model releases came as a wave. OpenAI shipped GPT-5.5 Instant as ChatGPT's default with a 52.5 percent reduction in hallucinations on high-stakes medical, legal and financial prompts, alongside a Spreadsheets surface that lands inside Excel and Sheets, and announced a $122 billion raise plus a US Department of Energy compute deal. Anthropic put Claude Opus 4.7 into general availability with vision and coding gains, doubled rate limits across Pro, Max and Enterprise, and signed for 300-plus megawatts of capacity from SpaceX's Colossus I in Memphis — the first concrete supply-side answer to the running "compute is the constraint" framing. Ten financial-services agent templates covering KYC, pitchbooks and month-end close shipped into Cowork on the same day Anthropic told customers that financial services is now its second-largest revenue source. Google made Gemini Embedding 2 generally available on API and Vertex AI as a stable multimodal text/image/video/audio primitive and pushed Workspace Intelligence into Gmail, Chat, Calendar and Drive with admin data-source controls. Meta opened its inaugural LlamaCon with the Llama API entering free preview, one-click keys for Llama 4 Scout and Maverick, and Cerebras and Groq partnerships pointing at the inference roadmap. Mistral closed the voice gap with Voxtral TTS at $0.016 per thousand characters in twenty multilingual voices, and promoted Medium 3.5 — a 128-billion-parameter dense model — to the default in Le Chat and Vibe with async cloud coding and remote agents. The Llama transition from open weights to a managed Meta-run platform may be the most consequential governance change underneath the noise.

Brussels resolved the trilogue uncertainty that had been overhanging compliance teams since yesterday. The Council and Parliament reached political agreement to simplify the AI Act: regulatory sandboxes are deferred to August 2027, the synthetic-content transparency grace period is cut from six months to three and now expires December 2 2026, and the full Act remains applicable on August 2 2026. The August cliff has not moved; the synthetic-content deadline has tightened. Compliance leads who were planning against a six-month grace have three. Read together with the Stanford HAI Index's 8x compliance-cost variance across jurisdictions and the AISI UK note that cyber-task length is doubling every 4.7 months, the picture in Brussels and Washington is converging on bilateral and multilateral action rather than purely EU regulatory action.

The connective tissue is MCP. AWS shipped its MCP Server into general availability with IAM guardrails, CloudWatch metrics and CloudTrail logging — the third hyperscaler MCP endorsement in a week and the one that makes MCP the de-facto agent-to-cloud verb across AWS, Google and Microsoft. Anthropic's finance agents and Meta's ad-account MCP integrations both landed the same day, and the radar shows MCP security CVEs and governance posts rising in parallel. The papers track agrees: five of today's top picks — governed multi-agent memory, query-time retrieval beating Mem0 by more than thirty points, sub-400ms agent discovery, tree-based credit assignment, evolving idea graphs — are memory or retrieval-architecture work, while a counterweight paper shows single-agent reasoning matching multi-agent setups at equal compute. Practitioner reading from Karpathy's 200-line microGPT, Willison's vibe-to-agentic convergence post and Every's compound-engineering essay all rhyme with the LinkedIn thesis that the RAG era is ending and the knowledge layer is next.

## Research & papers

**Governed Collaborative Memory as Artificial Selection in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems** — Cuadros et al. _Takeaway:_ Treats persistent multi-agent memory as a selection problem; layered architecture with constitutional / metric / human-ratified selection regimes plus provenance and version control. _Category:_ cs.MA. [arxiv 2605.04264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04264)

**Storage Is Not Memory: A Retrieval-Centered Architecture for Agent Recall** — Adler & Zehavi. _Takeaway:_ A six-layer SQLite-based agent memory system that preserves verbatim events and resolves them at query time; 93% accuracy on LoCoMo vs. 61.4% for Mem0. _Category:_ cs.IR/cs.AI. [arxiv 2605.04897](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04897)

**GRAIL: Deep Agentic Web Exploration for Agent Discovery** — Xu et al. _Takeaway:_ Sub-400ms agent discovery via fine-tuned SLM capability tagging + pseudo-document expansion; 79× faster than LLM parsing while beating vector search. _Category:_ cs.IR. [arxiv 2605.02489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02489)

**Tree-based Credit Assignment for Multi-Agent Memory System** — Mao et al. _Takeaway:_ RL-driven credit assignment for builder–summarizer–retrieval pipelines via tree-structured Monte Carlo averaging; trains heterogeneous specialization without task-specific labels. _Category:_ cs.MA. [arxiv 2605.04811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04811)

**Evolving Idea Graphs with Learnable Edits-and-Commits for Multi-Agent Scientific Ideation** — Dong et al. _Takeaway:_ Graph-structured multi-agent ideation with learned edit/commit control; outperforms text-only coordination on novelty/feasibility/clarity. _Category:_ cs.MA. [arxiv 2605.04922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04922)

**SWE-WebDevBench: Evaluating Coding Agent Application Platforms as Virtual Software Agencies** — Saxena et al. _Takeaway:_ 68-metric eval framework across six agentic-app platforms reveals a specification bottleneck and security failures even when output looks correct. [arxiv 2605.04637](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04637)

**Single-Agent LLMs Outperform Multi-Agent Systems on Multi-Hop Reasoning Under Equal Thinking Token Budgets** — _Takeaway:_ When normalized to equal compute, single-agent reasoning matches or beats multi-agent — useful counterweight to today's multi-agent memory wave. [arxiv 2604.02460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460)

**Benchmarking Retrieval Strategies for Biomedical RAG** — _Takeaway:_ Cross-encoder reranking dominates dense, hybrid, and expansion methods in biomedical RAG; contextual precision 0.852. [arxiv 2605.02520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02520)

**ARMATA: Auto-Regressive Multi-Agent Task Assignment** — Youssef et al. _Takeaway:_ End-to-end autoregressive allocation+routing; ~20% over Google OR-Tools / IBM CPLEX baselines. [arxiv 2605.04225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04225)

**Terminus-4B: Can a Smaller Model Replace Frontier LLMs at Agentic Execution Tasks?** — _Takeaway:_ Cost-efficiency study of finetuned SLMs vs. frontier models on terminal-execution agent tasks. [arxiv 2605.03195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03195)
