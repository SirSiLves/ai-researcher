## The day in 90 seconds

**Anthropic and OpenAI both move into enterprise-execution mode on the same Saturday.**

The frontier labs stopped competing on benchmarks and started competing on installation crews. Anthropic put self-hosted sandboxes and MCP tunnels in front of regulated buyers; OpenAI raised more than $4 billion to industrialise forward-deployed engineering as a standalone Palantir-shaped company. Underneath, the Model Context Protocol shipped its biggest revision since launch — and SAP wired Claude into the European ERP base while doubling n8n's valuation to $5.2 billion.

The lead story is the symmetry. Anthropic's May 22 update moves Managed Agents into the configuration regulated buyers have been waiting for: self-hosted sandboxes keep sensitive files, packages, and services inside the customer network while Anthropic orchestrates context, recovery, and multi-agent runs from outside, and MCP tunnels collapse what used to be N inbound holes per Claude surface into a single outbound connection. Stacklok's Craig McLuckie, who has been running tunnels in production for months, calls it the ceiling the laptop-as-broker model was about to hit. On the same weekend OpenAI launched its Deployment Company with TPG, Advent, Bain Capital, and Brookfield as co-leads, a nineteen-firm consortium behind it, and the Tomoro acquisition seeding 150 forward-deployed engineers from day one. It is the corporate-vehicle answer to Anthropic's KPMG, PwC, and Infosys partnerships, and it concedes the same point: deploying frontier AI into the Fortune 500 is a separate, capital-intensive business, not a research-lab side project.

The structural counterweight is the protocol layer catching up to the deployment story. The MCP 2026-07-28 release candidate replaces sticky-session sprawl with a stateless core, gives long-running work a contract through the Tasks extension, adds server-rendered UI through MCP Apps, and swaps ad-hoc auth for OAuth/OIDC alignment — with a ten-week implementer validation window and a formal deprecation policy. AWS, ServiceNow, Snowflake, and Weaviate are already shipping against it. The spec changes fix exactly the failure modes Saurabh Kurimella's "Why Most Enterprise MCP Architectures Are Already Broken" named yesterday; the deployment-layer race needs a protocol that holds at load, and it now has one. SAP Sapphire is the immediate proof point: Joule Studio enters Q3 with Claude as a primary reasoning engine, AWS for zero-copy data, Google Cloud and Microsoft for bidirectional A2A interop, NVIDIA for secure runtime, Mistral and Cohere as sovereign options, and n8n — newly valued at $5.2 billion after SAP's strategic stake doubled the October 2025 round — as the visual orchestration layer. Mercedes-Benz separately launched a global workflow-automation platform on n8n in the same window. The German workflow vendor is now the de-facto orchestration layer for European enterprise.

The third thread is the EU AI Act moving from a calendar fight to a scope fight. The Omnibus political agreement on 7 May deferred Annex III high-risk obligations sixteen months to 2 December 2027 and Annex I to 2 August 2028, added two prohibitions for non-consensual intimate AI imagery and CSAM-generating systems, and extended the generative-AI watermarking transitional window. On 19 May the Commission opened draft high-risk classification guidelines for consultation through 23 June — the most important EU-AI lobbying moment of the year. The productivity-and-optimisation exemption and the new permission for special-categories processing in bias mitigation are the substantive levers; Thierry Zoller's Pulse summary is the cleanest board-level rundown in circulation. Pair it with Ken Priore's data point that 53% of production MCP servers still use long-lived static secrets and only 8.5% use OAuth, and the governance gap that Article 14 was written for is staring straight at the agent stack everyone shipped this week.

## Research highlights

- **Gated DeltaNet-2: Decoupling Erase and Write in Linear Attention** — Hatamizadeh, Choi, Kautz. Linear-attention variant that separately gates erase and write into a fixed-size recurrent state, editing the compressed cache without scrambling existing associations. ([arXiv 2605.22791](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22791))
- **Vector Policy Optimization: Training for Diversity Improves Test-Time Search** — Bahlous-Boldi et al. Post-training that optimizes a vector of rewards instead of a scalar, so inference-time search procedures can actually find better rollouts. ([arXiv 2605.22817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22817))
- **Evaluating Commercial AI Chatbots as News Intermediaries** — Suzgun, Shen, Bianchi, Spangher. 14-day evaluation of six chatbots on how accurately their proprietary search + RAG pipelines surface emerging news across languages — first systematic look at retrieval-synthesis accuracy in shipped products. ([arXiv 2605.22785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22785))
- **MOSS: Self-Evolution through Source-Level Rewriting in Autonomous Agent Systems** — Cai et al. Agent rewrites its own source code as the evolution substrate, larger search space than prompt/skill mutation. ([arXiv 2605.22794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22794))
- **LCGuard: Latent Communication Guard for Safe KV Sharing in Multi-Agent Systems** — Asif, Mohammadi Amiri et al. Runtime check on KV-cache messages between agents to block jailbreaks/poisoning while preserving efficiency gains. ([arXiv 2605.22786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22786))
- **DeltaBox: Millisecond-Level Sandbox Checkpoint/Rollback for Stateful AI Agents** — Dong et al. Delta-encoded full sandbox snapshots cut C/R from hundreds of ms to single-digit ms, making high-frequency tree-search RL over real shells tractable. ([arXiv 2605.22781](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22781))
- **Maestro: RL to Orchestrate Hierarchical Model-Skill Ensembles** — Wu, Zhai et al. RL-trained orchestrator dynamically routes among a pool of LLMs and skills instead of pinning one model behind a fixed graph. ([arXiv 2605.22177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22177))
- **Efficient Agentic Reasoning Through Self-Regulated Simulative Planning** — Deng et al. Trains the agent to decide when to plan and how deeply; cuts token usage without losing accuracy. ([arXiv 2605.22138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22138))
