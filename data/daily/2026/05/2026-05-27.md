## Research highlights

- **[The MiniMax-M2 Series: Mini Activations Unleashing Max Real-World Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26494)** — MiniMax (~200 authors). Flagship MoE LLM, 229.9B total / 9.8B activated per token, explicitly designed end-to-end as "agent-native" rather than "chat-native." Agent-driven data pipelines producing verifiable trajectories. One of the few open releases this month positioning a new frontier-tier weight class as agent-first.
- **[VitaBench 2.0: Evaluating Personalized and Proactive Agents in Long-Term User Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27141)** — Meituan + NUS. Agents collaborating from *fragmented* interaction history rather than current request; adds personalized-modeling and proactive-interaction dimensions over long horizons. Constructive counterpart to drift/failure benchmarks — measures whether the agent remembers and acts on user state. HF-trending.
- **[MUSE-Autoskill: Self-Evolving Agents via Skill Creation, Memory, Management, and Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27366)** — Lin et al. Skill-centric framework operationalising skill creation, memory, lifecycle, and per-skill evaluation as first-class loops. Direct response to yesterday's SkillEvolBench finding that raw-trajectory reuse outperforms distilled skills — proposes the missing management layer. Important for anyone shipping Anthropic-Skills-style libraries or persistent agent runtimes.
- **[Efficient Agentic Reinforcement Learning with On-Policy Intrinsic Knowledge Boundary Enhancement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26952)** — Chen et al. Identifies a specific pathology of agentic RL training: policy learns to call tools redundantly, loses tools-needed-vs-parametric-suffices distinction. Useful diagnostic for anyone running RLVR / GRPO over tool-using agents — the redundant-tool-call failure mode is showing up in production.
- **[Learning to Act under Noise: Enhancing Agent Robustness via Noisy Environments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27209)** — Chen et al. (same Meituan/NUS group as VitaBench 2.0 — paired contributions). Documents the train-vs-deployment gap on tool-using agents: benchmarks are strong while production degrades from stochastic latency, partial tool failures, format drift. Trains under deliberately noisy environments and recovers robustness without hurting clean scores.
- **[UnityMAS-O: A General RL Optimization Framework for LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26646)** — Renmin/Tsinghua/Xiaohongshu. Unified RL post-training over user-defined multi-agent workflows with structured interaction + role-specific credit assignment. "The orchestration layer is the optimization target, not the prompts" — directly resonant with this week's harness-scaling framing.
- **[MemFail: Stress-Testing Failure Modes of LLM Memory Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26667)** — UC Berkeley. Breaks memory benchmarks into specific failure-mode taxonomies: retrieval gap, source confusion, write conflict, capacity overflow, summarization drift. Pairs cleanly with yesterday's MemIR provenance-role-collapse work — start of a memory-quality testing suite for persistent-memory agents in production.
- **[Rethinking Agentic RAG: Toward LLM-Driven Logical Retrieval Beyond Embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27123)** — Fudan. Argues "embed query, top-k by similarity" is the wrong substrate for agentic retrieval. Proposes logical-retrieval where the LLM directly drives the retrieval-decision process. Sits next to LLM-Wiki, RICE-PO, ICICLE in the rapidly forming "RAG-as-tool-use, not RAG-as-lookup" thesis.
- **[The Attribution Blind Spot: Detecting When Language Models Rely on Memory Rather Than Retrieved Context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26778)** — Yu et al. Standard RAG test (does answer match retrieved doc?) fails when the retrieved doc overlaps with pretraining data. Develops detectors distinguishing parametric-recall from retrieval-governed generation. Critical primitive for any high-stakes RAG deployment where grounding is a compliance requirement.
- **[Position: AI Safety Requires Effective Controllability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27117)** — Li, Feng, Sun. Reframes safety around effective controllability — stop conditions, override paths, constraint enforcement — as separable from preference alignment. Notable framing shift for EU AI Act / NIST AI RMF / ISO 42001 conversations where "human oversight" is a binding requirement that controllability operationalises.
- **[Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26526)** — CMU. Recent defenses assume harmful behavior is *learned through* fine-tuning rather than *elicited from* the base model. Authors show this breaks under simple attacks. Direct relevance to the open-weight licensing / ethical-use-clause debate; together with yesterday's Governance Horizon paper, downstream restriction mechanisms (license clauses, defenses, lineage) all decay sharply and predictably.
