## The day in 90 seconds

**OpenAI and Anthropic split the cybersecurity market the same Friday Cloudflare cuts 1,100 jobs while booking record revenue.**

The cybersecurity-application layer and the labour-market story landed on the same day, and they reframe each other. Both frontier labs moved on enterprise cyber-defence within hours of one another, and a profitable, growing cloud-peer used its earnings call to cut a fifth of its staff and blame the agents it sells.

The headline event is structural. OpenAI launched Daybreak, an AI cyber-defence platform built on a three-tier lineup — GPT-5.5 for general work, a GPT-5.5 variant with Trusted Access for Cyber for verified defensive tasks in authorised environments, and a permissive GPT-5.5-Cyber for red-teaming and pentesting. Day-one partners under Trusted Access include Akamai, Cisco, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, Fortinet, Oracle, Palo Alto, Zscaler, Okta, SentinelOne, Rapid7, Qualys and Snyk. Hours later, Snyk shipped its Claude integration into the Snyk AI Security Platform, putting Anthropic reasoning behind vulnerability discovery, prioritisation and developer-ready fixes across code, dependencies, containers and AI-generated artifacts. The same roster appears on both sides. The market did not split into camps so much as it forked through the same channel, and Snyk is now the load-bearing partner for an explicit competitive response to Anthropic's Project Glasswing and Mythos narrative. This is the most direct attempt yet to monetise frontier-model cybersecurity capability through a vetted-access enterprise tier, and it lands the same week the Mythos Preview's 73 percent expert-hacking-task success rate is being read in Washington as grounds for pre-deployment oversight.

The second thread is Cloudflare. The company announced on Thursday-evening earnings — crossing through Friday — that it is cutting roughly 1,100 staff, about 20 percent of its workforce, in the first mass layoff in its sixteen-year history. The cuts sit alongside record Q1 revenue of 639.8 million dollars, up 34 percent year-on-year and ahead of analyst estimates. The stock fell about 24 percent over the two-session window. CEO Matthew Prince framed the cuts as a direct consequence of agentic AI changing the work — Cloudflare's own internal AI usage is up 600 percent in three months. That framing is what makes this Exhibit A. Atlassian's March layoffs were a pivot story; Cloudflare is profitable, growing, sitting on a cybersecurity-partner badge in OpenAI's Daybreak launch, and still cutting. The same CEO that the rest of the industry is buying Daybreak from is the one telling the labour-market debate which roles do not come back.

The third thread sits underneath both. NVIDIA and IREN announced a strategic partnership to deploy up to 5 gigawatts of NVIDIA DSX-aligned AI infrastructure, with IREN issuing NVIDIA a five-year right to purchase up to 30 million ordinary shares at 70 dollars apiece — a stake worth up to 2.1 billion dollars and the second major NVIDIA equity bet of the week after the 3.2 billion in Corning. Sweetwater, the flagship 2-gigawatt Texas campus, becomes one of the largest single-site AI factories announced this year, and the highest-profile non-hyperscaler datacentre relationship for NVIDIA's DSX programme. Anthropic continued its verticalisation push the same day, releasing ten ready-to-run financial-services agent templates — KYC screening, pitchbooks, month-end close — and previewing "dreaming," a continual-learning feature that lets agents review prior sessions between runs, extract recurring patterns and surface insights without retraining. Around the edges, Ben Thompson reframed OpenAI's 4 billion-dollar Deployment Company and Tomoro acquisition as the mainframe-era playbook returning — armies of forward-deployed engineers, not self-serve — and Lawfare reported the Trump administration weighing a tech-exec working group on pre-deployment AI oversight. Read in sequence — NVIDIA buys compute distribution, Anthropic ships verticalised libraries with cross-session memory, both labs partner-stamp Snyk, Washington starts drafting the regulatory bracket — the day reads as the moment cybersecurity, compute, vertical agents and policy stop being separate stories.

## Research highlights

**Skill1: Unified Evolution of Skill-Augmented Agents via Reinforcement Learning** — Yaorui Shi, Yuxin Chen, Zhengxi Lu, Yuchun Miao. _Takeaway:_ Unified RL recipe for evolving skill-augmented agents — a concrete instantiation of the "skill library + RL" pattern that has been splintered across papers using inconsistent definitions of "skill." _Category:_ cs.CL. [arXiv:2605.06130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06130)

**Beyond Semantic Similarity: Rethinking Retrieval for Agentic Search via Direct Corpus Interaction** — Zhuofeng Li, Haoxiang Zhang, Cong Wei, Pan Lu. _Takeaway:_ Argues vector-similarity retrieval is the wrong primitive for agentic search and proposes direct corpus interaction — sharp pushback on the embeddings-first orthodoxy in RAG. Companion read to the smol.ai claim that DCI pushes BrowseComp-Plus 69%→80% on Sonnet 4.6. _Category:_ cs.IR. [arXiv:2605.05242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05242)

**SkillOS: Learning Skill Curation for Self-Evolving Agents** — Siru Ouyang, Jun Yan, Yanfei Chen, Rujun Han. _Takeaway:_ Treats skill curation (what to keep, what to retire) as a learned operation rather than a fixed policy — concrete advance for the "skill library" abstraction in long-horizon agent systems. _Category:_ cs.AI. [arXiv:2605.06614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06614)

**StraTA: Incentivizing Agentic Reinforcement Learning with Strategic Trajectory Abstraction** — Xiangyuan Xue, Yifan Zhou, Zidong Wang, Shengji Tang. _Takeaway:_ Strategic trajectory-level abstraction as the credit-assignment substrate for agentic RL — concrete contribution distinct from token-level methods. _Category:_ cs.LG. [arXiv:2605.06642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06642)

**AI Co-Mathematician: Accelerating Mathematicians with Agentic AI** — Daniel Zheng, Ingrid von Glehn, Yori Zwols, Iuliya Beloshapka. _Takeaway:_ DeepMind agentic AI paper on collaborative-mathematician workflows — important data point on whether the AlphaProof line generalizes from competition-style problems into open-ended research math. _Category:_ cs.AI. [arXiv:2605.06651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06651)

**Can RL Teach Long-Horizon Reasoning to LLMs? Expressiveness Is Key** — Tianle Wang, Zhaoyang Wang, Guangchen Lan, Xinpeng Wei. _Takeaway:_ Argues the bottleneck for long-horizon reasoning is policy expressiveness, not RL signal — sharp diagnostic for the recurring claim that current RL hits a ceiling at multi-step reasoning. _Category:_ cs.LG. [arXiv:2605.06638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06638)

**A²TGPO: Agentic Turn-Group Policy Optimization with Adaptive Turn-level Clipping** — Dingwei Chen, Zefang Zong, Zhipeng Ma, Leo Luo. _Takeaway:_ Turn-group policy optimization with adaptive clipping for agentic settings — concrete GRPO variant targeted at the multi-turn-with-tool-calls regime where vanilla GRPO struggles. _Category:_ cs.LG. [arXiv:2605.06200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06200)

**UniPool: A Globally Shared Expert Pool for Mixture-of-Experts** — Minbin Huang, Han Shi, Chuanyang Zheng, Yimeng Wu. _Takeaway:_ Globally shared expert pool for MoE — direct architectural alternative to the per-layer expert silo that has dominated MoE work. Relevant to the MoE scaling debate; pairs with EMO (emergent modularity at pretraining). _Category:_ cs.LG. [arXiv:2605.06665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06665)

_Also worth noting:_ [MiA-Signature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06416) (compact signature approximation of global activation for long-context, distinct from sparse-attention and KV-eviction approaches); [Auto Research with Specialist Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05724) (specialist-agent ensembles producing non-trivial training recipes); [EMO: Pretraining MoE for Emergent Modularity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06663); [Nonsense Helps: Prompt Space Perturbation Broadens Reasoning Exploration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05566).
