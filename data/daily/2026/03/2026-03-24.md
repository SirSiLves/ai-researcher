## The day in 90 seconds

**OpenAI retires Sora, Atlassian cuts 1,600 jobs, and a poisoned LiteLLM lands the same day Anthropic ships Auto mode.**

Tuesday was the day frontier-lab portfolio discipline, enterprise-SaaS workforce reallocation, and agent-stack supply-chain security all arrived at once. The clearest signal is OpenAI naming the price of consumer text-to-video and walking away from it. The most disquieting one is a credential stealer slipping into a package most agent harnesses depend on, hours before Anthropic shipped the permissions system that was supposed to make this kind of attack manageable.

OpenAI announced via X that Sora — both the consumer app and the API — is being discontinued in stages. Sora 1 was already pulled in the U.S. on 13 March; the Sora web and app experience closes on 26 April; the API shuts off on 24 September. The number that sets the precedent is the one in the help-center post: roughly one million dollars a day in compute against an installed base that fell from a peak near one million actives to fewer than 500,000. Compute is being redirected to coding tools and enterprise products with more predictable revenue. This is the first time a frontier lab has explicitly retired a flagship consumer product on unit-economics grounds, and it reframes GTC week — inference economics now drive portfolio decisions, vendors invest where they pay and retire where they do not.

The security thread is the one that will look most prescient in hindsight. LiteLLM versions 1.82.7 and 1.82.8 on PyPI shipped a base64-encoded credential stealer inside `litellm_init.pth` — a Python path file that runs on install, no import required — exfiltrating `~/.ssh/`, `~/.aws/`, crypto wallet directories, and shell history; the chain likely traces back to a recent Trivy CI compromise. The same day, Anthropic launched Auto mode for Claude Code, a permissions layer where a Sonnet 4.6 classifier gates each tool call against an extensive allow and soft-deny ruleset. Simon Willison's juxtaposition is the cleanest articulation of the year's central agent-security argument: a `pip install -r` allow rule would not have stopped the LiteLLM attack, because non-deterministic classifiers and deterministic sandboxes solve different halves of the problem and need to be deployed together. NVIDIA's NemoCLAW deep coverage — kernel-level sandbox plus a privacy router monitoring every outgoing agent communication — landed in the same news cycle and is now the compliance baseline Microsoft Defender for AI, Palo Alto Prisma AIRS, and CrowdStrike's agentic guardrails will be benchmarked against through the rest of the year.

The workforce thread is the one with the longest tail. Atlassian announced a 1,600-headcount reduction, ten percent of staff, redirecting resources toward AI development and enterprise sales. Plenty of vendors have shed headcount this cycle; what is new here is the framing. This is the first major enterprise-SaaS layoff where management explicitly couples staff reductions to AI investment — staff out, AI in — and the language lines up with Atlassian Rovo and Atlassian Intelligence shipping cycles already in flight. The Cloudflare AI-displacement layoff that will define the same conversation on 8 May is still six weeks away. Tuesday is the day the template was written. Around the edges, Oracle joined Snowflake, ServiceNow, Cisco, and NVIDIA in the enterprise control-plane wave by announcing the upcoming general availability of OCI Enterprise AI, and the papers cohort moved decisively from agents-as-architectures to agents-as-training-pipelines, with OpenResearcher publishing an open pipeline for long-horizon research-trajectory synthesis, LongCat-Flash-Prover instantiating tool-integrated formal RL against proof-assistant verifiers, and Huang et al. decomposing RLVR gradient updates into useful and harmful directions.

**Read this first:** [What to know about the Sora discontinuation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001152-what-to-know-about-the-sora-discontinuation) — the one item to open if you only have five minutes, because every other portfolio decision this quarter rhymes with it.

## Research highlights

- **OpenResearcher: A Fully Open Pipeline for Long-Horizon Deep Research Trajectory Synthesis** — Zhuofeng Li et al. Open pipeline for generating the long-horizon research trajectories used to train deep-research agents; concrete answer to the data bottleneck inside industrial labs. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.20278 — agent training data / cs.CL. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20278)
- **LongCat-Flash-Prover: Native Formal Reasoning via Agentic Tool-Integrated Reinforcement Learning** — Jianing Wang et al. Agentic tool-integrated RL recipe for native formal-proof generation — concrete instantiation of "tools-as-verifier" with the proof assistant grading trajectories. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.21065 — formal reasoning. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21065)
- **On the Direction of RLVR Updates for LLM Reasoning: Identification and Exploitation** — Kexin Huang et al. Decomposes RLVR gradient updates into useful and harmful directions; concrete diagnostic for why some RLVR runs collapse the base policy. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.22117 — RL post-training / cs.LG. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22117)
- **mSFT: Addressing Dataset Mixtures Overfitting Heterogeneously in Multi-task SFT** — Woosung Koh et al. Characterizes the under-discussed failure mode where multi-task SFT overfits some datasets while underfitting others; proposes a heterogeneous mixing scheme. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.21606 — SFT / cs.CL. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21606)
- **PivotRL: High-Accuracy Agentic Post-Training at Low Compute Cost** — Junkeun Yi et al. Compute-efficient agentic post-training recipe — entry in the "agent RL without industrial budget" thread, claims accuracy preservation under aggressive cost cuts. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.21383 — agent training. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21383)
- **REVERE: Reflective Evolving Research Engineer for Scientific Workflows** — Balaji Dinesh Gangireddi et al. Research-engineer agent that reflects and evolves its own workflow over scientific tasks — useful counterpart to one-shot research-agent benchmarks. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.20667 — research agents / cs.AI. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20667)
- **BubbleRAG: Evidence-Driven Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Black-Box Knowledge Graphs** — Duyi Pan et al. RAG over knowledge graphs whose schema is unknown — realistic enterprise setting where the KG is a queryable black box. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.20309 — RAG / cs.IR. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20309)
- **Omni-WorldBench: Comprehensive Interaction-Centric Evaluation for World Models** — Meiqi Wu et al. World-model benchmark evaluating interaction rather than rollout fidelity — fills the gap between rollout-FID metrics and what agents need from a simulator. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.22212 — world models / benchmarks. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.22212)
