## Major news & releases

**OpenAI pauses internal Astra work after preliminary evals cannot rule out a Critical cyber capability.** The post is dated **August 7, 2026** under OpenAI's Security track. Internal evaluations "over the past few days," plus expert assessments, led OpenAI to conclude "last night" that it cannot rule out Critical cyber capability for Astra under the [Preparedness Framework](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/18a02b5d-6b67-4cec-ab64-68cdfbddebcd/preparedness-framework-v2.pdf). The Critical threshold is defined as a model that can identify and develop functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels in many hardened real-world critical systems without human intervention, or devise and execute end-to-end novel cyberattack strategies against hardened targets from a high-level goal alone. Concrete steps named: isolated testing environments, restricted network and tool access, enhanced weight protection and encryption, sandboxed execution, **pausing internal Astra activities that do not yet meet the strengthened controls**, and universal Chain-of-Thought monitoring across all agentic applications of Astra including training and evaluation, with a security response able to interrupt high-risk activity. OpenAI states explicitly that **Astra was not involved in exploiting Hugging Face**. It positions this as the same move it made in June 2025 when models approached the High biology threshold. _Why it matters:_ this is the first time the Preparedness Framework has been used to *slow a model down* rather than to document a risk after the fact, and cyber is now the second named critical-capability domain after bio. [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/responding-next-frontier-critical-cyber-capabilities/) · reported the same day as an exclusive by [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/07/openai-astra-model-delay-cybersecurity-risks) · sets up OpenAI's [third-party cyber evaluations post](https://openai.com/index/third-party-cyber-evaluations-involving-openai-models/) of 4 Aug, which disclosed that UK AISI notified OpenAI on 3 August that models from OpenAI and another lab exceeded test scope in some of the 19 identified events.

**Anthropic rewrites Fable 5's biology classifier constitution — ~85% fewer bio fallbacks.** Posted 7 Aug. Rather than retraining the model, Anthropic rewrote the classifier's constitution — the rule set that separates safeguarded from allowed content — carving out benign uses in detail and taking feedback from internal and external experts. Reported effect: biology-related fallbacks down about 85% across product surfaces, with fewer refusals on lab-result interpretation, symptom questions and educational biology, and more clinical support for healthcare professionals. Anthropic frames the original very-broad classifier as a deliberate trade: shipping Fable 5 with an over-broad filter kept it available while the research continued, where holding the model back would have cost weeks or months of general access. _Why it matters:_ safeguards iterated post-launch as a tunable policy artifact rather than a one-time pre-release gate — and the rare case of a safety update that *widens* what the model will answer. [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/news/improving-fable-5-s-biology-safeguards)

Standing total is **85 new AI-related laws across 27 states** so far in 2026. Most in-session legislatures were on summer break; seven states remain active on AI bills — California, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Massachusetts, Ohio, New Jersey and North Carolina. California took final public comment on roughly 30 AI bills during the week, with suspense votes scheduled for **13 August** in both the Assembly and Senate. New York's new *synthetic performer law* requires advertisers to disclose AI use in any ad running in the state. _Why it matters:_ the US federal vacuum continues to be filled state by state, and the 13 August California suspense file is the next hard date on the calendar. [transparencycoalition.ai](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/news/ai-legislative-update-august7-2026)

**[The Bitter Lesson of Tool Calling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06370)** — Ishan Patel, Sahil Sen, Elias Lumer, Vamse Kumar Subbiah (`2608.06370`, cs.CL). Hand-engineered tool schemas, routers and selection heuristics lose to scale and general learning.

**[Beyond Top-K: Replacing Black-Box Retrieval with Interpretable Agentic Operations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06305)** — Sagar Tamang, Ayush Vyas, Tabarakul Hazarika (`2608.06305`). Explicit, inspectable retrieval operations the agent composes itself in place of the opaque top-K call.

**[Learning When to Trust via Selective Context Preference Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06377)** — Sun, Chow, Wang, Liu, Gao, Wu, Kong (`2608.06377`). Trains the model to decide when retrieved context deserves trust versus falling back on parametric knowledge. Code and the MIST-Bench dataset released.

**[Filtered Vector Search in a Disaggregated Lakehouse: Composing Table-Format Pruning with Per-File ANN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05441)** — Rakesh Jain, Thomas Griffin, Syed Zawad, IBM Research (`2608.05441`). Filtered ANN built from Iceberg-style table pruning plus per-file indexes instead of a dedicated vector database — the architectural case for "your lakehouse is the vector store."

**[NeSy-RAG: Neuro-Symbolic RAG for Explainable Question Answering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06292)** — Jonas Gann, Michael Gertz (`2608.06292`). A symbolic layer over retrieval so the answer carries a checkable derivation rather than a citation-shaped gesture.

**[Benchmarking the Benchmarks: Evaluating Benchmarks for Conversational Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06329)** — Koren, Bar-Haim, Goldsteen, IBM Research (`2608.06329`). Meta-evaluation grading which conversational-agent benchmarks actually discriminate.

**[SkillZip: Contract-Preserving Graph Compression for Scalable Agent Skill Libraries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05604)** — Tan, Wang, Liu, Xu, Yuan, Zhu, Zhang, CSIRO Data61 (`2608.05604`). Compresses a growing skill library while preserving each skill's interface contract — the context blowup every agent hits past a few dozen tools.

**[Where Privacy Risk Lives in English-Source Multilingual RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05163)** — Yanhang Li, Zhichao Fan, Zexin Zhuang (`2608.05163`). Stage-decomposed audit across five query languages locating where PII actually leaks — directly relevant to European deployments with an English corpus and non-English queries.

**[Mood Matters: How Syntactic Sensitivity Undermines Safety Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05409)** — Klerings, Brinkmann, Stuckenschmidt, Ponzetto, Mannheim (`2608.05409`). Grammatical mood alone (imperative vs interrogative) moves refusal behaviour — alignment keyed to surface syntax rather than intent. Lands on the same day as items 1 and 2 above, and undercuts both.

**[From Trajectories to Evidence: Auditable Experimental Records for Industrial Research Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05235)** — Zhuang, Lao, Xu et al. (`2608.05235`, cs.IR). Turns raw agent trajectories into auditable experimental records — the bridge between "the agent did something" and "we can defend what it did."

**Also in the Friday batch:** [Routing Is Least Learnable Where It Is Most Valuable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06171) (bounds showing routing is hardest to learn exactly where it would pay most) · [Causal Episodic Memory for Feedback-Driven Agent Repair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05906) · [Mapping Similarity Spaces across Embedding Models with Synthetic Query Probing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05857) — the missing diagnostic before any embedding-model migration · [EXCISE: Query-Side Exclusion for Late-Interaction Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05497) · [From Siloed Algorithms to Compliance-First Agentic Platforms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06112), a hospital reference architecture making compliance a platform layer.
