## The day in 90 seconds

**OpenAI ships machine-checked math while the industry's agent benchmarks fail an audit**

An internal OpenAI model called Astra produced new results on ten problems that had been open for a decade or more, and every argument arrived as a Lean certificate — machine-checkable, not merely plausible. On the same day, two separate audits found that the benchmarks the industry uses to score agents are wrong somewhere between 13 and 15 percent of the time. Verification is the day's whole story: one lab answered the question directly, and the standard instruments failed it.

Astra is the first time OpenAI has named its next major model inside a research publication, and the problems are not toys. The results span high-dimensional geometry, coding theory, arithmetic circuit complexity, group theory, operator algebras and lattice cryptography — sphere-packing bounds pushed down to the Cohn–Elkies threshold, the existence of non-sofic groups, a disproof of Connes's rigidity conjecture, an n⁴/log n arithmetic-formula lower bound for the permanent, and resolutions of Erdős problems 146, 180 and 183. The number that reframes the argument is the bill: roughly $2,000 of tokens. Independent community review is still pending, but the Lean formalisation is precisely what should make that review fast, and it answers the verification objection that has trailed every AI-mathematics claim since May.

The counterweight landed in the same 24 hours. Dong and colleagues audited 150 failure-scored trajectories across five computer-use benchmarks and found 15.3 percent of FAIL verdicts were simply wrong — 10.7 percent evaluator false negatives, 4.7 percent broken tasks. PAIChecker, separately, found 13.6 percent of SWE-bench Verified instances pair a problem statement with a test oracle describing different work. Agent capability, in other words, is being reported through instruments with known double-digit error rates. A third paper made the point from another direction: once token budgets are matched, plain repeated sampling with a majority vote beats self-refine and reflexion, which means most published reasoning-scaffold gains were just more tokens. Supabase's newly open-sourced Evals suite is the constructive answer — a public benchmark nobody can tune against, plus a private regression suite deliberately kept out of the scores.

Underneath both stories, the price war hardened into the dominant frame. DeepSeek's V4-Flash-0731 shipped as an official release with its architecture untouched at 284B-A13B; the gains came from re-post-training rather than a new design, and the API is now Responses-API native, which reduces swapping it into a Codex-shaped coding stack to a base-URL change. The circulating deltas put Terminal Bench at 56.9 to 82.7. OpenAI answered by cutting GPT-5.6 tiers, with Luna down roughly 80 percent, and Hacker News credited GLM 5.2, Kimi K3 and DeepSeek by name for forcing it. After a year of prices only rising, the direction has reversed — and the same open-weight pressure is what has Washington and Silicon Valley arguing about regulation again.

Which sets up tomorrow. The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations apply from 2 August, binding deployers as well as providers across four cases — direct interaction with individuals, AI-generated content, emotion recognition and biometric categorisation, and deepfakes — with fines reaching €15M or 3 percent of worldwide turnover and no omnibus delay. Munich's regional court, meanwhile, handed GEMA an injunction against Suno that reaches the use of protected works in training, not just the generated output. Provenance duties and training-stage exposure both stop being theoretical this weekend.

**Read this first:** [OpenAI: ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/) — the Lean certificates and the $2,000 price tag are the two facts that change what an AI research claim now has to prove.

## Research highlights

- **[A Comparative Study of MCP and A2A for Inter-Agent Coordination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23884)** — Predoaia et al. First implementation-grounded head-to-head: the same software-engineering task built twice, scored against industry-derived requirements (agent discoverability, multi-part messaging, multi-turn conversation) rather than compared at spec level. `cs.MA — 2607.23884`

- **[How Benchmarks Mis-Score Computer-Use Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28367)** — Dong et al. Audit of 150 failure-scored trajectories across five web/enterprise/desktop CUA benchmarks: **15.3% of FAIL verdicts are wrong** — 10.7% evaluator false negatives, 4.7% broken tasks. Scripted oracles are quietly deflating reported agent capability. `cs.AI — 2607.28367`

- **[PAIChecker: PR-Issue Misalignment in SWE-Bench-Like Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28587)** — Wang, Xu, He. **13.6% of SWE-bench Verified instances** have PR-issue pairings that don't match, across five misalignment patterns. Companion to the CUA audit; together they make benchmark plumbing the measurement bottleneck. `cs.AI/cs.SE — 2607.28587`

- **[Sample More, Reflect Less](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28576)** — Mirzaei. Properly powered replication (seven methods, three model sizes, two benchmarks, with confidence intervals): plain repeated sampling plus majority vote beats self-refine and reflexion **once token budgets are matched**. Most "reasoning scaffold" gains are just more tokens. `cs.CL/cs.AI/cs.LG — 2607.28576`

- **[GLM-RAG: Graph Language Models for Graph-Based RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28397)** — Arseven et al. Benchmarks graph-language-model retrievers against GNN and plain vector search on single- and multi-hop RAG, with explicit focus on **transfer to unseen domains** — the axis most KG-RAG papers skip. `cs.AI/cs.CL/cs.IR — 2607.28397`

- **[Separating Capability from Permission: Governance Framework for Agentic AI Autonomy Levels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23438)** — Zheng et al. Splits **Allowed** Autonomy Levels (what an agent is authorised to do given risk and oversight) from **Autonomous Capability** Levels (what it technically can do), five rungs each. A clean vocabulary for deployment policy. `cs.MA/cs.AI — 2607.23438`

- **[Stop Shipping AI Agents on Faith: Capability Is Not Production Readiness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27677)** — Bousetouane. The ProofAgent Index — a four-dimension deployment-evidence score (Evaluation, Context, Compliance, Governance) — arguing demos and behavioural tests say nothing about surviving production constraints. `cs.MA — 2607.27677`

- **[ChronoMem: Version Control and Semantic Rollback for Agent Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27773)** — Su et al. Agent memory today is forward-only — no way to inspect, version or revert. Adds whole-memory snapshots, version history and semantic rollback inside Google's ADK. `cs.CL/cs.AI — 2607.27773`

_Also: [RAGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26339) layered defence against RAG data poisoning · [ORCA-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28545), 1,079 root-cause tasks against a live OpenTelemetry microservice stack._
