## The day in 90 seconds

**Brussels and California switched on AI provenance rules on the same calendar day**

The EU AI Act's transparency article became applicable and California's SB 942 became operative within hours of each other, and the alignment was deliberate — Sacramento moved its own date to match Brussels. For anyone shipping generative features into either market, labelling and machine-readable provenance stopped being a roadmap item today and became a filing you can be complained about.

Article 50 now requires chatbots to disclose they are chatbots, deepfakes to be labelled, and generated content to carry machine-readable marks. The more consequential change is procedural: the Commission opened a complaints tool, a whistleblower tool and a dedicated channel for downstream providers of general-purpose models, so any individual or business can now file and the receiving authority is obliged to assess. Penalty ceilings run to €35M or 7% of worldwide turnover for prohibited practices and €15M or 3% for transparency breaches, with one softener — systems already on the market have until 2 December 2026 to meet the marking requirement. California's law arrives by a different mechanism to the same end: providers with more than a million California users must embed C2PA-compatible provenance in images, video and audio, publish a free detection tool and offer a visible disclosure. Roughly 190 organisations signed the accompanying EU Code of Practice, and the half worth reading is the deployers — Lufthansa, Getty Images, Bulgari, Lenovo — the first concrete sign these duties are propagating out of the model layer into ordinary companies.

The counterweight came from Hangzhou. Alibaba launched Qwen3.8-Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts scoring 86.6 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 — between Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.6 Sol — and promised open weights within a week, alongside a smaller Qwen3.8-27B that Hacker News found more interesting than the flagship. It would be the first time Alibaba has opened a Max-class model, and the stock rose about 7% in Hong Kong on the news. Every model launch on the HN front page today was Chinese, and no US frontier launch reached the top ten. Thinking Machines made the structural version of the same argument: Inkling-Small, 276B total and 12B active under Apache 2.0, beats its own roughly 3.5× larger parent at coding, 80.2% against 77.6% on SWE-bench Verified. And Andrew Ng reported that Claude Code and Codex both declined to security-review his team's open-source agent, while Kimi K3 and GLM 5.2 completed the job. Keeping an open-weight model in the stack is becoming an operational decision rather than an ideological one.

Underneath both stories, the artifacts the industry uses to establish trust are failing their own audits. JFrog found LLM-fabricated SQLite advisories waved through NVD and CISA's enrichment programme — the cited code does not exist and the proofs of concept do not trigger — which turns unvalidated CVE submission into a flooding attack surface for any organisation required to patch everything assigned to it. A census of 1,847 MCP servers found 52% abandoned and only 17% production-grade, with 82% of audited implementations vulnerable to path traversal and a five-server setup consuming around 55,000 tokens before the user has said anything. A validity audit of agent-safety benchmarks found an "always positive" policy scoring 0.690 on R-Judge, above five models that genuinely discriminate. Advisories, servers and benchmark scores are all being produced faster than anyone checks them, which is worth holding in mind as the compliance regimes above start asking for evidence.

**Read this first:** [The Commission's own release on what commenced on 2 August](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august) — three widely-shared LinkedIn posts this week give three incompatible accounts of which obligations actually took effect, and the primary source settles it in a page.

## Research highlights

Today's arXiv batch has a single through-line: **measurement validity and the tool/permission layer.** Four independent groups argue the thing being measured is mis-specified; two argue agent safety leaks at the tool layer rather than the model.

- **[Safety, or Just Capability? A Validity Audit of Agent-Safety Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28685)** (2607.28685) — Runs R-Judge, InjecAgent, AgentHarm and AgentDojo as measurements to be validated across up to 22 models. On any binary trace-judgment benchmark scored by F1, an **"always positive" policy scores 0.690 on R-Judge — above five models that actually discriminate.** The three broad benchmarks rank the same 18 models differently, and the apparent capability/safety trade-off is largely a small-panel artifact (ρ = −0.64 at n=7 → +0.02 at n=18).
- **[Tool Specifications Matter: Uncovering and Mitigating Safety Risks in AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29254)** (2607.29254) — The standout. White-box analysis shows **schema-formatted tool specifications themselves weaken the model's internal refusal signals.** SafeKeep decouples judgment from execution — judge against *flattened textual* specs, execute against the original schema: refusal on harmful requests 23.8% → 70.6%, prompt-injection success 25.6% → 2.5%, task capability preserved.
- **[Reproducing LightMem: Naive RAG Is Just as Good for Memory Management](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29104)** (2607.29104, UQ/CSIRO/Google) — Sharp negative result. Swapping only the retriever over a fixed store moves accuracy 58.1% → 75.5%; naive RAG over raw turns generally wins at matched retrieval depth; LightMem only leads under tight token budgets, and oracle evaluation shows memory construction discards answer-relevant information. Read with **[Zero-Mem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29377)** (2607.29377), which asks whether structured agent memory needs generation at all — no LLM call outside final QA, entity–context graph plus temporal hierarchy, memory-operation time cut 57.6%.
- **[Model or Harness? An Interaction-Centric Taxonomy for Localizing Agent Failures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28802)** (2607.28802) — Reframes failure analysis as repair assignment: the same visible failure may need post-training, harness engineering, environment redesign, or benchmark repair. 41 failure modes each assigned to an *edge* between two components plus a fault side; best frontier-model judge reaches κ = 0.76 against human labels.
- **[CAGE: Certified Authorization under Typed-Return Uncertainty for Tool-Using Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29190)** (2607.29190) — Certifies runtime permission decisions over a declared neighborhood (one admissible binding fault plus bounded numerical drift), and proves that certifying the categorical and numerical channels **separately does not compose** — perturbations safe on each channel alone can jointly make an action unsafe.
- **[TokTier: Exact Stateful Tokenization for Agentic LLM Serving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29678)** (2607.29678) — Rare hard data on agent serving economics: across 153,951 calls the median appends ~1.4K characters, and at a 94.1% prompt-cache hit rate **tokenization alone reaches up to 64% of time-to-first-token.** Incremental repair in 0.5–1.1 ms with byte-identical token IDs, up to 437× faster than HF tokenization.
- **[MerchantBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28956)** (2607.28956) — 365-day order-level simulation on 98,843 real product records with 26 tools, coupling observable supplier events to delayed outcomes. Eight LLMs, two frameworks, 48 full-year runs: the best configuration reaches **27.3% of the mean final net assets achieved by human participants.**
- **[AISPA: User-Centric System Prompt Auditing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28617)** (2607.28617) — 3,249 instructions from system prompts across 88 commercial products, classified protective vs problematic. Enormous spread (some orgs average 60+ protective instructions per product, others fewer than 5) and protection is broad but shallow: 98.9% have at least one protective instruction, **only 24% cover all eight dimensions.** Concrete substrate for disclosure-style governance — and it lands the same day Article 50 does.

Also: **[Know It, Act on It](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29433)** finds agents routinely recall a stated user preference yet fail to act on it, worst for health and therapy; **[Benchmarks Are Not Validation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28840)** is a practitioner position paper from financial-institution validators arguing benchmark scores are not approval evidence; **[ExtractBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29677)** scores value accuracy, record completeness, grounding *and* measured cost together across 4,869 enterprise document pages.
