## The day in 90 seconds

**A summer safety scorecard, a hard pivot to power, and a developer-trust backlash**

Two days before Gemini 3.5 Pro and WAIC Shanghai reset the model race, Wednesday's real news sat underneath it. No frontier model launched; instead the first independent lab-safety report card of the summer graded the whole field poorly, hundreds of billions in private capital and public spending moved toward power and chips, and developers spent the day dissecting how their own AI tools leak secrets. The scaffolding around the models moved far more than the models did.

The Future of Life Institute published its Summer 2026 AI Safety Index, the season's first independent cross-lab scorecard, and the curve is unforgiving: the best grade in the class is a C+. Anthropic earned it, leading five of six domains; OpenAI and Google DeepMind landed at C, Meta at D+, and xAI, DeepSeek and Mistral effectively failed. The sharpest finding wasn't the low ceiling but the backsliding — several labs have quietly walked back "red-line" safety commitments, a number of them promises made during fundraising. The timing gives the report teeth: it lands roughly three weeks before the EU AI Act's general-purpose-model obligations start carrying fines on August 2, handing anyone choosing a model a governance axis to set beside the benchmark one. A parallel courtroom fight sharpened the same theme, as the New York Times and fellow publishers asked a Manhattan judge to sanction OpenAI for allegedly claiming it couldn't search its models for their copyrighted work when it already had.

Beneath the governance noise, the build-out's money kept moving away from models and toward the physical limits underneath them — power and memory. Pipeline operator Williams pulled $5.34 billion from a Blackstone-led consortium, with Apollo and KKR, for a 49% stake in a new "Power Innovation" venture: five behind-the-meter generation projects built to feed data centers directly and skip the multi-year wait for grid interconnection. On a far larger scale, South Korea committed roughly $880 billion over a decade — split across memory-chip fabs at Samsung and SK Hynix and a wave of AI data centers, with an 8.4-gigawatt capacity target by 2029 — to defend its roughly 60% share of the HBM market. Meta, for its part, began renting out spare capacity as a "Meta Compute" neocloud alongside a new enterprise Business Agent Platform, backed by up to $145 billion of 2026 infrastructure spending. Private capital and industrial policy are now aimed squarely at the same wall.

The third thread was a mood shift among the people who actually run these tools. Hacker News's front page filled with agent-security stories: a "memory heist" write-up showing how a determined attacker can still coax private context out of Claude's memory feature reached 558 points, paired with Simon Willison's teardown of where Claude's web_fetch is hardened against exfiltration and where it isn't; a Cursor zero-day that quietly runs a local git binary drew 428; and OpenAI's move to start encrypting Codex sub-agent prompts pulled 422, read by many as an anti-distillation clampdown. The research slice pointed the same way — one paper argues that once an LLM is the brain of a system, isolation must be treated as a first-class safety principle, exactly the sandboxed-sub-agent fix the memory-heist thread converged on. The recurring undertone — "we forgot 50 years of computer security overnight" — marks a pivot from capability chatter toward harder questions about what an agent does with the access you hand it. It makes a fitting overture to July 17, when Gemini 3.5 Pro and WAIC Shanghai will drag attention back to raw capability.

## Research highlights

- **[Cost-Governed RAG (arXiv:2607.12188)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12188)** — Navnit Shukla. Unified per-tenant cost attribution spanning retrieval *and* generation in multi-tenant LLM systems; a governance/FinOps primitive for production RAG.
- **[Transforming LLMs into Efficient Cross-Encoders via Knowledge Distillation for RAG Reranking (arXiv:2607.11933)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11933)** — distills LLM cross-encoder reranking quality into a cheaper student to escape the quadratic cost that keeps high-accuracy rerankers out of real-time RAG.
- **[Ring-Zero: Scaling Zero RL to a Trillion Parameters for Emergent Reasoning (arXiv:2607.12395)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12395)** — pushes verifiable-reward "zero RL" (no human-annotated data) to trillion-parameter scale; a frontier-scale training result.
- **[A Threshold Exceedance Framework for CBRN Uplift Evaluation in Frontier Language Models (arXiv:2607.12200)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12200)** — a concrete dangerous-capability eval methodology for judging whether model access materially raises a non-expert's ability to plan CBRN harm.
- **[Isolation as a First-Class Principle for LLM-Agent System Safety (arXiv:2607.12406)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12406)** — argues that once an LLM is the "brain" of a system, safety is a system-level property; offers a taxonomy for treating isolation as the containment principle. (Echoes today's HN "memory heist" fix: read memory in a sandboxed sub-agent.)
- **[MemOps: Benchmarking Lifecycle Memory Operations in Long-Horizon Conversations (arXiv:2607.12893)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12893)** — evaluates the full agent-memory lifecycle (write / update / consolidate / retire), not just recall.
- **[Who Grades the Grader? Co-Evolving Evaluation Metrics and Skills (arXiv:2607.12790)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12790)** — when no reliable verifier exists, "Double Ratchet" co-evolves an inspectable metric alongside the skill loop, recovering 88–110% of ground-truth-metric lift across code, text-to-SQL, and report generation.
- **[Vertical Standardisation for High-Risk AI under the EU AI Act: Algorithmic Hiring (arXiv:2607.12588)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12588)** — maps EU AI Act obligations onto a specific high-risk vertical where no European standards yet exist.
- _Also:_ [JoLT KV-cache compression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12550), [RCWT: coordination-token task-budget displacement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12216) (GPT-4.1-mini, Claude Haiku 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Flash all degrade once residual task evidence falls to a few hundred tokens), and the [Agentic Service-Oriented Computing manifesto](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12619).
