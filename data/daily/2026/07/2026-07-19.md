## The day in 90 seconds

**Europe moves to own its AI stack as China's open weights reset the frontier**

SAP paid more than €1 billion to stand up a European frontier lab betting that enterprise AI's future is tabular, not chat — even as Moonshot's 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3 keeps rattling US labs and the EU's power to fine model makers switches on in two weeks. Sunday was quiet on new releases but loud on where the industry is being pulled: toward sovereignty, structured data, and a hardening regulatory deadline.

The day's freshest hard news came from Walldorf. SAP closed its acquisition of Freiburg-based Prior Labs on July 17 — roughly 18 months after the startup was founded — in a deal reported above €1 billion, and pledged to invest more than €1 billion over four years to grow it into what it calls a "globally leading frontier AI lab." The target is unusual: Prior Labs pioneered Tabular Foundation Models, and its TabPFN series was published in Nature. CTO Philipp Herzig framed the wager plainly — enterprise AI's greatest untapped opportunity is models built for structured data, the spreadsheets and databases that run companies, rather than another chat-shaped LLM. Prior Labs keeps its brand, leadership, and open-model publishing under SAP, giving Europe's largest software vendor a sovereign research arm aimed squarely at the data it already sits on.

The counterweight came from China. Moonshot AI's Kimi K3, released July 16, spent the weekend as a geopolitics story: a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model — the largest open-weight release ever — with a 1M-token context, native multimodality, and benchmark scores (93.5% GPQA Diamond, 91.2% BrowseComp) that put it atop the Frontend Code Arena ahead of Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol. At $3/$15 per million tokens with open weights promised by July 27, it keeps compressing the gap to closed US labs, and TechCrunch reported it had "rattled" the industry. Alibaba's Qwen 3.8, read widely as a rushed answer, landed within days; commenters now count 12-plus providers claiming to beat Opus 4.5 and expect DeepSeek V4 imminently. On Hacker News, the weekend's dominant read was that a Chinese open-weight model reaching frontier parity is now inevitable, with commenters predicting Western governments will soon reframe open-weight access itself as a national-security question. Thinking Machines' 975B-parameter Inkling stands as the credible US open-weights counterweight — but the week's momentum is Chinese.

Underneath the headlines, the enterprise stack kept consolidating around the unglamorous problem of running agents in production. Snowflake's Cortex Sense entered private preview grounding agents in metadata and semantic views rather than scanning every table, lifting retrieval accuracy from 24.1% to 86.3% while cutting cost from $1.76 to $0.59 per query — a concrete marker of the shift from vector-search RAG to context engineering; the radar shows vector-database demand surging 16.6% on the week as retrieval budgets tilt from embeddings toward grounding. Oracle pushed an AI-native builder for governed "Fusion Agentic Applications," racing SAP and Salesforce to make agent-building a first-class suite capability, and the MCP spec's largest revision since launch entered release-candidate validation, standardizing the plumbing (roughly 97 million monthly downloads, 5,800-plus servers) beneath production agents. The near-term calendar is unusually dense: Kimi's open weights land July 27, the MCP spec finalizes July 28, and on August 2 the EU AI Act's enforcement toolkit for general-purpose model providers activates — information requests, direct model evaluation, market withdrawal, and fines up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover. Europe's two sovereignty levers, buying a lab and writing the rules, arrive in the same fortnight.

## Research highlights

Sunday slice — arXiv RSS returns empty on weekends (`skipDays`), so today's papers are the freshest Jul-16 cs.CL batch via papers.cool; HF Papers was unreachable.

- **[OmniaBench: Benchmarking General AI Agents Across Diverse Scenarios](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14989)** — 1,431 executable tasks over a 90/354-domain taxonomy; frontier models struggle badly — Claude-Sonnet-5 and GPT-5.6-Sol reach only ~58.5% / 57.1% Pass@1, with persistent gaps in planning, constraint maintenance, and adaptive correction.
- **[SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777)** — Converts completed trajectories into natural-language "hindsight skills," then distills the skill-induced probability shift back into the policy as a dense token-level signal jointly optimized with outcome-based RL — closing the sparse-reward gap on text and vision agent tasks.
- **[Partition, Prompt, Aggregate: Statistical Self-Consistency in LMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15277)** — ETH Zurich / MPI-IS. A reference-free eval: do an LLM's prior-weighted conditional estimates obey the law of total probability across a partition? Frontier models widely violate it — a "macro fallacy" where subpopulation estimates beat direct population-level ones.
- **[In-Place Tokenizer Expansion for Pre-trained LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15232)** — Liquid AI. Upgrade a pretrained model's tokenizer by continuing its BPE merges on a multilingual corpus; applied to an 8B MoE to ship LFM2.5-8B-A1B with a 128K vocab, yielding ~2.2–3.7× per-character decode speedups on Hindi/Vietnamese/Thai.
- **[Mask-Aware Policy Gradients for Diffusion LMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15200)** — Formalizes masked-diffusion generation as a two-stage-action MDP so the policy gradient decomposes into token + masking terms; optimizing both gives SOTA MDLM results — 87.1% GSM8K, 53.4% MBPP.
- **[D-Cut: Adaptive Verification Depth Pruning for Batched Speculative Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14647)** — Cross-request draft-token pruning + a runtime cost model lift average speculative-decoding speedup from 1.26× to 1.65× under high concurrency (up to 3× on MoE) — restoring acceleration where long-draft baselines are slower than autoregressive decoding.
- **[Rubrics on Trial: Evolving Rubrics from a Single Query](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15092)** — Grows an eval-rubric set from empty with no human annotations or training, validating each candidate against synthetic rubric-conditioned response pairs; best average accuracy across five preference-benchmark suites.
- **[Grokipedia vs Wikipedia: An LLM Audit of Political Neutrality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15146)** — Four LLM judges (incl. Grok itself) rate Grokipedia less neutral than Wikipedia across 1,394 article pairs, favoring economically right-wing and penalizing socially liberal figures.
