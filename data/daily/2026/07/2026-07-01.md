## The day in 90 seconds

**Anthropic owned the AI news cycle today — and for the first time, the tone turned against it.**

Three separate Anthropic stories dominated Hacker News, and none of them were flattering. A day after Claude Sonnet 5 launched, the independent benchmarks arrived, a reverse-engineering writeup accused Claude Code of secretly fingerprinting requests, and the US government lifted export controls on two Claude models in exchange for new cyber-blocking classifiers. The through-line: the "trust the frontier labs" consensus cracked, loudly, and Anthropic was the one on the receiving end.

The loudest story was a steganography claim. A writeup on thereallo.dev (2,327 points, 698 comments — the day's runaway top thread) alleged that Claude Code embeds hidden markers in outbound requests to fingerprint usage, widely read as anti-distillation tracking. The thread split between "betrayal of trust" and "anti-cheat is fine," but the notable shift is what got upvoted: "you can't trust any of the big labs," pointed squarely at Anthropic, ran as a majority take rather than a fringe one for the first time this cycle. That sentiment landed the same day the Department of Commerce lifted export controls on Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 — the primary-source June 30 letter is posted in-thread — after Anthropic agreed to add classifiers blocking more cybersecurity tasks. Commenters flagged that Fable 5 reportedly can no longer be used for coding under those classifiers, and called the whole arrangement policy theater with an unpredictable core: state power is now an explicit release variable.

Underneath the controversy, Sonnet 5 moved from launch to verdict. Priced at an introductory $2/$10 through August 31 (against Opus 4.8's $5/$25) and defaulting on Free and Pro, it's being read as roughly GLM-5.2-level capability at about twice the cost and twice the speed — weaker on trivia and combined tool-calling, and, per its own system card, less capable than Sonnet 4.6 at CyberGym vulnerability discovery. The deliberate cyber suppression is no longer a talking point; it's a measurable product tradeoff. A new tokenizer that maps the same text to ~1.35x more tokens quietly eats into the headline price cut. Meanwhile OpenAI previewed its GPT-5.6 series (Sol, Terra, Luna) with Cerebras serving at up to 750 tokens/sec in July, and Google shipped Gemini 3.5 Flash plus Managed Agents — an API call that provisions a sandboxed Linux environment for an agent to run code and browse. The frontier cadence stays relentless.

The quieter but arguably more durable story was where enterprise money moved: SAP's ~$60M strategic investment doubled n8n's valuation to $5.2B, making it Germany's most valuable AI company and embedding its multi-agent workflow canvas directly into Joule Studio (GA targeted Q3). The orchestration canvas — not the model — is emerging as the enterprise battleground, a theme echoed by ServiceNow's Autonomous Workforce expansion and today's highest-signal paper mapping what MCP, A2A, and ACP structurally cannot express. A useful counterweight to all the agent hype came from a data-backed corrective: only 35% of organizations have deployed agents broadly, and 68% say half or fewer of their employees use agents daily.

**Read this first:** [Claude Code is steganographically marking requests](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48734373) — the thread, not the blog post, is where developer trust in Anthropic visibly turned; it's the story everyone will be arguing about tomorrow.

## Research highlights

- **[Governance Gaps in Agent Interoperability Protocols: What MCP, A2A, and ACP Cannot Express](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31498)** — Kang & Diponegoro. Systematic map of what the agent-interop stack structurally cannot express — enterprise governance constraints fall through the gaps while identity/discovery/messaging are covered. _cs.MA._
- **[Generative Skill Composition for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32025)** — Agents compose modular skills generatively rather than selecting from a fixed library, improving coverage on complex multi-step tasks. _cs.CL._
- **[CORTEX: Token-Level Hallucination Detection in RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31033)** — Localizes hallucinated spans inside long-form RAG outputs at token granularity via comparative internal representations, rather than flagging whole responses. _cs.CL._
- **[One Retrieval to Cover Them All: Session-Level RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31156)** — Co-occurrence-aware KB reorganization so a single retrieval serves a whole session of related enterprise questions. _cs.IR._
- **[Addressing Over-Refusal in LLMs with Competing Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31748)** — Kim & Kumar. Competing reward signals cut safety-induced over-refusal without weakening refusal of genuinely harmful prompts. _cs.LG._
- **[ComplianceGate: Classifier-Gated Multi-Tier LLM Routing for Regulated Industries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31163)** — A classifier gate routes queries across model tiers to enforce PII/compliance constraints before requests reach endpoints. _cs.LG._
- **[SeKV: Resolution-Adaptive KV Cache with Hierarchical Semantic Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31145)** — Tackles the long-context KV-cache bottleneck with resolution-adaptive hierarchical memory instead of a flat cache. _cs.CL._
