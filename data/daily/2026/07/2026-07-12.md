## The day in 90 seconds

**Three frontier labs shipped in 48 hours, and the enterprise quietly standardized on MCP**

This Sunday closes a week in which OpenAI, xAI, and Anthropic all pushed major releases inside a single 48-hour window, while a Chinese lab quietly matched the frontier on domestic silicon. The more durable story, though, sits underneath the headlines: every large workflow platform converged on Anthropic's Model Context Protocol as the standard way agents reach enterprise systems. For buyers, the takeaway is that capability is now table stakes; the fight has moved to price, distribution, and standards.

OpenAI anchored the blitz on July 9, moving its GPT-5.6 family — Sol, Terra, and Luna — to general availability across ChatGPT, the API, and Codex, folding Codex into the main app and launching ChatGPT Work, a business surface that drafts docs, sheets, and decks from context pulled across a user's connected apps. A day earlier, xAI released Grok 4.5, its first model built for coding and long-horizon agentic work and trained partly on real Cursor sessions; at $2 and $6 per million tokens it undercuts Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 by roughly 60% while spending a fraction of the output tokens per task — a pitch built on cost, not benchmark supremacy. Anthropic brought Claude Cowork to web and mobile with background agents that run scheduled work even when no device is online. And in the background, Meituan open-sourced LongCat-2.0, a 1.6-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model trained and served entirely on domestic Chinese chips that reportedly edges GPT-5.5 on SWE-bench Pro — the first trillion-parameter model built without NVIDIA hardware, and a reminder that the frontier is no longer a purely American contest.

The quieter thread may matter more. In the same week, ServiceNow made its MCP Server generally available in every Now Assist SKU, opening its full "system of action" to any AI agent; Salesforce shipped three Agentforce Commerce agents to GA; SAP launched a managed Joule Studio; and Snowflake positioned native MCP as the "control plane for the agentic enterprise." It is the clearest signal yet that MCP has won the enterprise tool layer, not merely joined it, and the platform layer has stopped debating whether to expose its tools to outside agents and is now racing to own agent governance and context on top of one shared standard. Underneath the applications, the money follows the same logic: Together AI raised an $800M Series C at an $8.3B valuation, more than doubling its early-2025 mark, as capital keeps pooling in the inference layer that serves all these agents. For anyone building retrieval, the companion signal is VentureBeat's report that context architecture is displacing vector-only RAG, with hybrid-retrieval intent tripling to roughly 33% in Q1.

What to watch next is a deadline, not a launch. The EU AI Act's enforcement clock is now three weeks from August 2, when obligations on general-purpose AI providers take effect and the Commission gains real teeth — information requests, model access, recalls, and fines up to €15M or 3% of global turnover. The grace period is ending for every frontier lab that serves Europe, and the GPAI Code of Practice is the main on-ramp. The cadence shows no sign of slowing either: Google's twice-delayed Gemini 3.5 Pro is now pointed at July 17 and DeepSeek V4 reaches general availability mid-month. The week's loudest sentiment marker, meanwhile, was legal rather than technical — Apple's trade-secret suit against OpenAI topped Hacker News at 1,622 points, with the thread reading it as the effective end of OpenAI's hardware ambitions.

## Research highlights

_Weekend run: arXiv RSS skips Sat/Sun, so today's set is drawn from papers.cool's cs.CL feed — the latest batch, all published 2026-07-09._

- **[UniClawBench: A Universal Benchmark for Proactive Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08768)** — Chen et al. (HKU-MMLab). First capability-driven benchmark for *proactive* agents over 400 bilingual tasks, evaluated live in Docker with a closed-loop executor/supervisor/user-agent design — deliberately disentangling base-model capability from agent-framework design.
- **[WebSwarm: Recursive Multi-Agent Orchestration for Deep-and-Wide Web Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08662)** — Song et al. Progressive recursive delegation where search nodes solve directly or spawn children that return evidence upward; beats single- and multi-agent baselines on BrowseComp-Plus, WideSearch, DeepWideSearch and GISA.
- **[Do You Need a Frontier Model as a Citation Verifier?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08700)** — Leung et al. Over 1,248 human-reviewed rubric decisions, cheaper judges stay competitive (GPT-5-mini leads source-relevance F1 at 0.908), but scalar F1 hides directional bias — calibrate the judge before using citation rubrics as an RL reward.
- **[When the Judge Changes, So Does the Measurement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08535)** — Yang et al. Frames evaluator-replacement drift as a measurement-validity problem: judge upgrades are not interchangeable, stronger judges only reduce (not remove) position/verbosity bias, and repeated-sample juries add little when errors correlate.
- **[Two Axes of LLM Abstention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08456)** — Wagner. Refusing wrong answers vs refusing unanswerable/false-premise questions are separate axes: answer-confidence tracks correctness but is blind to answerability, while a hidden-state probe does the reverse (0.69–0.77 AUROC on false-premise CREPE). The blind spot doesn't shrink with scale.
- **[Hidden Decoding at Scale: Latent Computation Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08186)** — Liu et al. Fixed-backbone scaling along the *sequence-length* dimension: each token expands into n streams whose KV cache serves as context, with near-linear attention cost. First sequence-length scaling shown at 100B+ MoE (WeLM-HD4-617B) — a practical continued-pretraining path.
- **[MAESTRO: Markov-chain Approximated Expert Sparsification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08601)** — Goel et al. Structured MoE pruning that models expert-activation trajectories as ergodic Markov chains; beats SOTA by up to 10.6% retention at a strict 50% compression, attacking the always-resident expert-bank memory bottleneck.

_Also: [XAlpha](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08332) (memory-driven multi-agent quant researcher), [UltraX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08646) (adaptive pretraining-data editing), [SQuaD-SQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08161) (small-model text-to-SQL via distillation)._
