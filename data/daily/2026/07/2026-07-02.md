## The day in 90 seconds

**Anthropic gets its flagship back — and a public trust audit**

The first frontier model ever suspended by government directive came back online July 1. In the same news cycle, Anthropic's own users were dissecting hidden fingerprints in Claude Code and silently downgraded verification in Claude Science. Model availability and vendor trust are now both live variables — and underneath the drama, the agent economy quietly picked up real price tags.

Anthropic restored Fable 5 across its API, apps, and Claude Code after the June 12–30 export-control suspension, twinned with Mythos 5 — the same model with cyber safety classifiers lifted, restricted to vetted cyberdefense partners. Pricing holds at $10/$50 per million tokens with a 1M context window, affected users get 50 percent extra weekly limits through July 7, and CAIS and Scale AI's updated Remote Labor Index puts Fable 5 ahead of every public model at 16.1 percent on 240 real remote-work projects. The redeployment caps a week in which Anthropic also shipped Claude Science, Claude Tag, a self-hosted apps gateway, and general availability in Microsoft Foundry — an enterprise control plane assembling around the models, not just the models themselves. The precedent matters more than the episode: frontier-model availability is now a policy instrument, switched off and on by directive, and every future release inherits that template.

The counterweight arrived the same day. Hacker News's biggest thread — 1,299 points — dissected hidden hostname-keyed fingerprints that Claude Code embeds in requests as an anti-distillation measure, with "fraud against paying customers" among the upvoted takes and Codex CLI's open-source status repeatedly raised as the alternative. With open-weight GLM 5.2 undercutting it on price-performance — the open-weight wave is the only rising topic on the radar board — the cost and trust math of closed frontier models is being audited in public.

Underneath, the agent economy kept hardening into ordinary commerce. Salesforce takes its Agentforce Help Agent to general availability this month at a flat $2 charged only when the agent resolves a case end to end, after 4.3 million inquiries handled at 70 percent resolution on its own help site; NVIDIA launched a revenue-sharing partner model that makes it co-owner of AI-cloud economics rather than a chip seller; and Cognition's Devin Security Swarm found 36 of 50 real vulnerabilities, more than any other AI scanner tested. Two deadlines to mark: the largest-ever MCP revision — stateless core, MCP Apps, Tasks — is frozen as a release candidate with under four weeks until the July 28 spec, and EU AI Act enforcement for general-purpose model providers lands August 2, five weeks out; Germany constituted its federal KI-Taskforce today to get ahead of it.

## Research highlights

- **Seed2.0 Model Card** — ByteDance Seed. _Takeaway:_ new frontier series built around an eval system abstracted from real user needs and realistic complexity rather than legacy benchmarks. [2607.00248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00248)
- **AutoMem: Automated Learning of Memory as a Cognitive Skill** — Wu, Zhu, Zhang, Wang, Yeung-Levy. _Takeaway:_ memory management as a trainable skill (file-system ops as first-class actions); memory optimization alone lifts long-horizon performance ~2–4x and makes a 32B open model competitive with Claude Opus 4.5 / Gemini 3.1 Pro Thinking. [2607.01224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01224)
- **Can Agents Generalize to the Open World?** — Lv et al. _Takeaway:_ "OpenAgent" setting (shifting queries, tool sets, interaction dynamics) breaks agents trained on static tool-use benchmarks. [2607.01084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01084)
- **Jailbreaking Function-Calling LLMs via Simulated Moderation Traces** — Liu, Wang, Luo, Jia. _Takeaway:_ injected fake moderation traces jailbreak tool-calling LLMs — a structural vulnerability of stateful function-calling runtimes; directly relevant to MCP/tool-use security. [2607.00481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00481)
- **MemSyco-Bench: Benchmarking Sycophancy in Agent Memory** — Xiang et al. _Takeaway:_ retrieved long-term memories can induce sycophancy — agents over-align with the user at the cost of factual accuracy. [2607.01071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01071)
- **One Retrieval to Cover Them All: Session-Level RAG** — Ratnakar, Zhu, Cheng, Vijayakumar. _Takeaway:_ a single retrieval covers only 41% of a session-level need; co-occurrence-aware KB reorganization raises it to 58%, cuts retrieval calls 34% — argues session coverage should replace single-query recall as the enterprise RAG metric. [2606.31156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31156)
- **Staleness–Learning-Rate Scaling Laws for Asynchronous RLHF** — Song et al. _Takeaway:_ scaling laws linking rollout staleness to learning rate — practical guidance for high-throughput decoupled RLHF. [2607.01083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01083)
- **Self-GC: Self-Governing Context for Long-Horizon Agents** — Hao et al. _Takeaway:_ agent context is too structured for chronological pruning or end-of-window summaries; agents should govern their own context as a first-class mechanism. [2607.00692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00692)
