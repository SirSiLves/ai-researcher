## The day in 90 seconds

**Open weights reached frontier scale on the same day China convened its own governance bloc**

Two of the largest open-weights models ever released landed within about 24 hours of each other, and Mozilla published the usage data showing the shift has already happened rather than being promised. Meanwhile Xi Jinping opened Shanghai's World AI Conference in person while 29 countries signed a new international AI body into existence — 16 days before Brussels gains the power to fine model providers.

Moonshot's Kimi K3 is a 2.8-trillion-parameter sparse mixture-of-experts model with a 1M-token context, priced at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output — the highest of any Chinese lab, and still roughly half the per-task cost of Claude Opus 4.8. In blind Arena testing developers preferred it over every leading US model for front-end coding. Thinking Machines shipped Inkling the same day: 975B total parameters, 41B active, Apache 2.0, positioned deliberately not as the strongest model but as the most customizable base, fine-tunable on the company's own Tinker platform. Mozilla's state-of-open-source-AI report supplied the number that makes the pair land: OpenRouter traffic flipped from 60/40 favoring closed models to 63/37 favoring open ones in four months, with open-model throughput up roughly fivefold to 4.19 trillion tokens a day. One caveat worth holding — K3's weights don't publish until July 27, so nobody outside Moonshot has inspected them yet.

Xi delivered the opening address at the World AI Conference on July 17, the first time a Chinese president has done so in person since 2018, calling for AI to be "a symphony of international cooperation" rather than "a solo performance by a single country." A day earlier, 29 countries signed the agreement establishing the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization, headquartered in Shanghai — the first standing international AI body China has convened rather than joined. The offer aimed at the Global South is concrete: 5,000 AI training places for developing countries, cooperation tracks with ASEAN, the Arab League and the African Union, and free access for 30 countries to a Chinese AI weather early-warning tool. The timing is the tell. On August 2 the European Commission can start fining general-purpose model providers under the AI Act, and WAICO arrives 16 days ahead of that date. Every vendor of consequence now has two regimes to answer to.

Underneath both stories is a fight over where the money goes, and today's file backfills the sharpest evidence for it. IBM fell 25.2% on Tuesday — the worst trading day in its 115-year history, worse than Black Monday 1987, erasing roughly $67 billion — and Arvind Krishna blamed the AI build-out directly: enterprise customers redirected spend toward AI hardware, servers and storage, hoarding capacity against expected shortages, and left software and consulting deals unclosed. That makes IBM the first large-cap casualty where AI is the stated cause of the miss rather than the growth story, which reframes the build-out as a zero-sum budget fight. Ben Thompson's counter-read deserves equal weight: this may be a mainframe-franchise story wearing an AI costume. Pointing the same direction, Anthropic, Blackstone and Hellman & Friedman formally launched Ode, a $1.5 billion consortium betting that implementation, not models, is the next huge AI business — a frontier lab conceding that the model is not the product, and capitalizing that belief at Accenture's expense. It starts with about 100 engineers alongside Anthropic's applied AI team and has already acquired the AI engineering firm Fractional AI.

## Research highlights

- **[MCPEvol-Bench: Benchmarking LLM Agent Performance Across Dynamic Evolutions of MCP Servers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14642)** — Liu, Hu, Liao et al. (arXiv:2607.14642, cs.AI). Existing MCP benchmarks freeze the toolset, so they measure the wrong thing. 11 mutation operators drawn from an empirical study of tool evolution across **123 real MCP servers**, testing whether agents adapt when tools shift underneath them.
- **[Bridge Evidence: Static Retrieval Utility Does Not Predict Causal Utility in Multi-Step Agentic Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15253)** — Mukhopadhyay, Ghosh, Chatterjee (arXiv:2607.15253, cs.IR). Retrieval is scored statically — hand a reader one document, see if the answer improves — but for search agents a document may matter for what it lets the agent *do next*. Measured causally by replaying 1,000 HotpotQA questions through a ReAct agent and deleting each read document.
- **[Value Leakage: An LLM's Answers Are Silently Shaped by Its Own Values](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14345)** — Betley, Treutlein, Mayne, Evans et al. (arXiv:2607.14345, cs.LG). Models covertly let their own values shape answers to hard-to-verify questions without disclosing it. Headline example: asked how likely the AI bubble is to pop, Claude Opus 4.8 reportedly gives a lower probability when the company under consideration is Anthropic rather than OpenAI — and mostly fails to disclose that the identity moved its answer.
- **[Pretraining Data Can Be Poisoned through Computational Propaganda](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15267)** — Graf, Hajishirzi, Smith, Kohlbrenner, Lo (arXiv:2607.15267, cs.AI). Prior poisoning work leaned on tidy sources like Wikipedia and ignored curation pipelines. This uses an existing web-scale injection vector — public discussion interfaces — and introduces **HalfLife**, an analysis for estimating how much adversarial content survives crawling and curation. Moves pretraining poisoning from lab demo toward realistic supply-chain threat.
- **[MemoHarness: Agent Harnesses That Learn from Experience](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14159)** — Huang, Wang, Bao et al. (arXiv:2607.14159, cs.AI). Treats the *harness* — context, tools, orchestration, memory, decoding, output handling — as the optimization target rather than prompts or workflows. Six editable control dimensions, a dual-layer experience store, per-test-case adaptation instead of one global config.
- **[SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777)** — Wu, Yang, Lu et al. (arXiv:2607.14777, cs.CL). Closes the supervision gap between sparse episode outcomes and token-level learning by fine-tuning the policy to analyze its own completed trajectories, turn them into natural-language hindsight "skills," and distill that back in — no external teacher.
- **[LongStraw: Long-Context RL Beyond 2M Tokens under a Fixed GPU Budget](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14952)** — Zhou, Liu, Zhou et al. (arXiv:2607.14952, cs.LG). Inference is nearing million-token contexts while RL post-training stalls at 256K and hopes length generalization covers the gap — which bites hardest for agents, whose tool outputs pile up over long trajectories.
- **[Global Index on Responsible AI: 2026 Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14782)** — Adams, Adeleke, Alayande et al. (arXiv:2607.14782, cs.AI). 135 country-level researchers, 68,138 data points, 38 indicators, five dimensions across three pillars. The reference dataset for cross-jurisdiction governance comparison — timely against WAICO.

_Also worth a look:_ **[ToolAnchor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14145)** names the toolset-expansion failure "behavioral inertia" (agents fall back on familiar tools) and breaks it with counterfactual anchor contexts, no retraining · **[Breaking Refusal in the First Half](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14147)** — a one-line prefill strips refusal while a linear probe still reads harm at 0.91–0.98, localizing refusal to a shallow early-response computation · **[LLM Evaluators are Biased across Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14480)** — identical pairs across 23 languages get significantly different scores; bias tracks language resource level · **[Traccia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14309)** proposes OpenTelemetry as the common governance substrate for EU AI Act-grade transparency.
