## The day in 90 seconds

**The morning after the launch blitz: benchmarks bite, the platform war takes over**

Yesterday three labs shipped frontier models in a single afternoon. Today the independent scoreboards arrived and complicated every launch-day claim, while the real contest quietly moved from whose model is smartest to whose software owns your workday — and a visibly tired developer audience began asking whether any of it counts as progress.

Grok 4.5 is the clearest cautionary tale. Twenty-four hours of outside testing put xAI's model at #4 on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index yet handed it the single best agentic tool-use result on the board, plus first place on Snorkel's GDPval+ suite of roughly 2,000 expert workplace tasks at 29%, ahead of GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8. The catch is severe: hallucination roughly doubled, from 25% to 54%, even as accuracy climbed to 52% — a model that is better at calling tools and more often confidently wrong about facts. xAI's claim that Grok burns 4.2× fewer tokens than Opus 4.8 on SWE-bench Pro implies as much as 17× lower per-task cost if it transfers, though its context window quietly halved to 500k. The reliability split is why "which benchmark?" is now the only honest answer to "is it any good?" — and why the evaluation-gap thread keeps climbing the radar.

The strategically louder story is OpenAI's. Alongside the GPT-5.6 general release it folded Chat, Work, and Codex into one desktop application, sunset the Atlas browser, and pitched ChatGPT Work as an agent that runs for hours across your connected apps and hands back finished sheets, slides, and web apps. Applications chief Fidji Simo framed the move as ChatGPT going "from a chat interface to a full computing environment" — an unmistakable strike at Anthropic's Claude Cowork, which reached web and mobile three days earlier. Meta undercut the whole field on price: Muse Spark 1.1, its first credible agentic-coding model since Llama 4, opened on the Meta Model API at $1.25 in / $4.25 out per million tokens, below Grok's $2/$6, with Zuckerberg breaking a three-year silence on X to announce it. Yet the loudest signal wasn't a spec sheet — GPT-5.6 topped Hacker News at nearly 1,500 points, its comments openly reconsidering which coding agent to trust by default, beside a 399-point thread titled "I think I have LLM burnout."

Underneath the launches, money and rules pushed the opposite way — toward owning your agents rather than renting them. SambaNova closed the first tranche of a $1B round at an $11B valuation and named JPMorganChase an on-prem inference partner, a rare non-Nvidia win inside the world's largest bank; Prime Intellect raised $130M at a $1B valuation, on a reported $100M annual run-rate, selling an "AI-lab-in-a-box" so enterprises can train their own agents off closed APIs; and Deutsche Telekom said it would double the NVIDIA-built sovereign "AI factory" it runs in Munich. Brussels, meanwhile, unveiled an Action Plan to build EU capacity to test frontier models before they reach the market, targeted operational by 2027 — weeks before the AI Act bites on August 2, and the same week Grok deferred its own EU launch. Why all of this matters landed on Hacker News too: GitLost, a clean trick that made GitHub's AI agent leak private repositories, drew 536 points and pushed AI-security agents to the single topic the radar promoted to "emerging" — a reminder that the binding constraint on autonomous agents is no longer capability but trust.

**Read this first:** [Grok 4.5's first-day verdict on Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/grok-4-5) — the single page that reframes yesterday's launch, and the cleanest illustration of why a higher benchmark no longer settles anything.

## Research highlights

- **[WebSwarm: Recursive Multi-Agent Orchestration for Deep-and-Wide Web Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08662)** — Song, Zhang, Dong, … Dou. Progressive recursive-delegation: each search node couples a local objective with a search mode and either solves it or spawns children, returning evidence upward. Beats single- and multi-agent baselines on BrowseComp-Plus, WideSearch, DeepWideSearch, GISA.
- **[Who Broke the System? Failure Localization in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07989)** — Xia et al. AgentLocate pins a multi-agent failure to both the responsible agent *and* the earliest decisive step, via an LLM judge + multi-perspective verification + confidence-aware aggregation. Cheap on tokens/runtime.
- **[Context Graphs for Proactive Enterprise Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07721)** — Kumar. RAG/agent stacks stay reactive; a live relational graph of enterprise entities, relationships, and state transitions lets agents surface actionable info before a human asks. (Retrieval read of the day.)
- **[Remember When It Matters: Proactive Memory Agent for Long-Horizon Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08716)** — Wu et al. Names "behavioral state decay" — requirements, prior attempts, open subgoals get buried as trajectories grow — and runs a separate memory agent that treats memory as active intervention, not passive retrieval.
- **[Token-Flow Firewall: Semantic Runtime Auditing for Persistent AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08395)** — Wang et al. Persistent state, reusable skills, and tool calls create a far larger semantic attack surface than chat; since most security-critical interactions travel as natural-language token flows, they add a runtime firewall that semantically audits them. (Direct match to today's GitLost thread.)
- **[Persuasion Attacks Can Decrease Effectiveness of CoT Monitoring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08066)** — Za et al. Persuasion-style attacks measurably degrade a chain-of-thought monitor's ability to catch misaligned/deceptive behavior — a hole in a safety mechanism many labs count on.
- **[When the Judge Changes, So Does the Measurement: Auditing LLM-as-Judge Reliability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08535)** — Yang et al. An LLM-as-judge score can move with candidate responses held fixed, simply because the evaluator changed; frames it as a measurement-validity problem. (Feeds today's "evaluation gap" radar riser.)
- **[Jet-Long: Efficient Long-Context Extension with Dynamic Bifocal RoPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07740)** — Tang et al. Cheaper, more stable long-context extension for RAG, repo-level coding, and agent traces that push inputs an order of magnitude past pretraining length.
