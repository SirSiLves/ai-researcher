## The day in 90 seconds

**The open-weight frontier moved twice in 48 hours — the day before Shanghai's big show**

Two labs on opposite sides of the US-China divide shipped frontier-class open models in the same 48-hour window, and they landed the day before the World AI Conference opens in Shanghai. If you build on closed APIs, the price floor under you just dropped again.

Moonshot quietly made Kimi K3 available to logged-in users on July 16, ahead of any formal announcement: a Mixture-of-Experts model of roughly 2.5 trillion total parameters, native context past a million tokens, multimodal input, and early benchmark chatter that matches or edges Anthropic's Opus 4.8 — at a fraction of the run cost. Full weights are promised "in the coming days." A day earlier, Mira Murati's Thinking Machines released Inkling, its first open model, under Apache 2.0: an MoE with 975 billion total parameters (about 41 billion active), trained on 45 trillion tokens of text, image, audio and video. The pairing frames an explicit contest — an American open model answering a Chinese one within a day — and it reads sharply against Nathan Lambert's warning this week that open weights above a certain capability threshold may face a realistic ban within six months. Hacker News is already picking Inkling apart: strong at instruction-following, weaker at coding, and about 30% bigger than GLM 5.2 for no obvious gain.

Underneath the model noise, the money says the build-out is accelerating, not cooling. TSMC posted a record second quarter — roughly $40.2 billion in revenue at a 67.7% gross margin — and did the thing chip investors watch for: it raised full-year guidance again, lifting its 2026 growth outlook above 40% and its capital budget to a record $60–64 billion, with another $100 billion earmarked for US fabs. "Our conviction in the multi-year AI megatrend remains very high," CEO C.C. Wei said. The startup capital is just as loud: DeepSeek is reportedly raising up to 50 billion yuan at a roughly $74 billion valuation while it preps a Shanghai STAR Market listing, and Indian coding startup Emergent hit $1.5 billion as part of a single day that saw more than $1 billion raised across eight AI deals.

The quieter, more durable story is the plumbing. The Model Context Protocol published its July 28 release candidate — the biggest revision since launch — rebuilding the core to run stateless on ordinary round-robin HTTP, with no sticky sessions, plus server-rendered UIs (MCP Apps) and long-running work (Tasks). At roughly 97 million monthly downloads and 5,800-plus servers, that is production infrastructure, not a spec draft. Alongside it, the day's clearest engineering signal is retrieval: the "RAG-MCP" pattern argues that once an agent has 200-plus tools, you should retrieve the handful that match rather than stuff a bigger context window, while Meta FAIR's Cluster-with-Auctions paper reports up to 4.7x vector-search throughput at equal recall. It fits the week's throughline — the real work has moved to building systems around agents (harnesses, context, evals), not just with them. And Coinbase gave the adoption number of the day — 95 to 100% of its code is now AI-assisted, up from about 40% in February, with engineers running five to ten coding agents in parallel.

All of it converges on Shanghai. WAIC opens July 17 at record scale — 1,000-plus exhibitors, 300-plus product debuts, and a High-Level Meeting on Global AI Governance — colliding with an expected Gemini 3.5 Pro launch and the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency duty, which lands August 2, just over two weeks out. Watch whether the open-weights momentum survives contact with the governance agenda.

## Research highlights

- **[Ring-Zero: Scaling Zero RL to a Trillion Parameters for Emergent Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12395)** — scales verifiable-reward RL (no human labels) to a 1T-param model via a stability pipeline; reports self-verification and parallel reasoning emerging spontaneously at scale. One of the first public looks at RL dynamics at the 1T frontier.
- **[Agora: Collective and Permissionless Internet-Scale Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13332)** — "Protocol Learning" shards a model across heterogeneous, preemptible consumer GPUs so no party holds full weights; trains Pluralis-8B on 500B tokens via 330 volunteer nodes at ~63% of a centralized H100 baseline. A concrete challenge to datacenter-bound pretraining.
- **[Where Should RL Post-Training Compute Go?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13389)** — a FLOP-accounting framework for GRPO that decomposes budget into search / learning / reward-eval; the optimal split shifts with model size, budget and reward type. Argues RL papers should report *how* FLOPs are divided.
- **[Learning Latency-Aware Orchestration for Multi-Agent Systems (LAMaS)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13359)** — learns latency-aware execution graphs plus a runtime controller that prunes redundant agent interactions; cuts end-to-end latency >50% while holding or improving accuracy.
- **[AgentCheck: A Reproduce-Intervene-Mitigate Workbench for LLM Agents over MCP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11098)** — turns an MCP server into a fault-injection surface (12 fault types) to test agent mitigations before deployment; finds failures are usually silent, confident use of bad tool outputs, and stale-data faults persist through retries.
- **[Oracle Agent Memory as an Enterprise Memory Substrate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13157)** — frames agent memory as a database-native lifecycle with scope control; 93.8% on LongMemEval at ~10.7× fewer tokens than flat-history baselines.
- **[Cluster with Auctions for Vector Search (CwA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13728)** — Meta FAIR (Douze, Jégou et al.) decouple the query probing function from the DB partition in ANN search; up to 4.7× throughput at equal recall when query and database distributions differ. Directly relevant to production vector stores.
- **[Final Authority in AI Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13040)** — reading the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF and Singapore/Japan/Canada instruments, argues for distributed operational accountability: final authority with the deployer/consequence-bearer, upstream authority reserved for frontier-capability gating.
