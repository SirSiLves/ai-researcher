## The day in 90 seconds

**Agent infrastructure grows up — just as the trust layer springs leaks**

The plumbing beneath AI agents took a decisive step toward production today, led by the Model Context Protocol's largest revision since launch. But the same cycle exposed how leaky the tooling above that plumbing still is, as a wire-level teardown of xAI's Grok CLI became the sharpest agent-security story of the week. Beneath both, the models themselves slid further into a price war — even as regulators and rival giants began fighting over who gets to control them.

The headline is infrastructure finally built for scale. MCP's 2026-07-28 Release Candidate rewrites the protocol around a stateless core — no sessions, no init handshake, servers that can sit behind a plain load balancer — and realigns authentication with OAuth 2.1 and OpenID Connect, removing the two biggest blockers that had kept MCP servers out of production. It also adds an extensions framework, sandboxed HTML UIs (MCP Apps) and a Tasks extension for long-running work. The enterprise-delivery bets pushed the same way: Snowflake opened Cortex Sense, a runtime knowledge layer that assembles business context at query time and claims 86% accuracy on structured questions against 24% for a generic frontier model, while Microsoft's new $2.5B Frontier Company embeds roughly 6,000 engineers inside customer organizations — a wager, echoing AWS's Forward Deployed unit, that the bottleneck is now integration, not raw capability.

The counterweight is trust. A gist dissecting exactly what xAI's Grok build CLI uploads — your entire repository plus git history, regardless of what the agent is asked to read — drew 509 points on Hacker News and spawned a viral "it exfiltrated my home directory" companion. It landed amid a wider backlash: a disputed-but-loud claim that Claude Code sends 33,000 tokens before it even reads your prompt, and a hardening "prefer opencode plus a bring-your-own model" consensus. Show HN's Clawk, a disposable Linux VM for coding agents, looks like the market already routing around the problem. For a field racing to ship agents into production, the day's loudest signal is that developers increasingly distrust the runners they are sold.

The third thread is price. Meta shipped Muse Spark 1.1 — natively multimodal, 1M-token context, built for agentic work — on its first real developer API at $1.25 and $4.25 per million input and output tokens, explicitly undercutting OpenAI and Anthropic. DeepSeek graduated V4 to general availability with a 1M-token context across the lineup and, more pointedly, demand-shaped pricing that bills peak hours at twice the off-peak rate. With Gemini 3 Deep Think now reaching the Gemini API, frontier competition has moved onto agentic-coding turf and is being fought on cost. An open-weight, MIT-licensed frontier model priced by time of day is a genuinely new lever — a reminder that the cost floor is increasingly set outside the big US labs.

What to watch is who controls the models. Apple sued OpenAI and Jony Ive's io Products on July 10, accusing them of a coordinated campaign to steal AI-hardware trade secrets — a stunning reversal for the 2024 ChatGPT-in-iPhone partners. The regulatory clock is louder still: the EU AI Act's GPAI supervision, evaluation and fine powers switch on August 2, three weeks out, while Washington has already applied export controls to an AI model rather than the chips, ordering Anthropic to deny all foreign nationals access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5. Nathan Lambert even reports White House talk of an executive order to "manage" open models — the fight over open weights is only beginning.

**Read this first:** [MCP's 2026-07-28 Release Candidate](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/) — the stateless-plus-OAuth rewrite is the single change that turns MCP from a demo protocol into production infrastructure, and much of today's other news is downstream of it.

## Major news & releases

**Meta ships Muse Spark 1.1 and opens the Meta Model API.** On Jul 9 Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Spark 1.1 — a natively multimodal reasoning model (text/image/video/PDF/audio in) built for agentic work, with a 1M-token context, built-in context compression, and native multi-agent orchestration — as a public preview on the new **Meta Model API**, its first real developer API, at $1.25/$4.25 per M input/output tokens. Meta enters the crowded agentic-coding race with price as the wedge. [ai.meta.com](https://ai.meta.com/blog/introducing-muse-spark-meta-model-api/)

**DeepSeek V4 graduates to GA with peak/off-peak API pricing.** The mid-July release converts the April V4 preview (V4-Pro, V4-Flash) to GA with a **1M-token context across the whole lineup** and stronger agentic/math/code. DeepSeek also introduced time-of-day pricing — peak hours billed at 2× off-peak — and retires the legacy `deepseek-chat`/`deepseek-reasoner` names on Jul 24. An open-weight (MIT) frontier model using demand-shaped pricing is a new competitive lever. [api-docs.deepseek.com](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/news/news260424/)

**Apple sues OpenAI over alleged trade-secret theft for AI hardware.** In a Jul 10 federal suit (N.D. Cal.), Apple accuses OpenAI and Jony Ive's io Products of a coordinated campaign to steal hardware trade secrets, alleging OpenAI's hardware chief (ex-Apple) directed interviewees to bring "actual parts" for "show and tell." OpenAI denied it. The highest-profile IP fight yet over consumer AI hardware — and a stunning reversal for the 2024 ChatGPT-in-iPhone partners. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/10/apple-openai-lawsuit-trade-secrets.html)

**MCP publishes the 2026-07-28 spec Release Candidate.** Its largest revision since launch rewrites the protocol around a **stateless core** (removes sessions, drops the init handshake, works behind a plain load balancer), realigns auth with **OAuth 2.1 / OpenID Connect**, and adds an extensions framework, **MCP Apps** (sandboxed interactive HTML UIs), and a **Tasks** extension for long-running work. Stateless scaling + real OAuth remove the two biggest blockers to running MCP servers in production. [modelcontextprotocol.io](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/)

**Microsoft launches Frontier Company, a $2.5B "deployment, not models" bet.** Announced Jul 2, the new operating business embeds ~6,000 industry and engineering experts inside customer organizations to co-design and continuously improve AI systems against measurable outcomes — explicitly model-diverse (OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft AI, open-source). It mirrors AWS's $1B Forward Deployed Engineering unit: the hyperscalers are racing to own enterprise AI *delivery*, betting the bottleneck is now integration, not raw capability. [blogs.microsoft.com](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/07/02/microsoft-frontier-company-ai-engineering-that-amplifies-and-protects-your-intelligence/)

**Snowflake opens Cortex Sense to private preview — a runtime knowledge layer for agents.** Cortex Sense assembles data, business definitions, and operational knowledge for agents *at query time*, and Snowflake reports 86% accuracy on structured questions with full business context vs 24% for generic frontier models alone. The retrieval/knowledge-infra frontier: enterprises moving past vector-only RAG toward runtime context assembly, with the data-cloud vendors bidding to own the grounding layer. [snowflake.com](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/enterprise-ai-agents-grounded-context/)

**The non-GPU inference layer keeps shifting.** The first product of Nvidia's ~$20B non-exclusive license of Groq's LPU architecture — the Nvidia Groq 3 LPU (Samsung 4nm) — is slated to reach customers in Q3 2026, while Cerebras trades above a $40B market cap post-IPO and SambaNova claims 600–700 tok/s vs ~250 for GPUs. Specialized inference silicon is consolidating even as wafer-scale challengers find hyperscaler homes. [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/05/nvidia-groq-deal-ai-chip-startups-in-play/)

## Research highlights

- **[Toward Auditable AI Scientists: A Hypothesis Evolution Protocol for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09195)** — Takahara & Mizoguchi. Turns hypothesis generation/evaluation/evolution into explicit, auditable operations; a HEP agent runs the full hypothesis–test–evidence–belief loop and exploits the protocol more as the base LLM strengthens. _(arXiv:2607.09195, cs.AI)_
- **[Remember When It Matters: Proactive Memory Agent for Long-Horizon Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08716)** — Wu et al. (Meta AI). Treats memory as an active intervention, surfacing decision-relevant state exactly when a decision needs it — attacking the failure mode where retrieval-only memory misses what matters. _(arXiv:2607.08716)_
- **[HaloGuard 1.0: An Open-Weights Constitutional Classifier for Multilingual AI Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02079)** — a 0.8B input-safety classifier (46-policy constitution, 46 languages) that tops seven prompt-safety benchmarks (avg F1 90.9), beating open guards up to 27B. _(arXiv:2607.02079)_
- **[CheckRLM: Knowledge-Thought Coherence Checking in Retrieval-Augmented Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02262)** — wires RAG into the chain of thought: extracts factual claims from the reasoning chain, localizes inconsistencies at inference, and applies minimal-cost corrections. A clean retrieval-checks-reasoning pattern. _(arXiv:2607.02262)_
- **[Know Your Source: A Public Knowledge Store for Media Background Checks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02383)** — attacks the RAG-fact-checking assumption that retrieved evidence is reliable; MEDIAREF enables reproducible, low-cost source-critical reasoning across 200 media sources. _(arXiv:2607.02383)_
- **[Will Scaling Improve Social Simulation with LLMs?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02464)** — Ziems et al. Across 85 Qwen3-arch models, opinion/behavioral simulation improves with scale for well-represented populations, but longitudinal forecasting, underrepresented views, and calibration to human biases scale slowly or not at all. _(arXiv:2607.02464)_
