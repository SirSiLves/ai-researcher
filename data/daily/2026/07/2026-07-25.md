## The day in 90 seconds

**Opus 5's coronation meets its cold shower as open weights go political**

A day after launch, Claude Opus 5 sits atop the Artificial Analysis intelligence index, leads Hacker News, and is already live on Snowflake — yet the first week's practitioner reviews are markedly cooler than the benchmarks. At the same time, the open-versus-closed fight moved from forums to Washington, days before Moonshot frees Kimi K3's 2.8-trillion-parameter weights on Monday.

Twenty-four hours after Anthropic shipped it, Claude Opus 5 owns the scoreboard. It sits #1 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — 61 at its "max" setting and 60 at "xhigh," with Opus 5 variants and Fable 5 filling most of the top slots — leads Hacker News at 1,720 points and 1,214 comments, and arrived same-day in public preview on Snowflake's Cortex AI. The detail practitioners keep repeating is not a benchmark at all: Opus 5 ships with no data-retention requirement, unlike Fable's thirty-day window, which several readers call the real unlock for regulated buyers. But the counter-signal is just as loud. Every's Dan Shipper, testing it in daily work, calls the model "brilliant in flashes, frustrating in practice" — it argues with instructions, quits tasks early, and breaks skills and plugins built around the previous model. The most-cited agentic moment captures the split: handed a part drawing it could not actually see, Opus 5 wrote its own computer-vision pipeline mid-task to read it. Number-watchers add that the effort curve is non-monotonic, with "max" scoring below "xhigh" on Frontier-Bench.

The structural story sits a layer down, and today it turned political. A coalition of more than twenty technology companies — Nvidia, Microsoft and Meta among the named signatories, with Google and Amazon conspicuously absent — pressed the Trump administration to back open-weight models and warned against "premature restrictions," Nvidia publishing the position paper behind the push. The timing is deliberate: on Monday, Moonshot frees Kimi K3's full 2.8-trillion-parameter, one-million-token weights under a Modified MIT license — the largest open-weight release yet, ranked fourth among frontier models in independent testing, past Opus 4.8 — while UK and US safety institutes ready a rare joint government readout of its cyber capabilities. The catch is physical: the roughly 1.4-terabyte download means "open" is not the same as cheaply self-hostable. Hacker News sentiment, tellingly, has hardened against Anthropic and OpenAI as the fight sharpens.

Beneath the model news, the money is repricing around inference. Alphabet posted its first-ever negative quarterly free cash flow — roughly −$5.8B — as data-center spending outran operating cash and it lifted 2026 capex guidance to $195–205B; Intel beat estimates and raised its own capex to $20B; AMD paired with Cerebras to split inference across mixed silicon; and Etched raised $300M at a $10.3B valuation for transformer-only inference chips. Serving models, not training them, is now the industry's largest and fastest-growing cost — and that cost finally has a paying demand side, as Norm AI raised a $120M Series C to turn regulations into compliance agents and agent startups took roughly 58% of July's $1.8B in agent funding. That backdrop makes the week ahead the real event: Kimi K3's weights land Monday, the Model Context Protocol ships its biggest revision on 28 July, and on 2 August the EU AI Act's enforcement powers for general-purpose models switch on — even as US lawmakers float a bipartisan "Kill Switch Act" that would let Homeland Security throttle or halt an advanced model mid-crisis, at up to $20M a day.

## Research highlights

_Saturday note: all six arXiv RSS feeds returned zero items (weekend skipDays); papers.cool surfaced Friday's 24 Jul announcement, deduped against yesterday's file._

- **[Sample-Efficient Learning from Agent Experience](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21051)** (2607.21051) — "Experience Distillation" folds an agent's in-context trial-and-error into its weights with no extra environment calls, retaining ≥64.8% of in-context gains (vs 3.8% for plain SFT) and matching RL on 749 SWE tasks with ≥9.6× fewer samples.
- **[Anti-Periodic Positional Encoding: Möbius Boundary Conditions Make In-Context Retrieval Reliable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21405)** (2607.21405) — a one-line RoPE frequency swap lifts needle-in-a-haystack retrieval from 63%±31% to 90%±6% at unchanged perplexity; a zero-cost hedge against the retrieval "seed lottery." The most directly useful RAG result of the day.
- **[QuantiBias: Benchmarking Quantization-Induced Bias in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21063)** (2607.21063) — quantized models pass every standard short-form safety check yet volunteer stereotypes in ~24–27% of open-ended answers across eight languages — a selective bias gap current safety evals miss.
- **[Token Budget Saturation and Mechanistic Early Detection of Reasoning Non-Convergence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21433)** (2607.21433) — converged CoT runs hit 90.3% on AIME vs 6.6% for budget-exhausting ones; linear probes on layer-20 activations flag doomed runs as early as token 50, enabling early-exit/adaptive compute.
- **[Capital Markets LLM Reliability Score (CM-LRS)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21340)** (2607.21340) — a seven-dimension workflow-level reliability rubric for regulated finance ranks Sonnet 4.6 (4.31), Opus 4.7 (4.30), GPT-5.5 (4.09) near-tied above Llama 3.3 70B, with the gap concentrated in retrieval/synthesis, not extraction.
- **[One More Turn, Less Regret (RegretBench)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21143)** (2607.21143) — treats clarification as a hidden-intent sequential policy and scores regret against a reference, exposing that equal-accuracy assistants differ sharply in interaction cost and stopping behavior.
