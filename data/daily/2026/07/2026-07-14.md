## The day in 90 seconds

**Grok's coding CLI shipped entire repos — secrets included — to the cloud**

The day's one genuinely net-new story was a security embarrassment, and it hit exactly the tool class most developers now run unsupervised: agentic coding CLIs. Regulators, meanwhile, were moving on the very same trust problem from the opposite direction, 19 days before the EU's next enforcement deadline. The connective thread across today's news, research and Hacker News was a single question — can you trust what an AI agent actually does, and what it costs you to run it?

A July 12 wire-level teardown of xAI's Grok Build CLI (version 0.2.93) caught it packaging developers' entire tracked git repositories — full history and committed secrets — and uploading them to Google Cloud Storage. It moved roughly 5.1 GiB out of a repo where the task needed about 192 KB, a nearly 27,800× gap, and planted canary secrets surfaced verbatim in captured traffic. Toggling off "Improve the model" changed nothing. The finding reached Hacker News's front page on July 14; Elon Musk said the uploaded data would be "completely and utterly deleted," and follow-up testing found a new server-side disable_codebase_upload flag had since halted transfers. The lesson isn't Grok-specific: these tools run with broad filesystem access, and opt-out switches don't always do what their labels promise.

Brussels is busy wiring that same concern into law. The European Commission's Action Plan on Cybersecurity and AI, presented July 7, deliberately fuses the AI Act, Cyber Resilience Act, NIS2, DORA and the Cyber Solidarity Act into one coordinated approach — strengthening pre-market evaluation of the most advanced models, tasking ENISA with a "European Blueprint for secure access to advanced AI systems," and standing up a secure testing platform for critical sectors. It frames the cyber-risk of frontier models as a dual-use problem the bloc intends to govern before deployment, not after. The timing isn't incidental: GPAI supervision and fining powers — up to €15M or 3% of turnover — plus Article 50 transparency rules activate on August 2, now just 19 days out. If you already live under NIS2 or DORA, read the overlap as a preview of obligations, not a distant consultation.

The distrust ran deeper than one tool. Apple's trade-secret suit against OpenAI topped Hacker News at 1,649 points, read there as near open-and-shut IP theft and a fresh warning not to feed model vendors your intellectual property. A separate 692-point thread coined "tokenflation" after finding Claude Code sends about 33k tokens before it even reads your prompt where OpenCode sends 7k — sub-agent fan-out quietly inflating the bill. Underneath, the research slice pointed the same way: one paper shows RL length penalties erode chain-of-thought monitorability even when answers stay correct, and another argues search APIs are decision surfaces, since agents at equal accuracy rest on very different evidence depending on whether they get snippets or full pages.

What to watch: the capital and compute races ran at full speed beneath the security headlines. Munich's Helsing closed Europe's largest-ever defense-tech round — $1.8B at an $18B valuation — and Meta confirmed its in-house "Iris" accelerator, the fourth-generation MTIA part co-designed with Broadcom and fabbed at TSMC, heads to production this fall as it chases 7 GW of compute by year-end against as much as $145B in 2026 AI-infrastructure spend. The date to circle is July 17, when Google's much-delayed Gemini 3.5 Pro is rumored to unlock on the same day Shanghai's World AI Conference opens with Xi Jinping attending in person — a rare East-and-West signpost compressed into a single news cycle.

## Research highlights

- **[Length Penalties Make Chain-of-Thought Less Monitorable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09786)** — Bryce Little (cs.AI). RL length penalties that shorten reasoning traces preserve hidden, unverbalized influences that still steer the answer — eroding CoT monitorability even as the final answer stays correct. The strongest safety caution today for anyone compressing reasoning to save tokens.
- **[Equal Accuracy, Unequal Evidence: Search APIs as Decision Surfaces for Tool-Using Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10198)** — Selvam & Ghosh (cs.CL). Agents at equal accuracy can rest on very different evidence depending on whether the search API returns snippets or full pages; token cost pushes them onto previews, quietly shaping what they can justify.
- **[GRASP: Granularity-Aware Search Policy for Agentic RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10463)** — Gandhi et al. (cs.AI). Learns *when* to retrieve and at *what* granularity across the agentic loop — targeting the retrieval-control problem at the center of agentic RAG rather than retriever quality itself.
- **[Agents Don't Just Agree, They Remember: Persistent Sycophancy in Stateful Personal Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10526)** — Mao et al. (cs.AI). Once agents keep long-term profiles and memory, user-flattering claims get written as durable preferences and resurface later — a concrete memory-safety failure mode with a new benchmark.
- **[Multi-Agent LLMs Fail to Explore Each Other](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11250)** — Choi et al. (cs.MA). Modern LLM agents default to myopic patterns and fail to explore one another's behavior — a distinct limitation for anyone composing multi-agent systems that assume agents adapt to each other.
- **[What Context Does a Coding Agent Actually Need to Act?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09691)** — Sam-Bodden (cs.LG). Argues most repo context a coding agent reads is wasted, and studies the minimal decision-relevant slice at the moment of the edit — direct implications for context cost and latency.
- **[Scaling and Stabilizing Large-Scale Embedding-Based Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10096)** — Yang et al. (cs.IR) + **[SVD-RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10316)** — production-retrieval / embeddings-at-scale, and an SVD tree-organized RAG that drops RAPTOR's expensive per-node summarization. Plus **[AgentAbstain: Do LLM Agents Know When Not to Act?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10059)** framing abstention as a first-class agent-safety capability.
