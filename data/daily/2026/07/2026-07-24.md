## The day in 90 seconds

**Anthropic ships Opus 5 and files to go public as OpenAI lands on AWS**

Claude Opus 5 delivers near-frontier intelligence at roughly half the price of Fable 5, and it arrived the same day Anthropic confidentially filed to go public. But the enterprise moat that carried Claude this far narrowed on two fronts at once — OpenAI's models reached AWS and the open-weight cost floor dropped again — even as fresh numbers proved enterprise agents are finally paying for themselves, and two hard deadlines loom next week.

Anthropic's fourth Claude 5 model — after Sonnet 5, Fable 5, and the cyber-focused Mythos 5 — is the everyday workhorse of the family, pitched as "close to the frontier intelligence of Claude Fable 5 at half the price" and posting new state-of-the-art marks on Frontier-Bench and the GDPval-AA knowledge-and-coding suite; only Mythos 5 still outscores it, and only on security. A per-request low/medium/high effort toggle now lets developers trade cost against capability on every single call, Opus 5 becomes the default model on Claude Max, and it shipped the same day into GitHub Copilot for long-running agentic coding. The timing is anything but incidental. Hours earlier, Anthropic filed a confidential draft S-1 with the U.S. SEC — its formal first move toward an IPO, with ARR reportedly climbing from $9B to $45B in six months — and Opus 5's aggressive price reads as a deliberate land-grab, pushing strong agentic capability down the cost curve exactly as rivals and regulators close in.

The counterweight arrived from several directions at once. OpenAI's frontier models and its Codex agent are now available on AWS Bedrock, narrowing a distribution edge that mattered more than it looked: as one heavily-upvoted Hacker News comment put it, an "unimaginable amount of enterprises picked up Claude just because it was available in AWS." Beneath the frontier, the price floor fell again: DeepSeek's MIT-licensed V4 line graduated to stable today, retiring its legacy deepseek-chat and deepseek-reasoner aliases at 15:59 UTC, keeping a 1M-token context and adding peak/off-peak pricing — the first half of an open-weight double-header, with Moonshot freeing Kimi K3's weights on 27 July. Microsoft, meanwhile, threw its own 137-billion-parameter MAI-Code-1-Flash into the coding arena, though a skeptical Hacker News promptly picked apart its benchmark framing. What makes the whole race rational is that governed enterprise agents are, at last, converting to real money: ServiceNow reported agentic deployments up ninefold in nine months and its AI business crossing $1B in annual contract value, while SAP's cloud grew 24% and its Joule Work agent heads to market in Q3.

Two deadlines then dominate the week ahead. On Monday the Model Context Protocol ships its largest revision since launch — a stateless core that scales over ordinary HTTP, authorization rebuilt on OAuth and OpenID Connect, and long-running Tasks moved into a new extensions framework — a direct answer to the auth-and-scaling complaints that kept it out of production, and a quiet rewrite of the interoperability layer beneath most enterprise agent stacks. Then on 2 August the EU AI Act's enforcement powers for general-purpose models switch on, letting Brussels demand documentation, run its own model evaluations, order recalls, and levy fines up to €15M or 3% of global revenue. Underneath both, a slower structural shift is gathering: standalone vector-database adoption is falling as hybrid and agentic retrieval triples toward a third of intent — the radar's fastest-rising topic at +31.6% over seven days — and "the RAG era is ending" became the day's recurring refrain, one engineer recounting how fifteen lines of Postgres replaced a $2,000-a-month vector database.

## Research highlights

**[AREX: Towards a Recursively Self-Improving Agent for Deep Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21461)** — Lu, Li, Luo et al. Exploits the discovery-vs-verification asymmetry: an inner loop gathers evidence and drafts a provisional answer while an outer loop audits it constraint-by-constraint and launches targeted follow-ups, with a learned context-update policy sustaining recursive self-improvement over long horizons. #1 HF paper today (115 upvotes).

**[DynamicMCPBench: Trace-Grounded, Effect-Scored Benchmark for LLM Agents over Live MCP Servers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20531)** — Kaminski et al. Scores agents on whether they reproduce the path-agnostic _effects_ of a known-good trajectory over live, stateful MCP servers rather than matching a brittle final answer — run across 24 models and 121 servers. The evaluation counterpart to this week's MCP spec news.

**[ChannelGuard: Safe Models Do Not Compose into Safe Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19430)** — Hossain et al. Over 2,100 traces, a planner→worker→verifier→synthesizer pipeline that looks perfectly safe (0.000 attack success) owes it almost entirely to the cloud provider's server-side filter (54 of 60 blocks on Azure GPT-5); every inter-agent hop is an unmonitored channel that outcome-only reporting hides.

**[GRADRAG: Cross-Component Prompt Adaptation for Coordinated Multi-Agent RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21324)** — Pedinotti, Santus. Models a multi-agent RAG pipeline as a computational graph and propagates evaluator feedback upstream, co-optimizing retrievers, graph constructors, and answerers via prompt adaptation instead of tuning each in isolation.

**[Chronofy: Temporal-Logical Decay for Information Validity in Time-Aware RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20560)** — Syed et al. Attacks "temporal hallucination" by reserving a temporal subspace inside Matryoshka embeddings so fact age is structurally irremovable, folding learnable exponential-decay functions into graph retrieval rather than filtering recency post-hoc.

**[IssueTrojanBench: Benchmarking AI Coding Agents Against Malicious Issue Requests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20759)** — Singh, Yang, Chen. Probes production coding agents (Cursor, Claude Code, Codex Desktop) for induced API misuse, data exfiltration, and persistent dev-environment compromise arising from agentic tool-use autonomy rather than the LLM backbone alone.

**[LLMs Get Lost in Evolving User Intent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20734)** — Tack, Laban, Neville. Turns static single-turn tasks into multi-turn conversations where intent is revealed, revised, and redirected mid-stream, exposing how poorly current agents track evolving goals — a failure mode invisible in the fully-specified setups they're trained on.

_Also: [NVIDIA-labs OO Agents (NOOA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20709)_ (an agent _is_ a Python object — methods are actions, docstrings are prompts, a `...` body is completed at runtime) and _[Agentic Context Management](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21503)_ (agent memory as a full lifecycle to curb per-turn token cost).
