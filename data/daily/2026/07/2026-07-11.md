## The day in 90 seconds

**Workflow vendors concede the base model; enterprise AI's value moves to orchestration**

No frontier model shipped on this mid-summer Saturday, and arXiv skipped its weekend batch — but the quiet let a backlog of the week's enterprise releases surface with a single, unmistakable through-line. From ServiceNow to Snowflake to Salesforce, the companies closest to enterprise budgets are betting that the money in AI has migrated away from the model itself and toward the layer that grounds, governs, orchestrates, and distributes it. Meanwhile, the loudest rooms on the internet weren't debating a product at all — developers spent the day on Apple's trade-secret lawsuit against OpenAI and on fresh doubts about an AI "proof" of a famous math conjecture.

The clearest tell came from ServiceNow, which on July 9 began rolling out Azure OpenAI, AWS Claude, and Google Gemini as the default models behind its out-of-the-box Now Assist skills, retiring its in-house Now LLM as the standard choice. A large workflow vendor publicly conceding that third-party frontier models beat its own is about as clean a signal as the market produces: the base model is becoming a commodity input, and the value accrues to whoever owns the governed execution around it. Snowflake pressed the same case from the data side — its new Cortex Sense grounding layer, in private preview, lifts agent accuracy on an internal benchmark from 24.1% to 86.3% while cutting per-query cost from $1.76 to $0.59 against a frontier agent. Salesforce, meanwhile, moved its Agentforce Commerce trio — Shopper, Buyer, and Merchant agents — to general availability, with the Shopper agent embedding natively inside ChatGPT. Grounding, governance, and distribution, not raw model IQ, are where all three are placing their chips.

The day's actual center of gravity, though, was a courtroom. Apple filed suit — reported July 10 — alleging that former employees carried trade secrets to OpenAI, and that OpenAI coached new hires to conceal their Apple departures to preserve their access. It became the single biggest AI conversation of the day, drawing 1,450 points and 807 comments on Hacker News, with the New York Times and Wall Street Journal versions also riding the front page. It is a litigation story rather than a technical one, but a telling marker in a frontier-lab talent war where poaching and retention now rank alongside compute as strategic assets. The same front page showed the flip side of the mood: a claim that GPT-5.6 "Sol Ultra" had proved the Cycle Double Cover Conjecture pulled 501 points but hardened into skepticism once readers noticed the released prompt told the model to assume a complete proof exists — part of a benchmark-versus-reality doubt the radar now flags as its fastest-rising theme.

What to watch is the regulatory clock. EU AI Act obligations for high-risk systems and general-purpose models become enforceable on August 2 — three weeks out, carrying fines up to €35M or 7% of turnover — and Germany is set to hand the Bundesnetzagentur oversight of AI operated by its states and municipalities, even as xAI has already deferred Grok 4.5's EU launch citing conformity review. On the product side, the near-term pipeline runs through voice and open weights: OpenAI's full-duplex GPT-Live is rolling into ChatGPT, NVIDIA has open-weighted a unified audio-text model called Audex, DeepSeek's long-trailed V4 is expected any day, and the MCP specification's release candidate is marching toward its July 28 date.

## Research highlights

_Saturday: arXiv skips weekend announcements, so five of six category feeds were empty; picks are new/cross submissions from Friday's cs.CL batch, deduped against the Jul-10 file._

- **From Solvers to Research: LLM-Driven Formal Mathematics at the Research Frontier** — Jiang, Liang, … Terence Tao, Wei Wang. Position paper + review arguing AI4Math must shift from predefined theorem "solvers" to formal research agents that attack open, under-specified conjectures; maps limitations in datasets, tooling, and human-AI collaboration. [2607.07779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07779)
- **DeepSearch-World: Self-Distillation for Deep Search Agents in a Verifiable Environment** — Geng, He, … Fung. A deterministic 420K-task env; with no distillation from a stronger model the 9B agent hits 31.2% BrowseComp / 61.5% GAIA / 93.4% HotpotQA. Environment, training pool, and model released. [2607.07820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07820)
- **Do You Need a Frontier Model as a Citation Verifier?** — Leung et al. On 1,248 human-reviewed citation-rubric decisions, cheaper judges stay competitive (GPT-5-mini tops source-relevance F1 at 0.908), but scalar F1 hides a directional bias an RL reward loop would reinforce — calibrate the judge before using rubrics as reward. [2607.08700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08700)
- **RAG Must Move Beyond Factual Grounding to Represent Diverse Opinions** — Agrawal et al. (freshly cross-listed). Only 1 of 34 RAG benchmarks addresses opinion synthesis; formalizes factual (minimize entropy) vs opinion (preserve entropy) queries and warns of echo-chamber / minority-voice-suppression risk. [2604.12138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12138)
- **What LLM Forecasters Know but Don't Say** — Sarfati, Tiwari, … Ho. Representation-pooling probes calibrate forecaster LLMs far better than their outputs and double as lie detectors; forecasts are largely fixed before reasoning begins, so routing by pre-reasoning answer spread saves 30–47% of tokens with no accuracy loss. [2607.08046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08046)
- **Two Axes of LLM Abstention: Answer Correctness and Question Answerability** — Wagner. Refusing likely-wrong answers vs refusing unanswerable/false-premise questions are separate axes a single confidence threshold can't tell apart; the answerability blind spot doesn't shrink with scale, but a hidden-state probe recovers it (0.69–0.77 AUROC). [2607.08456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08456)
- **TACO: Tail-Aware Credit Calibration for LLM RL** — Lou, Xu, … Braverman. Names "Positive-Credit Contamination" in critic-free RL (GRPO-style) and dampens risky positive updates to erroneous low-probability tokens, improving stability across 3 LLMs / 8 benchmarks. [2607.07976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07976)
- **Collective Intelligence with Foundation Models** — de Curtò, de Zarzà. A solver→critic→aggregator multi-agent framework where model *heterogeneity* — not structure or redundant sampling — is the key driver (0.64 vs 0.54 step accuracy at lower variance). [2607.07729](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07729)
