## Research highlights

**[Capability Advertisement as a Market for Lemons: A Trust Layer for Heterogeneous Agent Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03034)** — Gaurav Naresh Mittal. Argues MCP/A2A registries naively treat advertised agent capabilities as static and truthful, creating an adverse-selection problem, and proposes a trust/verification layer. The most pointed of several agent-trust papers today and a direct theoretical counterpoint to the "just open the MCP server" enterprise enthusiasm.

**[OpenAgenet/OAN: Open Infrastructure for Trusted Agent Interconnection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03161)** — Jinliang Xu. A protocol-neutral trust layer for open multi-operator agent networks: identity provenance, governance state, discovery authorization, and freshness checks before one agent invokes another (companion architecture paper [2606.03163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03163)).

**[Economy of Minds: Emerging Multi-Agent Intelligence with Economic Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02859)** — Zhenting Qi et al. A Hayek-inspired agent economy where agents bid in auctions for the right to act; economic signals induce decentralized credit assignment and planning without global orchestration or explicit communication.

**[What Benchmarks Don't Measure: Evaluating Abstention Competence in Autonomous Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02965)** — Victor Ojewale, Suresh Venkatasubramanian. Names "compliance bias" — RLHF-trained agents structurally tend to proceed even without sufficient inputs or authorization; argues evals must measure whether an agent *should* have acted at all.

**[When Helping Hurts: Multi-Agent Debate for Data Cleaning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02866)** — Chirag Parmar et al. Across 3 benchmarks, 4 model families, and 6,000+ conditions, debate's sign reverses — degrading generation (−1.6 to −15.5pp) via "critique-induced confusion" while improving error detection. A useful corrective to multi-agent-debate hype.

**[Adaptive Latent Agentic Reasoning (ALAR)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02871)** — Dongwon Jung et al. Dual-mode framework using compact latent reasoning for routine agent turns and escalating to explicit chain-of-thought only when needed, cutting multi-turn reasoning overhead.

**[Thinking Past the Answer: Harmful Overthinking in Large Reasoning Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02835)** — Simone Caldarella et al. Challenges "longer reasoning is always better" — once an LRM reaches the right answer, extra reasoning can degrade it; quantifies harmful overthinking against test-time compute.
