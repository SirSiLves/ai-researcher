## The day in 90 seconds

**Washington orders Anthropic to pull its two strongest models, and the AI thesis wobbles.**

For the first time, a government has forced a frontier lab to suspend public access to its flagship models. The how and why are contested — Anthropic cites a US directive, a WSJ scoop pins it on a rival's lobbying — but the precedent is set, and it is reverberating through markets, open-weight communities, and the enterprise agent stack.

The dominant story is unambiguous: Anthropic confirmed, via both a [statement](https://www.anthropic.com/news) and its [status page](https://status.claude.com/), that it suspended public access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 under a US government directive. A WSJ report alleges the trigger was not a safety finding but Amazon CEO talks with US officials — a competitor's lobbying rather than a regulator's verdict. On Hacker News the suspension cluster drew 2,954 points and over 2,150 comments and effectively turned the front page into a referendum. The stakes are larger than one lab: this is the first instance of a state pulling a frontier lab's strongest public models, and the open question is whether governments are now willing to gatekeep access to the most capable LLMs — and what that does to an investment thesis built on their availability.

Underneath the headline, a structural realignment in enterprise agents came into sharp focus, with SAP playing both sides. SAP took a roughly $60M stake in Berlin's n8n at a $5.2B valuation — more than double its October 2025 mark, making n8n Germany's most valuable AI company — and will embed its workflow canvas natively in Joule Studio by Q3 2026. At the same time, SAP's API Policy v4/2026 took effect June 9, barring external AI agents from autonomously calling SAP APIs, even as Salesforce and ServiceNow keep theirs open. The absorb-and-wall strategy is now explicit: SAP is buying the orchestration layer while walling off the gateway into enterprise systems of record.

The suspension also handed the open-weight camp a rallying cry. An "Open source AI must win" manifesto pulled 1,410 points, and the release of Kimi K2.7-Code reignited explicit Kimi-versus-Claude price comparisons, with organizations signaling intent to migrate off Anthropic on cost. That sharpens Nathan Lambert's standing argument that the open-versus-closed question is now economics, not capability, into live migration intent. Watch tomorrow for whether the WSJ lobbying angle holds up, and whether other labs read the suspension as a reason to hedge toward open weights or sovereign deployments.

**Read this first:** [Statement on US government directive to suspend access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5](https://www.anthropic.com/news) — the primary-source account of an unprecedented government action against a frontier lab.

## Research highlights

- **[The Cold-Start Safety Gap in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07867)** — tool-calling agents are most vulnerable at session start and get safer after a few benign tasks; introduces the SODA benchmark across 7 models / 4 families.
- **[MiniMax Sparse Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13392)** — blockwise sparse attention over GQA with a lightweight top-k KV-block index branch, targeting million-token agentic/long-context workloads without quadratic cost.
- **[Getting Better at Working With You (TRACE)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13174)** — compiles user corrections into runtime-enforced rules for coding agents; Mem0-style memory alone still violates 57.5% of applicable preference checks — preference recall ≠ compliance.
- **[Evoflux: Inference-Time Evolution of Executable Tool Workflows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12674)** — evolves executable workflow graphs at inference time for MCP-style tool use by compact models, instead of small-corpus distillation.
- **[EvoBrowseComp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13120)** — a contamination-free, evolving benchmark (400 EN + 400 ZH) for search agents; static benchmarks let models score high via parametric recall rather than genuine retrieval.
- **[Dense Latent Communication Across Heterogeneous Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13594)** — cross-model KV-cache (latent) communication to avoid the lossy decode/re-encode cost of text-based agent-to-agent messaging.
- **[Risk Under Pressure: Compute-Aware Adversarial Robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11409)** — fixed-budget attack-success-rate hides true jailbreak cost; proposes a compute-aware robustness framework.
- **[HarnessBridge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12882)** — a trainable, end-to-end module that replaces hand-engineered agent harnesses, improving long-horizon scalability.
