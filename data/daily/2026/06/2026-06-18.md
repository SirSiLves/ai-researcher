## The day in 90 seconds

**Anthropic plants a Korea flag while the public sours on the word "AI"**

The frontier labs spent today building empire — a Seoul office, a G7 push for a US-led coalition, enterprise gateways turning agent access into a toll. But the loudest signal came from outside the industry: Americans, by wide margins, no longer believe the story. The gap between what AI vendors are selling and what consumers want has become the day's real headline, and it surfaced not in a press release but in two surveys that landed at the top of Hacker News.

Anthropic's expansion was the dominant thread. Its third APAC office, after Tokyo and Bengaluru, opened in Seoul under KiYoung Choi, arriving with an MOU with Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT for Korean-language safety evaluations, a four-university research tie-up spanning KAIST, Korea University, Yonsei and POSTECH, and a roster of launch deployments that reads like Korea's corporate index: NAVER rolling out Claude Code org-wide, Samsung SDS putting Claude Cowork and Claude Code across Samsung Electronics, plus LG CNS, Nexon and Hanwha running Claude through AWS Bedrock with in-region controls. Underneath the expansion, two reality checks cut the other way. OpenAI's new LifeSciBench — 750 tasks authored by 173 PhD-level biotech and pharma scientists, with over a thousand attached figures, structures and sequence files — saw its best model pass just 36.1%, a sober counterweight to AI-for-science hype and exactly the kind of grounded eval that regulators and enterprises increasingly demand before trusting agents with high-stakes work. The enterprise story, meanwhile, quietly shifted from features to tollbooths. ServiceNow, SAP and Workday are all erecting metered, pay-per-operation gateways for external agents touching their data, eroding two decades of per-seat pricing as the incumbents work out how to monetize — and gate — the third-party agents now reaching into their systems. In parallel, Microsoft pushed its Work IQ APIs to GA on June 16, collapsing all of Microsoft 365 into ten generic MCP tools with progressive disclosure rather than hundreds of data-specific ones — a concrete bet that the semantic context layer, not tool sprawl, is the true bottleneck for enterprise agents, and the foundation for its always-on Scout agent carrying a governed Entra identity.

**OpenAI releases LifeSciBench — and the best model passes only 36%.** A 750-task, expert-authored benchmark built with 173 PhD-level biotech/pharma scientists across seven life-science workflows, with 1,062 attached artifacts (figures, sequence files, structures). The strongest model scores just 36.1%; GPT-Rosalind (now on GPT-5.5's agentic coding) leads but absolute pass rates stay modest (21.6% on hardest genomics to 63.2% on wet-lab protocol support). _Why it matters:_ a rigorous, externally-judged reality check on AI-for-science hype — the grounded eval enterprises and regulators want before trusting agents with high-stakes scientific work. [link](https://openai.com/index/introducing-life-sci-bench/)

**Microsoft ships Work IQ APIs to GA — 10 generic MCP tools as the agent gateway to Microsoft 365.** GA June 16, Work IQ builds a continuously-updated semantic understanding of an organization from email, calendar, meetings, chats, files, people and line-of-business systems, and exposes it to agents as just 10 generic tools over MCP (with progressive disclosure) rather than hundreds of data-specific tools. It underpins Microsoft's new "Autopilots" category and the flagship Scout always-on agent, which carries its own governed Entra identity. _Why it matters:_ collapsing M365 access to a handful of MCP tools is a concrete bet that the context/semantic layer — not tool sprawl — is the real bottleneck for enterprise agents. [link](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2026/06/02/announcing-the-new-work-iq-apis/)

**ServiceNow, SAP and Workday turn agent access into a metered toll.** As external AI agents erode two decades of per-seat pricing, the big enterprise platforms are erecting paid gateways: ServiceNow's Action Fabric (Claude as launch partner) charges per agent operation; SAP routes agents through its Business Accelerator Hub to reach business rules, also metered; Workday's CEO frames agent-access charging as significant upside. _Why it matters:_ how incumbents monetize — and gate — third-party agents touching their data will shape the economics of the entire agentic-enterprise market. [link](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/servicenow-sap-and-workday-make-ai-agents-pay-to-play/)

## Research highlights

**[CEO-Bench: Can Agents Play the Long Game?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18543)** — Chen, Narasimhan, Liu. A long-horizon agent benchmark simulating 500 days of running a startup (pricing, marketing, budgeting under uncertainty), probing capabilities short-horizon SWE/customer-service benchmarks miss.

**[DeFAb: A Verifiable Benchmark for Defeasible Abduction in Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18557)** — Cooper, Velasquez. A logic solver hits 100% but the best frontier model reaches only 65% (dropping to 23.5% under rendering-robust evaluation) — a sharp capabilities-and-robustness gap.

**[SproutRAG: Attention-Guided Tree Search with Progressive Embeddings for Long-Document RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18381)** — Abaskohi et al. Hierarchical RAG organizing sentence-level chunks into progressively larger semantically coherent units via attention-guided tree search, avoiding LLM-guided chunking cost and summarization loss.

**[ToolChain-CRC: Conformal Risk Control for Agentic AI Under Retrieval and Tool-Use Drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18467)** — Opoku, Banahene. Treats each agent run as a full trajectory and builds calibrated step-level risk scores, catching cases where a final answer looks fine but retrieval was weak or a tool output was wrong.

**[Towards Scalable Customization and Deployment of Multi-Agent Systems for Enterprise Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18502)** — Dashore et al. A unified framework for adapting compact models to enterprise domains (continual pretraining + SFT + preference optimization) and deploying multi-agent workflows under real production latency/cost constraints.

**[SAE Interventions are Unreliable: Post-Intervention Recovery of Suppressed Behavior](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18322)** — Sparse-autoencoder feature interventions used for steering/safety can be silently reversed as suppressed behavior re-emerges downstream — a caution for SAE-based control and alignment auditing.

**[Breaking the Solver Bottleneck: Training Task Generators at the Learnable Frontier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18284)** — Wolf et al. Targets agentic-RL's emerging bottleneck — supply of valid, solvable, frontier-difficulty tasks — by training an RL task generator without costly solver-in-the-loop rollouts.
