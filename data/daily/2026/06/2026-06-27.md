## The day in 90 seconds

**Washington starts hand-picking who gets frontier AI — as the EU pushes its rules back 16 months**

The U.S. government now decides, organization by organization, who may use the most capable AI models — a regime that hardened today from footnote to dominant story. Underneath the policy noise, the money quietly moved: not into training the next model, but into the layer that serves it.

The day belonged to access control. OpenAI confirmed the U.S. government will individually pre-approve which organizations may use GPT-5.6 (the just-previewed "Sol" line), and in parallel cleared Anthropic's "Mythos" model for release only to vetted domestic organizations. The gating now spans both frontier labs at once — the first sustained government pre-clearance regime over commercial AI. Hacker News reacted with open hostility: the top thread drew 1,128 points and read the move as regulatory capture and incumbent lock-in, with commenters repeatedly predicting a flight to Chinese and open-weight models ("like PostgreSQL vs Oracle"). That prediction already has receipts — TechCrunch reports Asian startups shipping Mythos-like models within a day, and HN's top research item is a DeepSeek inference paper, praised because DeepSeek still publishes while U.S. labs go quiet.

Brussels moved in the opposite direction. The European Parliament adopted its "digital omnibus" simplification position 569–45, resetting the AI Act's most consequential deadlines. Stand-alone Annex III high-risk obligations — biometrics, critical infrastructure, employment, law enforcement — slip from August 2026 to December 2027; embedded safety-component systems move to August 2028. A new Article 5 prohibition bans AI-generated CSAM and non-consensual intimate imagery ("nudifier" apps), with compliance due December 2026. For every EU and Swiss compliance roadmap, the runway enterprises were sprinting down just got 16 months longer, even as the nudifier ban tightens. Parliament and Council must still formally adopt the deal before August 2.

Beneath the policy story, capital repriced the stack. Two mega-raises landed days apart, both betting that inference serving — not training — is where enterprise spend, margins and lock-in concentrate: Baseten closed a $1.5B Series F at up to $13B (roughly 160% above its $5B Series E five months earlier, on ~$600M annualized revenue, +1,900% YoY, and 1B+ inference requests a day), while Groq raised $650M to push its LPU cloud toward 200 MW. Qualcomm is reportedly in advanced talks to buy Jim Keller's RISC-V chip firm Tenstorrent for $8–10B, and a separate thesis surfaced entirely: General Intuition's $320M Series A at $2.3B, betting that action-labeled gameplay footage — not internet text — is the underrated corpus for spatial-temporal reasoning and embodied agents.

Stand-alone high-risk systems in Annex III (biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, law enforcement, border management) move from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027; high-risk AI embedded as safety components moves to 2 Aug 2028. Parliament and Council still must formally adopt the provisional deal before 2 Aug 2026. _Why it matters:_ the biggest reset of the AI Act timeline since it took force — most high-risk obligations enterprises were racing toward just moved ~16 months out, even as GPAI enforcement and the nudifier ban tighten. [link](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/general-intuitions-2-3b-bet-that-video-games-can-train-ai-agents-for-the-real-world/)

## Research highlights

- **[NRT-Bench: Multi-Turn Red-Teaming of LLM Agents in Safety-Critical Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20408)** — Hanwool Lee et al. A nuclear-plant control-room sim with a five-role LLM operator team; adaptive multi-turn attacks breach a hard safety limit in 8.7–12.1% of runs across four frontier models, harm measured objectively rather than LLM-judged.
- **[Continual Learning Bench: Evaluating Frontier AI in Real-World Stateful Environments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661)** — Parth Asawa et al. A benchmark for whether systems actually improve through sequential experience — a capability current eval stacks don't measure.
- **[Enhancing Decision-Making with LLMs through Multi-Agent Fictitious Play](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19308)** — Applies game-theoretic fictitious play to LLM multi-agent systems, distributing subtasks across cooperative agents to improve execution-complex decisions.
- **[Self-Aware Vector Embeddings for RAG (SmartVector)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20598)** — Augments dense embeddings with temporal awareness, confidence decay and relational structure; reports accuracy gains over plain cosine RAG and a measurable drop in stale-answer rates.
- **[Empirical Comparison of Agent Communication Protocols for Task Orchestration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22823)** — Head-to-head evaluation of MCP/A2A-style agent protocols for multi-agent orchestration — useful grounding for the interop-standards debate.
- **[SoK: Agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07379)** — A systematization separating retrieval-as-tool designs from static pipelines, plus a taxonomy and evaluation framework for agentic RAG.
- **[Agent Skills for LLMs: Architecture, Acquisition, Security, and the Path Forward](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12430)** — Renjun Xu, Yang Yan. Composable, on-demand skill packages as an alternative to retraining — directly relevant to the agent-skills wave dominating GitHub today.
