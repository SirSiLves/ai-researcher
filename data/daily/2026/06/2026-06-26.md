## Research highlights

- **[Life After Benchmark Saturation: A Case Study of CORE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26158)** — Nadgir, Kapoor, Kirgis et al. A saturated agent benchmark can still surface six neglected dimensions (construct validity, cost, robustness) beyond raw accuracy — an argument against retiring benchmarks the moment they're "beaten."
- **[The Verification Horizon: No Silver Bullet for Coding Agent Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26300)** — Wang, Liu, Chen et al. The "verifying is easier than producing" intuition empirically *inverts* for modern coding agents — direct consequences for RL reward design.
- **[Temporal Validity in Retrieval Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26511)** — Neeraj Yadav. Adds a time model to RAG so agents stop retrieving both stale and current versions of a changed fact (e.g. a renamed API) — a common long-lived agent-memory failure mode.
- **[Necessary but Not Sufficient: Temperature Control and Reproducibility in LLM-as-Judge Safety Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26185)** — Hiroki Tamba. Grader temperature 0 does *not* guarantee reproducible pass/fail verdicts — a caution for anyone gating deployment on LLM-as-judge.
- **[Detecting and Controlling Sycophancy with Cascading Linear Features](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26155)** — Bohacek, Jain, Patel et al. Activation-steering that detects and suppresses sycophancy with less contrastive-pair data.
- **[Governing Actions, Not Agents: Institutional Attestation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26298)** — Salfeld-Nebgen. Govern consequential, irreversible agent actions (prescribing, prod deploys) via institutional attestation rather than monitoring agents themselves — a concrete governance primitive.
- **[Kiko: Programming Agents to Enact Interaction Protocols](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26156)** — Christie, Singh, Chopra. Better abstractions for decentralized multi-agent decision-making — relevant to agent-interop standards work.
