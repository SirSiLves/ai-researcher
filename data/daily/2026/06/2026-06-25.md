## The day in 90 seconds

**Frontier labs reach for their own silicon and agent runtimes as the talent war turns public**

The center of gravity moved up the stack today. After a week of document-AI and retrieval plumbing, the news that matters is about owning the foundations: OpenAI designed its own inference chip, three of the big platforms shipped server-hosted autonomous agents, and a Nobel laureate walked from one lab to a rival. The throughline is consolidation — of compute, of the agent runtime, and of scientific talent — even as Europe finally turned its AI-content rules into something vendors must implement. If yesterday was about ingesting documents, today was about owning the machines, the runtimes, and the people who build them.

The headline is OpenAI and Broadcom unveiling "Jalapeño," OpenAI's first in-house inference processor, co-designed with Celestica and taped out in nine months — a cycle the company calls the fastest ever for a high-performance ASIC. Early testing claims performance-per-watt "substantially better than current state-of-the-art," with gigawatt-scale deployment landing via Microsoft from the end of 2026. The signal is unambiguous: the frontier labs no longer intend to rent GPUs for the inference workloads that will define their economics. They want the full stack down to custom silicon, and the SpaceX–Reflection AI deal — $150M a month through 2029, roughly $6.3 billion of GB300 capacity for an open-weight challenger reportedly raising at a $25 billion valuation — shows that the compute itself is hardening into a handful of mega-campuses everyone else leases. Reflection, founded by ex-DeepMind researchers and now SpaceX's fourth external Colossus tenant after Anthropic, Google, and Cursor, is pitching itself as the American, open-weight answer to the frontier — a reminder that the silicon race and the open-versus-closed fight are now the same story.

Underneath the chip story, the agent runtime became a first-party platform feature everywhere at once. Google moved Gemini 3.5 Flash to GA and previewed Managed Agents — stateful, autonomous agents running in Google-hosted Linux sandboxes, including a general-purpose "Antigravity" agent that plans, codes, and browses. Microsoft's Build 2026 wrapped the same idea in a governed agent stack with identity and security controls inside Azure and M365, alongside its own first-party MAI models. Anthropic shipped Claude Tag, turning @Claude into a Slack teammate with per-channel access scoping, and disclosed that 65% of its product team's code now comes from the internal version. The competitive question has shifted from model quality to who hosts, governs, and remembers the agent — a tension one sharp LinkedIn read framed as "rent the agent, own the memory." ServiceNow opening its full system of action to any external agent, with Anthropic as first design partner, points the same direction: the value is migrating to whoever controls the governed runtime and the context it accumulates.

The third thread is people and rules. John Jumper, AlphaFold 2 co-lead and 2024 Chemistry Nobel laureate, left DeepMind for Anthropic days after Transformer co-author Noam Shazeer left Google for OpenAI; Alphabet fell as much as 7.2% intraday, a rare public price tag on a talent loss. In Brussels, the EU published the final Code of Practice implementing Article 50 — machine-readable provenance marking plus deployer labelling of deepfakes and AI text — moving European governance from "the deadline slipped" to concrete compliance scaffolding vendors must wire in before the August 2026 transparency duties bite. A newly appointed 60-expert Scientific Panel to support enforcement underscores that the bloc is staffing up to actually apply the rules, not just publish them.

**Read this first:** [OpenAI + Broadcom unveil "Jalapeño" inference chip](https://openai.com/index/openai-broadcom-jalapeno-inference-chip/) — the clearest signal yet that frontier labs intend to own the full inference stack, not rent it, and the day's most structurally consequential move.

## Research highlights

- **[Is GraphRAG Needed?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25656)** — Long Chen et al. Standardized head-to-head of regular/Graph/Modular/Agentic RAG across 9 scenarios; practical guidance on when each variant pays off.
- **[Improved Large Language Diffusion Models (iLLaDA)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25331)** — Shen Nie et al. 8B masked diffusion LM, fully bidirectional attention, 12T tokens, variable-length generation — credible non-autoregressive alternative to causal LLMs.
- **[Constraint Tax in Open-Weight LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25605)** — Fangzheng Li et al. Reproducible production failure: open-weight models stop invoking tools when tool-calling and JSON-schema constraints are enabled together. Directly relevant to agent reliability.
- **[BitNet Text Embeddings (BITEMBED)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25674)** — Zhen Li et al. Extreme low-bit LLM embeddings attacking both inference cost and vector-index storage — retrieval deployment economics.
- **[ReM-MoA: Reasoning Memory Sustains Mixture-of-Agents Scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24437)** — Heng Ping et al. Ranked reasoning-memory keeps MoA accuracy improving with depth instead of plateauing.
- **[Bayesian Control for Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24453)** — Theodore Papamarkou et al. Agent orchestration as cost-sensitive sequential hypothesis testing — uncertainty-aware tool-use over fixed rules.
- **[Can Trustless Agents Be Trusted? (ERC-8004)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26028)** — Xihan Xiong et al. First empirical audit of a permissionless trust layer (Identity/Reputation/Validation registries) for cross-org agent economies — emerging interop standard.
- **[Manipulation Is Task-Dependent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25899)** — Adeeb Zaman et al. 13,590 scenarios × six frontier models — single-axis benchmarks understate manipulation risk.
