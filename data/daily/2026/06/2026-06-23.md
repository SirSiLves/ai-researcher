## The day in 90 seconds

**Agents become a budget line as the platform giants fight over who runs them**

Gartner put a hard number on what everyone suspected: AI-agent software is now real enterprise spend, not a pilot. The day's other current ran underneath that — a hardening fight between SAP, ServiceNow and Salesforce over who owns the layer agents actually execute on, while MCP quietly moved from conference talk to shipped production plumbing.

The headline is money. Gartner forecasts worldwide AI-agent software spending of roughly $206.5 billion in 2026 — up 139% from $86.4 billion in 2025 — heading to $376.3 billion in 2027, the fastest-growing slice of an enterprise software market it now sizes at $2.59 trillion in total AI spend. That reframes "agents" from a line on a slide to a line in a budget, and it lands the same week the vendors stop being polite about who collects it. ServiceNow and Salesforce are going "headless," opening their platforms as the execution layer for any agent — Claude, Copilot, or homegrown — via Action Fabric, while SAP blocks external AI agents from its APIs outright. All three are moving to meter and charge for agent access. The control-point war over the orchestration layer is now the defining enterprise-platform fight of the year.

The structural shift beneath that fight is MCP crossing from debate into deployment. Cognizant's Neuro multi-agent accelerator can now discover and invoke ServiceNow AI Agents directly over MCP — no custom connectors, with ServiceNow's governance, access controls and audit logging intact — and the accelerator ships open source. Zensar launched ZenseAI.AgentMesh the same day with connectors across SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Snowflake and Databricks. The systems-integrator layer is racing to own enterprise agent governance, and MCP is the substrate they are building on. Meanwhile the cost-per-capability race kept sliding: Google shipped Gemini 3.5 Flash — claimed to beat its own 3.1 Pro on key benchmarks at Flash-tier speed — and cut Ultra from $250 to $200 a month with a new $100 Developer tier.

Watch the safety thread that crystallized today, because it cuts against all the agent optimism. Two top Hacker News discussions — that Claude Code's "Extended Thinking" output is a summary rather than the model's real chain-of-thought, and a "Prompt Injection as Role Confusion" reframing — converged with three fresh arXiv papers (AgentRiskBOM, GIF, and a contextual-integrity audit of computer-use agents). The role-confusion work carries the sharpest finding: human red-teamers hit near-100% attack success against models that ace static injection benchmarks, meaning those benchmarks badly overstate how robust agents actually are. As spend pours into autonomous agents that retrieve private data, call tools and act without approval, the evidence that we can't yet reliably secure them is piling up in the same news cycle.

## Research highlights

- **[Sakana Fugu Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21228)** — Tang, Cetin, Xu, Sun, Nielsen et al. (Sakana AI). Combines the distinct domain specializations of multiple frontier LLMs into a single stronger model — capability composition across providers.
- **[PlanBench-XL: Long-Horizon Planning of LLM Tool-Use Agents in Large-Scale Tool Ecosystems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22388)** — Liu, Lin, Qian, Wang, Acikgoz et al. Benchmark stressing tool discovery, implicit sub-goal inference, and adaptation over long horizons — where most agent evals fall short.
- **[The Token Tax of Epistemic Accuracy: RAG vs Long-Context for Document-Grounded GenAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20898)** — Hamilton, Singh, Wise, Yousif, Carvalho, Shan et al. Quantifies the accuracy-vs-cost trade-off between RAG and long-context, showing evidence allocation before generation drives epistemic accuracy.
- **[Self-Compacting Language Model Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23525)** — Li, Zhang, Jurayj, Wang, Jin, Farajtabar et al. Lets agents decide when/what to compact in long traces instead of fixed-interval threshold compaction — directly relevant to agentic memory and context-window management.
- **[AgentRiskBOM: A Risk-Scoping Security Bill of Materials for Agentic AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21877)** — Dutta, Moharir. Extends the bill-of-materials concept to agents that retrieve private context, call tools, write files, and act without human approval — a concrete governance artifact.
- **[Capable but Careless: Do Computer-Use Agents Follow Contextual Integrity?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23189)** — Goel, Gurevych. Audits whether computer-use agents respect contextual-integrity privacy norms across email/calendar/to-do apps — a cross-application privacy risk largely overlooked.
- **[GIF: Locally Sound Geometric Information Flow Control for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23277)** — Storek, Holzer, Zhang, Jana. Information-flow control to mitigate prompt injection and data-exfiltration in agentic systems — pairs with today's HN role-confusion thread.
- **[RaMem: Contextual Reinstatement for Long-term Agentic Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22844)** — Yang, Kan, Li et al. Memory system that reinstates relevant past context for agents across extended, evolving interactions.
