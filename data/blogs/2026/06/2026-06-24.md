## Long-form picks

- **Welcome to the AGI era of AI governance** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects). _Takeaway:_ Reads the US directive forcing Anthropic to suspend access to Mythos 5 / Fable 5 as the "starting gun" of a new governance era — a government that has internalized the AGI moment, was unprepared, and is now acting fast. Argues policy is shifting from voluntary commitments to coercive controls. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/welcome-to-the-agi-era-of-ai-governance]

- **GLM-5.2 is the step change for open agents** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects). _Takeaway:_ Z.ai's GLM-5.2 (MIT-licensed, released June 16) is the first open model to mix it up with OpenAI/Anthropic on agent leaderboards — its max mode roughly matching Opus 4.8's no-thinking effort. Frames open vs closed as diverging exponentials rather than a fixed gap. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/glm-52-is-the-step-change-for-open]

- **GLM-5.2 is probably the most powerful text-only open weights LLM** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Hands-on read on why GLM-5.2 now tops the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index among open weights, and what an MIT-licensed frontier-adjacent model means for self-hosting and the open ecosystem. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/17/glm-52/]

- **Statement on the US government directive to suspend access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Walks through the Commerce Department export-control move that pulled Anthropic's newest models — including blocking foreign-national Anthropic employees — and why this is an unprecedented intervention in frontier model distribution. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/13/us-government-directive-to-suspend-access/]

- **AI Governance by Phone Call** — Lawfare. _Takeaway:_ Reports the Mythos 5 / Fable 5 withdrawal traces to Andy Jassy raising jailbreak concerns with US officials, after which Commerce imposed export controls by Friday evening — governance happening informally, by phone, ahead of any formal regime. [https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-governance-by-phone-call]

- **'Voluntary' Until the Government Is Your Customer** — Lawfare. _Takeaway:_ Dissects the June 2 executive order "Promoting Advanced AI Innovation and Security": it disclaims a licensing mandate, but argues "voluntary" commitments look very different when the federal government is the buyer. [https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/voluntary--until-the-government-is-your-customer]

- **RL Systems: Mind the Gap — Matching Trainer and Generator Throughput** — SemiAnalysis. _Takeaway:_ Engineering deep-dive on the throughput mismatch between trainers and generators in RL post-training systems, framed against a coding-assistant market already $30B+ ARR and tracking toward $100B by year end. [https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/rl-systems-mind-the-gap-matching]

- **Q: Why is "error analysis" so important in LLM evals, and how is it performed?** — Hamel Husain. _Takeaway:_ Argues error analysis is the single most important eval activity — gather representative traces, have annotators write open-ended notes, and let that decide which evals to write in the first place, rather than starting from generic metrics. [https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/why-is-error-analysis-so-important-in-llm-evals-and-how-is-it-performed.html]

- **Running Python code in a sandbox with MicroPython and WASM** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Practical pattern for safely executing model-generated Python in-browser via MicroPython compiled to WASM — directly relevant to code-interpreter and agent tool-use sandboxing. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/6/micropython-in-a-sandbox/]
