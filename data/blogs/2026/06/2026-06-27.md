# AI Blogs — 2026-06-27

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **Welcome to the AGI era of AI governance** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (Jun 14). _Takeaway:_ Argues the US executive forcing Anthropic to cut access to its latest Claude 5 Mythos/Fable models is "the starting gun" of a new governance era where the state, not the lab, gates frontier release. A substantive position piece on where model-access control is heading. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/welcome-to-the-agi-era-of-ai-governance]

- **Banning Open Source AI Would Be A Mistake** — Nathan Lambert (w/ Kevin Xu), Interconnects (Jun 19). _Takeaway:_ Op-ed for a non-technical audience making the policy case that restricting open-weight models would forfeit the diverse, vibrant ecosystem the US still leads, just as governance pressure mounts. Directly in tension with the access-gating trend above. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/the-next-phase-of-open-models]

- **Scaling Laws, Carefully** — Lilian Weng, Lil'Log (Jun 24). _Takeaway:_ A careful walk through scaling laws — why training loss falls as a power law in model size, data, and compute — and where the naive reading breaks down. Reference-grade explainer rather than a hot take. [https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-06-24-scaling-laws/]

- **GLM-5.2 is probably the most powerful text-only open-weights LLM** — Simon Willison (Jun 17). _Takeaway:_ Hands-on read on Z.ai's GLM-5.2, released first to coding-plan subscribers then opened under MIT license. Frames why the strongest open-weights frontier may now be Chinese, with implications for the open-vs-closed debate. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/17/glm-52/]

- **What happened after 2,000 people tried to hack my AI assistant** (via Simon Willison) — Fernando Irarrázaval (Jun 26). _Takeaway:_ A public challenge to exfiltrate secrets from an Opus-4.6-backed email assistant survived 6,000 attempts and $500 in tokens with no leak — concrete evidence that frontier-lab anti-prompt-injection training is biting, with Willison's caveat that this is no guarantee for production. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/26/hack-my-ai-assistant/]

- **Previewing GPT-5.6 Sol** (annotated by Simon Willison) — OpenAI (Jun 26). _Takeaway:_ OpenAI previews the GPT-5.6 family (Sol/Terra/Luna) to a limited set of trusted partners "at the request of the U.S. government" before broad release — a governance signal as much as a model launch, plus new prompt-caching pricing mechanics. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/26/openai/]

- **AI and Liability** (annotated by Simon Willison) — Bruce Schneier (Jun 25). _Takeaway:_ On the German ruling holding Google liable for its AI Overviews: Schneier argues AI agents are legal agents of their deployer, so "the AI did it" cannot become a liability shield. Sharp framing for the EU governance crowd. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/25/ai-and-liability/]
