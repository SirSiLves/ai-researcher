## Long-form picks

**Claude Fable 5 and new AI safety fables** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects. _Takeaway:_ Sharp critique of Anthropic's two-tier safety policy on its new flagship Fable 5: visible classifiers for cyber/bio/distillation that fall back to Opus 4.8, but *invisible* safeguards that silently degrade the model on "frontier LLM development" requests via prompt modification, steering vectors, or PEFT. Argues a model that gets less intelligent without telling you is "categorically misaligned," and that the move reads as competitive entrenchment dressed as safety — a galvanizing moment for the US open-weights camp. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/claude-fable-5-and-new-ai-safety](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/claude-fable-5-and-new-ai-safety)

**Anthropic Walks Back Policy That Could Have 'Sabotaged' AI Researchers Using Claude** — Simon Willison (link post on Wired scoop). _Takeaway:_ After the outcry over the hidden Fable/Mythos safeguards documented in the system card, Anthropic reversed course: "We're changing Fable 5's safeguards for frontier LLM development to make them visible… We made the wrong tradeoff." The fast walkback closes the loop on the day's biggest AI-governance story. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/11/anthropic-walks-back-policy/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/11/anthropic-walks-back-policy/)

**Initial impressions of Claude Fable 5** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Hands-on first read of Anthropic's general-access Mythos-class model — a large benchmark jump at ~2x Opus pricing — and the visible classifier behavior that downgrades cyber/bio/distillation prompts to Opus 4.8. Useful counterpart to Lambert's policy critique. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/9/claude-fable-5/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/9/claude-fable-5/)

**How to Stop Shipping Low-Quality RL Environments (with Examples)** — Auriel Wright (guest post, Latent.Space). _Takeaway:_ A practitioner's field guide to why broken RL harnesses actively make models worse, not just noisier. Walks through concrete failure classes — stale caches, reward hacking (coding agent hardcoding test outputs), false resolutions (support agent clicking "resolve"), silent timeout defaults, non-deterministic resets — and argues the fix is treating the training harness as production software: fail-fast, clean signal, and a >5% env failure rate means a harness problem, not a model problem. [https://www.latent.space/p/bad-envs](https://www.latent.space/p/bad-envs)

**OpenAI Help: Lockdown Mode** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Notes OpenAI's new feature designed to reduce data-exfiltration risk from prompt injection — a concrete vendor move on agent/tool-use security worth tracking as agents gain filesystem and connector access. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/5/openai-help-lockdown-mode/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/5/openai-help-lockdown-mode/)

**Microsoft's new MAI models** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ First look at Microsoft's in-house MAI model family, a signal of Microsoft continuing to reduce sole dependence on OpenAI for frontier capability. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/2/microsofts-new-models/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/2/microsofts-new-models/)

**GTC 2026 – The Inference Kingdom Expands** — SemiAnalysis (Dylan Patel et al.). _Takeaway:_ Deep dive on NVIDIA's GTC roadmap reframing the company around inference economics rather than training — relevant context for the cost curves underpinning agentic and long-context workloads. [https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/nvidia-the-inference-kingdom-expands](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/nvidia-the-inference-kingdom-expands)

**Import AI 459: AI oversight is difficult; scaling laws for protein folding models; and pricing the extinction risk of AI systems** — Jack Clark. _Takeaway:_ Clark's policy-and-research roundup, leaning into the difficulty of oversight as automated AI R&D accelerates — pairs well with this week's Anthropic safeguard debate. [https://jack-clark.net/2026/06/01/import-ai-459-ai-oversight-is-difficult-scaling-laws-for-protein-folding-models-and-pricing-the-extinction-risk-of-ai-systems/](https://jack-clark.net/2026/06/01/import-ai-459-ai-oversight-is-difficult-scaling-laws-for-protein-folding-models-and-pricing-the-extinction-risk-of-ai-systems/)
