## Long-form picks

- **Open and closed models are on different exponentials** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (Jun 1). _Takeaway:_ Argues closed labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, soon Google) win the high-margin "absolute frontier intelligence" market via integration returns, while open models capture a larger-but-commoditized share across the diffusion stack; the two ecosystems compound on separate curves, so RSI "unassailable advantage" claims are overblown. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-and-closed-models-are-on-different]

- **Microsoft's new MAI models** — Simon Willison (Jun 2). _Takeaway:_ Notes on Microsoft's MAI-Thinking-1 (1T params, 35B active, claimed preferred over Sonnet 4.6) and MAI-Code-1-Flash (137B/5B active for Copilot); includes a candid correction walking back the initial "low parameter / clean licensed data" read after the technical paper revealed a standard 1.2T-page web crawl. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/2/microsofts-new-models/]

- **I think Anthropic and OpenAI have found product-market fit** — Simon Willison (May 27). _Takeaway:_ Positions coding agents (Claude Code/Cowork, Codex) past the Opus 4.5 / Codex 5.2 threshold as the first durable PMF where users will pay a real premium for marginal intelligence. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/27/product-market-fit/]

- **Running Python code in a sandbox with MicroPython and WASM** — Simon Willison (Jun 6). _Takeaway:_ Hands-on engineering pattern for safely executing untrusted/LLM-generated Python in a WASM-isolated MicroPython sandbox — a practical primitive for agent tool-use safety. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/6/micropython-in-a-sandbox/]

- **Import AI 459: AI oversight is difficult; scaling laws for protein folding; pricing AI extinction risk** — Jack Clark (Jun 1). _Takeaway:_ Roundup centered on the difficulty of scalable oversight, plus an economists' estimate (UVA / Anthropic / Bank of Canada) that the US "AI economy" grew ~$250B in 2025 at ~2,600%/yr in quality-adjusted terms yet stays largely invisible in GDP. [https://jack-clark.net/2026/06/01/import-ai-459-ai-oversight-is-difficult-scaling-laws-for-protein-folding-models-and-pricing-the-extinction-risk-of-ai-systems/]

- **White House Releases Executive Order on AI** — Lawfare (Jun 2). _Takeaway:_ Analysis of Trump's "Promoting Advanced AI Innovation and Security" EO directing agencies to harden AI-enabled cyber defense and coordinate secure deployment with industry — the latest step in the 2026 federalism/preemption fight. [https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/white-house-releases-executive-order-on-ai]

- **How to Work and Compound with AI** — Eugene Yan (May). _Takeaway:_ Practitioner framing (now writing from inside Anthropic) on compounding leverage from AI in daily knowledge work rather than one-off prompting. [https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/]

- **The Revenge of the Data Scientist** — Hamel Husain (Mar 26, still circulating). _Takeaway:_ Walks through five eval pitfalls — starting with off-the-shelf generic metrics (helpfulness/coherence/hallucination scores) that are useless for diagnosing real failures — and argues every pitfall traces back to skipping data-science fundamentals (EDA, error analysis, experimental design). [https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/revenge/]

- **CPUs are Back: The Datacenter CPU Landscape in 2026** — SemiAnalysis (Dylan Patel et al.). _Takeaway:_ Data-heavy breakdown of 2026 server-CPU launches (Intel Clearwater Forest / Diamond Rapids, AMD Venice) against AI-driven N3 capacity strain, with effective N3 utilization projected past 100% in H2 2026. [https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/cpus-are-back-the-datacenter-cpu]
