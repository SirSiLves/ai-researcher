## Long-form picks

- **OpenAI Help: Lockdown Mode** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ OpenAI's just-launched Lockdown Mode limits outbound network requests to block the exfiltration leg of the "lethal trifecta" prompt-injection chain — a deterministic, non-AI-evaluated control Willison rates highly, while noting its existence admits default ChatGPT isn't robust against determined data-exfil attacks. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/5/openai-help-lockdown-mode/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/5/openai-help-lockdown-mode/)

- **My bets on open models, mid-2026** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects). _Takeaway:_ A 13-point thesis arguing the open-vs-closed race is now an economics question, not a capability one: closed labs will pull ahead in agentic/RL-driven coding work where they own user-feedback distribution, while Chinese open-weight labs face funding pressure first and the US slowly regains open-model adoption share from early 2027. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/my-bets-on-open-models-mid-2026](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/my-bets-on-open-models-mid-2026)

- **Import AI 455: Automating AI Research** — Jack Clark. _Takeaway:_ Clark lays out why he thinks takeoff toward automated AI R&D is underway, charting agent task-duration scaling (GPT-3.5 ~30s in 2022 → Opus 4.6 ~12h in 2026) and predicting a "model trains its successor" proof-of-concept within a year or two. [https://jack-clark.net/2026/05/04/import-ai-455-automating-ai-research/](https://jack-clark.net/2026/05/04/import-ai-455-automating-ai-research/)

- **The Revenge of the Data Scientist** — Hamel Husain. _Takeaway:_ Pushes back on the "evals are dead" claim by walking through five recurring eval pitfalls — starting with off-the-shelf generic metrics that are useless for diagnosing real app failures — all rooted in skipping data-science fundamentals like EDA and experimental design. [https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/revenge/](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/revenge/)

- **LLM Research Papers: The 2026 List (January to May)** — Sebastian Raschka (Ahead of AI). _Takeaway:_ A curated, annotated sweep of notable LLM research from the first five months of 2026 — architecture shifts (KV sharing, compressed attention), open-weight releases, and inference-time scaling — useful as a single index into the year's technical literature. [https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/llm-research-papers-2026-part1](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/llm-research-papers-2026-part1)

- **NVIDIA Cosmos 3, Nemotron 3 Ultra, and RTX Spark** — swyx (Latent.Space / AINews). _Takeaway:_ NVIDIA went full-stack open with Cosmos 3 (omnimodal world models for physical AI — weights, code, datasets, recipes) plus Nemotron 3 Ultra, a 550B open-weight model, and a "Cosmos Coalition" with partners including Runway, sharpening its open-model play. [https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-nvidia-cosmos-3-nemotron-3](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-nvidia-cosmos-3-nemotron-3)

- **How to Work and Compound with AI** — Eugene Yan. _Takeaway:_ From his Anthropic vantage point, Yan argues the leverage isn't one-off AI use but compounding workflows — investing in reusable context, evals, and tooling so each AI-assisted task makes the next one cheaper and better. [https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/)
