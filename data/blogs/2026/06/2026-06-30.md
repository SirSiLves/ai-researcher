# AI Blogs — 2026-06-30

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **GLM-5.2 is the step change for open agents** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects. _Takeaway:_ Argues Z.ai's GLM-5.2 is the first open-weights model that genuinely closes the gap with closed labs on agentic/tool-use tasks, not just static benchmarks — a real inflection for the open ecosystem. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/glm-52-is-the-step-change-for-open]

- **Open and closed models are on different exponentials** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects. _Takeaway:_ Makes a non-obvious case that open and closed model quality are improving on structurally different curves, so naive "gap is closing" framing misreads where each is headed. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-and-closed-models-are-on-different]

- **GLM-5.2 is probably the most powerful text-only open weights LLM** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Hands-on review of the MIT-licensed GLM-5.2 release from Z.ai, with concrete prompts and observations on where it matches frontier closed models and where it still falls short. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/17/glm-52/]

- **Scaling Laws, Carefully** — Lilian Weng, Lil'Log. _Takeaway:_ A 25-minute deep dive revisiting scaling laws via Lovelace et al.'s ~300-model sweep (15M–1B params), modeling model-size × data-repetition interaction explicitly rather than as a simple diminishing return. [https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-06-24-scaling-laws/]

- **Patterns for Building Cybersecurity Evals** — Eugene Yan. _Takeaway:_ Practitioner playbook (now from inside Anthropic) on designing evals specifically for cybersecurity tasks — task definition, scoring rules, and the failure modes generic LLM evals miss. [https://eugeneyan.com/writing/cybersecurity-evals/]

- **Evals Skills for Coding Agents** — Hamel Husain. _Takeaway:_ Documents an engineering pattern where coding agents are pointed at their own traces and experiments via eval "skills" that complement vendor MCP servers, teaching agents what to do with instrumentation data. [https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-skills/]

- **Are AI agents actually slowing us down?** — Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer. _Takeaway:_ Takes a substantive contrarian position: as more engineers adopt agents daily, sloppier software, more outages, and slower shipping velocity are showing up — questioning the productivity narrative with field evidence. [https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/are-ai-agents-actually-slowing-us]

- **Welcome to the AGI era of AI governance / Banning Open Source AI Would Be A Mistake** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects. _Takeaway:_ Policy argument that open-source AI restrictions would be a strategic error, framed against the shifting US executive-order landscape on frontier-model evaluation. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/banning-open-source-ai-would-be-a]

## Governance & policy picks

- **White House Releases Executive Order on AI** — Lawfare. _Takeaway:_ Analysis of the June 2 EO "Promoting Advanced AI Innovation and Security," whose frontier-model evaluation framework is explicitly voluntary — until the government becomes your customer. [https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/white-house-releases-executive-order-on-ai]

- **The EU Cloud and AI Development Act** — Lawfare. _Takeaway:_ Breaks down the June 3 European Technological Sovereignty Package (semiconductors, AI, cloud, open source) and what the "Digital Omnibus" deferral of high-risk AI Act obligations actually changes. [https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-eu-cloud-and-ai-development-act]

- **AI regulatory compliance in 2026: EU AI Act, US orders, and state laws** — Collibra blog. _Takeaway:_ Operational view of how 2026 is the year EU AI Act obligations move from "coming" to "in effect," and how the spring Digital Omnibus deferred several use-based high-risk duties past August. [https://www.collibra.com/blog/ai-regulatory-compliance-in-2026-eu-ai-act-us-orders-and-state-laws-and-how-to-operationalize]
