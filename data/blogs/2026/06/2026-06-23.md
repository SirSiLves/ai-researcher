## Long-form picks

- **GLM-5.2 is probably the most powerful text-only open weights LLM** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Z.ai shipped GLM-5.2 to coding-plan subscribers then released full open weights under MIT; hands-on notes argue it now leads the text-only open-weights field. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/17/glm-52/]
- **GLM-5.2 is the step change for open agents** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects). _Takeaway:_ Frames GLM-5.2 less as a benchmark bump and more as the first open model genuinely usable for long-horizon agentic work, narrowing the closed-model gap. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/glm-52-is-the-step-change-for-open]
- **Open and closed models are on different exponentials** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects). _Takeaway:_ Argues the open and closed ecosystems improve along distinct curves driven by different economics, so naive "open is catching up" framing misreads the dynamics. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-and-closed-models-are-on-different]
- **Welcome to the AGI era of AI governance** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects). _Takeaway:_ Treats the US government forcing Anthropic to cut access to its top Mythos/Fable models as the "starting gun" for a new, coercive phase of AI governance. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/welcome-to-the-agi-era-of-ai-governance]
- **Statement on the US government directive to suspend access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Documents the first-ever use of export-control law against an AI model, including the directive's reach over foreign-national Anthropic employees. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/13/us-government-directive-to-suspend-access/]
- **AI Governance by Phone Call** — Lawfare. _Takeaway:_ Analyzes how the Commerce Department letter to Anthropic turns informal executive pressure into a de facto governance lever, sidestepping formal rulemaking. [https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-governance-by-phone-call]
- **Microsoft's new MAI models** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Walks through Microsoft's first-party MAI-Thinking-1 and MAI-Code-1-Flash, a signal that hyperscalers are now shipping their own frontier-adjacent reasoning and coding models. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/2/microsofts-new-models/]
- **LLM Research Papers: The 2026 List (January to May)** — Sebastian Raschka (Ahead of AI). _Takeaway:_ Curated, annotated roundup of the year's notable LLM papers on training, reasoning, agents, and efficiency — a high-signal reading list rather than a recap. [https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/llm-research-papers-2026-part1]
- **RL Systems Mind the Gap: Matching Trainer and Generator Throughput** — SemiAnalysis. _Takeaway:_ Deep technical look at why mismatched trainer/generator throughput bottlenecks RL training systems, framed against a $30B+ ARR coding-assistant market. [https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/rl-systems-mind-the-gap-matching]
- **Stop Saying Half of 2026 US Datacenter Capacity Is Canceled** — SemiAnalysis. _Takeaway:_ Debunks the viral claim tracing to an April Bloomberg piece, separating genuine delays from misread capacity numbers in the AI build-out. [https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/stop-saying-half-of-2026-us-datacenter]
- **Q: How do I evaluate agentic workflows?** — Hamel Husain. _Takeaway:_ Practitioner guidance on treating agents as a black box ("did we meet the goal?") plus step-level diagnostics for tool choice, parameter extraction, and context retention. [https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/how-do-i-evaluate-agentic-workflows.html]
- **The Moral of Fable** — Every (Chain of Thought). _Takeaway:_ Argues through hands-on use that Claude Fable 5 is the strongest coding model yet, and what that implies for how engineers should restructure their workflows. [https://every.to/chain-of-thought/the-moral-of-fable]
