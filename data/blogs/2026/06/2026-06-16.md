# AI Blogs — 2026-06-16

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **Statement on the US government directive to suspend access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ First-hand log of the export-control directive (issued 5:21pm ET Fri) that forced Anthropic to disable Fable 5 / Mythos 5 for all users, including its own foreign-national staff; Willison scripted the Anthropic API and caught his own access being cut at 6:59pm PT, with the model returning a 404 redirect to Opus 4.8. The stated trigger was a narrow "read this codebase and fix the flaws" jailbreak that he and Anthropic argue is replicable on other public models (incl. GPT-5.5). [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/13/us-government-directive-to-suspend-access/]

- **Welcome to the AGI era of AI governance** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects). _Takeaway:_ Reads the Fable/Mythos suspension as the "starting gun" of a new governance era where releases get gated on vibes by an executive branch with little technical depth. Argues an export ban on model weights is lasting bad policy, that Anthropic's years of nuclear-weapons-grade rhetoric helped manufacture this moment, and warns the open-source crowd celebrating the move isn't ready for when the same rapid-response policy turns on open models. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/welcome-to-the-agi-era-of-ai-governance]

- **The Inference Shift** — Ben Thompson (Stratechery). _Takeaway:_ Splits LLM compute into three workloads — training, "answer inference" (human in the loop, speed matters) and "agentic inference" (no human, trade speed for capacity). The argument that agents reshape both the magnitude and the shape of compute demand is why he no longer thinks we're in a bubble — a useful frame for reading the current data-center/CPU buildout. [https://stratechery.com/2026/the-inference-shift/]

- **Claude Fable is relentlessly proactive** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Hands-on notes on Fable 5's behavior just before the shutdown: the model is markedly more agentic/proactive than predecessors, volunteering follow-up actions and tool calls. Useful as a capability snapshot of the exact model the government moved to restrict. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/11/fable-is-relentlessly-proactive/]

- **AI Tooling for Software Engineers in 2026** — Gergely Orosz (Pragmatic Engineer). _Takeaway:_ Survey-backed snapshot: 95% of respondents use AI tools weekly+, 75% for at least half their work, and Claude Code went zero-to-#1 in eight months, overtaking Copilot and Cursor. Counterweight to the hype: devs generating 2x+ the code raises quality/reliability/tech-debt concerns, and teams are starting to hit usage limits and cut AI spend. [https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/ai-tooling-2026]

- **Evals Skills for Coding Agents** — Hamel Husain. _Takeaway:_ Practitioner pattern for pointing coding agents at evals — "documentation tells the agent what to do, telemetry tells it whether it worked, evals tell it whether the output is good." Cites OpenAI's Harness Engineering case (3 engineers, 5 months, ~1M LOC) where improving the infra around the agent mattered more than improving the model. [https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-skills/]

- **CPUs are Back: The Datacenter CPU Landscape in 2026** — SemiAnalysis. _Takeaway:_ Argues demands on datacenter CPU and DRAM are intensifying in 2026 with a wave of new CPU generations — the often-ignored non-GPU half of the agentic-inference buildout. Pairs with the Stratechery inference-shift thesis on where compute spend is actually going. [https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/cpus-are-back-the-datacenter-cpu]
