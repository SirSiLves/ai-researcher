## Long-form picks

- **My bets on open models, mid-2026** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects). _Takeaway:_ A 13-point thesis arguing the open-vs-closed race is now an economics question, not a capabilities one — Chinese open-weight labs hit funding trouble first, RL-on-real-usage (Claude Code / Codex feedback loops) is the first axis where closed labs can durably pull ahead, and the US slowly regains open-model adoption ground from early 2027. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/my-bets-on-open-models-mid-2026]

- **AI enthusiasts are in a race against time, AI skeptics are in a race against entropy** — Simon Willison (linking Charity Majors). _Takeaway:_ Frames the enthusiast/skeptic split inside engineering teams as both groups being right — shipping code faster than humans can read it draws down a trust account built over years — and casts the missing "feedback loop between the two camps" as an organizational design problem, not a tooling one. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/4/ai-enthusiasts-ai-skeptics/]

- **Running Python code in a sandbox with MicroPython and WASM** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Hands-on writeup of `datasette-agent-micropython`, which gives an agent an `execute_micropython` tool backed by a WASM-sandboxed interpreter with per-conversation persistent state; he locked GPT-5.5 xhigh inside and it has not broken out — a concrete, reproducible pattern for safe agent code execution. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/6/micropython-in-a-sandbox/]

- **Import AI 455: Automating AI Research** — Jack Clark (Import AI). _Takeaway:_ Marshals the task-horizon data (GPT-3.5 ~30s in 2022 → Opus 4.6 ~12h in 2026), the MLE-Bench jump to 64.4%, and Claude's 52× CPU-optimization speedups to argue automated AI R&D takeoff is already underway; notes Greenblatt doubling his 2028 full-automation estimate to 30%. [https://jack-clark.net/2026/05/04/import-ai-455-automating-ai-research/]

- **LLM Research Papers: The 2026 List (January to May)** — Sebastian Raschka (Ahead of AI). _Takeaway:_ Curated survey arguing 2026 architecture work has moved past "bigger transformers" toward hybrid architectures and representation geometry; a useful map of which directions are actually compounding versus noise. [https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/llm-research-papers-2026-part1]

- **China's Unitree Will Dominate Global Robotics** — SemiAnalysis. _Takeaway:_ Argues Unitree's G1 humanoids are crossing into viable deployments with three new designs incoming (including a direct Western competitor), reading the robotics supply chain the way SemiAnalysis usually reads silicon — embodied AI as the next contested compute frontier. [https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/chinas-unitree-will-dominate-global]
