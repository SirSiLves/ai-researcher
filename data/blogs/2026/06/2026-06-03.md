## Long-form picks

- **Open and closed models are on different exponentials** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects). _Takeaway:_ Argues the open-vs-closed debate is now fundamentally economic: coding agents proved a market that will pay a steep premium for top-tier intelligence, so closed labs gate their best models behind APIs while open weights chase a different curve. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-and-closed-models-are-on-different](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/open-and-closed-models-are-on-different)

- **Microsoft's new MAI models** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Hands-on read of Microsoft's first in-house text LLMs, MAI-Thinking-1 (35B reasoning) and MAI-Code-1-Flash (5B, purpose-built for Copilot/VS Code) — a signal Microsoft is reducing OpenAI dependency at the model layer. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/2/microsofts-new-models/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/2/microsofts-new-models/)

- **Uber Caps Usage of AI Tools Like Claude Code to Manage Costs** — Simon Willison (link blog). _Takeaway:_ Uber capping employees at $1,500/month per AI coding tool is an early, concrete data point that agentic-coding token spend is becoming a real enterprise budget line, not a rounding error. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/3/uber-caps-usage/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/3/uber-caps-usage/)

- **Hackers Simply Asked Meta AI to Give Them Access to High-Profile Instagram Accounts. It Worked** — via Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ A support-bot social-engineering case where an agent with account-action privileges was talked into granting access — a vivid argument for treating agent tool-permissions as an attack surface. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/1/hackers-simply-asked-meta-ai/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/1/hackers-simply-asked-meta-ai/)

- **Import AI 458: Reckoning with the future; and a singularity story** — Jack Clark. _Takeaway:_ Clark's Oxford Cosmos/HAI lecture on confronting near-term AI consequence; pairs policy framing with a narrative on trajectory, useful for governance readers tracking how frontier-lab leadership talks about risk. [https://jack-clark.net/2026/05/26/import-ai-458-reckoning-with-the-future-and-a-singularity-story/](https://jack-clark.net/2026/05/26/import-ai-458-reckoning-with-the-future-and-a-singularity-story/)

- **CPUs are Back: The Datacenter CPU Landscape in 2026** — SemiAnalysis. _Takeaway:_ Makes the non-obvious infra case that RAG and agentic workloads are driving renewed demand for general-purpose CPU compute (Intel Clearwater Forest/Diamond Rapids, AMD Venice) alongside accelerators. [https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/cpus-are-back-the-datacenter-cpu](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/cpus-are-back-the-datacenter-cpu)

- **A Dream of Spring for Open-Weight LLMs: 10 Architectures from Jan–Feb 2026** — Sebastian Raschka (Ahead of AI). _Takeaway:_ Architecture-by-architecture teardown of the early-2026 open-weight wave (Qwen3-Coder-Next, GLM-5, Kimi K2.5, MiniMax M2.5), concluding gains come more from data/recipes than novel architecture. [https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/a-dream-of-spring-for-open-weight](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/a-dream-of-spring-for-open-weight)

- **How the Executive Branch Is Reshaping AI Federalism** — Lawfare. _Takeaway:_ Tracks the Trump administration's June 2 EO on AI cybersecurity and its pivot from light-touch to stricter model oversight, framed against state-vs-federal preemption — core reading for the governance slice. [https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/how-the-executive-branch-is-reshaping-ai-federalism](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/how-the-executive-branch-is-reshaping-ai-federalism)
