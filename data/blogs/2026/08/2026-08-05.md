## Long-form picks

- **New release of LLM adds support for reasoning traces, OpenAI Responses, server-side tools, and smarter logging** — Simon Willison, 4 Aug 2026.
  _Takeaway:_ Reasoning traces become a first-class logged artifact rather than throwaway stdout — the interesting design claim is that server-side tool calls and reasoning tokens need to live in the same local log store if you want reproducible agent debugging. Practical read for anyone building their own harness rather than buying one.
  https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/4/new-release-of-llm/

- **Evals Skills for Coding Agents** — Hamel Husain.
  _Takeaway:_ A packaged set of eval "skills" distilled from helping 50+ companies and teaching 4,000+ students, aimed at the failure modes people hit when coding agents themselves instrument the app, run the experiment, and analyse the data. The argument: the bottleneck has moved from writing evals to stopping agents from grading their own homework.
  https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-skills/

- **Context engineering with Dex Horthy** — Gergely Orosz / The Pragmatic Engineer.
  _Takeaway:_ Horthy (who coined "context engineering") pushes past it to *harness engineering* and *loop engineering* — the claim being that the durable leverage is in the wrapper around the model, not the prompt. Concrete on how far you can automate the SDLC before code quality degrades.
  https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/context-engineering-with-dex-horthy

- **Gemma 4 and what makes an open model succeed** — Nathan Lambert / Interconnects.
  _Takeaway:_ Substantive position piece on why open-weight releases win or die — distribution, licence clarity and ecosystem tooling over raw benchmark position. Useful counterweight to the "open models are converging on frontier" framing.
  https://www.interconnects.ai/p/gemma-4-and-what-makes-an-open-model

- **AI Won't Automatically Make Legal Services Cheaper** — Lawfare.
  _Takeaway:_ Contrarian and well-argued: advanced AI will not, by default, help consumers get better legal outcomes at lower cost, because the binding constraints are procedural and institutional, not drafting throughput. A good template for interrogating "AI will collapse the cost of X" claims in any regulated profession.
  https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-won-t-automatically-make-legal-services-cheaper

- **Fighting AI Cyberattacks Starts With Knowing They're Happening** — Lawfare.
  _Takeaway:_ Argues the observability gap is the real problem — we cannot govern AI-enabled offensive capability we cannot measure — and proposes an AI Security Review Board modelled on the CSRB. Directly relevant to anyone writing agent-security policy.
  https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/fighting-ai-cyberattacks-starts-with-knowing-they-re-happening

- **Inside the AI Index: 12 Takeaways from the 2026 Report** — Stanford HAI.
  _Takeaway:_ Data-backed rather than vibes-based: AI-specific governance roles up 17% in 2025, share of businesses with *no* responsible-AI policy down from 24% to 11%, but implementation still blocked by knowledge gaps (59%), budget (48%) and regulatory uncertainty (41%). The headline framing is a widening gap between capability and readiness.
  https://hai.stanford.edu/news/inside-the-ai-index-12-takeaways-from-the-2026-report

## Medium roundup

### model-context-protocol, mcp

- **[6 Critical Challenges Facing the MCP in 2026](https://medium.com/@MattLeads/6-critical-challenges-facing-the-mcp-in-2026-06258e914402)** — Matt Mochalkin. Critical rather than promotional read on where the protocol strains: governance, multi-tenant access, and the roadmap direction that would let an MCP server act as an agent itself.
- **[CLI-Based Agents vs MCP: The 2026 Showdown That Every AI Engineer Needs to Understand](https://lalatenduswain.medium.com/cli-based-agents-vs-mcp-the-2026-showdown-that-every-ai-engineer-needs-to-understand-7dfbc9e3e1f9)** — Lalatendu Keshari Swain. Frames a real live debate — shell-out-to-CLI versus protocol-mediated tool access — instead of assuming MCP wins by default.

### rag, retrieval-augmented-generation, vector-database

- **[Do You Still Need a Vector Database in 2026? (Vector databases are dying — here's the production evidence)](https://medium.com/data-science-collective/vector-databases-are-dying-heres-the-production-evidence-8c17b54687e2)** — Data Science Collective. Contrarian with numbers: vector search is becoming plumbing inside existing stores rather than a standalone product category.
- **[Modern RAG in 2026: The Components That Actually Matter](https://medium.com/data-science-collective/modern-rag-in-2026-the-components-that-actually-matter-3f6a138ef117)** — Anubhav. The through-line is Modular RAG plus the merger of RAG with agent loops — the system reasons about *whether* to retrieve, what, and when to stop, rather than retrieving once.

### agentic-ai, ai-engineering

- **[Harness Engineering for AI Agents in 2026](https://medium.com/@visrow/harness-engineering-for-ai-agents-in-2026-114fcb8edf9e)** — Vishal Mysore. Names the emerging discipline: build the programmatic wrapper that governs the execution environment so failures become structurally impossible to repeat, rather than trying to make the model smarter.
- **[Context Engineering for AI Agents](https://medium.com/data-science-collective/context-engineering-for-ai-agents-116e4a5d003a)** — Farhad Malik. Working definition plus mechanics of filling the context window with exactly the right information at each step of an agent trajectory.

### ai-governance, responsible-ai

- **[EU AI Act 2026: 7 Compliance Traps That Kill Enterprise Projects](https://medium.com/@wasowski.jarek/dont-wait-for-a-delay-august-2026-is-real-eu-ai-act-3801bd967b0f)** — Jaroslaw Wasowski. Post-deadline (2 Aug 2026) reality check: a realistic programme for an enterprise with 20–50 AI systems runs 18–24 months, so the delay debate was never the operative variable.
- **[The 2026 Compliance Cliff: When Data Credibility Becomes a Regulated Asset](https://medium.com/@tim_62250/the-2026-compliance-cliff-when-data-credibility-becomes-a-regulated-asset-66d4a505733e)** — Tim de Rosen. Argues the sharp edge is retroactive: 2024–2025 AI-generated data feeding commercial decisions now needs post-facto verification or exclusion.
