## Long-form picks

**Who Writes the AI Constitution?** — Nicolas McMullan & Kevin Frazier, Lawfare, 13 Aug 2026.
_Takeaway:_ The sharpest governance argument this week, and it cuts against the grain: model "constitutions" are First Amendment-protected speech, because unlike almost everything else in a training pipeline they are written by identifiable humans, published under a company name, and read as a statement of values. That makes constitutional AI the one lever where authors act on model character *ex ante* rather than nudging outputs *ex post* via RLHF or API safeguards — and also the one the government can least directly regulate. The fallback levers they map out are disclosure mandates, procurement leverage, and clauses against criminal conduct.
[link](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/who-writes-the-ai-constitution)

**alchemy-utils 0.1a0 / 0.1a1** — Simon Willison (simonwillison.net), 12–13 Aug 2026.
_Takeaway:_ A useful data point on where coding agents actually land in 2026: Willison describes this as "literally a shower project" — he handed Codex and GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra a research-spike prompt (same core API as sqlite-utils, but SQLAlchemy-backed; red/green TDD; test against PostgreSQL, SQLite and DuckDB; commit early and often) and it took "very few follow-up prompts" to reach alpha quality. The follow-up is the interesting part: a first-run import took nearly an hour, he had Codex optimise it, and it came down to ~35 seconds. The published prompt transcript is the reusable artifact here, not the library.
[link](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/12/alchemy-utils/)

**Gemini is Cooked but GCP is Cooking** — Max Kan, Joey Brookhart, Doug O'Laughlin & Dylan Patel, SemiAnalysis, 7 Aug 2026. _(archive-scan backfill — never carried this month)_
_Takeaway:_ The contrarian split that most Google coverage misses: DeepMind's long-term position and Google Cloud's short-term position are moving in opposite directions, with GCP posting >100% YoY revenue growth precisely because it is monetising other people's models rather than winning on its own. Worth reading against the reflex of treating "Google's AI performance" as a single number.
[link](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/gemini-is-cooked-but-gcp-is-cooking)

**Making Fable Cheaper Than Opus** — Cognition, 13 Jul 2026. _(archive-scan backfill — never carried this month; Cognition has published nothing since 28 Jul)_
_Takeaway:_ The most concrete argument yet for pricing agentic work per *task* rather than per token. Fable 5 costs 2x per token vs Opus 4.8, but as lead orchestrator in Cognition's Fusion architecture it scored higher on FrontierCode 1.1 *and* cost less per run — because a stronger orchestrator burns fewer trajectories. If you are doing model selection for a multi-agent harness on sticker price, this is the post that says why that is the wrong metric.
[link](https://cognition.com/blog/making-fable-cheaper-than-opus)

## Medium roundup

### ai-agents / agentic-ai
- **[Agent Orchestration Is Becoming the Real Multi-Agent Problem](https://medium.com/@balajibal/agent-orchestration-is-becoming-the-real-multi-agent-problem-1922d4ddb28c)** — balaji bal. Argues the hard part has migrated from agent capability to *routing*: naive single-shot LLM routing over a full agent registry doesn't hold up, and the pattern that does is two-stage — retrieve a candidate shortlist from registry metadata, embeddings, tags or prior success history, then apply rules or an LLM only over that smaller set.
- **[AI Agent Orchestration in 2026: What Actually Works](https://medium.com/@vision_16700/ai-agent-orchestration-in-2026-what-actually-works-33c3399d3f60)** — ClawPod. The claim worth arguing with: the teams winning with agents aren't the ones on the best models, they're the ones with the clearest agent specifications, the best observability, and the most deliberate human-AI handoff design. Frames a single do-everything agent as both a single point of failure and a cognitive-overload problem.

### model-context-protocol / mcp
- **[Model Context Protocol (MCP): The Complete Engineering Guide — Architecture, Internals, and Real-World Use Cases](https://medium.com/@rishabhkr954/model-context-protocol-mcp-the-complete-engineering-guide-architecture-internals-and-0d7b5d988b08)** — Rishabh Kumar. The most complete internals-level walkthrough currently on Medium (JSON-RPC 2.0 core, transport-agnostic client-server model). Note the framing is still the *stateful* protocol — read it alongside the 2026-07-28 stateless-core spec rather than instead of it.

### ai-engineering
- **[A Production Guide to Langfuse: Observability, Prompts, and Evaluation for LLM Applications](https://medium.com/@ssehgal6745/a-production-guide-to-langfuse-observability-prompts-and-evaluation-for-llm-applications-b4a3604bb25f)** — Sachin Kumar. Best one-line framing of a distinction teams keep collapsing: observability tells you what happened, evaluation tells you if it was good — and you need both correlated in a single trace (prompts, outputs, token counts, retrieval results, eval scores).

### rag / retrieval-augmented-generation
- **[RAG in 2026: Architecture Shifts, Emerging Patterns, and What It Means for Java Developers](https://medium.com/@elammarisoufiane/rag-in-2026-architecture-shifts-emerging-patterns-and-what-it-means-for-java-developers-6f2803e39787)** — soufiane ELAMMARI. Unusual for being written for the JVM/Spring side of the house rather than the Python default. The transferable idea is Modular RAG: every pipeline component independently replaceable and composable, which is what makes retrieval failures debuggable in the first place.

### vector-database
- **[How to Choose the Best Embedding Model for RAG in 2026: 10 Models Benchmarked](https://milvusio.medium.com/how-to-choose-the-best-embedding-model-for-rag-in-2026-10-models-benchmarked-4efc9508a193)** — Milvus. Actual numbers rather than vibes: the CCKM benchmark runs 10 models released across 2025–26 through cross-modal retrieval, cross-lingual search, long-document accuracy and dimension compression. The compression axis is the one most teams under-test and the one that decides your index cost.

### ai-safety
- **[Red-Teaming Autonomous Agents: A Field Guide to Agentic AI Security](https://medium.com/innernet-world/red-teaming-autonomous-agents-a-field-guide-to-agentic-ai-security-532b2f837167)** — Himanshu Agarwal, InnerNet World. The load-bearing observation: a model can pass a jailbreak scan cleanly and still fail once it is wired to a tool that reads poisoned data — capabilities chained by an attacker who never touched your network become an exfiltration engine. Argues the 2026 methodological shift is autonomous, agent-orchestrated red teaming (attacker LLM given a natural-language objective, selecting attacks and composing transforms itself), and notes EU AI Act August obligations are what is suddenly making adversarial-testing documentation a procurement requirement.

### ai-governance / responsible-ai
- **[The Complete Guide to EU AI Regulatory Compliance in 2026](https://atul4u.medium.com/the-complete-guide-to-eu-ai-regulatory-compliance-in-2026-everything-you-need-to-know-about-the-ai-720a8947496e)** — TechEon. The practical companion to the 2 Aug 2026 enforcement date: Annex III high-risk requirements, Article 10 documented data-quality criteria, Article 14 operational human oversight, conformity assessment, CE marking and EU database registration are all now live obligations, not roadmap items.
