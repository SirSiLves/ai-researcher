## Long-form picks

- **LiteLLM Hack: Were You One of the 47,000?** — Daniel Hnyk (futuresearch.ai), via Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ BigQuery PyPI download data shows the compromised LiteLLM packages (1.82.7 + 1.82.8) were downloaded 46,996 times in the ~46 minutes they were live. Of 2,337 dependents, 88% did not pin versions in a way that would have avoided the bad release. A concrete blast-radius number for the cooldown discussion. [https://futuresearch.ai/blog/litellm-hack-were-you-one-of-the-47000/](https://futuresearch.ai/blog/litellm-hack-were-you-one-of-the-47000/)
- **Thoughts on slowing the fuck down** — Mario Zechner (HN), via Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ The creator of OpenClaw's Pi agent framework argues we've traded engineering discipline for an addiction to LOC throughput. Agents accumulate small "booboos" at a rate humans can't review, producing cognitive debt and "merchants of complexity". Prescription: cap daily AI-generated LOC, hand-write architecture/APIs. [https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47517539](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47517539)

- **How open model ecosystems compound** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects. _Takeaway:_ Argues there will not be a single shared foundation model that all open work builds on, because building the best model is now an art of integrating hardware/data/infra and evolving them all simultaneously. Concludes the ecosystem needs an "open model consortium" — possibly the only financially viable way to keep open weights at the frontier. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/how-open-model-ecosystems-compound](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/how-open-model-ecosystems-compound)
