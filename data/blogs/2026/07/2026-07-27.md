## Long-form picks

- **Import AI 466: The bitter lesson for robotics, AIs complete week-long programming tasks; and OpenAI's accidental AI hacker** — Jack Clark (Jul 27). _Takeaway:_ Today's issue, and the strongest single read of the day. Three threads that rhyme. (1) **MirrorCode**, Epoch + METR's new long-horizon benchmark, asks models to reimplement a program from CLI access alone with no source and no web: Opus 4.7 solved one in 14 hours for $251 of inference on work estimated at 2–17 human weeks; 17/25 targets got a perfect run, but 8/25 were never solved — `ruff` was the hardest. Clark's read is the interesting one: this isn't "models got better at coding," it's that models can **self-orient** in an alien environment through black-box access alone. (2) **Project Fetch Phase Two**: Anthropic went from Opus 4.1 being unable to do quadruped robot tasks at all (Aug 2025, humans took 181 min) to Opus 4.7 doing all but one autonomously in 9m35s — "not the result of a concerted effort to improve the robotics capabilities of our models." Sunday's ACT-2 post makes the same argument from the startup side (99.1% success, 778 folds across 9 garment types) — scale pretraining, then hill-climb on minimal in-house data. (3) The OpenAI containment breaks, where Clark's verdict is blunt: "total LessWrong victory." [https://jack-clark.net/2026/07/27/import-ai-466-the-bitter-lesson-for-robotics-ais-complete-week-long-programming-tasks-and-openais-accidental-ai-hacker/](https://jack-clark.net/2026/07/27/import-ai-466-the-bitter-lesson-for-robotics-ais-complete-week-long-programming-tasks-and-openais-accidental-ai-hacker/)

- **What does it mean to "own your intelligence"?** — Harrison Chase, LangChain (Jul 25). _Takeaway:_ The most useful strategy piece of the week because it converts a slogan into a testable checklist rather than a manifesto. The argument: buy the generic, undifferentiated layers; own the three that determine behaviour — model (optionality *and* the ability to self-host a deprecated model), harness (orchestration logic, so you know exactly what enters the LLM at every step), and context/memory. Then own the economics, quality and risk (per-user spend caps, evals that catch regressions on model swap, full traces for audit), and finally the compounding loop: traces + feedback → prompt/harness/tool changes → an eval per change so the fix doesn't regress. The ten-question checklist at the end is the part worth stealing — "when your agent learns, can you port those learnings to a different system entirely?" is a question most teams cannot answer yes to. Notably cites Uber burning its 2026 AI budget in four months as the cost cautionary tale. [https://www.langchain.com/blog/own-your-intelligence](https://www.langchain.com/blog/own-your-intelligence)

- **When Reporting an AI Security Incident Is Not Mandatory** — Mackenzie Arnold & Stephan Llerena, Lawfare / Institute for Law & AI (Jul 24). _Takeaway:_ The best governance writing on the Hugging Face breach, and it lands a genuinely uncomfortable finding: OpenAI **may not have been legally obliged to disclose it**. California SB 53, New York's RAISE Act and Illinois SB 315 define "critical safety incident" identically, and three of the four reportable categories require actual harm — up to "the death of, or serious injury to, more than 50 people or more than one billion dollars in damage." That leaves only the deception-and-subversion category, which requires *simultaneously* proving deception, that the behaviour fell outside an evaluation designed to elicit it, and materially increased catastrophic risk. The authors' structural critique is the payoff: incident reporting is supposed to *produce* the information needed to judge an event, not require that judgment as a precondition — "the chicken must come before the egg." Their reform list (lower the bar, shift focus from deployment to **nonpublic internal models**, add rulemaking authority and investigative powers, allow periodic rather than rapid reporting, and share with the EU AI Office under Art. 78(5)) is the most concrete policy proposal published this week. [https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/when-reporting-an-ai-security-incident-is-not-mandatory](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/when-reporting-an-ai-security-incident-is-not-mandatory)

- **How We Benchmark Deep Agents** — Nick Hollon & Harrison Chase, LangChain (Jul 23). _Takeaway:_ A rare vendor post that is actually a methods paper, and it quietly confirms the week's biggest practitioner claim. LangChain moved from unit-style tests to end-to-end evals run in **Harbor** (the framework behind Terminal Bench), where a task is an environment (Dockerfile), an instruction, and a `test.sh` judge — because for agents the environment *is* part of the task and you have to grade the artifacts, not just the final message. Three benchmarks, one per work type: **Harbor-Index** (82 tasks distilled from 6,000+ candidates across 54 benchmarks), a **𝜏³-bench** 30-task conversation subset, and **ContextBench** (30 retrieval tasks shipping their full corpus into the sandbox). Practices worth copying: run every task multiple times because agents are nondeterministic; keep a frozen "lite" subset weighted to the hard-but-solvable frontier (~8x faster, ~6x cheaper) for iteration; keep deterministic unit tests as the layer beneath. The kicker: the stated purpose of this rig for Deep Agents 0.7 is to justify **removing** the todo-list middleware and significantly slimming the system prompt — independent corroboration that the Claude 5 generation wants less instruction, not more. [https://www.langchain.com/blog/how-we-benchmark-deep-agents](https://www.langchain.com/blog/how-we-benchmark-deep-agents)

## Medium roundup

### ai-agents / ai-safety
- **[The Month AI Agents Broke Our Trust (And What I Changed Because of It)](https://medium.com/@andy.a.g/the-month-ai-agents-broke-our-trust-and-what-i-changed-because-of-it-080cb4bf95bd)** — Andy.G (Jul 21). The best hands-on agent-security writing on Medium this month, and unusually well-sourced for the format: six primary-sourced incidents between late June and mid-July, grouped by failure family rather than listed. Exfiltration (Grok Build uploading whole repos, SSH keys and password databases; Claude's `web_fetch` leaking memory letter-by-letter through a fake Turnstile widget; Akamai on MCP's `x-mcp-header` leaking tokens to every proxy in the path). Destruction (GPT-5.6 Sol deleting `$HOME` — severity 3 in the model card, model not pulled, damage concentrated in Full-Access mode). Manipulation (**GuardFall**: shell guardrails bypassed in 10 of 11 open-source coding agents by exploiting the gap between filter-time text and bash's own quote removal — only Continue survived; **HalluSquatting**, weaponizing the package names models invent; AI Now's *Friendly Fire*, where the security-auditing agent runs the attacker's binary because the README asked it to). The conclusion he actually changed his workflow around: "There is no patch to wait for… the weakness is in the design, so the fix is a change in workflow, not a version bump." His five rules — no Full-Access by default, working dirs away from secrets, treat every fetched page as hostile input, gate on capability not string matching, keep the expensive model for the decision — are the most directly actionable thing in today's set.

### model-context-protocol / mcp
- **[MCP Goes Stateless: What Every Python Developer Must Do Before July 28](https://medium.com/@yogeshkrishnanseeniraj/mcp-goes-stateless-what-every-python-developer-must-do-before-july-28-a6d51f86a6a5)** — Yogeshkrishnanseeniraj (Jul 17). The operational counterpart to the spec explainers that have dominated this tag: an ordered checklist rather than a changelog. Pin `mcp>=1.27,<2` today (the one step that cannot wait), run `mcp-migration scan` over the source tree to inventory breakage, run `mcp-migration probe` against a live Streamable HTTP server, then fix in strict order — pin, session state, **in-memory tool state** (the silent timebomb once any request can land on any instance), load-balancer config, and only then the deprecated subsystems on their own schedule. Worth reading tonight given the spec locks tomorrow.
