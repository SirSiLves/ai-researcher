# AI Blogs — 2026-07-21

## Long-form picks

- **Reverse-engineering is cheap now** — Simon Willison (Jul 20). _Takeaway:_ Argues LLMs have collapsed the cost of reverse-engineering — minified JS, undocumented binaries, obscure formats — turning specialist, hours-long work into a quick prompt, and asks how much "security through obscurity" is still worth once that barrier falls. (Companion Jul 21 post covers "Nativ," a local-model runner for the Mac.) [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/20/cheap-reverse-engineering/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/20/cheap-reverse-engineering/)

- **Kimi K3: The open-weights escalation** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects) (Jul 20). _Takeaway:_ Reads Moonshot's Kimi K3 (2.8T-param MoE, pitched this week as Opus-4.8-class quality at roughly Sonnet-5 pricing) as an escalation in the open-weights race — the latest data point in Lambert's running argument that open models are closing the frontier gap faster than the closed labs assumed. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/kimi-k3-the-open-weights-escalation](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/kimi-k3-the-open-weights-escalation)

- **Import AI 465: Open vs closed gaps; Kimi K3; Demis' big policy plan** — Jack Clark (Jul 20). _Takeaway:_ This week's issue threads the narrowing open-vs-closed capability gap, the Kimi K3 release, and a major policy proposal attributed to Demis Hassabis — Clark's usual "here's the research, here's why it matters for governance" framing. [https://jack-clark.net/2026/07/20/import-ai-465-open-vs-closed-gaps-kimi-k3-demis-big-policy-plan/](https://jack-clark.net/2026/07/20/import-ai-465-open-vs-closed-gaps-kimi-k3-demis-big-policy-plan/)

- **Who's Afraid of Chinese Models?** — Ben Thompson (Stratechery) (Jul 20). _Takeaway:_ Positions the rise of Chinese open-weight models (DeepSeek / Qwen / Kimi lineage) against US labs' moats and export/policy strategy — a strategic-not-technical read on why open Chinese releases matter more than benchmark deltas. [https://stratechery.com/2026/whos-afraid-of-chinese-models/](https://stratechery.com/2026/whos-afraid-of-chinese-models/)

- **Controlling Reasoning Effort in LLMs** — Sebastian Raschka (Jul 18). _Takeaway:_ Technical survey of how to dial test-time reasoning up or down — reasoning-effort settings, thinking-budget tokens, and the accuracy/latency/cost trade-offs — with the hands-on, mechanism-level detail Raschka is known for. [https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/controlling-reasoning-effort-in-llms](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/controlling-reasoning-effort-in-llms)

- **The Pulse: What can we learn from Bun's rapid Rust rewrite with AI?** — Gergely Orosz (Pragmatic Engineer) (Jul 16). _Takeaway:_ Uses Bun's AI-assisted Rust rewrite as a case study in how far AI coding tooling has come for large, performance-sensitive codebases — and where human engineering judgment still sets the ceiling. [https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/the-pulse-what-can-we-learn-from-buns-rapid-rust-rewrite-with-ai/](https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/the-pulse-what-can-we-learn-from-buns-rapid-rust-rewrite-with-ai/)

- **The Commercial Landscape of AI Sovereignty Offerings** — Stanford HAI (Jul 14). _Takeaway:_ New policy brief surveying the fast-growing "AI sovereignty" market as nations spend billions to cut reliance on US providers; argues sovereignty is a spectrum of interdependence and that policymakers should calibrate dependencies rather than chase full self-sufficiency. [https://hai.stanford.edu/policy/the-commercial-landscape-of-ai-sovereignty-offerings](https://hai.stanford.edu/policy/the-commercial-landscape-of-ai-sovereignty-offerings)

- **What will be left for us to work on?** — Arvind Narayanan & Sayash Kapoor (AI Snake Oil / Normal Tech) (Jul 13). _Takeaway:_ The "AI as normal technology" authors on which human work survives as AI automates tasks — their characteristically skeptical, diffusion-paced take that reframes the automation question around jobs-as-bundles-of-tasks rather than wholesale replacement. [https://www.normaltech.ai/p/what-will-be-left-for-us-to-work](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/what-will-be-left-for-us-to-work)

- **How GPT-5.6 Changes Knowledge Work** — Dan Shipper (Every / Chain of Thought) (Jul 10). _Takeaway:_ Hands-on account of how GPT-5.6 shifts day-to-day knowledge work and workflows — practitioner observations rather than benchmarks, from someone running an AI-native media/software shop. [https://every.to/chain-of-thought/how-gpt-5-6-changes-knowledge-work](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/how-gpt-5-6-changes-knowledge-work)

- **Congress Should Do Something: The Case for (Fixing) the Great American AI Act** — Lawfare (Jul 8). _Takeaway:_ Calls GAAIA the best federal frontier-AI-safety framework yet proposed but net-negative as written, because its sweeping preemption of state AI laws would strip existing protections; context is the post-"Mythos" shift in AI-safety politics. [https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/congress-should-do-something--the-case-for-(fixing)-the-great-american-ai-act](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/congress-should-do-something--the-case-for-(fixing)-the-great-american-ai-act)

- **Harness Engineering for Self-Improvement** — Lilian Weng (Jul 4). _Takeaway:_ A 31-minute deep dive reframing recursive self-improvement around "harness engineering" — improving the training pipeline, tools, and deployment scaffolding around a model rather than only its weights — as the practical near-term path to compounding capability gains. [https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/)

- **Improving agents is a data mining problem** — LangChain blog. _Takeaway:_ Argues the real bottleneck to better agents is mining production traces and failure data — not prompt tweaking — and treating agent improvement as a data/eval loop; a process claim aimed at teams stuck iterating by vibes. [https://www.langchain.com/blog/improving-agents-is-a-data-mining-problem](https://www.langchain.com/blog/improving-agents-is-a-data-mining-problem)

- **Long Horizon Document Agents** — LlamaIndex blog. _Takeaway:_ Practitioner write-up on building agents that sustain long-horizon document workflows (parse → extract → reason → iterate), tied to the "2026 is the year of long-horizon agents" thesis and LlamaIndex's parsing/extraction stack. [https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/long-horizon-document-agents](https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/long-horizon-document-agents)
