# AI Blogs — 2026-07-25

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

**Theme of the day:** the day belongs to **Claude Opus 5**, which Anthropic shipped on Friday (Jul 24) — its fourth model in under two months, after Mythos 5, Fable 5 and Sonnet 5. The analyst writing splits along the usual fault line. On the "capabilities" side, Simon Willison's annotated launch read walks the claim that Opus 5 reaches near-Fable-5 frontier intelligence at half the price, and the number-watchers pick at a genuinely odd detail — its highest (max) effort setting scores *below* the notch beneath it on Frontier-Bench. On the "does it actually work" side, Every's first-week vibe check is a useful cold shower: brilliant in flashes, but it argues with instructions, quits early, and breaks existing skills/plugins. Running underneath the launch is a quieter governance thread that has nothing to do with which model tops which leaderboard: Stanford HAI convenes on the regulatory vacuum around AI mental-health tools, and practitioners digest the EU AI Act's surprise 16-month slip for standalone high-risk systems. The infra-practitioner corner stays on its months-long refrain — an MCP server is a service not a script, agents die in production from tool/context/orchestration failures rather than dumb models, and "maximum context" is a cost, not a feature.

## Long-form picks

- **Introducing Claude Opus 5** — Simon Willison (Jul 24). _Takeaway:_ Willison's characteristic annotated coverage of the Friday launch. Opus 5 is pitched as an everyday model that comes close to Fable 5's frontier intelligence at half the price — new SOTA on coding/knowledge-work evals like Frontier-Bench and GDPval-AA — while holding Opus 4.8's pricing at $5/$25 per million tokens. Notable context he draws out: this is Anthropic's fourth model release in under two months, an unusually blistering cadence that reframes "Opus" from halo model to daily driver. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/24/introducing-claude-opus-5/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/24/introducing-claude-opus-5/)

- **Vibe Check: Claude Opus 5 Is Brilliant in Flashes, Frustrating in Practice** — Every / Dan Shipper (Jul 24). _Takeaway:_ The practitioner counterweight to launch-day benchmark euphoria. In its first week inside Every's workflow, Opus 5 argued with instructions, stopped before the work was finished, and didn't play well with the team's existing skills and plugins (their "compound engineering" setup) — the argument being that extracting the model's real, considerable strength may require dismantling the scaffolding you already built around the previous one. A concrete reminder that headline eval wins and day-to-day usability are different axes. [https://every.to/vibe-check/opus-5](https://every.to/vibe-check/opus-5)

- **Exploring the Dangers of AI in Mental Health Care** — Stanford HAI (Jul 24). _Takeaway:_ A deliberate off-cycle counterpoint to the model-launch news. HAI convened policymakers, academics, clinicians, AI developers and patient advocates to map the regulatory gaps around AI tools used for therapy and emotional support — where chatbots may underperform human therapists and can introduce biases and failure modes with genuinely dangerous consequences. Useful signal on where responsible-AI attention is heading as consumer "AI companion/therapist" products outrun the rules meant to govern them. [https://hai.stanford.edu/news/exploring-the-dangers-of-ai-in-mental-health-care](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/exploring-the-dangers-of-ai-in-mental-health-care)

### model-context-protocol
- **[MCP Deep Dive, Part 3: Build a Production-Grade MCP Server From Scratch](https://medium.com/@randhir.jassal/mcp-deep-dive-part-3-build-a-production-grade-mcp-server-from-scratch-1debcc955888)** — Randhir Jassal. Continues the week's "an MCP server is a service, not a script" thread with implementation detail: typed tool errors instead of protocol crashes, exposing read data as URI-addressed resources, pagination/cancellation, health endpoints and per-tool telemetry — the stuff that separates a demo from something an agent can hammer thousands of times a day.

### agentic-ai
- **[How I'd Build an AI Agent That Actually Survives Production](https://medium.com/@fahimulhaq/how-id-build-an-ai-agent-that-actually-survives-production-8d4889f45a65)** — Fahim ul Haq. Argues most agent failures aren't model failures — they're tool, context-management, and orchestration-logic failures — and that the execution layer must catch errors and hand the orchestrator structured, explicitly-retryable information rather than opaque exceptions.

### prompt-engineering
- **[The Hidden Cost of "Maximum Context" in AI Coding Agents: Specs, Memory, Chunking, and Token Burn](https://codechefvaibhavkashyap.medium.com/the-hidden-cost-of-maximum-context-in-ai-coding-agents-specs-memory-chunking-and-token-burn-d73e13e3d833)** — Vaibhav Kashyap. Timely against the Opus 5 / big-context launch chatter: because most agent spend is prefill, not generation, "keep everything in context" scales cost roughly linearly with steps — a dense, relevant 20K-token pack usually beats a messy 200K-token dump.

### ai-governance
- **[The EU AI Act Deadline Just Got Pushed 16 Months. Here's Why That's the Wrong Moment to Relax.](https://medium.com/@mohamedaasir1992/the-eu-ai-act-deadline-just-got-pushed-16-months-heres-why-that-s-the-wrong-moment-to-relax-e2a494750237)** — Aasir Waseer. Unpacks the June amendment that slid compliance for standalone Annex III high-risk systems (employment, credit scoring, healthcare-adjacent decisions) from Aug 2 2026 to Dec 2 2027 — while transparency/AI-content-labelling rules still land Aug 2 — and argues the extra runway is a trap, not a reprieve.
