## Long-form picks

**The OpenAI Super App, ChatGPT = Codex, Whither Chat** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (Jul 14, 2026). _Takeaway:_ Thompson reads OpenAI's move to refashion Codex into the new ChatGPT front-end as a bet on an agentic "super app," and asks whether OpenAI is quietly abandoning the plain chat category it pioneered — a strategic pivot from conversation to task execution. [https://stratechery.com/2026/the-openai-super-app-chatgpt-codex-whither-chat/](https://stratechery.com/2026/the-openai-super-app-chatgpt-codex-whither-chat/)

**What is "loop engineering?"** — Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer (Jul 14, 2026). _Takeaway:_ Orosz dissects the buzzy "loop engineering" label and finds real substance underneath — event triggers, cron-driven agent loops, and guardrails against AI slop — while pressing on whether it is a durable discipline or a "here today, gone tomorrow" trend. [https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/what-is-loop-engineering](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/what-is-loop-engineering)

**What will be left for us to work on?** — Arvind Narayanan, AI Snake Oil / Normal Tech (Jul 13, 2026). _Takeaway:_ Narayanan's ICML 2026 keynote argues that even as AI automates more tasks, human work does not evaporate but shifts up the stack to judgment, specification, and verification — an "AI as normal technology" rebuttal to both automation doom and acceleration hype. [https://www.normaltech.ai/p/what-will-be-left-for-us-to-work](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/what-will-be-left-for-us-to-work)

**datasette code-frequency chart on GitHub** — Simon Willison (Jul 13, 2026). _Takeaway:_ Willison uses his own Datasette repo's GitHub code-frequency chart as a concrete, personal data point on how much coding agents and Opus 4.5-class models have changed his output volume — a rare quantified look at one practitioner's productivity shift. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/13/datasette-code-frequency/](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/13/datasette-code-frequency/)

**Do Automated Evals Work?** — Hamel Husain, Parlance Labs (Jul 11, 2026). _Takeaway:_ A benchmark of automated eval systems against 100 human-annotated traces finds naive automation (what many frameworks ship) tends to fail; automated evaluators only pay off after manual error analysis, aimed at failures that persist once prompts are fixed — and a 100% pass rate is a red flag that the evals are too easy. [https://parlance-labs.com/blog/posts/auto-evals/](https://parlance-labs.com/blog/posts/auto-evals/)

**How GPT-5.6 Changes Knowledge Work** — Dan Shipper, Every / Chain of Thought (Jul 10, 2026). _Takeaway:_ Shipper walks through how GPT-5.6 shifts knowledge work from doing tasks toward orchestrating and reviewing model output, with hands-on examples of where the newest frontier model changes day-to-day workflows for writers, analysts, and builders. [https://every.to/chain-of-thought/how-gpt-5-6-changes-knowledge-work](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/how-gpt-5-6-changes-knowledge-work)

**Harness Engineering for Self-Improvement** — Lilian Weng (Jul 4, 2026). _Takeaway:_ Weng's long-form essay reframes recursive self-improvement (RSI) around "harness engineering" — the tools, memory, and evaluation scaffolding wrapped around a model that actually enables it to improve — tracing the lineage from I. J. Good and Yudkowsky to today's agent harnesses. [https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/)
