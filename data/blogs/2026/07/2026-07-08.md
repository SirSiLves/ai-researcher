## Long-form picks

- **Harness Engineering for Self-Improvement** — Lilian Weng, Lil'Log (2026-07-04). _Takeaway:_ Argues that recursive self-improvement (RSI) is coming but won't begin with a model rewriting its own weights — it begins with the *harness*: the software wrapped around a base model that decides how it thinks, which tools it calls, what it remembers, and how its output gets judged. Organizes recent work on auto-research pipelines, self-improving agents, and evolutionary program search, and extends "agent framework" into a fuller discipline spanning workflow/loop engineering, evaluation, permission controls, and persistent-state management — i.e., closer to runtime and software-systems design than to prompting. A careful, reference-dense survey (I. J. Good 1965 → Yudkowsky 2008 → today's frontier-lab R&D acceleration) rather than a hot take, and a useful conceptual frame for the "agents that improve agents" debate. [link](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/)

- **The Pentagon Designates Anthropic as a Supply Chain Risk** — James Goodwin & Arvind Salem, Lawfare (2026-07-07). _Takeaway:_ Uses the DoD's designation of Anthropic's top models as a "supply chain risk" — which bars Pentagon contractors from working with the company — to make a broader governance argument: the fight is a symptom of how dependent both government and the private sector have become on a tiny handful of frontier-AI vendors. Frames AI procurement as compounding two long-running erosions of civil-service authority — privatization and the political leverage of large private vendors — moving decision-making further from publicly accountable civil servants. On-slice AI-governance analysis, distinct from the export-controls and federalism essays logged in prior daily files. [link](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/lawfare-daily--the-pentagon-designates-anthropic-as-a-supply-chain-risk)

### ai-agents
- **[The Architecture of Agency: A Deep Technical Guide to Agentic AI Systems in 2026](https://medium.com/@nraman.n6/the-architecture-of-agency-a-deep-technical-guide-to-agentic-ai-systems-in-2026-9df63b37f6df)** — NJ Raman (Apr 2026). Genuine engineering deep-dive rather than a survey: walks the reasoning loops, memory architectures, tool-calling protocols, and orchestration patterns behind production agents in 2026, and — more usefully — is candid about the failure modes and the still-unsolved problems that remain after two years of heavy investment, arguing multi-agent systems have become a first-class design pattern.

### ai-engineering
- **[Production LLM Systems: What I Learned Building Real-World AI Pipelines](https://jothsna.medium.com/production-llm-systems-what-i-learned-building-real-world-ai-pipelines-7d556c95e1a0)** — Jothsna Chowdary (Apr 2026). Hands-on retrospective, not a roadmap: makes the case that production LLM work is "20% model, 80% engineering," with the hard part being everything around the model — data pipelines, evaluation, serving infra, monitoring, cost management, and the accumulation of small decisions that decide whether a system holds up in production.
