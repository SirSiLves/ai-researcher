# AI Blogs — 2026-05-21

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **[Google I/O, Gemini Spark, Antigravity](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/20/google-io/)** — Simon Willison (May 20). _Takeaway:_ The closest-read I/O post-mortem this cycle. Antigravity ships as four things at once — desktop app, Go-written CLI agent, "Antigravity SDK" (open-source Python wrapper around a bundled closed-source Go binary), and the original VS Code-fork IDE. The under-reported headline is that the Apache-2.0 Gemini CLI is being deprecated on June 18 in favor of the closed-source Antigravity CLI; the open-source piece in Google's developer surface is being quietly retired. His prompt-injection probe of Gemini Spark surfaces that "fully managed, secure runtime on Google Cloud" is doing all the load-bearing work — there's no model-level mitigation story yet.

- **[A quote from SpaceX S-1](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/20/spacex-s1/)** — Simon Willison (May 20). _Takeaway:_ Pulls the load-bearing paragraph from the SpaceX S-1: in May 2026 SpaceX entered Cloud Services Agreements with Anthropic for COLOSSUS / COLOSSUS II capacity at $1.25B/month through May 2029 (~$40B total). Willison's added beat: deciding whether an Anthropic deployment "harms humanity" now passes through infrastructure ultimately controlled by Elon — "sounds like a new form of supply chain risk for Anthropic to me." The cleanest one-liner on what the SpaceX/Anthropic deal actually means once it's inside an S-1.

- **[Gemini 3.5 Flash: more expensive, but Google plan to use it for everything](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/19/gemini-35-flash/)** — Simon Willison (May 19). _Takeaway:_ Gemini 3.5 Flash ships straight to GA (no preview), gets wired into the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, and Vertex / AI Studio — and triples in price (from $0.50/$3 to $1.50/$9 per M in/out, 6× Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite). Across all three frontier labs the pattern is now unambiguous: with cheap-tier API growth saturating, providers are probing API price tolerance while reserving the most aggressive deployments for first-party consumer surfaces. Pair with his May 19 PyCon recap for the full six-month arc.

- **[The last six months in LLMs in five minutes](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/19/5-minute-llms/)** — Simon Willison (May 19). _Takeaway:_ Annotated slides from his PyCon US 2026 lightning talk. The shape of the period: a "November 2025 inflection point" where the supposedly best model changed hands five times between Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google in six months; coding agents got genuinely good; and laptop-available models (Qwen3.6, Gemma 4, etc.) started wildly outperforming expectations. The pelican-on-a-bicycle SVG benchmark continues to function as the most honest no-train-leak vibes check available. Still the single best 5-minute orientation for anyone catching up.

- **[Google I/O, World Models, I/O Spaghetti](https://stratechery.com/2026/google-i-o-world-models-i-o-spaghetti/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 20). _Takeaway:_ Thompson's I/O essay. Two distinct stories collide on the same stage: (1) the strategic story — Gemini 3.5 Flash + Omni world model + Spark + Antigravity reads as "AI everywhere" maturing into a coherent platform play, and (2) the organizational story — whether DeepMind is actually aligned with Google's business objectives, or whether the I/O sprawl reflects unresolved internal coordination cost. The "I/O Spaghetti" frame is doing real analytical work: more SKUs and more surfaces don't automatically equal more strategy.

- **[Shifting Alliances in a Changing World](https://stratechery.com/2026/shifting-alliances-in-a-changing-world/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 18 weekly wrap, still the structural read of the week). _Takeaway:_ Thompson's synthesis: the SpaceX/Anthropic compute deal, OpenAI's "Deployment Company" reorg, and Apple-Intel rapprochement all read as the same story — a structural shift from "answer inference" (human-in-the-loop, latency-sensitive, ~chat tokens) to "agentic inference" (humans absent, throughput-dominant, multi-hour traces) is reshuffling who needs whose chips, datacenters, and distribution. The clearest one-paragraph statement of why every frontier-lab capex story this spring rhymes.

- **[Import AI 456: RSI and economic growth; radical optionality for AI regulation; and a neural computer](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-456-rsi-and-economic-growth)** — Jack Clark (May 11, the freshest Import AI). _Takeaway:_ Three load-bearing pieces in one issue. (1) An economic-modeling result from Forethought + Columbia + UVA: even modest RSI — ~13% automation across sectors, or 20% automation of hardware R&D alone — could push the economy into a "singularity" growth regime within ~six years. (2) "Radical Optionality" — a policy framework from the Institute for Law & AI urging governments to build regulatory infrastructure now, while the option still exists. (3) Decoupled DiLoCo from Google DeepMind: distributed training across geographically separated clusters as the practical complement to RSI economics. Reads as the pendant to Import AI 455 — Clark is now drawing the macroeconomic and policy implications, not just the engineering ones.

- **[Notes from inside China's AI labs](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/notes-from-inside-chinas-ai-labs)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 7, still the load-bearing China essay of the month). _Takeaway:_ Trip report from the Hangzhou→Shanghai axis. Lambert's structural read: the open-weights cadence isn't "China catching up" — it's a different equilibrium where labs publish-then-build, releases are cultural signaling as much as product, and the talent pool routes through Alibaba/DeepSeek/Moonshot/Qwen rather than US-style poaching. Pairs with his May 12 "How open model ecosystems compound" and May 4 "The distillation panic" — together they're the freshest, most-cited Lambert essays right now.

- **[How open model ecosystems compound](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/how-open-model-ecosystems-compound)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 12, paid). _Takeaway:_ The bookend to the China trip report. Lambert formalises the compounding mechanism — shared eval infra, shared post-training recipes, shared distillation targets — that means open-weights releases aren't independent draws but each one lifts the next. Builds the structural case that closed-vs-open isn't a horse race; they're different equilibria with different compounding curves. Read alongside the Forethought RSI piece in Import AI 456 for the two macro stories converging.

- **[AI Agents Must Follow the Law](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-agents-must-follow-the-law)** — Lawfare (mid-May, the foundational agent-governance essay anchoring the May Lawfare AI track). _Takeaway:_ Argues that for agents granted access to government or enterprise workflows, the deployer needs a mechanism to verify the agent will obey law _even when instructed not to_ — tort liability and fines after the fact don't scale to agentic deployments. Frames Law-Following AI (LFAI) as a deployment precondition, not an alignment afterthought. The piece the EU AI Act omnibus and the US oversight conversation are now both circling.

- **[How to Work and Compound with AI](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/)** — Eugene Yan (early May, still the most-shared eugeneyan.com piece in circulation). _Takeaway:_ Five principles for getting AI work to compound rather than reset: (1) provide good context, (2) encode your taste as config, (3) make verification easy, (4) delegate bigger tasks, (5) close the loop. The "encode taste as config" principle is the underrated one — it's the practical operationalization of the context-engineering thesis, and the bridge between one-shot prompting and durable agentic workflows.

- **[The Impact of AI on Software Engineers in 2026: Key Trends](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineers-2026)** — Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer. _Takeaway:_ Survey of 906 engineers and engineering leaders (median 11–15 years experience): 95% use AI tools weekly, agents now used by 55% of developers, Claude Code went from zero to most-used tool in 8 months. Employers pay for more AI coding tools than individuals do; "max" plans (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex) run ~$100–200/month per engineer. The "Shippers" cohort benefits most but adds tech debt fastest; less-skilled engineers uplevel via AI but generate slop. The cleanest empirical snapshot of where AI-augmented software work actually sits going into mid-2026.

- **[Recent Developments in LLM Architectures: KV Sharing, mHC, and Compressed Attention](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/)** — Sebastian Raschka, Ahead of AI (May 16). _Takeaway:_ The freshest Raschka architecture roundup, tracking how new open-weight LLMs from Gemma 4 to DeepSeek V4 are reducing long-context costs via KV sharing, multi-head compression (mHC), and compressed attention variants. The technical companion to Lambert's "How open model ecosystems compound" — Raschka shows the actual mechanism by which open releases are compounding on each other's architectural choices.

- **[Inside the AI Index: 12 Takeaways from the 2026 Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/inside-the-ai-index-12-takeaways-from-the-2026-report)** — Stanford HAI (still the canonical AI Index 2026 summary post the May 20 webinar). _Takeaway:_ The numbers being quoted everywhere this month. Global corporate AI investment $581.7B in 2025 (+130% YoY). GenAI hit 53% global population adoption in three years (faster than the PC or the internet). U.S. consumer surplus from GenAI ~$172B/yr by early 2026, median value per user tripled YoY. AI datacenter power capacity now 29.6 GW (≈ NY State at peak, or Switzerland/Austria national consumption). Awkward findings: Foundation Model Transparency Index *dropped* from 58 to 40; only 23% of the U.S. public expects AI to positively affect their work vs 73% of AI experts; AI incident database recorded 362 incidents in 2025 (up from 233 in 2024).

- **[Everything we shipped at Interrupt 2026](https://www.langchain.com/blog/interrupt-2026-overview)** — LangChain (May 14 conference recap, still the most-cited agent-platform debrief this month). _Takeaway:_ The most concrete "agents in production" debrief of the month. Four shipped products: LangSmith Engine (auto-clusters production failures and opens PRs with fixes), SmithDB (purpose-built trace DB, 15× faster on core LangSmith workloads), Managed Deep Agents (hosted runtime for the open-source Deep Agents harness — planning, subagent delegation, file I/O, long-running threads), and Sandboxes GA. LangChain Labs is a new applied-research effort on continual learning from agent traces.

### prompt-engineering
- **[Context Engineering Has Eaten Prompt Engineering](https://medium.com/@codewithrashid/context-engineering-has-eaten-prompt-engineerin-eb3a869c0362)** — Rashid Mahmood (May 2026). The load-bearing claim — 65% of developers cite missing or rotted context as the leading cause of poor AI code quality, ahead of model capability and framework choice — is the empirical case for treating context as infrastructure rather than a prompt file.
- **[Prompt Engineering Is Dead: Why Context Engineering Is the Only Skill That Matters in 2026](https://rustcodeweb.medium.com/prompt-engineering-is-dead-why-context-engineering-is-the-only-skill-that-matters-in-2026-cdb1fe0b349b)** — Rustcodeweb (May 2026). ### rag / retrieval-augmented-generation
- **[Most Companies Doing GenAI Are Really Just Doing RAG: RAGOps Explained for Analysts](https://medium.com/@squalliahmed/most-companies-doing-genai-are-really-just-doing-rag-ragops-explained-for-analysts-31e0933ac70f)** — Ahmed Squalli (May 2026). The taxonomy other 2026 RAG posts are now converging on.
- **[RAG is DEAD! The contrarian frame: classical retriever-generator RAG is dead, what's killed it is agentic RAG — specialized agents handling query decomposition, retrieval, validation, and synthesis in parallel. Reads cleanly against Squalli's RAGOps frame — both authors actually agree on the destination, they're just naming the transition differently.

### vector-database
- **[Do You Still Need a Vector Database in 2026?](https://medium.com/data-science-collective/vector-databases-are-dying-heres-the-production-evidence-8c17b54687e2)** — Data Science Collective (May 2026). The freshest production-evidence post on the tag: examines every named-company deployment, hard metric, and migration failure story across Pinecone, Qdrant, Milvus, Weaviate, and pgvector. Conclusion converges with the Rupareliya guide — for most teams, pgvector on Postgres comfortably handles up to 50M vectors and integrates with existing infra; specialised vector DBs are an upgrade path, not a starting point.

### model-context-protocol / mcp
- **[The Model Context Protocol Crisis: What 30 CVEs Teach Us About Building Secure AI Agents](https://medium.com/@khayyam.h/the-model-context-protocol-crisis-what-30-cves-teach-us-about-building-secure-ai-agents-95e16497d249)** — Khayyam H. (May 2026). Anatomy of the 30+ MCP CVEs filed Jan–Feb 2026: 43% exec/shell injection, 20% client/proxy infra, 13% auth bypass, plus a CVSS 9.6 RCE in a package with 437K downloads. Root cause: MCP was designed for functionality, not security — auth optional, encryption not required, no standard tool-level access control. Required reading for anyone deploying MCP servers.
- **[Model Context Protocol (MCP): Evolution, Capabilities, and the Rise of Peta](https://bytebridge.medium.com/model-context-protocol-mcp-evolution-capabilities-and-the-rise-of-peta-ff2967b45d48)** — ByteBridge (May 2026). The 2026 MCP state-of-the-protocol piece — covers the Linux Foundation Agentic AI Foundation governance handoff (December 2025), the move toward stateless/distributed transports for horizontal scaling, and the 10,000+ active public MCP servers count. The "where MCP actually is mid-2026" reference.

### agent-protocols
- **[A2A, MCP, AG-UI, A2UI: The Essential 2026 AI Agent Protocol Stack](https://medium.com/@visrow/a2a-mcp-ag-ui-a2ui-the-essential-2026-ai-agent-protocol-stack-ee0e65a672ef)** — Vishal Mysore (May 2026). The frame the rest of the agent-protocols tag is converging on.
- **[MCP and A2A: The Protocols Building the AI Agent Internet](https://medium.com/@aftab001x/mcp-and-a2a-the-protocols-building-the-ai-agent-internet-bc807181e68a)** — Aftab (Feb 2026, still the most-linked primer on the tag). The "MCP is vertical, A2A is horizontal" decomposition — MCP for tool access, A2A for agent orchestration — has held up as the durable mental model for the protocol layer.

- **[Why AI Governance Is Becoming the Most Important Enterprise Skill in 2026](https://medium.com/data-science-collective/why-ai-governance-is-becoming-the-most-important-enterprise-skill-in-2026-1bfdfc59cfdf)** — Manish Shivanandhan, Data Science Collective (May 2026). The skills-and-careers frame on the discipline that just stopped being a side project — companies are using AI faster than they know how to govern it, and the role gap is widening.
- **[The Decision Continuity Gap: Why Your AI Governance Has No Memory](https://medium.com/@drrarkimmfields/the-decision-continuity-gap-why-your-ai-governance-has-no-memory-1dc3f9630633)** — Dr. Rarkimm Fields (May 2026). The operational gap behind the EU AI Act omnibus documentation demands now landing on May 7.
