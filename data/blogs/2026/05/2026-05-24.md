# AI Blogs — 2026-05-24

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **[Building LangGraph: Designing an Agent Runtime from first principles](https://www.langchain.com/blog/building-langgraph)** — Christian Bromann & Nick Hollon, LangChain (May 21). _Takeaway:_ The freshest long-form practitioner piece on the blog list. Walks through how LangGraph implements the load-bearing primitives of a production agent runtime — parallelisation, streaming, checkpointing, human-in-the-loop, tracing, task queue — from first principles rather than as bolt-ons. Pairs with the May 20 "Agentic Engineering" swarms piece as the runtime/control-plane companion to Anthropic's Managed Agents and Deep Agents v0.6 narrative: the harness, not the model, is where the differentiation lives, and the harness has its own architecture that's now being formalised.

- **[Agentic Engineering: How Swarms of AI Agents Are Redefining Software Engineering](https://www.langchain.com/blog/agentic-engineering-redefining-software-engineering)** — LangChain (May 20, still the freshest "control plane" piece). _Takeaway:_ Drops the strongest production datapoints in circulation this week: in a pilot of 20+ debugging workflows, coordinated agent execution produced a 93% reduction in time-to-root-cause vs historical baselines, saving 200+ engineering hours across 512 sessions in a single month; development workflows showed 65% reduction in execution time, with the biggest gains in compressing downstream testing — not code generation. The framing that lands: agentic engineering isn't a better coding agent; it's a control plane orchestrating cross-team workflows with long-term memory and traceability across the full SDLC. Reference architecture is LangGraph + LangSmith + the new Engine.

- **[On the &lt;dl&gt;](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/23/on-the-dl/)** — Simon Willison (May 23, the freshest Willison post). _Takeaway:_ A small but characteristic Willison link-post about the HTML `<dl>` element after reading Ben Meyer's piece — useful as the Sunday counterpoint to the heavyweight agent-runtime content elsewhere on this list: Willison continues to operate at the layer of "what should the primitives actually look like" rather than the vendor announcement cycle. Pairs with his May 21 Datasette Agent post as evidence the same engineer is shipping both the agent platform and the primitive-level scrutiny.

- **[New Approach to Scaling Laws Could Change How AI Models Are Trained](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/new-approach-to-scaling-laws-could-change-how-ai-models-are-trained)** — Stanford HAI (May 21). _Takeaway:_ Researchers leveraging statistical concepts from measurement science and education have greatly reduced the computational demand of predicting how the largest LLMs scale up. Lands in the same week as Anthropic's $1.25B/month SpaceX compute commitment: if the cost of _predicting_ scaling outcomes drops by orders of magnitude, the marginal experiment economics change, even as the absolute capex grows. The empirical companion to Lambert's "open ecosystems compound" thesis — same compounding logic now reaching the prediction layer.

- **[AI Agents Must Follow the Law](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-agents-must-follow-the-law)** — Lawfare (May 15, still the freshest LFAI piece on the topic page). _Takeaway:_ Names the conceptual problem under "Law-Following AI": AI agents shouldn't follow literally every law a human is bound by — that would be over-burdensome and impracticable — but should be bound to a subset designed to safeguard the legal order and citizens' most basic interests. The constitutional companion to the "AI Regulation Knife Fight" / "Advice and Consent" pair from earlier in the week: those name the political fight and the institutional rail; this names the design-space the rail has to fit into.

- **[Mythos Fallout, U.S. Government Weighs AI Model Regulation](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/mythos-fallout--u.s.-government-weighs-ai-model-regulation)** — Lawfare (May 8, still load-bearing). _Takeaway:_ The Trump administration is openly considering stricter oversight of US AI models specifically because of their cybersecurity impact — the structural prior under which Opus 4.7's automated detection-and-blocking of prohibited cyber requests reads as preemptive de-risking. Pairs with "AI Agents Must Follow the Law" as the policy / model-controls / agent-controls triple.

- **[Continual learning for AI agents](https://www.langchain.com/blog/continual-learning-for-ai-agents)** — Harrison Chase, LangChain (May 5, still the freshest framework piece). _Takeaway:_ Learning in AI agents happens at three distinct layers — model weights, harness (code + instructions + tools that are always present), and context (instructions/skills that live outside the harness and configure it). The framing that the model layer is one of three and arguably not the most important is the formal version of the slogan "Agent = Model + Harness" from Vishal Mysore — and the conceptual ground under both LangChain Labs and SmithDB / LangSmith Engine.

- **[Import AI 457: AI stuxnet; cursed Muon optimizer; and positive alignment](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-457-ai-stuxnet-cursed-muon)** — Jack Clark (May 18, still the freshest Import AI). _Takeaway:_ Three load-bearing pieces. (1) "AI stuxnet" — speculative argument that a superintelligence might enforce AI non-proliferation the way nuclear states do: through subtle, hard-to-find bugs (the "fast16" scenario) that degrade an actor's capacity for certain kinds of science. (2) Tilde Research showing Muon optimizer inherits row-norm anisotropy on tall matrices, permanently killing a significant fraction of MLP neurons — a real, current bug damaging model quality across multiple recent training runs. (3) "Positive alignment" as a frame complement to refusal-style alignment. The engineering-and-threat-model layer of the week.

- **[Shifting Alliances in a Changing World](https://stratechery.com/2026/shifting-alliances-in-a-changing-world/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 18 weekly wrap, still the structural read). _Takeaway:_ Thompson's synthesis: SpaceX/Anthropic compute deal, OpenAI's "Deployment Company" reorg, and Apple-Intel rapprochement read as the same story — a structural shift from "answer inference" (human-in-loop, latency-sensitive, ~chat tokens) to "agentic inference" (humans absent, throughput-dominant, multi-hour traces) is reshuffling who needs whose chips, datacenters, and distribution. The clearest one-paragraph statement of why every frontier-lab capex story this spring rhymes.

- **[How open model ecosystems compound](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/how-open-model-ecosystems-compound)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 12, still the structural China/open piece). _Takeaway:_ Formalises the compounding mechanism — shared eval infra, shared post-training recipes, shared distillation targets — that means open-weights releases aren't independent draws but each one lifts the next. The 80%-of-compute-is-R&D claim is the load-bearing data point: in an open ecosystem like China's, you amortise the expensive part across labs. Builds the structural case that closed-vs-open isn't a horse race; they're different equilibria with different compounding curves.

- **[How to Work and Compound with AI](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/)** — Eugene Yan (May 3, still the most-shared individual-practitioner piece). _Takeaway:_ Five principles for getting AI work to compound rather than reset: (1) provide good context, (2) encode your taste as config, (3) make verification easy, (4) delegate bigger tasks, (5) close the loop. "Encode taste as config" remains the underrated one — the practical operationalisation of the context-engineering thesis, and the bridge between one-shot prompting and durable agentic workflows.

- **[Recent Developments in LLM Architectures: KV Sharing, mHC, and Compressed Attention](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/recent-developments-in-llm-architectures)** — Sebastian Raschka, Ahead of AI (May 16, still the freshest deep architecture piece). _Takeaway:_ The 2026 architecture story is no longer "more parameters" but long-context efficiency: KV-cache size, memory traffic, and attention cost are now the binding constraints as reasoning models and agent workflows keep more tokens around. Walks through KV sharing and per-layer embeddings in Gemma 4, layer-wise attention budgeting in Laguna XS.2, compressed convolutional attention in ZAYA1-8B, and mHC plus compressed attention in DeepSeek V4. The technical companion to the "agents need cheap long context" narrative driving everything from Datasette Agent to Claude Managed Agents.

### rag / retrieval-augmented-generation
- **[RAG in 2026: Architecture Shifts, Emerging Patterns, and What It Means for Java Developers](https://medium.com/@elammarisoufiane/rag-in-2026-architecture-shifts-emerging-patterns-and-what-it-means-for-java-developers-6f2803e39787)** — Soufiane Elammari (May 2026). Two structural claims worth the read: classical retriever-generator RAG is dead, replaced by Modular RAG (each pipeline component independently replaceable) and Agentic RAG (the LLM decides whether/what/when to retrieve). And: the "million-token context windows will kill RAG" prediction has _not_ materialised in production, because filling a 1M-token context per call costs orders of magnitude more than a 10K context with retrieval. The clearest 2026 status snapshot on the discipline.
- **[Building Your Own RAG: A Step-by-Step Guide Using LangChain and a Vector Database](https://medium.com/codetodeploy/building-your-own-rag-a-step-by-step-guide-using-langchain-and-a-vector-database-21d0566d3c51)** — Siddhartha Pramanik, CodeToDeploy (May 3, 2026). The freshest step-by-step practitioner build on the tag — covers chunking, embedding, retrieval, and reranking against current model APIs, with the load-bearing claim that "the quality of your RAG system lives and dies by how you chunk your documents."

### vector-database
- **[Top 15 vector databases in 2026: A production decision guide from 100+ enterprise deployments](https://medium.com/@pratik-rupareliya/top-15-vector-databases-in-2026-a-production-decision-guide-from-100-enterprise-deployments-dd58a04f51a5)** — Pratik Rupareliya (May 2026). Empirical companion to Elammari's "Modular RAG" architectural piece.
