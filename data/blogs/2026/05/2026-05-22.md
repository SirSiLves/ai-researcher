# AI Blogs — 2026-05-22

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **[Datasette Agent](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/21/datasette-agent/)** — Simon Willison (May 21). _Takeaway:_ Willison ships the first release of Datasette Agent, the project where his three-year-old `LLM` Python library and Datasette finally converge into a single conversational agent over SQLite. The under-the-radar engineering content: the live demo runs on Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite ("cheap, fast and has no trouble writing SQLite queries"), the agent is extensible via plugins (charts via Observable Plot, image generation via ChatGPT Images 2.0, code execution in a Fly Sprites sandbox), and a `uv` one-liner runs the whole stack against `gemma-4-26b-a4b` in LM Studio. The takeaway buried in the post is the practitioner claim that "open weight models released in the past six months are increasingly able to handle" reliable tool calls plus SQL — i.e. the local-models-can-do-real-agent-work line, this time with a working demo behind it. The cleanest single-developer "what an agent actually looks like in 2026" post this week.

- **[AI progress creates more work for humans, not less](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/ai-progress-creates-more-work-for-humans-not-less)** — Dan Shipper, Chain of Thought / Every (May 21). _Takeaway:_ The structural argument the labour-displacement debate has been missing. Shipper formalises what he calls the "expert paradox": as AI cheapens yesterday's competence, the volume of generic output explodes, and expert differentiation becomes _more_ valuable — only experts can turn AI-generated drafts into production-ready work, or build the systems that absorb the new volume. Lived datapoint as the load-bearing evidence: Every has automated every workflow it can with agents, and has still grown from 4 to 30 human employees since GPT-3. Reads as the practitioner counter-narrative to the Pragmatic Engineer "Shippers add tech debt fastest" finding — same phenomenon, different framing of who benefits.

- **[Advice and Consent for Major Governmental AI Deployments](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/advice-and-consent-for-major-governmental-ai-deployments)** — Cullen O'Keefe, Lawfare (May 20). _Takeaway:_ The argument that before the executive branch deploys advanced AI in its most abuse-prone functions, it should require congressional approval — applying the constitutional advice-and-consent model to AI deployment, not just appointments. Builds the institutional rails the May Law-Following AI essay was missing: LFAI tells you the agent must obey law, advice-and-consent tells you who gets to authorise the deployment in the first place. The cleanest US-side companion to the EU Digital Omnibus governance frame.

- **[New in Deep Agents v0.6](https://www.langchain.com/blog/deep-agents-0-6)** — Sydney Runkle, LangChain (May 13, still the freshest Deep Agents release post-Interrupt). _Takeaway:_ The release that turns the Interrupt 2026 keynote into shippable infrastructure. v0.6 is structured around four performance axes — model layer, agent layer, scale, and over-time durability — and is the concrete answer to "what does 'managed Deep Agents' actually mean?" Sits beneath Hunter Lovell's May 20 follow-up on Deep Agents patterns; together they're the agent-platform technical reading for the week.

- **[The last six months in LLMs in five minutes](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/19/5-minute-llms/)** — Simon Willison (May 19, still the canonical orientation piece). _Takeaway:_ Annotated slides from his PyCon US 2026 lightning talk. The shape of the period: a "November 2025 inflection point" where the supposedly best model changed hands five times between Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google in six months; coding agents got genuinely good; laptop-available models (Qwen3.6, Gemma 4, etc.) started wildly outperforming expectations. The pelican-on-a-bicycle SVG benchmark continues to function as the most honest no-train-leak vibes check available. Still the single best 5-minute orientation for anyone catching up.

- **[Gemini 3.5 Flash: more expensive, but Google plan to use it for everything](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/19/gemini-35-flash/)** — Simon Willison (May 19). _Takeaway:_ Gemini 3.5 Flash ships straight to GA (no preview), gets wired into the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, and Vertex / AI Studio — and triples in price (from $0.50/$3 to $1.50/$9 per M in/out, 6× Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite). Across all three frontier labs the pattern is now unambiguous: with cheap-tier API growth saturating, providers are probing API price tolerance while reserving the most aggressive deployments for first-party consumer surfaces.

- **[Shifting Alliances in a Changing World](https://stratechery.com/2026/shifting-alliances-in-a-changing-world/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 18 weekly wrap, still the structural read of the week). _Takeaway:_ Thompson's synthesis: the SpaceX/Anthropic compute deal, OpenAI's "Deployment Company" reorg, and Apple-Intel rapprochement all read as the same story — a structural shift from "answer inference" (human-in-the-loop, latency-sensitive, ~chat tokens) to "agentic inference" (humans absent, throughput-dominant, multi-hour traces) is reshuffling who needs whose chips, datacenters, and distribution. The clearest one-paragraph statement of why every frontier-lab capex story this spring rhymes.

- **[Import AI 457: AI stuxnet; cursed Muon optimizer; and positive alignment](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-457-ai-stuxnet-cursed-muon)** — Jack Clark (May 18, freshest Import AI). _Takeaway:_ Three load-bearing pieces. (1) "AI stuxnet" — a speculative argument that a superintelligence might enforce AI non-proliferation the way nuclear states enforce nuclear non-proliferation: through subtle, hard-to-find bugs (the "fast16" scenario) that degrade an actor's capacity for certain kinds of science. (2) Tilde Research showing the Muon optimizer inherits row-norm anisotropy on tall matrices, permanently killing a significant fraction of MLP neurons — a real, current bug damaging model quality across multiple recent training runs. (3) "Positive alignment" as a frame complement to refusal-style alignment. Reads as the pendant to 456: Clark turning from macroeconomics back to the engineering and threat-model layer.

- **[How open model ecosystems compound](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/how-open-model-ecosystems-compound)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 12, paid, still the structural China/open piece). _Takeaway:_ Lambert formalises the compounding mechanism — shared eval infra, shared post-training recipes, shared distillation targets — that means open-weights releases aren't independent draws but each one lifts the next. The 80%-of-compute-is-R&D claim is the load-bearing data point: in an open ecosystem like China's, you amortise the expensive part across labs. Builds the structural case that closed-vs-open isn't a horse race; they're different equilibria with different compounding curves.

- **[AI Value Capture — The Shift To Model Labs](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/ai-value-capture-the-shift-to-model)** — SemiAnalysis (mid-May, the headline-number piece this cycle). _Takeaway:_ The freshest SemiAnalysis story changes the picture: AI labs are now capturing _all_ the value, up from almost none a year ago. Anthropic ARR went from $9B to $44B+, with inference gross margins expanding from 38% to over 70% — and margin expansion happened despite Opus 4.5 cutting per-token prices 3× (from $15/$75 to $5/$25), thanks to Hopper→Blackwell, Trainium software gains, and per-token cost collapse. The empirical companion to Thompson's "inference shift" essay: token economics, not just deployment architecture, are doing the value-capture work.

- **[The Pulse: Forward deployed engineering heats up again](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-forward-deployed-engineering)** — Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer (May 19). _Takeaway:_ The mid-2026 ground-truth read on the labor market. Two notable signals: tech job losses rising while big-company engineers self-report 100% AI usage; vibe coding and agentic engineering are merging into a single discipline rather than competing styles. Pairs with his May 20 "AI's impact on software engineers Part 2" podcast — the empirical pendant to Shipper's expert-paradox argument: at the firm level, AI-augmented work is creating both layoffs and unmet demand for senior judgment.

- **[How to Work and Compound with AI](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/)** — Eugene Yan (May 3, still the most-shared individual-practitioner piece in circulation). _Takeaway:_ Five principles for getting AI work to compound rather than reset: (1) provide good context, (2) encode your taste as config, (3) make verification easy, (4) delegate bigger tasks, (5) close the loop. The "encode taste as config" principle is the underrated one — the practical operationalization of the context-engineering thesis, and the bridge between one-shot prompting and durable agentic workflows.

- **[Inside the AI Index: 12 Takeaways from the 2026 Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/inside-the-ai-index-12-takeaways-from-the-2026-report)** — Stanford HAI (still the canonical AI Index 2026 summary). _Takeaway:_ The numbers being quoted everywhere this month. Global corporate AI investment $581.7B in 2025 (+130% YoY). GenAI hit 53% global population adoption in three years (faster than the PC or the internet). U.S. consumer surplus from GenAI ~$172B/yr by early 2026. AI datacenter power capacity now 29.6 GW (≈ NY State at peak). Foundation Model Transparency Index *dropped* from 58 to 40; only 23% of the U.S. public expects AI to positively affect their work vs 73% of AI experts; AI incident database recorded 362 incidents in 2025 (up from 233 in 2024).

- **[Everything we shipped at Interrupt 2026](https://www.langchain.com/blog/interrupt-2026-overview)** — LangChain (May 14 conference recap, still the load-bearing agent-platform debrief). _Takeaway:_ The most concrete "agents in production" debrief of the month. Four shipped products: LangSmith Engine (auto-clusters production failures and opens PRs with fixes), SmithDB (purpose-built trace DB, 15× faster on core LangSmith workloads), Managed Deep Agents (hosted runtime for the open-source Deep Agents harness — planning, subagent delegation, file I/O, long-running threads), and Sandboxes GA. LangChain Labs is a new applied-research effort on continual learning from agent traces.

### prompt-engineering
- **[Context Engineering Has Eaten Prompt Engineering](https://medium.com/@codewithrashid/context-engineering-has-eaten-prompt-engineerin-eb3a869c0362)** — Rashid Mahmood (May 2026). The load-bearing claim — 65% of developers cite missing or rotted context as the leading cause of poor AI code quality, ahead of model capability and framework choice — is the empirical case for treating context as infrastructure rather than a prompt file.
- **[Prompt Engineering Is Dead: Why Context Engineering Is the Only Skill That Matters in 2026](https://rustcodeweb.medium.com/prompt-engineering-is-dead-why-context-engineering-is-the-only-skill-that-matters-in-2026-cdb1fe0b349b)** — Rustcodeweb (May 2026). ### rag / retrieval-augmented-generation
- **[RAG in 2026: Architecture Shifts, Emerging Patterns, and What It Means for Java Developers](https://medium.com/@elammarisoufiane/rag-in-2026-architecture-shifts-emerging-patterns-and-what-it-means-for-java-developers-6f2803e39787)** — Soufiane Elammari (May 2026, freshest tagged piece). The two structural claims worth the read: classical retriever-generator RAG is dead, replaced by Modular RAG (each pipeline component independently replaceable) and Agentic RAG (the LLM decides whether/what/when to retrieve). And: the "million-token context windows will kill RAG" prediction has _not_ materialised in production, because filling a 1M-token context per call costs orders of magnitude more than a 10K context with retrieval. The clearest 2026 status snapshot on the discipline.
- **[Most Companies Doing GenAI Are Really Just Doing RAG: RAGOps Explained for Analysts](https://medium.com/@squalliahmed/most-companies-doing-genai-are-really-just-doing-rag-ragops-explained-for-analysts-31e0933ac70f)** — Ahmed Squalli (May 2026). The taxonomy other 2026 RAG posts are now converging on: ~80% of enterprise AI pilots are still RAG underneath, and RAGOps is the operational discipline — DevOps for retrieval pipelines, eval frameworks, and knowledge base lifecycle.

### model-context-protocol / mcp
- **[The Model Context Protocol Crisis: What 30 CVEs Teach Us About Building Secure AI Agents](https://medium.com/@khayyam.h/the-model-context-protocol-crisis-what-30-cves-teach-us-about-building-secure-ai-agents-95e16497d249)** — Khayyam H. (May 2026). Anatomy of the 30+ MCP CVEs filed Jan–Feb 2026: 43% exec/shell injection, 20% client/proxy infra, 13% auth bypass, plus a CVSS 9.6 RCE in a package with 437K downloads. Root cause: MCP was designed for functionality, not security — auth optional, encryption not required, no standard tool-level access control. Required reading for anyone deploying MCP servers.
- **[Model Context Protocol (MCP): Evolution, Capabilities, and the Rise of Peta](https://bytebridge.medium.com/model-context-protocol-mcp-evolution-capabilities-and-the-rise-of-peta-ff2967b45d48)** — ByteBridge (May 2026). The 2026 MCP state-of-the-protocol piece — covers the Linux Foundation Agentic AI Foundation governance handoff (December 2025), the move toward stateless/distributed transports for horizontal scaling, and the 10,000+ active public MCP servers count.

Pairs with the MCP CVEs post above as the two-tier security read on agent infra.
