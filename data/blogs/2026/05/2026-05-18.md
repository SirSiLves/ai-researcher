# AI Blogs — 2026-05-18

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **[Data Center Discontent, Understanding the Opposition, Fixing the Problem](https://stratechery.com/2026/data-center-discontent-understanding-the-opposition-fixing-the-problem/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 18). _Takeaway:_ Reframes datacenter NIMBY-ism as a real, legitimate externality story rather than an obstructionist one — the noise, water draw, grid load, and tax-abatement-without-jobs profile of a hyperscaler buildout is genuinely costly to host communities, and the only solution that scales is direct compensation to neighbors (not Pigovian taxes, not zoning fights). The companion datacenter-economics piece to last week's "Inference Shift" and the May 7 xAI/Anthropic Colossus deal coverage.

- **[Everything we shipped at Interrupt 2026](https://www.langchain.com/blog/interrupt-2026-overview)** — LangChain blog (May 13–14 conference recap). _Takeaway:_ The most concrete "agents in production" debrief of the month. Four shipped products: LangSmith Engine (auto-clusters production failures and opens PRs with fixes), SmithDB (purpose-built trace DB, 15x faster on core LangSmith workloads, P50 trace tree loads at 92ms), Managed Deep Agents (hosted runtime for the open-source Deep Agents harness — planning, subagent delegation, file I/O, long-running threads), and Sandboxes GA (hardware-virtualized microVMs with durable threads, checkpointing, and human-in-the-loop). LangChain Labs is a new applied-research effort on continual learning from agent traces with Harvey, NVIDIA, Prime Intellect, Fireworks, and Baseten.

- **[How open model ecosystems compound](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/how-open-model-ecosystems-compound)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 12). _Takeaway:_ Pairs Ai2's Olmo 3 cost breakdown with Epoch AI's frontier-lab study to argue that ~80% of frontier-model compute is spent on R&D (not the final pretraining run). Implication: in an ecosystem where everyone publishes openly — i.e. China — the *next* training run starts with the previous lab's R&D priced in. This is the structural argument behind the Chinese open-model release cadence: the cost curve is shared across labs, so they can build longer than outside observers expect.

- **[The Pulse: Did capacity shortages turn Anthropic hostile to devs?](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-did-capacity-shortages)** — Gergely Orosz, Pragmatic Engineer (still the canonical reference, anchored by the May 6 Dario Amodei quote: "We originally planned for 10x growth, and we've seen something more like 80x growth in revenue and usage"). _Takeaway:_ Ties "dumber Claude" complaints, Claude Code paid-account pulls, and the SpaceX capacity deal into one capacity-scarcity story — and notes the irony that Anthropic banned xAI developers from Claude in January, then in May rented Colossus capacity from xAI/SpaceX. Field-level context for the Anthropic conference announcements.

- **[Not so locked in any more](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/14/not-so-locked-in/)** — Simon Willison (May 14, still the freshest substantive essay). _Takeaway:_ Read against the backdrop of Bun's Zig→Rust migration: when AI-assisted programming makes large-scale language ports tractable, "we are locked into our language choice" stops being load-bearing for technical roadmaps. The lock-in is now the tooling and ecosystem, not the syntax — a small but consequential shift in how teams should weight technology bets.

- **[Inside Anthropic's 2026 Developer Conference](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/inside-anthropic-s-2026-developer-conference)** — Every / Chain of Thought (last updated May 16). _Takeaway:_ Single best post-conference debrief on Code w/ Claude. Headline isn't the SpaceX/Colossus capacity deal (everyone covered that) — it's the structural shift: doubled rate limits across Pro/Max, peak-hour throttles removed, API limits up ~17x on some tiers, plus Claude Managed Agents. Reads the announcements as Anthropic finally collapsing the dev-vs-capacity friction that fueled three months of complaints.

- **[Inside the AI Index: 12 Takeaways from the 2026 Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/inside-the-ai-index-12-takeaways-from-the-2026-report)** — Stanford HAI. _Takeaway:_ The numbers that will be quoted for the next 12 months. Global corporate AI investment $581.7B in 2025 (+130% YoY). GenAI hit 53% global population adoption in three years (faster than the PC or the internet). U.S. consumer surplus from GenAI ~$172B/yr by early 2026. Two awkward findings: the Foundation Model Transparency Index *dropped* from 58 to 40, and only 23% of the U.S. public expects AI to positively affect their work versus 73% of AI experts. New 2026-report data point: AI datacenter power capacity now 29.6 GW — roughly all of New York State at peak demand — and annual GPT-4o inference water use alone may exceed the drinking water needs of 1.2M people.

- **[AI Agents Must Follow the Law](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-agents-must-follow-the-law)** — Lawfare (May 15). _Takeaway:_ Before granting AI agents access to government or enterprise workflows, the deployer needs a mechanism to verify the agent will obey law *even when instructed not to* — tort liability and fines after the fact don't scale to agentic deployments. Frames law-following AI (LFAI) as a deployment precondition rather than an alignment afterthought, and lays out which laws agents must follow, with what rigor, and how to handle legal uncertainty. The agent-governance counterpart to this week's executive-branch AI federalism coverage.

- **[Import AI 455: Automating AI Research](https://jack-clark.net/2026/05/04/import-ai-455-automating-ai-research/)** — Jack Clark (May 4). _Takeaway:_ Anthropic's published numbers on automating AI engineering — Claude Opus 4 hit 2.9x mean speedup (May '25), Opus 4.5 16.5x (Nov '25), Opus 4.6 30x (Feb '26), Claude Mythos Preview 52x (Apr '26) — and OpenAI's stated goal of an "automated AI research intern by September 2026." On AI-optimization benchmarks the top systems hit 25–28% vs a human score of 51%. Clark's argument: AI engineering is being automated; what remains uncertain is how much of AI *research* collapses on the same curve.

- **[Import AI 457: AI stuxnet; cursed Muon optimizer; and positive alignment](https://jack-clark.net/2026/05/18/import-ai-457-ai-stuxnet-cursed-muon-optimizer-and-positive-alignment/)** — Jack Clark (May 18, today). _Takeaway:_ Three threads. (1) Researchers find fast16, a decades-old virus repurposed to target precision-engineering CAD software — the first credible "AI stuxnet" template, and a reminder that the supply-chain attack surface for AI-augmented engineering is older than the AI. (2) The Aurora optimizer fixes the dead-neuron pathology that has dogged Muon since adoption. (3) A new position paper argues for "positive alignment" — design targets framed as human flourishing rather than only refusal/safety — as a complementary research agenda to ASL-tier capability gating.

- **[Latest open artifacts #21: Gemma 4, DeepSeek V4, Kimi K2.6, MiMo 2.5, GLM-5.1, and CAISI's V4 assessment](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/latest-open-artifacts-21-open-model)** — Florian Brand & Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 16). _Takeaway:_ The companion data dump to last week's "open model ecosystems compound" essay. One window into how dense the May 2026 open-weights release cadence has become — Gemma 4, DeepSeek V4, Kimi K2.6, MiMo 2.5, and GLM-5.1 all shipped inside a few weeks, plus the U.S. Center for AI Standards & Innovation's V4 evaluation. The release tempo is itself the argument: open-model labs are now shipping faster than closed labs publish papers.

- **[Introducing Auto-Triage](https://cognition.ai/blog/auto-triage)** — Cognition (May 18, today). _Takeaway:_ Devin now monitors Sentry/Datadog/PagerDuty for bugs, alerts, and incidents; when a new report fires, it spins up an investigation, links related reports, and opens PRs autonomously. The first agent product to formally claim the on-call / triage seat rather than the IDE seat — a more honest test of agent autonomy than coding benchmarks, because incident response is bounded, observable, and immediately falsifiable.

- **[Proceedings of the 2025 Workshop on Law-Following AI](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/proceedings-of-the-2025-workshop-on-law-following-ai)** — Cullen O'Keefe, Christoph Winter, Matthijs Maas, Janna Tay; Lawfare (May 8). _Takeaway:_ Forty-plus interdisciplinary scholars on how to design agentic systems that *refuse illegal orders*. Threads: the limits of post-hoc liability for governmental AI agents; automated legal reasoning and evaluation; the risks of "perfect enforcement" via automated compliance; and the agent–principal relationship as a first-class design surface. The academic backbone for the May 15 "AI Agents Must Follow the Law" op-ed already in this list.

### model-context-protocol / mcp
- **[The Model Context Protocol Crisis: What 30 CVEs Teach Us About Building Secure AI Agents](https://medium.com/@khayyam.h/the-model-context-protocol-crisis-what-30-cves-teach-us-about-building-secure-ai-agents-95e16497d249)** — Khayyam H. Required reading for anyone deploying MCP servers in 2026.
