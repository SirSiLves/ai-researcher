# AI Blogs — 2026-05-20

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **[Gemini 3.5 Flash: more expensive, but Google plan to use it for everything](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/19/gemini-35-flash/)** — Simon Willison (May 19). _Takeaway:_ Notes on Google's I/O drop: Gemini 3.5 Flash ships straight to GA (no preview), gets routed into the Gemini app, AI Mode in Search, and Vertex/AI Studio — and triples in price (from $0.50/$3 to $1.50/$9 per M in/out), 6× the price of Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite. The pattern is now unambiguous across all three frontier labs: with cheap-tier API growth saturating, providers are probing API price tolerance while reserving the most aggressive deployments for first-party consumer surfaces. Pair with his May 19 PyCon recap for the full six-month arc.

- **[The last six months in LLMs in five minutes](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/19/5-minute-llms/)** — Simon Willison (May 19). _Takeaway:_ Annotated slides from his PyCon US 2026 lightning talk. The shape of the period: a "November 2025 inflection point" where the supposedly best model changed hands five times between Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google in six months; coding agents got genuinely good; and laptop-available models (Qwen3.6, Gemma 4, etc.) started wildly outperforming expectations. The pelican-on-a-bicycle SVG benchmark continues to function as the most honest no-train-leak vibes check available. The single best 5-minute orientation for anyone catching up.

- **[Shifting Alliances in a Changing World](https://stratechery.com/2026/shifting-alliances-in-a-changing-world/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 18 weekly wrap). _Takeaway:_ Thompson's synthesis of the week: the SpaceX/Anthropic compute deal, OpenAI's "Deployment Company" reorg, and Apple-Intel rapprochement all read as the same story — a structural shift from "answer inference" (human-in-the-loop, latency-sensitive, ~chat tokens) to "agentic inference" (humans absent, throughput-dominant, multi-hour traces) is reshuffling who needs whose chips, datacenters, and distribution. The clearest one-paragraph statement of why every frontier-lab capex story this spring rhymes.

- **[The Inference Shift](https://stratechery.com/2026/the-inference-shift/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May). _Takeaway:_ The companion long-form to "Shifting Alliances": defines the answer-vs-agentic inference split as the load-bearing distinction for the rest of the decade, then traces it through Cerebras's upsized IPO ($150–$160 from $115–$125, 30M shares from 28M), the Nvidia GTC product roadmap, and why hyperscaler buildouts are increasingly priced against agentic-trace tokens rather than chat tokens. Reads as the structural argument the SemiAnalysis CPU and HBM coverage has been hinting at all month.

- **[Import AI 455: Automating AI Research](https://jack-clark.net/2026/05/04/import-ai-455-automating-ai-research/)** — Jack Clark (still the freshest substantive Import AI issue this month, with #456+ now overdue). _Takeaway:_ Clark's "this is why I think takeoff is happening" essay. Evidence stack: MLE-Bench tracking 25–28% for Opus 4.6 / GPT 5.4 vs ~51% human; KernelBench code-optimization speedup curve (Opus 4: 2.9× in May 2025 → Opus 4.5: 16.5× Nov 2025 → Opus 4.6: 30× Feb 2026 → Mythos Preview: 52×); OpenAI's stated September-2026 goal of an "automated AI research intern"; and Anthropic publishing on automated alignment researchers. Clark explicitly does not expect this to land in 2026, but argues we'll see "model end-to-end trains its successor" within a year or two. The piece every Lawfare / AI-Index reader should hold next to the policy timelines.

- **[Notes from inside China's AI labs](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/notes-from-inside-chinas-ai-labs)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 7, the load-bearing China essay of the month). _Takeaway:_ Trip report from the Hangzhou→Shanghai axis. Lambert's structural read: the open-weights cadence isn't "China catching up" — it's a different equilibrium where labs publish-then-build, releases are cultural signaling as much as product, and the talent pool routes through Alibaba/DeepSeek/Moonshot/Qwen rather than US-style poaching. Pairs with his May 12 "How open model ecosystems compound" and May 4 "The distillation panic" — together they're the freshest, most-cited Lambert essays right now.

- **[The distillation panic](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/the-distillation-panic)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 4). _Takeaway:_ Why the alleged "Chinese labs are jailbreaking US APIs to distill" panic is mostly noise — the open-weights ecosystem doesn't need it. The signal: anxiety about distillation is a tell that the US closed-source bet is structurally worried about the open-weights compound, not the other way around. The bookend essay to "Notes from inside China's AI labs".

- **[AI Agents Must Follow the Law](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-agents-must-follow-the-law)** — Lawfare (mid-May, the foundational agent-governance essay anchoring the May Lawfare AI track). _Takeaway:_ Argues that for agents granted access to government or enterprise workflows, the deployer needs a mechanism to verify the agent will obey law _even when instructed not to_ — tort liability and fines after the fact don't scale to agentic deployments. Frames Law-Following AI (LFAI) as a deployment precondition, not an alignment afterthought. Required reading before any agent-pilot procurement decision this quarter.

- **[Mythos Fallout, U.S. Government Weighs AI Model Regulation](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/mythos-fallout--u.s.-government-weighs-ai-model-regulation)** — Lawfare (May 8, the freshest piece on US-side enforcement). _Takeaway:_ Tracks the NYT-reported White House plan to convene a tech-executive + government group to propose oversight procedures for new frontier model releases — including the option of a formal government review process pre-deployment. The Mythos-Preview safety findings are the on-the-record catalyst. Reads as the US-side pendant to the EU AI Office's August powers covered yesterday — both regimes are starting to ask the same question, with very different tooling.

- **[How to Work and Compound with AI](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/)** — Eugene Yan (early May). _Takeaway:_ Five principles for getting AI work to compound rather than reset: (1) provide good context, (2) encode your taste as config, (3) make verification easy, (4) delegate bigger tasks, (5) close the loop. The "encode taste as config" principle is the underrated one — it's the practical operationalization of the context-engineering thesis, and the bridge between one-shot prompting and durable agentic workflows. Still the most-shared eugeneyan.com piece in circulation.

- **[The Impact of AI on Software Engineers in 2026: Key Trends](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-impact-of-ai-on-software-engineers-2026)** — Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer. _Takeaway:_ Survey of 900+ engineers and engineering leaders. Headline findings: ~30% of respondents have hit AI-tool usage limits; most companies (not individuals) pay for AI coding tools; ~15% explicitly flagged tool cost as a concern; "Shippers" benefit most while "Builders" complain about AI-slop output from less-skilled colleagues; the role of engineering manager is becoming more hands-on, not less. The cleanest empirical snapshot of where AI-augmented software work actually sits going into mid-2026.

- **[Inside the AI Index: 12 Takeaways from the 2026 Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/inside-the-ai-index-12-takeaways-from-the-2026-report)** — Stanford HAI (still the canonical AI Index 2026 summary as Stanford runs its May 20 webinar). _Takeaway:_ The numbers being quoted everywhere this month. Global corporate AI investment $581.7B in 2025 (+130% YoY). GenAI hit 53% global population adoption in three years (faster than the PC or the internet). U.S. consumer surplus from GenAI ~$172B/yr by early 2026 (median value per user tripled YoY). Two awkward findings: the Foundation Model Transparency Index *dropped* from 58 to 40, and only 23% of the U.S. public expects AI to positively affect their work versus 73% of AI experts. AI datacenter power capacity now 29.6 GW.

- **[Everything we shipped at Interrupt 2026](https://www.langchain.com/blog/interrupt-2026-overview)** — LangChain (May 14 conference recap, still the most-cited agent-platform debrief this month). _Takeaway:_ The most concrete "agents in production" debrief of the month. Four shipped products: LangSmith Engine (auto-clusters production failures and opens PRs with fixes), SmithDB (purpose-built trace DB, 15× faster on core LangSmith workloads), Managed Deep Agents (hosted runtime for the open-source Deep Agents harness — planning, subagent delegation, file I/O, long-running threads), and Sandboxes GA. LangChain Labs is a new applied-research effort on continual learning from agent traces.

### rag / retrieval-augmented-generation
- **[Most Companies Doing GenAI Are Really Just Doing RAG: RAGOps Explained for Analysts](https://medium.com/@squalliahmed/most-companies-doing-genai-are-really-just-doing-rag-ragops-explained-for-analysts-31e0933ac70f)** — Ahmed Squalli (May 2026). - **[RAG in 2026: Architecture Shifts, Emerging Patterns, and What It Means for Java Developers](https://medium.com/@elammarisoufiane/rag-in-2026-architecture-shifts-emerging-patterns-and-what-it-means-for-java-developers-6f2803e39787)** — Soufiane Elammari (May 2026). Practical complement to the Squalli RAGOps frame above.

### model-context-protocol / mcp
- **[The Model Context Protocol Crisis: What 30 CVEs Teach Us About Building Secure AI Agents](https://medium.com/@khayyam.h/the-model-context-protocol-crisis-what-30-cves-teach-us-about-building-secure-ai-agents-95e16497d249)** — Khayyam H. (May 2026). Anatomy of the 30+ MCP CVEs filed Jan–Feb 2026: 43% exec/shell injection, 20% client/proxy infra, 13% auth bypass, plus a CVSS 9.6 RCE in a package with 437K downloads. Root cause: MCP was designed for functionality, not security — auth optional, encryption not required, no standard tool-level access control. Required reading for anyone deploying MCP servers.
- **[What is Model Context Protocol (MCP)?](https://devopslearning.medium.com/what-is-model-context-protocol-mcp-9f75c0e3c9eb)** — Prashant Lakhera (May 2026). The cleanest current "intro to MCP" on the tag — open standard, single integration surface, fast IDE→enterprise adoption arc. The piece to send to a manager who has heard the acronym and needs the 5-minute version.

### agent-protocols
- **[A2A, MCP, AG-UI, A2UI: The Essential 2026 AI Agent Protocol Stack](https://medium.com/@visrow/a2a-mcp-ag-ui-a2ui-the-essential-2026-ai-agent-protocol-stack-ee0e65a672ef)** — Vishal Mysore (May 2026). The clearest current map of the agent protocol stack: MCP (agent↔tools), A2A (agent↔agent), AG-UI (agent↔human surface), A2UI (agent↔automated UI). The frame the rest of the agent-protocols tag is starting to converge on; reads as the practical follow-up to last week's Ashu Kumar A2A primer.

### ai-governance
- **[Why AI Governance Is Becoming the Most Important Enterprise Skill in 2026](https://medium.com/data-science-collective/why-ai-governance-is-becoming-the-most-important-enterprise-skill-in-2026-1bfdfc59cfdf)** — Manish Shivanandhan, Data Science Collective (May 2026). The skills-and-careers frame on the discipline that just stopped being a side project — companies are using AI faster than they know how to govern it, and the role gap is widening.
- **[The Decision Continuity Gap: Why Your AI Governance Has No Memory](https://medium.com/@drrarkimmfields/the-decision-continuity-gap-why-your-ai-governance-has-no-memory-1dc3f9630633)** — Dr. Rarkimm Fields (May 2026). Anchors against McKinsey's 2026 AI Trust Maturity Survey (~500 orgs, only one-third report governance/agentic-governance maturity ≥3): most AI governance programs are decision-by-decision rather than archived, with no audit trail explaining why a model was deployed, retired, or constrained. The operational gap behind the EU AI Office's August documentation demand.
- **[The Governed Skill.md for Agentic AI](https://medium.com/@oracle_43885/the-governed-skill-for-agentic-ai-913006a7966e)** — Valdez Ladd (May 2026). The freshest concrete artifact on the tag: defines a Policy Manifest format (scope, risk mappings, required controls, guardrails, validation checkpoints, telemetry, escalation, change governance) as the unit of governance for an agentic skill. The format other governance posts will likely cite as the worked example this summer.
