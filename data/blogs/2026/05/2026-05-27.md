# AI Blogs — 2026-05-27

## Long-form picks

- **[A quote from Kyle Ferrana](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/27/kyle-ferrana/)** — Simon Willison (May 27 link-post, the freshest Willison post). _Takeaway:_ Willison surfaces a Kyle Ferrana quote re-frames "shields" via a Star Trek line ("Shields can reduce damage we sustain. Not immunity. Not hubris. Just prudence. It's not precaution—it's strategy."). Reads as the May-27 micro-companion to the May-26 "the trust layer is not fixable" Paul Graham post — the same author is now sketching out the design stance once you accept the operations layer has failed. Lands the May-26-27 Willison thread as the cultural-economy framing of the week.

- **[Some ideas for what comes next, May 2026](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/some-ideas-for-what-comes-next-may)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 26, still the freshest Interconnects piece into May 27). _Takeaway:_ Lambert's mid-2026 stocktake — 2026 is the first year he doesn't expect any breaks in the acceleration curve, models keep getting more capable, the economics get real, and risks come to the foreground. Now sharpened by a new beat on Mythos as a "remarkable technical achievement in software engineering and cybersecurity" — the bellwether of the compute acceleration available to the largest U.S. labs. Pairs with Lambert's May-12 "open ecosystems compound" piece as the calibrated update — the third leg of the 2026 open-models tripod.

- **[The pressure](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/26/the-pressure/)** — Simon Willison (May 26, still load-bearing into May 27). _Takeaway:_ Notes on the unprecedented pressure the curl maintainer team faces as AI-assisted security issues flood the project's inbox at a rate the volunteer reviewer base cannot absorb. The atomic, current-week instance of the broader "trust layer breaks on the receive side" thread Willison opened with the Paul Graham post — same structural problem, two surface areas (review-economy, mail-economy). The empirical underpinning of "AI is creating economic externalities the recipient has to swallow" — which Stratechery's Inference Shift names at the macro level.

- **[The Inference Shift](https://stratechery.com/2026/the-inference-shift/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 26 daily update, freshest Stratechery piece). _Takeaway:_ Splits inference into two regimes — "answer inference" (humans in loop, latency-sensitive, chat-token workloads) and "agentic inference" (humans absent, throughput-dominant, multi-hour traces). The structural implication: if latency tolerance loosens, the case for cutting-edge HBM and top-tier GPUs weakens for the agentic half of demand — slower/cheaper memory (traditional DRAM) and Cerebras/Groq-style speed chips each get their own market. Lands as Cerebras lifts its IPO range from $115–$125 to $150–$160 — markets are pricing the split into the cap table. The May-26 sharpening of the May-11 "Shifting Alliances" thesis.

- **[A study of hiring algorithms in the wild](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/)** — Stanford HAI (May 26 news item, still front-of-page into May 27). _Takeaway:_ The first large-scale study of hiring algorithms in production — Bommasani, Bana, Creel, Jurafsky, Liang — finds concerning patterns to how systems reject candidates. The structural complement to HAI's May-21 scaling-laws piece: HAI is publishing both the upstream economics (cheaper scaling prediction) and the downstream deployment harms (algorithmic hiring) inside the same month. The empirical underpinning of the "responsible AI lags capability" framing in the 2026 AI Index Report.

- **[AI's impact on software engineers in 2026: key trends, Part 2](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/ai-impact-on-software-engineers-part-2)** — Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer (May 20, still the structural read into May 27). _Takeaway:_ Part 2 of Orosz's mid-2026 stocktake — tradeoffs of AI tooling, why adopting AI at the company level is hard, what's changed in two years. The load-bearing claim: companies are still in the experimentation phase, several respondents report unsustainable AI-tooling budgets, and AI speeds up engineers focused on getting things done but adds tech debt faster and "might build the wrong things." The operations-layer counterpart to the "Agent = Model + Harness" Medium framing — the harness only helps if the org can pay for it.

- **[State of the software engineering job market in 2026](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/state-of-the-job-market-2026)** — Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer (~May 26, freshest Pulse). _Takeaway:_ The number of SWE jobs listed is still lower than May 2021. US and UK are the only countries where vacancies are up; Canada flat, Germany and France down. The macro datapoint that pairs cleanly with Orosz's Part-2 tooling piece: the hiring market is moving toward US-headquartered companies hiring mostly in the US and a little in the UK, while AI-tooling pressure compresses headcount elsewhere. The empirical companion to the May-20 "Forward deployed engineering heats up again" beat.

- **[Building LangGraph: Designing an Agent Runtime from first principles](https://www.langchain.com/blog/building-langgraph)** — Christian Bromann & Nick Hollon, LangChain (May 21, still the freshest framework deep-dive). _Takeaway:_ Walks through how LangGraph implements the load-bearing primitives of a production agent runtime — parallelisation, streaming, checkpointing, human-in-the-loop, tracing, task queue — from first principles rather than bolt-ons. Companion to the May-20 "Agentic Engineering" swarms piece as the runtime/control-plane counterpart to Anthropic's Managed Agents and Deep Agents v0.6 narrative: the harness, not the model, is where the differentiation lives, and the harness has its own architecture now being formalised.

- **[Datasette Agent](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/21/datasette-agent/)** — Simon Willison (May 21, still the freshest Datasette-Agent piece). _Takeaway:_ Public release announcement for Datasette Agent — the live, version-bumped reference implementation of Willison's "local data + agent harness" advocacy. Worth keeping as the on-ramp piece for anyone wanting a concrete walkthrough of what the host platform has to provide at the primitive level (query tools, schema introspection, result-summarisation) that vendor demos paper over. Pairs with the LangGraph piece as the small-scale companion to the framework deep-dive.

- **[AI Agents Must Follow the Law](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-agents-must-follow-the-law)** — Lawfare (May 15, still the freshest LFAI piece on the topic). _Takeaway:_ Names the conceptual problem under "Law-Following AI": AI agents shouldn't follow literally every law a human is bound by — that would be over-burdensome and impracticable — but should be bound to a subset designed to safeguard the legal order and citizens' most basic interests. The constitutional companion to "AI Governance by Phone Call" / "AI Will Write Complex Laws" — the political fight, the institutional rail, and now the design-space the rail has to fit into.

- **[AI Governance by Phone Call](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-governance-by-phone-call)** — Lawfare (May 20). _Takeaway:_ Argues that before the executive branch deploys advanced AI systems in its most abuse-prone functions, it should receive congressional approval. The federalism / advice-and-consent companion to "AI Agents Must Follow the Law" — same author cluster, now naming the institutional check that has to be in place before the legal-design question becomes operationally binding.

- **[Import AI 455: Automating AI Research](https://jack-clark.net/2026/05/04/import-ai-455-automating-ai-research/)** — Jack Clark (May 4, still the freshest Import AI post — no new May-26/27 issue). _Takeaway:_ Names the load-bearing acceleration datapoint of the month: Claude Opus 4 achieved a 2.9× mean speedup in May 2025; this rose to 16.5× with Opus 4.6 in November 2025, 30× with Opus 4.6 in February 2026, and 52× with Claude Mythos Preview in April on CPU optimization tasks. Clark argues AI can today automate vast swathes of AI engineering, and pairs it with reporting that OpenAI wants to build an "automated AI research intern by September of 2026." The engineering/threat-model layer this month — sits naturally alongside Lambert's Mythos beat.

- **[Recent Developments in LLM Architectures: KV Sharing, mHC, and Compressed Attention](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/recent-developments-in-llm-architectures)** — Sebastian Raschka, Ahead of AI (May 16, still the freshest architecture deep-dive). _Takeaway:_ The 2026 architecture story is no longer "more parameters" but long-context efficiency: KV-cache size, memory traffic, and attention cost are now the binding constraints as reasoning models and agent workflows keep more tokens around. Walks through KV sharing and per-layer embeddings in Gemma 4, layer-wise attention budgeting in Laguna XS.2, compressed convolutional attention in ZAYA1-8B, and mHC plus compressed attention in DeepSeek V4. The technical companion to "The Inference Shift" — efficient long-context kernels are the on-chip prerequisite for the agentic-inference half of Thompson's split.
