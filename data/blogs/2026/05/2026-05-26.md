# AI Blogs — 2026-05-26

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **[Some ideas for what comes next, May 2026](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/some-ideas-for-what-comes-next-may)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 26, the fresh May-26 piece on the tag). _Takeaway:_ Lambert's mid-2026 stocktake — 2026 is the first year where he doesn't expect any breaks in the AI acceleration curve, models keep getting more capable, the economics get real, and the risks come to the foreground. Two load-bearing claims: (1) the open-vs-closed gap has widened to ~5–6 months with no equivalent open model, and "the robustness of the best closed frontier models" could stretch that closer to 12+ months; (2) open models will be used disproportionately for automated enterprise agents and low-cost domains, while closed frontier models capture the high-value Claude-Code-like agentic revenue. Direct counterpart to his May 7 "Notes from inside China's AI labs" and May 12 "How open model ecosystems compound" — the third leg of the 2026 open-models tripod.

- **[The Inference Shift](https://stratechery.com/2026/the-inference-shift/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 26 daily update, freshest Stratechery piece). _Takeaway:_ Splits inference into two regimes — "answer inference" (humans in loop, latency-sensitive, ~chat-token workloads) and "agentic inference" (humans absent, throughput-dominant, multi-hour traces). The structural implication: if latency tolerance loosens, the case for cutting-edge HBM and top-tier GPUs weakens for the agentic half of demand — slower/cheaper memory (traditional DRAM) and Cerebras/Groq-style speed chips each get their own market. Lands as Cerebras lifts its IPO range from $115–$125 to $150–$160 — the markets are pricing the split into the cap table. The May-26 sharpening of Thompson's May-11 "Shifting Alliances" thesis: training matters, Nvidia still dominates, but the inference market is bifurcating.

- **[A quote from Paul Graham](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/26/paul-graham/)** — Simon Willison (May 26 link-post). _Takeaway:_ Willison flags Graham's public claim that he can no longer finish reading emails written by AI for human founders — "it feels like being lied to." Paired with Willison's May-24 Armin Ronacher post on machine-voiced issue reports, this lands as the May-26 cultural-economy datapoint of the month: as more inbound communication routes through an LLM, the social contract of who you're answering breaks down on the receiving side. The structural counterpart to the harness-engineering thread on the Medium tags — the operations layer is fixable; the trust layer is not.

- **[Notes on Pope Leo XIV's encyclical on AI](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/25/encyclical-on-ai/)** — Simon Willison (May 25, still load-bearing into Mon May 26). _Takeaway:_ Willison's read of Magnifica Humanitas — the first papal encyclical to take AI as its central subject, signed on the 135th anniversary of Rerum Novarum. The signal worth keeping: a global institutional voice has now framed AI as comparable in stakes to the first industrial revolution and bound the framing into Catholic social teaching. Pairs with the Lawfare "AI Agents Must Follow the Law" thread as the meta-governance layer — model-controls, agent-controls, policy, and now civic theology — that the digest is now tracking in parallel.

- **[Release: datasette 1.0a30](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/24/datasette/)** — Simon Willison (May 24, freshest Datasette release post). _Takeaway:_ Latest 1.0 alpha, shipped in lockstep with the datasette-agent 0.1a4 release. Worth keeping as the live, public, version-bumped reference implementation of the "local data + agent harness" pattern Willison advocates: every alpha shows what the host platform has to provide at the primitive level (query tools, schema introspection, result-summarisation) that vendor demos paper over.

- **[A study of hiring algorithms in the wild](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/)** — Stanford HAI (May 26, freshest HAI news item). _Takeaway:_ The first large-scale study of hiring algorithms in production — Bommasani, Bana, Creel, Jurafsky, Liang — finds concerning patterns to how systems reject candidates. The structural complement to the May-21 scaling-laws piece: HAI is now publishing both the upstream economics (cheaper scaling prediction) and the downstream deployment harms (algorithmic hiring) inside the same month. The empirical underpinning of the "responsible AI lags capability" framing in the 2026 AI Index Report.

- **[AI's impact on software engineers in 2026: key trends, Part 2](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/ai-impact-on-software-engineers-part-2)** — Gergely Orosz, The Pragmatic Engineer (May 20, still the structural read into May 26). _Takeaway:_ Part 2 of Orosz's mid-2026 stocktake — tradeoffs of AI tooling, why adopting AI at company-level is hard, and what's changed in two years. The piece worth keeping: companies are still in the experimentation phase, several respondents say their AI-tooling budgets are unsustainable, and AI tools speed up engineers focused on getting things done but add tech debt faster and "might build the wrong things." The operations-layer counterpart to "Agent = Model + Harness" — the harness only helps when the org around it can actually pay for it.

- **[Building LangGraph: Designing an Agent Runtime from first principles](https://www.langchain.com/blog/building-langgraph)** — Christian Bromann & Nick Hollon, LangChain (May 21, still the freshest framework deep-dive — no new May-26 LangChain blog post). _Takeaway:_ Walks through how LangGraph implements the load-bearing primitives of a production agent runtime — parallelisation, streaming, checkpointing, human-in-the-loop, tracing, task queue — from first principles rather than as bolt-ons. Companion to the May-20 "Agentic Engineering" swarms piece as the runtime/control-plane counterpart to Anthropic's Managed Agents and Deep Agents v0.6 narrative: the harness, not the model, is where the differentiation lives, and the harness has its own architecture that's now being formalised.

- **[New Approach to Scaling Laws Could Change How AI Models Are Trained](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/new-approach-to-scaling-laws-could-change-how-ai-models-are-trained)** — Stanford HAI (May 21, still load-bearing on the page). _Takeaway:_ Researchers leveraging statistical concepts from measurement science and education have greatly reduced the computational demand of predicting how the largest LLMs scale up — could save millions in training costs. Sits naturally alongside the SpaceX/Anthropic $1.25B/month compute commitment: if the cost of _predicting_ scaling outcomes drops by orders of magnitude, the marginal experiment economics shift even as the absolute capex grows. The empirical companion to Lambert's "open ecosystems compound" thesis — same compounding logic now reaching the prediction layer.

- **[How open model ecosystems compound](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/how-open-model-ecosystems-compound)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 12, still the structural open-models piece this month). _Takeaway:_ Formalises the compounding mechanism — shared eval infra, shared post-training recipes, shared distillation targets — that means open-weights releases aren't independent draws but each one lifts the next. The 80%-of-compute-is-R&D claim is the load-bearing data point: in an open ecosystem like China's, you amortise the expensive part across labs. Pairs directly with the May-26 "Some ideas for what comes next" follow-up — the structural argument plus the calibrated update.

- **[AI Agents Must Follow the Law](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-agents-must-follow-the-law)** — Lawfare (May 15, still the freshest LFAI piece on the topic). _Takeaway:_ Names the conceptual problem under "Law-Following AI": AI agents shouldn't follow literally every law a human is bound by — that would be over-burdensome and impracticable — but should be bound to a subset designed to safeguard the legal order and citizens' most basic interests. The constitutional companion to the "AI Regulation Knife Fight" / "Advice and Consent" pair from earlier in the month: those name the political fight and the institutional rail; this names the design-space the rail has to fit into. Pairs naturally with Magnifica Humanitas as the legal/theological doublet of the week.

- **[Import AI 457: AI stuxnet; cursed Muon optimizer; and positive alignment](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-457-ai-stuxnet-cursed-muon)** — Jack Clark (May 18, still the freshest Import AI). _Takeaway:_ Three load-bearing pieces. (1) "AI stuxnet" — speculative argument that a superintelligence might enforce AI non-proliferation the way nuclear states do: through subtle, hard-to-find bugs (the "fast16" scenario) that degrade an actor's capacity for certain kinds of science. (2) Tilde Research showing Muon optimizer inherits row-norm anisotropy on tall matrices, permanently killing a significant fraction of MLP neurons — a real, current bug damaging model quality across multiple recent training runs. (3) "Positive alignment" as a frame complement to refusal-style alignment. The engineering-and-threat-model layer of the month.

- **[Recent Developments in LLM Architectures: KV Sharing, mHC, and Compressed Attention](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/recent-developments-in-llm-architectures)** — Sebastian Raschka, Ahead of AI (May 16, still the freshest architecture deep-dive on the tag). _Takeaway:_ The 2026 architecture story is no longer "more parameters" but long-context efficiency: KV-cache size, memory traffic, and attention cost are now the binding constraints as reasoning models and agent workflows keep more tokens around. Walks through KV sharing and per-layer embeddings in Gemma 4, layer-wise attention budgeting in Laguna XS.2, compressed convolutional attention in ZAYA1-8B, and mHC plus compressed attention in DeepSeek V4. The technical companion to "The Inference Shift" — efficient long-context kernels are the on-chip prerequisite for the agentic-inference half of Thompson's split.

- **[AI Safety Is Solving the Wrong Problem](https://medium.com/@mrsandelin/ai-safety-is-solving-the-wrong-problem-bcde08cf7e90)** — Markus Sandelin (May 1, 2026, still the most-shared contrarian piece on the tag). The interpretability companion to Sandelin's critique: makes the bet that interpretability inside the model is more tractable than filtering at the boundary.
