# AI Blogs — 2026-05-31

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

**I think Anthropic and OpenAI have found product-market fit** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Argues April 2026 is a new inflection point: both labs quietly moved enterprise plans to full API pricing (GPT-5.5 at 2x GPT-5.4, Opus 4.7 ~1.4x Opus 4.6), and coding agents (Claude Code/Cowork, Codex) are the first product with real revenue legs — Willison's own usage would have cost ~$2,180/mo at API rates vs $200 in subs. Frames the Uber/Microsoft "AI is too expensive" stories as customers sucking air through their teeth and saying yes. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/27/product-market-fit/]

**Some ideas for what comes next, May 2026** — Nathan Lambert (Interconnects). _Takeaway:_ Six reasons open models are further behind than benchmarks suggest — no open equivalent of the "Opus 4.5 in Claude Code" agent moment yet, even Gemini lacks a Claude Code/Codex competitor, so open models will specialize toward enterprise/low-cost agents rather than driving knowledge work. Also flags power structures (Vatican encyclical, China travel curbs on AI talent, Anthropic supply-chain designation) asserting themselves on AI. [https://www.interconnects.ai/p/some-ideas-for-what-comes-next-may]

**Recent Developments in LLM Architectures: KV Sharing, mHC, and Compressed Attention** — Sebastian Raschka (Ahead of AI). _Takeaway:_ A visual tour of long-context efficiency tweaks across recent open-weight LLMs (Gemma 4 to DeepSeek V4), separating residual-path changes (mHC) from attention-path changes (CSA/HCA) and compressed-attention caches, tracing the lineage from GPT-2 to DeepSeek V4-Pro one component at a time. [https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/recent-developments-in-llm-architectures]

**sqlite AGENTS.md** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ SQLite added an AGENTS.md aimed not at its own devs but at people pointing agents at the codebase — and explicitly does NOT accept agentic code (a recent commit even removed the word "currently"). Meanwhile the forum was flooded with AI-generated bug reports, forcing a split-off Bug Forum. A sharp signal of how OSS maintainers are drawing lines around agent contributions. [https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/27/sqlite-agents/]

**How to Work and Compound with AI** — Eugene Yan. _Takeaway:_ Practitioner framework for compounding leverage with AI: every finished artifact becomes context for the next session, each correction updates a config that reduces future errors. Five principles — provide good context, encode taste as config, make verification easy, delegate bigger tasks, close the loop. [https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/]

**Evals Skills for Coding Agents** — Hamel Husain. _Takeaway:_ Introduces evals-skills, an installable plugin distilling lessons from helping 50+ companies and teaching 4,000+ students, to guard against common eval mistakes. Reinforces his cost hierarchy of evaluators — cheap assertions/reference checks vs LLM-as-Judge needing 100+ labeled examples and weekly maintenance — and warns naive automation usually fails. [https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-skills/]

**The Agent Development Lifecycle** — LangChain. _Takeaway:_ Lays out a Build → Test → Deploy → Monitor lifecycle for production agents, alongside the Deep Agents deploy beta. Notes open models like GLM-5 and MiniMax M2.7 now match closed frontier models on core agent tasks (file ops, tool use, instruction following). [https://www.langchain.com/blog/the-agent-development-lifecycle]
