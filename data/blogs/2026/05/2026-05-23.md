# AI Blogs — 2026-05-23

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **[Inside Anthropic's 2026 Developer Conference](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/inside-anthropic-s-2026-developer-conference)** — Dan Shipper, Chain of Thought / Every. _Takeaway:_ The freshest practitioner debrief on the Code w/ Claude San Francisco event, written live from the floor and now the load-bearing summary of what Anthropic actually shipped. The three signals worth pulling out: (1) the SpaceX/Colossus deal — Anthropic gets _all_ the capacity in the latter's supercluster, the structural counterweight to Google's TPU advantage; (2) Claude Managed Agents, which packages multi-agent orchestration, "dreaming" (agents learning from prior sessions), and "outcomes" (run-until-target loops) as a hosted product — i.e., the bet that the harness, not the model weight, is the moat; (3) doubled Claude usage limits paired with the new Code w/ Claude developer-conference circuit (SF → London → Tokyo) as the deliberate developer-acquisition push. Reads as the empirical companion to Ben Thompson's "inference shift" thesis: token economics now favor whoever owns the deployment surface, and Anthropic just bought one.

- **[Datasette Agent](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/21/datasette-agent/)** — Simon Willison. _Takeaway:_ Willison ships the first release of Datasette Agent, the project where his three-year-old `LLM` Python library and Datasette finally converge into a single conversational agent over SQLite. The under-the-radar engineering content: the live demo runs on Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite ("cheap, fast and has no trouble writing SQLite queries"), the agent is extensible via plugins (charts via Observable Plot, image generation via ChatGPT Images 2.0, code execution in a Fly Sprites sandbox), and a `uv` one-liner runs the whole stack against `gemma-4-26b-a4b` in LM Studio. The takeaway buried in the post: open-weight models released in the past six months are now reliably handling tool calls plus SQL — the local-models-can-do-real-agent-work line, with a working demo behind it. Continues to be the cleanest single-developer "what an agent actually looks like in 2026" post in circulation.

- **[AI progress creates more work for humans, not less](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/ai-progress-creates-more-work-for-humans-not-less)** — Dan Shipper, Chain of Thought / Every (May 21). _Takeaway:_ The structural argument the labour-displacement debate has been missing. Shipper formalises what he calls the "expert paradox": as AI cheapens yesterday's competence, the volume of generic output explodes, and expert differentiation becomes _more_ valuable — only experts can turn AI-generated drafts into production-ready work, or build the systems that absorb the new volume. Load-bearing datapoint: Every has automated every workflow it can with agents, and has still grown from 4 to 30 human employees since GPT-3. Reads as the practitioner counter-narrative to the Pragmatic Engineer "Shippers add tech debt fastest" finding — same phenomenon, different framing of who benefits.

- **[Recent Developments in LLM Architectures: KV Sharing, mHC, and Compressed Attention](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/recent-developments-in-llm-architectures)** — Sebastian Raschka, Ahead of AI (May 16, still the freshest deep architecture piece). _Takeaway:_ The 2026 architecture story is no longer "more parameters" but long-context efficiency: KV-cache size, memory traffic, and attention cost have become the binding constraints as reasoning models and agent workflows keep more tokens around. Walks through KV sharing and per-layer embeddings in Gemma 4, layer-wise attention budgeting in Laguna XS.2, compressed convolutional attention in ZAYA1-8B, and mHC plus compressed attention in DeepSeek V4. The technical companion to the "agents need cheap long context" narrative driving everything from Datasette Agent to Claude Managed Agents.

- **[The AI Regulation Knife Fight](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-ai-regulation-knife-fight)** — Lawfare (May 15). _Takeaway:_ The structural read of the US-side governance churn this month. The Trump administration is openly contested on whether US intelligence agencies should play a bigger role in pre-deployment evaluation of frontier models, with policy positions inside the administration in flux. Pairs with [Advice and Consent for Major Governmental AI Deployments](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/advice-and-consent-for-major-governmental-ai-deployments) (Cullen O'Keefe, May 20) as the institutional-design pendant — together they map both the live political fight and the constitutional rail being proposed to settle it.

- **[Dominating AI Requires Understanding AI](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/dominating-ai-requires-understanding-ai)** — Lawfare (May 13). _Takeaway:_ The argument that the US "AI dominance" framing is empty if it isn't backed by capacity to evaluate, test, and secure frontier systems. The companion-of-the-week to "Knife Fight" above: one piece names the political incoherence, the other names the institutional capability the dominance frame would actually require.

- **[New in Deep Agents v0.6](https://www.langchain.com/blog/deep-agents-0-6)** — Sydney Runkle, LangChain (May 13, still the freshest Deep Agents release post-Interrupt). _Takeaway:_ The release that turns the Interrupt 2026 keynote into shippable infrastructure. v0.6 is structured around four performance axes — model layer, agent layer, scale, and over-time durability — and is the concrete answer to "what does 'managed Deep Agents' actually mean?" The headline practitioner claim: open-weight models like Kimi, Qwen, and DeepSeek can now run capable agents at 20x+ lower cost than closed frontier APIs via Harness profiles. Sits underneath the Anthropic Managed Agents announcement above as the open-source pendant to the same productisation move.

- **[The last six months in LLMs in five minutes](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/19/5-minute-llms/)** — Simon Willison (May 19, still the canonical orientation piece). _Takeaway:_ Annotated slides from his PyCon US 2026 lightning talk. The shape of the period: a "November 2025 inflection point" where the supposedly best model changed hands five times between Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google in six months; coding agents got genuinely good; laptop-available models (Qwen3.6, Gemma 4, etc.) started wildly outperforming expectations. The pelican-on-a-bicycle SVG benchmark continues to function as the most honest no-train-leak vibes check available. Still the single best 5-minute orientation for anyone catching up.

- **[Shifting Alliances in a Changing World](https://stratechery.com/2026/shifting-alliances-in-a-changing-world/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 18 weekly wrap, still the structural read of the cycle). _Takeaway:_ Thompson's synthesis: the SpaceX/Anthropic compute deal, OpenAI's "Deployment Company" reorg, and Apple-Intel rapprochement all read as the same story — a structural shift from "answer inference" (human-in-the-loop, latency-sensitive, ~chat tokens) to "agentic inference" (humans absent, throughput-dominant, multi-hour traces) is reshuffling who needs whose chips, datacenters, and distribution. The clearest one-paragraph statement of why every frontier-lab capex story this spring rhymes — and the structural prior under which Shipper's Code w/ Claude debrief above lands as data.

- **[Import AI 457: AI stuxnet; cursed Muon optimizer; and positive alignment](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-457-ai-stuxnet-cursed-muon)** — Jack Clark (May 18, freshest Import AI). _Takeaway:_ Three load-bearing pieces. (1) "AI stuxnet" — a speculative argument that a superintelligence might enforce AI non-proliferation the way nuclear states enforce nuclear non-proliferation: through subtle, hard-to-find bugs (the "fast16" scenario) that degrade an actor's capacity for certain kinds of science. (2) Tilde Research showing the Muon optimizer inherits row-norm anisotropy on tall matrices, permanently killing a significant fraction of MLP neurons — a real, current bug damaging model quality across multiple recent training runs. (3) "Positive alignment" as a frame complement to refusal-style alignment. The engineering-and-threat-model layer this week.

- **[How open model ecosystems compound](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/how-open-model-ecosystems-compound)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 12, paid, still the structural China/open piece). _Takeaway:_ Lambert formalises the compounding mechanism — shared eval infra, shared post-training recipes, shared distillation targets — that means open-weights releases aren't independent draws but each one lifts the next. The 80%-of-compute-is-R&D claim is the load-bearing data point: in an open ecosystem like China's, you amortise the expensive part across labs. Builds the structural case that closed-vs-open isn't a horse race; they're different equilibria with different compounding curves.

- **[The Pulse: Antigravity 2.0 takes 'IDE' out of its new IDE](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-antigravity-20-takes-ide)** — Gergely Orosz, Pragmatic Engineer (May 21). _Takeaway:_ The freshest Pulse. Feedback on Antigravity 2.0's redesigned IDE is overwhelmingly negative — bugs, poor UX, weak model support, and aggressive Gemini-token burn. Coming a week after the May 19 Pulse on "forward deployed engineering heats up again" (job losses rising while big-company engineers self-report 100% AI usage), it's the second consecutive Orosz column where the user-facing AI-IDE story diverges sharply from the vendor framing.

- **[How to Work and Compound with AI](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/)** — Eugene Yan (May 3, still the most-shared individual-practitioner piece in circulation). _Takeaway:_ Five principles for getting AI work to compound rather than reset: (1) provide good context, (2) encode your taste as config, (3) make verification easy, (4) delegate bigger tasks, (5) close the loop. "Encode taste as config" remains the underrated one — the practical operationalization of the context-engineering thesis, and the bridge between one-shot prompting and durable agentic workflows.

### rag / retrieval-augmented-generation
- **[RAG in 2026: Architecture Shifts, Emerging Patterns, and What It Means for Java Developers](https://medium.com/@elammarisoufiane/rag-in-2026-architecture-shifts-emerging-patterns-and-what-it-means-for-java-developers-6f2803e39787)** — Soufiane Elammari (May 2026, freshest tagged piece). Two structural claims worth the read: classical retriever-generator RAG is dead, replaced by Modular RAG (each pipeline component independently replaceable) and Agentic RAG (the LLM decides whether/what/when to retrieve). The clearest 2026 status snapshot on the discipline.
