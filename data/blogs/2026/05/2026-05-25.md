# AI Blogs — 2026-05-25

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **[Release: datasette-agent 0.1a4](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/24/datasette-agent/)** — Simon Willison (May 24, freshest Willison release-post). _Takeaway:_ Second alpha of the LLM-powered agent for Datasette, four days after the May 21 launch announcement. Worth keeping on the list as a live, public, version-bumped reference implementation of the "local data + agent harness" pattern Willison has been advocating — every alpha shows what the harness has to do at the primitive level (query tools, schema introspection, result-summarisation) that vendor demos paper over. Pairs naturally with the May 24 datasette 1.0a30 and datasette-fixtures 0.1a0 releases — the host platform is moving in lockstep with the agent on top of it.

- **[A quote from Armin Ronacher](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/24/armin-ronacher/)** — Simon Willison (May 24). _Takeaway:_ Short Willison link-post quoting Ronacher on the rising volume of issue reports written in machine-generated voice. The signal worth keeping: as more contributors route inputs through an LLM, maintainers can't tell whose voice they're answering, which warps the social contract of open source. The cultural-economy counterpart to all the "Code w/ Claude" tooling pieces — when everyone has an agent, the receiving side (maintainer, code reviewer, on-call) becomes the bottleneck.

- **[On the <dl>](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/23/on-the-dl/)** — Simon Willison (May 23, still load-bearing into Mon). _Takeaway:_ Link-post on the HTML `<dl>` element after reading Ben Meyer's piece. The freshest Sunday-counterpoint to the agent-runtime heavyweights: Willison continues to operate at the layer of "what should the primitives actually look like" rather than the vendor-announcement cycle. Pairs with his May 21 Datasette Agent launch as evidence the same engineer is shipping both the agent platform and the primitive-level scrutiny.

- **[Building LangGraph: Designing an Agent Runtime from first principles](https://www.langchain.com/blog/building-langgraph)** — Christian Bromann & Nick Hollon, LangChain (May 21, still the freshest framework deep-dive — no May 22-25 LangChain blog posts). _Takeaway:_ Walks through how LangGraph implements the load-bearing primitives of a production agent runtime — parallelisation, streaming, checkpointing, human-in-the-loop, tracing, task queue — from first principles rather than as bolt-ons. Companion to the May 20 "Agentic Engineering" swarms piece as the runtime/control-plane counterpart to Anthropic's Managed Agents and Deep Agents v0.6 narrative: the harness, not the model, is where the differentiation lives, and the harness has its own architecture that's now being formalised.

- **[New Approach to Scaling Laws Could Change How AI Models Are Trained](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/new-approach-to-scaling-laws-could-change-how-ai-models-are-trained)** — Stanford HAI (May 21, still freshest HAI piece). _Takeaway:_ Researchers leveraging statistical concepts from measurement science and education have greatly reduced the computational demand of predicting how the largest LLMs scale up. Sits naturally alongside Anthropic's $1.25B/month SpaceX compute commitment: if the cost of _predicting_ scaling outcomes drops by orders of magnitude, the marginal experiment economics change even as the absolute capex grows. The empirical companion to Lambert's "open ecosystems compound" thesis — same compounding logic now reaching the prediction layer.

- **[AI Agents Must Follow the Law](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-agents-must-follow-the-law)** — Lawfare (May 15, still the freshest LFAI piece on the topic page). _Takeaway:_ Names the conceptual problem under "Law-Following AI": AI agents shouldn't follow literally every law a human is bound by — that would be over-burdensome and impracticable — but should be bound to a subset designed to safeguard the legal order and citizens' most basic interests. The constitutional companion to the "AI Regulation Knife Fight" / "Advice and Consent" pair from earlier in the month: those name the political fight and the institutional rail; this names the design-space the rail has to fit into.

- **[Mythos Fallout, U.S. Government Weighs AI Model Regulation](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/mythos-fallout--u.s.-government-weighs-ai-model-regulation)** — Lawfare (May 8, still load-bearing). _Takeaway:_ The Trump administration is openly considering stricter oversight of US AI models specifically because of their cybersecurity impact — the structural prior under which Opus 4.7's automated detection-and-blocking of prohibited cyber requests reads as preemptive de-risking. Pairs with "AI Agents Must Follow the Law" as the policy / model-controls / agent-controls triple.

- **[Continual learning for AI agents](https://www.langchain.com/blog/continual-learning-for-ai-agents)** — Harrison Chase, LangChain (May 5, still the freshest framework piece on memory). _Takeaway:_ Learning in AI agents happens at three distinct layers — model weights, harness (code + instructions + tools that are always present), and context (instructions/skills that live outside the harness and configure it). The framing that the model layer is one of three and arguably not the most important is the formal version of the slogan "Agent = Model + Harness" — and the conceptual ground under both LangChain Labs and SmithDB / LangSmith Engine.

- **[Import AI 457: AI stuxnet; cursed Muon optimizer; and positive alignment](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-457-ai-stuxnet-cursed-muon)** — Jack Clark (May 18, still the freshest Import AI). _Takeaway:_ Three load-bearing pieces. (1) "AI stuxnet" — speculative argument that a superintelligence might enforce AI non-proliferation the way nuclear states do: through subtle, hard-to-find bugs (the "fast16" scenario) that degrade an actor's capacity for certain kinds of science. (2) Tilde Research showing Muon optimizer inherits row-norm anisotropy on tall matrices, permanently killing a significant fraction of MLP neurons — a real, current bug damaging model quality across multiple recent training runs. (3) "Positive alignment" as a frame complement to refusal-style alignment. The engineering-and-threat-model layer of the month.

- **[Shifting Alliances in a Changing World](https://stratechery.com/2026/shifting-alliances-in-a-changing-world/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 18 weekly wrap, still the structural read into next Monday). _Takeaway:_ Thompson's synthesis — SpaceX/Anthropic compute deal, OpenAI's "Deployment Company" reorg, and Apple-Intel rapprochement read as the same story: a structural shift from "answer inference" (human-in-loop, latency-sensitive, ~chat tokens) to "agentic inference" (humans absent, throughput-dominant, multi-hour traces) is reshuffling who needs whose chips, datacenters, and distribution. The clearest one-paragraph statement of why every frontier-lab capex story this spring rhymes.

- **[How open model ecosystems compound](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/how-open-model-ecosystems-compound)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 12, still the structural China/open piece). _Takeaway:_ Formalises the compounding mechanism — shared eval infra, shared post-training recipes, shared distillation targets — that means open-weights releases aren't independent draws but each one lifts the next. The 80%-of-compute-is-R&D claim is the load-bearing data point: in an open ecosystem like China's, you amortise the expensive part across labs. Builds the structural case that closed-vs-open isn't a horse race; they're different equilibria with different compounding curves.

- **[How to Work and Compound with AI](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/)** — Eugene Yan (May 3, still the most-shared individual-practitioner piece). _Takeaway:_ Five principles for getting AI work to compound rather than reset: (1) provide good context, (2) encode your taste as config, (3) make verification easy, (4) delegate bigger tasks, (5) close the loop. "Encode taste as config" remains the underrated one — the practical operationalisation of the context-engineering thesis, and the bridge between one-shot prompting and durable agentic workflows.

- **[Recent Developments in LLM Architectures: KV Sharing, mHC, and Compressed Attention](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/recent-developments-in-llm-architectures)** — Sebastian Raschka, Ahead of AI (May 16, still the freshest deep architecture piece). _Takeaway:_ The 2026 architecture story is no longer "more parameters" but long-context efficiency: KV-cache size, memory traffic, and attention cost are now the binding constraints as reasoning models and agent workflows keep more tokens around. Walks through KV sharing and per-layer embeddings in Gemma 4, layer-wise attention budgeting in Laguna XS.2, compressed convolutional attention in ZAYA1-8B, and mHC plus compressed attention in DeepSeek V4. The technical companion to the "agents need cheap long context" narrative driving everything from Datasette Agent to Claude Managed Agents.

### ai-safety
- **[AI Safety Is Solving the Wrong Problem](https://medium.com/@mrsandelin/ai-safety-is-solving-the-wrong-problem-bcde08cf7e90)** — Markus Sandelin (May 1, 2026, still the most-shared contrarian piece on the tag).
