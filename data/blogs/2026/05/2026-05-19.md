# AI Blogs — 2026-05-19

_Collector: ai-blogs. Slice: long-form blogs + Medium tags._

## Long-form picks

- **[The last six months in LLMs in five minutes](https://simonwillison.net/2026/May/19/5-minute-llms/)** — Simon Willison (May 19, today). _Takeaway:_ Annotated slides from his PyCon US 2026 lightning talk. The shape of the period: a "November 2025 inflection point" where the supposedly best model changed hands five times between Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google in six months; coding agents got genuinely good; and laptop-available models (Qwen3.6, Gemma 4, etc.) started wildly outperforming expectations. The pelican-on-a-bicycle SVG benchmark continues to function as the most honest no-train-leak vibes check we have — every frontier release this period gets graded on it. The single best 5-minute orientation for anyone catching up.

- **[The EU AI Office gets real enforcement powers in August. Here's what it can actually do.](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/how-much-power-does-the-eu-ai-office-actually-have)** — Joel Christoph, Lawfare (May 18). _Takeaway:_ On August 2, the EU AI Office gains three concrete powers: (1) demand documentation from developers of the most powerful general-purpose AI models, (2) commission independent evaluations including source-code access, and (3) impose fines up to 3% of global annual turnover. The first time the AI Act stops being a paper threat — the regulatory pendant to the U.S. federalism stories Lawfare has been running all spring. Required reading before any frontier-lab compliance budget gets signed off.

- **[Data Center Discontent, Understanding the Opposition, Fixing the Problem](https://stratechery.com/2026/data-center-discontent-understanding-the-opposition-fixing-the-problem/)** — Ben Thompson, Stratechery (May 18). _Takeaway:_ Reframes datacenter NIMBY-ism as a real, legitimate externality story rather than an obstructionist one — the noise, water draw, grid load, and tax-abatement-without-jobs profile of a hyperscaler buildout is genuinely costly to host communities, and the only solution that scales is direct compensation to neighbors (not Pigovian taxes, not zoning fights). Companion to the May 19 Dithering episode "Data Center Unpopularity."

- **[Import AI 457: AI stuxnet; cursed Muon optimizer; and positive alignment](https://jack-clark.net/2026/05/18/import-ai-457-ai-stuxnet-cursed-muon-optimizer-and-positive-alignment/)** — Jack Clark (May 18). _Takeaway:_ Three threads. (1) Researchers find fast16, a ~20-year-old virus repurposed to target precision CAD software — the first credible "AI stuxnet" template, and a reminder that the supply-chain attack surface for AI-augmented engineering is older than the AI. (2) Tilde Research shows Muon inherits row-norm anisotropy on tall matrices, permanently killing MLP neurons; the new Aurora optimizer ("leverage-aware optimizer for rectangular matrices") fixes this — final smoothed loss 2.26 vs Muon 2.31 vs NorMuon 2.33, and +10 MMLU points over Muon. (3) Position paper arguing for "positive alignment" — design targets framed as human flourishing, not just refusal/safety — as a complementary research agenda to ASL-tier capability gating.

- **[The Pulse: Forward deployed engineering heats up again](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/the-pulse-forward-deployed-engineering)** — Gergely Orosz, Pragmatic Engineer (May 14). _Takeaway:_ Forward-deployed engineering (FDE) — Palantir's old playbook of customer-embedded engineers shipping production code — has become the AI industry default in twelve months. Anthropic and OpenAI both spun up FDE-style teams in late 2025; per the May 12 Information report, Google is now hiring hundreds. Salary bands: ~$238K average, high-$400Ks for senior, Cloud bands typically anchoring the top. The structural read: frontier labs can't scale enterprise deployment with traditional sales engineering — the model itself is the product *and* the bottleneck, so the engineers who deploy it have to live in the customer's codebase.

- **[How open model ecosystems compound](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/how-open-model-ecosystems-compound)** — Nathan Lambert, Interconnects (May 12, still the freshest substantive Lambert essay). _Takeaway:_ Pairs Ai2's Olmo 3 cost breakdown with Epoch AI's frontier-lab study to argue that ~80% of frontier-model compute is spent on R&D (not the final pretraining run). Implication: in an ecosystem where everyone publishes openly — i.e. China — the *next* training run starts with the previous lab's R&D priced in. The structural argument behind the Chinese open-model release cadence, and the load-bearing essay behind every "China is closer than the benchmark gap suggests" piece this month.

- **[How to Work and Compound with AI](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/working-with-ai/)** — Eugene Yan (May 2026). _Takeaway:_ Five principles for getting AI work to compound rather than reset: (1) provide good context, (2) encode your taste as config, (3) make verification easy, (4) delegate bigger tasks, (5) close the loop. The "encode taste as config" principle is the underrated one — it's the practical operationalization of the context-engineering thesis, and the bridge between one-shot prompting and durable agentic workflows.

- **[Everything we shipped at Interrupt 2026](https://www.langchain.com/blog/interrupt-2026-overview)** — LangChain blog (May 13–14 conference recap). _Takeaway:_ The most concrete "agents in production" debrief of the month. Four shipped products: LangSmith Engine (auto-clusters production failures and opens PRs with fixes), SmithDB (purpose-built trace DB, 15x faster on core LangSmith workloads), Managed Deep Agents (hosted runtime for the open-source Deep Agents harness — planning, subagent delegation, file I/O, long-running threads), and Sandboxes GA (hardware-virtualized microVMs with durable threads, checkpointing, and human-in-the-loop). LangChain Labs is a new applied-research effort on continual learning from agent traces.

- **[Inside the AI Index: 12 Takeaways from the 2026 Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/inside-the-ai-index-12-takeaways-from-the-2026-report)** — Stanford HAI. _Takeaway:_ The numbers that will be quoted for the next 12 months. Global corporate AI investment $581.7B in 2025 (+130% YoY). GenAI hit 53% global population adoption in three years (faster than the PC or the internet). U.S. consumer surplus from GenAI ~$172B/yr by early 2026. Two awkward findings: the Foundation Model Transparency Index *dropped* from 58 to 40, and only 23% of the U.S. public expects AI to positively affect their work versus 73% of AI experts. AI datacenter power capacity now 29.6 GW — roughly all of New York State at peak demand.

- **[Inside Anthropic's 2026 Developer Conference](https://every.to/chain-of-thought/inside-anthropic-s-2026-developer-conference)** — Every / Chain of Thought (May 7, updated May 17). _Takeaway:_ Still the single best post-conference debrief on Code w/ Claude. The headline isn't the SpaceX/Colossus capacity deal — it's the structural shift: doubled rate limits across Pro/Max, peak-hour throttles removed, API limits up ~17x on some tiers, plus Claude Managed Agents. Reads the announcements as Anthropic finally collapsing the dev-vs-capacity friction that fueled three months of complaints.

- **[AI Agents Must Follow the Law](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/ai-agents-must-follow-the-law)** — Lawfare (May 15). _Takeaway:_ Before granting AI agents access to government or enterprise workflows, the deployer needs a mechanism to verify the agent will obey law *even when instructed not to* — tort liability and fines after the fact don't scale to agentic deployments. Frames law-following AI (LFAI) as a deployment precondition rather than an alignment afterthought. Reads as the conceptual setup for the Joel Christoph EU enforcement piece above.

### model-context-protocol / mcp
- **[The Model Context Protocol Crisis: What 30 CVEs Teach Us About Building Secure AI Agents](https://medium.com/@khayyam.h/the-model-context-protocol-crisis-what-30-cves-teach-us-about-building-secure-ai-agents-95e16497d249)** — Khayyam H. (May 2026). Anatomy of the 30+ MCP CVEs filed Jan–Feb 2026: 43% exec/shell injection, 20% client/proxy infra, 13% auth bypass, plus a CVSS 9.6 RCE in a package with 437K downloads. Root cause: MCP was designed for functionality, not security — auth optional, encryption not required, no standard tool-level access control. Required reading for anyone deploying MCP servers.
- **[From toy AI to production: the MCP problem no one talks about, and how to save 90% on costs](https://medium.com/predict/from-toy-ai-to-production-the-mcp-problem-no-one-talks-about-and-how-to-save-90-on-costs-bb9b6843d329)** — Fabio Matricardi, Predict (May 2026). The cost angle: MCP servers cheerfully replay tool schemas into every turn's context window, and most teams discover the bill before they discover the gateway/caching pattern that fixes it.
- **[All you need is a MCP gateway — part 1](https://medium.com/artificial-intel-ligence-playground/all-you-need-is-a-mcp-gateway-part-1-4e5b73591b3a)** — Fabio Matricardi (May 2026). The "how to fix it" companion: a gateway sits between agent and MCP servers, caches schemas, multiplexes auth, and enforces tool-level allowlists. The pattern enterprise MCP rollouts are converging on.
