## Major announcements & releases

**Google I/O 2026 keynote: Gemini 3.5 Flash, Gemini Omni, Gemini Spark, and the agent-payments stack** — At Mountain View on May 19, Sundar Pichai opened I/O with the Gemini 3.5 family centered on **Gemini 3.5 Flash** (default model across Google services, claimed 4x faster than comparable frontier models at less than half the cost, beating Gemini 3.1 Pro on coding/agentic benchmarks); **Gemini Omni Flash**, a unified any-input-to-video generation model rolling to Gemini app, Google Flow, and YouTube Shorts (free for Shorts users, paid for Gemini app); and **Gemini Spark**, a "24/7 personal agent" running on dedicated Cloud VMs, built on Gemini 3.5 Flash plus an Antigravity-derived harness, with deep Gmail/Docs/Slides integration. Google reports Gemini at 900M+ monthly users across 230 countries (up from 400M a year ago). _Why it matters:_ this is Google's strongest model-narrative answer of the year — Flash + Omni + Spark closes the multimodal-by-architecture gap with OpenAI/Anthropic, and the Spark/AP2 combination is Google's first credible play to own the consumer agent surface ahead of any Apple/OpenAI hardware. **Agent Payments Protocol (AP2) ships with 60+ partners — Universal Cart + cryptographically-signed Mandates** — Alongside Gemini Spark, Google formally launched **AP2**, an open protocol for agent-initiated payments built on cryptographically-signed Mandates (verifiable credentials acting as tamper-proof user instructions), with 60+ launch partners including Adyen, American Express, Ant International, Coinbase, Etsy, Intuit, JCB, Mastercard, PayPal, Revolut, **Salesforce**, **ServiceNow**, UnionPay, and Worldpay. AP2 lands first in Spark, then Google products in coming months, paired with **Universal Cart** — a cross-merchant cart in Search, Gemini, YouTube and Gmail (US summer launch) that auto-tracks deals and stock. _Why it matters:_ this is the first time a frontier lab has shipped a complete agent-commerce stack — protocol + checkout surface + identity primitives — with credit-card networks, PayPal **and** crypto rails as named co-signers. If AP2 sticks, it becomes the default plumbing for agentic checkout across the web. [Google Cloud — Announcing AP2](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-agents-to-payments-ap2-protocol) ([GitHub: google-agentic-commerce/AP2](https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2), [Search Engine Land on Universal Cart](https://searchengineland.com/google-search-universal-cart-expands-ucp-and-ap2-477989))

**Claude Managed Agents: self-hosted sandboxes + MCP tunnels — enterprise data never leaves the perimeter** — Anthropic shipped two enterprise-defining features for Claude Managed Agents on May 19: (1) **Self-hosted sandboxes** — tool execution runs inside the customer's own infrastructure (or with managed providers Cloudflare, Daytona, Modal, Vercel), while only the orchestration/error-recovery loop stays on Anthropic infra; (2) **MCP tunnels** — agents reach private MCP servers through a single outbound, end-to-end-encrypted gateway with no inbound firewall rules and no public endpoints. Self-hosted sandboxes are in public beta; MCP tunnels in research preview. _Why it matters:_ this is the missing enterprise-deployability story for agent platforms — sensitive data (codebases, internal APIs, ticketing systems) never leaves the customer perimeter, which directly unblocks regulated industries (finance, healthcare, defense) that have been holding Claude Code and Managed Agents out of production. Pairs structurally with the Stainless acquisition: Anthropic now owns BOTH the SDK/MCP generation pipeline AND the private-network MCP runtime. [Claude — New in Claude Managed Agents](https://claude.com/blog/claude-managed-agents-updates) ([The New Stack analysis](https://thenewstack.io/anthropic-mcp-tunnels-sandboxes/), [the-decoder coverage](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-adds-self-hosted-sandboxes-and-mcp-tunnels-to-claude-managed-agents/))

**Antigravity 2.0 + Antigravity CLI + Antigravity SDK — Google's agent-IDE standalone** — Google announced **Antigravity 2.0**, a standalone desktop app that is the central home for agentic developer workflows, introducing **dynamic subagents** (parallelized workflows), **scheduled tasks** for background automation, and tight integrations with Google AI Studio, Android Studio and Firebase. Google also shipped **Antigravity CLI** (Gemini CLI is being deprecated into Antigravity CLI) and the **Antigravity SDK** for hosting custom agents on third-party infrastructure. **CodeMender** — DeepMind's autonomous code-security agent (72 upstreamed fixes across 4.5M-line codebases over 6 months) — is now integrated into Antigravity. _Why it matters:_ Google is making the same vertical-stack play Anthropic made with Claude Code: own the agentic IDE, the CLI, the SDK and the security-review agent. Subagent orchestration plus background scheduling moves Antigravity from "Gemini in an editor" to a credible Claude-Code / Cursor competitor at the developer-platform layer. [SiliconANGLE coverage](https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/19/google-accelerates-agent-native-software-development-expanded-antigravity-platform/) ([Google Developers Blog — Antigravity CLI transition](https://developers.googleblog.com/an-important-update-transitioning-gemini-cli-to-antigravity-cli/), [DeepMind — CodeMender](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-codemender-an-ai-agent-for-code-security/))

**Recursive Superintelligence emerges with $650M at $4.65B — Richard Socher / Tim Rocktäschel "self-improving AI" lab** — Recursive Superintelligence, the London-based startup co-founded by Richard Socher (ex-Salesforce chief scientist) and Tim Rocktäschel (UCL, ex-DeepMind), exited stealth with a $650M raise at a $4.65B valuation led by GV (Google's VC arm) and Greycroft, with participation from **Nvidia** and **AMD**. The team includes Yuandong Tian (Meta FAIR director), Alexey Dosovitskiy (Vision Transformer co-author), Josh Tobin (ex-OpenAI), Caiming Xiong, Tim Shi, and Jeff Clune; Peter Norvig is an adviser. Thesis: build a system that can autonomously identify its own weaknesses, design fixes, and apply them — "recursively self-improving AI" without human intervention. _Why it matters:_ this is the largest stealth-out raise of any "neolab" research vehicle in 2026, and the explicit self-improvement framing — paired with Nvidia + AMD both on the cap table — makes Recursive the most-watched test of whether a research-first lab can compete with OpenAI/Anthropic/Google on capability rather than distribution. **EU AI Act Omnibus political agreement (May 7) — extended HRAIS deadlines, nudifier ban, GPAI guidance** — The EU AI Act **Omnibus** political agreement of May 7, 2026 (covered widely through the 19th) makes the largest set of changes to the AI Act since adoption: extended compliance deadlines for high-risk AI systems (HRAIS) embedded in regulated products to **2 August 2028**, a new prohibition on "nudifier" applications generating intimate AI-generated content, removal of certain industrial applications from scope, and Article 50 transparency-obligation guidelines draft (also May 7). Enforcement (fines up to 3% global turnover or €15M) takes effect August 2, 2026. _Why it matters:_ extends the runway for European industrial AI deployers, formally redefines "high-risk" boundaries, and codifies the nudifier ban — the first piece of substantive AI-Act amendment with real downstream implications for both vendors and deployers. [Latham & Watkins analysis](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/ai-act-update-eu-resolves-to-change-rules-and-extend-deadlines) ([European Commission AI Act framework](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai), [GPAI Code of Practice](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai))
