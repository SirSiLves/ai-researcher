## Major announcements & releases

- **Anthropic Project Glasswing — Claude Mythos has flagged 10,000+ critical/high vulnerabilities in its first 30 days; program expanding to additional partners** — Anthropic published its first Project Glasswing progress update (covered widely May 25-26): Claude Mythos Preview, the dedicated vulnerability-detection model launched in April 2026, has identified more than 10,000 high- or critical-severity vulnerabilities across partner codebases in its first month. Partner specifics: Cloudflare 2,000 bugs (400 H/C), Mozilla 271 Firefox vulnerabilities (10x improvement over earlier Claude generations). Mythos itself estimates 6,202 H/C bugs in open-source projects alone (23,019 total including medium/low). The program is being extended to additional partners and to US + allied governments; Anthropic plans broader GA of Mythos-class models once safeguards mature. The bottleneck Anthropic explicitly calls out: maintainer/patch capacity, not detection — fixing the bugs is now slower than finding them. _Why it matters:_ this is the first hard production-scale evidence that frontier models materially compress the offensive-security stack, and it lands in the same week as the AISI Frontier AI Trends Report warning that oversight degrades as capabilities scale. Expect this number to feature in every EU AI Act high-risk consultation comment, every NIST AI RMF discussion, and every US executive-branch briefing on AI + critical infrastructure from now through Q3. The patch-capacity gap is also the new structural argument for paid maintainer programs, code-signing reform, and AI-assisted triage on the defender side. [https://www.anthropic.com/research/glasswing-initial-update](https://www.anthropic.com/research/glasswing-initial-update) (also [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/05/26/anthropic-project-glasswing-update/) and [CSO Online](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4176865/project-glasswing-has-uncovered-10000-vulnerabilities-anthropic.html))

- **Pope Leo XIV publishes first encyclical "Magnifica Humanitas" on AI — Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah co-presents at the Vatican; 42,300-word text warns of AI-fueled warfare and concentration of power** — Released May 25 (Pope's signature dated May 15, the 135th anniversary of Leo XIII's *Rerum Novarum*): the 42,300-word encyclical is Pope Leo XIV's most sweeping statement on AI to date. It urges governments, corporations and individuals to slow the pace of development, ensure AI remains under ethical and political oversight, and protect human dignity from "new forms of dehumanization." Specific warning: AI in autonomous warfare and in the concentration of power. The Vatican invited Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah to co-present at the Vatican release — a deliberate choice over OpenAI, Google, or Microsoft. _Why it matters:_ a 1.4-billion-member moral institution now has a formal doctrinal position on AI that EU regulators, ethics committees, and national legislatures will cite for years. The Anthropic on-stage role lands the same week Anthropic closes its $30B round at >$900B valuation and ships Claude's enterprise-compliance stack — Anthropic is being positioned (and is positioning itself) as the "credible safety-first lab" at the exact moment that procurement battle is being fought in regulated industries. The CNN angle (AI fueling warfare) will also be cited in the EU AI Act Article 6 high-risk consultation. - **AISI (UK) inaugural Frontier AI Trends Report headline-cycles further — biology PhD-surpassing models, cyber task-doubling every 8 months, self-replication evaluations 5% → 60% in two years** — The UK AI Security Institute's first public Frontier AI Trends Report (PDF + factsheet, published the week of May 22) is now anchoring this week's governance discussion. Key numbers: frontier models have far surpassed PhD-level expertise in biology QA since 2024, with chemistry catching up fast; apprentice-level cyber-task success rose 9% → 50% in two years; autonomous task duration is doubling roughly every 8 months and accelerating; self-replication evaluation pass rates have risen from under 5% to over 60% in two years. Safeguard side: between 2024-2025, Model B required ~40x more expert effort than its predecessor to jailbreak. AISI also published a supplementary report identifying 20+ pathways by which AI oversight techniques will degrade as systems advance. The New York Times profiled AISI's £360M frontier-model red-team operation in the same window. _Why it matters:_ this is the first time a government safety institute has issued a quantified trend report (not an advisory) arguing that oversight effectiveness is degrading. Combined with the Vatican encyclical and the Glasswing 10,000-vuln number, it gives EU AI Act consultation respondents a single citation pack for the "high-risk classification must be broad" argument. UK AISI is also becoming the de facto reference institute Western regulators cite — ahead of NIST and the EU AI Office. - **Anthropic puts programmatic Claude usage on a separate meter — Agent SDK, Claude Code GitHub Actions, claude-p, third-party-built agents move to fixed monthly credits at API list price starting June 15** — Announced May 13 via direct email to Max 20x subscribers; coverage and developer reaction cresting this week (May 25-26 InfoWorld + The New Stack pieces). Effective June 15, programmatic Claude usage is split off subscription pools and gets its own non-rollover monthly credit billed at API rates: Pro $20, Max 5x $100, Max 20x $200. Covered surfaces: Claude Agent SDK, `claude -p`, Claude Code GitHub Actions, any third-party app built on the Agent SDK. Interactive Claude Code, Claude Cowork and standard chat are unaffected. Workload math from independent analysts: effective price increases 12x to 175x depending on workload pattern (heavy-tool-use agentic loops are worst-hit). _Why it matters:_ this is the second billing restructure Anthropic has shipped in three months and the clearest signal yet that subscription-priced agent automation was structurally money-losing for Anthropic. For ISVs that built freemium agent products on top of Claude subscriptions (a huge segment after Code w/ Claude SF), the unit economics are now broken — expect a wave of "we're moving to API/usage-based billing" announcements from agent startups in June. Cursor, Cline, Aider, and Continue developer-tool teams are most exposed; OpenAI is the most obvious near-term beneficiary. [https://www.infoworld.com/article/4171274/anthropic-puts-claude-agents-on-a-meter-across-its-subscriptions.html](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4171274/anthropic-puts-claude-agents-on-a-meter-across-its-subscriptions.html) (also [The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/anthropic-agent-sdk-credits/) and [community FAQ + workload math](https://gist.github.com/MagnaCapax/d9177e35b355853f03c730dfcaa693ef))

- **EU AI Act consultation on high-risk classification draft guidelines now in active stakeholder phase — three draft documents under Article 6, deadline June 23, AI-content marking Code of Practice also in draft** — Carry-over with this week's update: the European Commission's draft guidelines on classifying high-risk AI systems under Article 6 (published May 19) are now in active stakeholder consultation through 23 June 2026, 22:00 CET. Three documents cover general high-risk principles, Article 6(1) + Annex I (AI as a product/safety component), and Article 6(2) + Annex III (the eight use-case categories: biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services incl. credit, law enforcement, migration, justice/democracy). The guidance is non-binding (CJEU has final interpretive authority). Bird & Bird and Hunton have both published first-read analyses noting significant uncertainty remains on AI-as-safety-component edge cases. Updated effective dates per the May 7 Omnibus political agreement still apply: stand-alone HRAIS December 2, 2027; embedded-in-products HRAIS August 2, 2028. _Why it matters:_ this is the live battleground for everyone running HR, credit-scoring, medical, or critical-infrastructure AI in Europe. SAP, Microsoft, Mistral, Cohere and the AI Pact signatories are lobbying actively. Combined with the Vatican + AISI reports landing the same week, EU consultation responses will be unusually well-armed with citations. [https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/consultations/targeted-consultation-draft-guidelines-classification-high-risk-artificial-intelligence-systems](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/consultations/targeted-consultation-draft-guidelines-classification-high-risk-artificial-intelligence-systems) (also [Bird & Bird first-read](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/the-commission's-draft-high-risk-ai-guidelines-under-the-eu-ai-act-a-first-read) and [heise.de German coverage](https://www.heise.de/news/Bruessel-ordnet-Algorithmen-EU-Kommission-konkretisiert-die-Hochrisiko-KI-11301376.html))
