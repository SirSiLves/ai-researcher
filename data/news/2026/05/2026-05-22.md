**OpenAI reasoning model disproves an 80-year-old Erdős conjecture in discrete geometry — verified by Alon, Wood, Bloom** — OpenAI announced (May 20, widely covered through May 22) that an internal general-purpose reasoning model independently disproved Paul Erdős's 1946 planar unit-distance conjecture, producing an infinite family of point configurations whose unit-distance count grows as n^(1+δ) with δ strictly above zero — polynomially faster than the long-conjectured ceiling. The proof used algebraic number theory (infinite class field towers, Golod-Shafarevich theorem), tools from one field to disprove a conjecture from another, and was independently verified by Noga Alon (Princeton), Melanie Wood, and Thomas Bloom. _Why it matters:_ this is the first time a general-purpose reasoning model (not a math-specialized system) has produced a verified novel result against a major open problem in combinatorial geometry. As an evidence point in the "AI accelerates discovery" claim that Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and OpenAI all need for their IPO narratives, it lands in exactly the right week. It's also a quiet signal on the capability-vs-cost debate — pure frontier-capability progress is still happening on the frontier side while Chinese mid-tier costs collapse. [OpenAI — model disproves discrete geometry conjecture](https://openai.com/index/model-disproves-discrete-geometry-conjecture/) ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/openai-paul-erdos-geometry-problem-cracked), [AIToolly](https://aitoolly.com/ai-news/article/2026-05-21-openai-reasoning-model-disproves-longstanding-erds-conjecture-in-discrete-geometry), [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/blog/openai-model-disproved-80-year-old-erdos-conjecture))
