## Major announcements & releases

**Anthropic confidentially files for a US IPO** — Anthropic said on June 1 it has confidentially filed a draft registration statement with the SEC for a US initial public offering, teeing up what could be a watershed listing for the AI sector. The filing follows its late-May $65B Series H at a $965B post-money valuation (up from $380B in February), which moved it ahead of OpenAI as the most valuable AI startup. A confidential filing keeps the S-1 private for ~60–90 days, so the public prospectus would land in late summer. _Why it matters:_ The first frontier lab to formally start the IPO clock — OpenAI is reported to be days/weeks behind — opening a blockbuster AI listing wave and the first real public-market test of frontier-lab economics. [https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-06-01/ai-giant-anthropic-confidentially-files-for-us-ipo]

**GitHub Copilot moves to usage-based billing (live today)** — As of June 1, all GitHub Copilot plans shift from premium-request units to "GitHub AI Credits" (1 credit = $0.01), consumed against published per-model token rates for input, output, and cached tokens. Pro+ includes $39/mo in credits, Business stays $19/user with $19 in credits, Enterprise $39/user with $39 in credits; code completions and next-edit suggestions stay unlimited and unmetered. Existing Business/Enterprise customers get promotional included usage for June–August, and credits pool across the org. _Why it matters:_ The clearest signal yet that the all-you-can-eat AI coding subscription is dead — escalating agentic-session inference cost is forcing the whole category onto metered pricing, and developers are bracing for "meter shock." [https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/]

**SAP blocks external AI agents while Salesforce and ServiceNow open up** — A widening enterprise-platform split came into focus: SAP's API Policy v4/2026 (§2.2.2) prohibits AI systems that independently schedule or execute API calls, forcing any external agent (Microsoft Copilot for Finance, Salesforce Agentforce, ServiceNow agents) to route through SAP's own Joule agents under A2A. Salesforce is going the opposite way — headless, MCP-first, welcoming third-party agents as first-class users — while ServiceNow's "Action Fabric" lets external agents in but mediates and meters them. _Why it matters:_ The agent-interoperability fight is now a platform-strategy war: SAP betting on gatekeeping via Joule, Salesforce betting on open MCP, with CIOs caught choosing which agent stack their data lives behind. [https://www.techzine.eu/blogs/applications/141323/sap-blocks-external-ai-agents-salesforce-and-servicenow-dont/]

**ServiceNow, SAP and Workday move to meter and charge for AI-agent access** — Building on the same shift, the big workflow/ERP platforms are turning third-party agent access into a billable resource: ServiceNow's Action Fabric meters operations an external agent completes and charges customers per usage; SAP and Workday are putting comparable "pay-to-play" gates on agent access to their systems of record. _Why it matters:_ As autonomous agents become the primary consumers of enterprise APIs, the platforms that own the data are monetizing agent traffic directly — a new enterprise-AI revenue line and a cost variable every agent-deployment plan now has to budget for. [https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/servicenow-sap-and-workday-make-ai-agents-pay-to-play/]

**Decart raises $300M at ~$4B valuation for AI-optimization software and world models** — Israeli AI firm Decart closed a $300M round (led by Radical Ventures, with NVIDIA, eBay/Adobe/Toyota Ventures, Atreides, Valor, and existing backers Sequoia, Zeev, Benchmark) at roughly a $4B valuation, with angels including Andrej Karpathy. The company builds inference-optimization software and real-time generative "world models." _Why it matters:_ NVIDIA-backed and Karpathy-endorsed, it's one of the louder bets that world models / real-time generative environments — not just chat LLMs — are the next infra frontier. [https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sjt9ncukgl]

**Exa raises $250M Series C at $2.2B for AI-native search/retrieval infrastructure** — AI-search startup Exa (a16z-led round) raised $250M at a $2.2B valuation to scale its "search engine for AI" — retrieval infrastructure designed to be consumed by agents and RAG pipelines rather than humans. _Why it matters:_ A rare large retrieval-layer raise; as agents do more autonomous web/document lookup, purpose-built agent-facing search is emerging as its own infrastructure category distinct from consumer search. [https://www.finsmes.com/2026/05/exa-labs-raises-250m-in-series-c-funding-at-2-2-billion-valuation.html]
