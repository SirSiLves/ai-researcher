## Major announcements & releases

**EU AI Act: Parliament adopts the "digital omnibus" — high-risk obligations slip to Dec 2027 / Aug 2028, nudifier ban added.** The European Parliament adopted its position on the simplification (omnibus) proposal amending the AI Act by a lopsided 569–45 (23 abstentions). Stand-alone high-risk systems in Annex III (biometrics, critical infrastructure, education, employment, law enforcement, border management) move from the 2 August 2026 deadline to 2 December 2027; high-risk AI embedded as safety components in products covered by EU sectoral law moves to 2 August 2028. The package also adds a new Article 5 prohibition banning AI that generates CSAM or non-consensual intimate/sexually explicit images of identifiable persons ("nudifier" apps), with compliance required by 2 December 2026. Parliament and Council still need to formally adopt the provisional deal, which they intend to finalize before 2 August 2026. _Why it matters:_ This is the single biggest reset of the EU AI Act timeline since it entered into force — most high-risk obligations enterprises were racing toward in August 2026 just moved ~16 months out, even as GPAI enforcement and the new nudifier ban tighten. [https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20260427IPR42011/ai-act-deal-on-simplification-measures-ban-on-nudifier-apps] (timeline: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/)

**General Intuition raises $320M Series A at a $2.3B valuation to train action/world models on gameplay data.** The round was led by Khosla Ventures with General Catalyst, Hillspire, Jeff Bezos, Eric Schmidt and DeepMind/MIT researchers participating, bringing total funding to ~$454M. Spun out of Medal (17M MAU game-clip sharing), General Intuition trains large action foundation models on action-labeled gameplay — records of exactly which buttons a player pressed and when — and is building world models to generate training environments for AI agents and robots. A first product is expected late summer / early autumn 2026, with a Series B already in motion. _Why it matters:_ A serious bet that gameplay footage, not internet text, is the most underrated training corpus for spatial-temporal reasoning — and a route to embodied/agentic models that learn from demonstrated actions rather than language. [https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/general-intuitions-2-3b-bet-that-video-games-can-train-ai-agents-for-the-real-world/] (also: https://www.axios.com/2026/06/26/general-intuition-ai-gaming)

**Baseten closes a $1.5B Series F at up to a $13B valuation as inference becomes AI's most contested layer.** The round was co-led by Altimeter, Conviction, Spark Capital, Sands Capital and Wellington (with IVP, Greylock, Battery, D.E. Shaw Ventures and others), structured in two tranches at $13B and $11B — a ~160% valuation jump from the $5B Series E just five months earlier. Baseten reports ~$600M annualized revenue (up ~1,900% YoY) and says it now serves more than one billion inference requests per day across 87 clusters in 18 cloud environments. _Why it matters:_ Inference serving — not training — is where enterprise spend and venture capital are now concentrating; Baseten's multi-cloud, model-agnostic posture is a direct bet that the serving layer is where margins and lock-in will sit. [https://www.baseten.co/blog/announcing-our-series-f/] (coverage: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/18/ai-inference-startup-baseten-reportedly-raising-1-5b-months-after-its-last-mega-round/)

**Groq raises $650M to scale its LPU-based AI inference cloud after NVIDIA's licensing deal.** Led by Disruptive and Infinitum (with reinvesting backers), the growth round funds global expansion of Groq's inference cloud toward 200 MW of capacity by 2027; the company runs 13 data centers across North America, Europe, the Middle East and APAC, serves 5M+ developers, and processes trillions of tokens weekly. The raise lands ~6 months after NVIDIA signed a non-exclusive license for Groq technology and hired several key executives. _Why it matters:_ Reinforces the same thesis as Baseten — specialized inference economics are now a fundable, multi-billion-dollar category — and shows Groq turning an NVIDIA technology deal into independent cloud momentum rather than absorption. [https://groq.com/newsroom/groq-raises-usd650m-to-scale-its-ai-inference-cloud-business] (coverage: https://siliconangle.com/2026/06/22/inference-chip-startup-groq-raises-650m-grow-cloud-platform/)

**Qualcomm reportedly in advanced talks to acquire AI-chip startup Tenstorrent for $8–10B.** Tenstorrent, the RISC-V-based AI accelerator company led by chip veteran Jim Keller, is the target of early acquisition discussions that would hand Qualcomm a credible AI-training/inference silicon line and a seat at the hardware table currently dominated by NVIDIA and AMD. _Why it matters:_ Another sign that the AI-silicon market is consolidating fast — a Qualcomm–Tenstorrent deal at this scale would create a new vertically integrated challenger built on open RISC-V rather than proprietary GPU architectures. [https://techcrunch.com/category/artificial-intelligence/] (background: Tenstorrent in sources.json hot-event watch since 2026-06-21)
