## Major announcements & releases

**OpenAI tells staff a new model is coming and an IPO is planned within a year — but recursive self-improvement could delay it** — Sam Altman briefed staff that OpenAI is readying a new frontier model (referred to internally as "5.6") and intends to pursue an IPO within roughly the next year, having already confidentially submitted a draft S-1. In the same message he flagged that if rapid recursive self-improvement (RSI) takeoff looks near — which he put at potentially "less than six months" away — it could be strategically advantageous to stay private rather than list. _Why it matters:_ A frontier lab is now openly tying its capital-markets timeline to an AGI-capability milestone, and is signaling another model release on the heels of GPT-5.5/Codex — capability cadence and governance posture are colliding in public. [https://fortune.com/2026/06/07/openai-superapp-pivot-chatbot-agentic-ai-ipo-codex-chatgpt/] (context: https://cryptobriefing.com/openai-ipo-delay-recursive-self-improvement/)

**Databricks targets a $165–175B valuation in a new round** — Databricks is reportedly in talks for a fresh funding round that could begin within the month and value the company at $165–175B, up sharply from the $134B it reached in late 2025. The company cites strong AI-driven demand, surpassing a $5.4B revenue run rate in February (up 65% YoY) with more than $1.4B of that run rate attributed to AI; CEO Ali Ghodsi has continued to favor private capital over a public listing. _Why it matters:_ The data+AI platform layer is still attracting frontier-lab-scale private capital, and Databricks' valuation trajectory is a barometer for enterprise willingness to pay for governed data-to-agent infrastructure. [https://www.pymnts.com/news/investment-tracker/2026/databricks-targets-165-billion-valuation-in-new-funding-round/]

**EU AI Act: final Code of Practice on AI-content marking & labelling due this month; GPAI Code now has ~24 signatories** — The European Commission's final Code of Practice implementing Article 50 (machine-readable marking of AI-generated content and clear labelling of deepfakes/AI text on matters of public interest) is slated for publication in June 2026; signing it grants a presumption of conformity with Article 50. Separately, the GPAI Code of Practice now counts roughly 24 signatories — including Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, Mistral, IBM, Cohere, Aleph Alpha, Amazon and ServiceNow — while Meta has declined and xAI signed only the Safety & Security chapter. _Why it matters:_ With high-risk obligations and the Commission's first enforcement actions both landing 2 August 2026, the transparency Code is the concrete compliance artifact GenAI providers must align to in the next 7 weeks. [https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-publishes-first-draft-code-practice-marking-and-labelling-ai-generated-content] (signatory status: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/)
