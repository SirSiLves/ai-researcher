## Major announcements & releases

**OpenAI previews the GPT‑5.6 series (Sol, Terra, Luna).** OpenAI began a limited preview of GPT‑5.6: Sol (flagship), Terra (a balanced everyday tier priced ~2x cheaper than GPT‑5.5), and Luna (lowest-cost fast tier). The new naming separates generation (the number) from durable capability tiers (Sol/Terra/Luna), and introduces a `max` reasoning effort plus an `ultra` mode that spawns subagents for complex work. Notably, at the U.S. government's request the launch is gated to a small set of trusted partners via API and Codex first — OpenAI explicitly pushed back on government-access-as-default while it works on a cyber Executive Order framework. Pricing per 1M tokens: Sol $5/$30, Terra $2.50/$15, Luna $1/$6; Sol also lands on Cerebras at up to 750 tok/s in July. _Why it matters:_ A frontier-tier release paired with the most aggressive safeguard stack OpenAI has shipped, and the first model launch shaped by a government pre-clearance process — a precedent the whole industry will watch. [https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/]

**OpenAI and Broadcom unveil "Jalapeño," OpenAI's first custom inference chip.** The two companies revealed Jalapeño, an LLM-optimized inference ASIC co-developed from design to tape-out in ~9 months — claimed to be among the fastest high-performance ASIC cycles ever. It targets inference (not training), promises performance-per-watt "substantially better" than current state of the art, and is positioned as the first in a multi-generation compute platform, with initial deployment aimed for end of 2026. _Why it matters:_ A direct move to own the inference stack and reduce NVIDIA dependence; the first concrete silicon from the OpenAI–Broadcom partnership. [https://openai.com/index/openai-broadcom-jalapeno-inference-chip/] (coverage: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/24/openai-and-broadcom-reveal-jalapeno-first-ai-chip-in-partnership.html)

**Mistral releases OCR 4, a SOTA document-intelligence model.** Mistral OCR 4 adds bounding boxes, block classification, and inline confidence scores, covers 170 languages across 10 language groups, and runs in a single self-hostable container as an ingestion layer for enterprise search, RAG, and domain retrieval pipelines. Independent annotators preferred it over every leading OCR/document-AI system tested (avg 72% win rate), and it tops OlmOCRBench (85.20). _Why it matters:_ Document ingestion is the unglamorous bottleneck of most enterprise RAG; a self-hostable SOTA OCR with confidence scores is a meaningful piece of retrieval infrastructure. [https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/]

**SpaceX signs a compute deal worth up to $6.3B with open-source lab Reflection AI.** Reflection AI (valued ~$25B) will pay SpaceX ~$150M/month starting July 1 for NVIDIA GB300 capacity at the Colossus 2 data center near Memphis — totaling ~$6.3B if it runs through 2029, with a 90-day termination clause after an initial three-month commitment. Reflection, founded by ex-DeepMind engineers Misha Laskin and Ioannis Antonoglou, plans to train and serve frontier open-source models. _Why it matters:_ SpaceX is turning Colossus into a commercial compute platform (recent deals with Anthropic, Google, Cursor), and an open-weights lab locking in GB300 capacity at this scale signals serious frontier ambitions for open models. [https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/22/spacex-ai-colossus-data-center-reflection.html] (also: https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/22/spacex-inks-compute-deal-with-reflection-ai-an-open-source-ai-lab/)

**The retrieval layer is shifting from "RAG pipeline" to agent-native knowledge/context infrastructure.** New entrants are reframing retrieval around agents rather than humans: Redis launched **Iris**, a context-and-memory platform that auto-generates MCP tools from business data models and adds an agent memory server on Redis Flex; Pinecone introduced **KnowQL**, a declarative query language for agents with six primitives (intent, filter, provenance, output shape, confidence, budget). Buyer intent for hybrid retrieval reportedly tripled (≈10% → 33%) in Q1, with retrieval optimization overtaking eval as the top enterprise investment priority. _Why it matters:_ Vendors are converging on multi-step, MCP-aware "context architecture" as the successor to single-shot vector RAG — a structural change in how enterprises wire knowledge into agents. [https://venturebeat.com/data/context-architecture-is-replacing-rag-as-agentic-ai-pushes-enterprise-retrieval-to-its-limits]

**EU AI Act: marking/labelling Code of Practice published as the August enforcement runway closes.** The Commission published (June 10) a dedicated Code of Practice on the marking and labelling of AI-generated content, distinct from the general-purpose AI Code. It arrives ahead of August 2, 2026, when the Commission's enforcement powers over GPAI model providers — including fines — enter into application, with the AI Office already running compliance assessments and engaging providers. _Why it matters:_ Concrete transparency guidance plus a hard enforcement date make Q3 2026 the moment EU AI governance moves from voluntary alignment to binding obligation for GPAI providers. [https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai] (tracker: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/)
