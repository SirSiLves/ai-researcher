## Major announcements & releases

**Meta ships Muse Spark 1.1 and opens the Meta Model API.** On July 9 Meta Superintelligence Labs released Muse Spark 1.1, a natively multimodal reasoning model (text, image, video, PDF, audio in → text out) built for agentic work, with a 1M-token context, built-in context compression, and native multi-agent orchestration (it can run as a primary agent delegating to subagents, or as a subagent). Meta launched it as a public preview on the new **Meta Model API** — its first real developer API — at $1.25/$4.25 per million input/output tokens, undercutting OpenAI and Anthropic on price. _Why it matters:_ this is the developer-facing sibling to last week's Muse Image, and it puts Meta directly into the crowded agentic-coding model race with price as the wedge. [https://ai.meta.com/blog/introducing-muse-spark-meta-model-api/](https://ai.meta.com/blog/introducing-muse-spark-meta-model-api/) (context: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/meta-enters-the-crowded-ai-coding-battle-with-muse-spark-1-1/), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/09/meta-launches-flagship-muse-spark-1-1-model-multi-agent-upgrades/))

**DeepSeek V4 graduates to general availability with industry-first peak/off-peak API pricing.** DeepSeek's mid-July official release converts the April V4 preview (V4-Pro, V4-Flash) to GA, standardizing a **1M-token context across the entire lineup** and adding stronger agentic task execution, math, and code. Alongside GA, DeepSeek introduced time-of-day API pricing — peak hours (09:00–12:00 and 14:00–18:00) billed at 2× the off-peak rate — and will retire the legacy `deepseek-chat`/`deepseek-reasoner` model names on July 24. _Why it matters:_ an open-weight (MIT) frontier model with a 1M context and demand-shaped pricing is a new competitive lever — DeepSeek is nudging load off peak hours the way cloud and power markets do. [https://api-docs.deepseek.com/news/news260424/](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/news/news260424/) (context: [TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/06/30/deepseek-to-launch-v4-in-mid-july-with-new-peak-time-api-pricing/))

**Apple sues OpenAI, alleging systematic trade-secret theft for its AI hardware.** In a July 10 federal suit (N.D. Cal.), Apple accuses OpenAI — and Jony Ive's io Products — of a coordinated campaign "at every level" to steal hardware trade secrets, claiming OpenAI's hardware chief Tang Tan (ex-Apple) directed Apple interviewees to bring "actual parts" for "show and tell," and that a departing engineer kept an Apple laptop loaded with confidential documents on unannounced products. OpenAI denied the allegations, saying it has "no interest in other companies' trade secrets." _Why it matters:_ a stunning reversal for the 2024 ChatGPT-in-iPhone partners, and the highest-profile IP fight yet over the race to build consumer AI hardware. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/10/apple-openai-lawsuit-trade-secrets.html) (also: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/10/apple-sues-openai-over-alleged-trade-secret-theft/), [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/07/10/apple-sues-openai-alleging-ai-company-stole-trade-secrets/)) _(vendor permalink not applicable — both firms are parties)_

**MCP publishes the 2026-07-28 spec Release Candidate — its largest revision since launch.** The Model Context Protocol RC (final ships July 28) rewrites the protocol around a **stateless core**: it removes sessions, drops the initialization handshake, and lets a remote MCP server run behind a plain round-robin load balancer with clients caching `tools/list` responses. It realigns authorization with **OAuth 2.1 / OpenID Connect** (MCP servers are now formally OAuth 2.1 resource servers), adds an extensions framework, and introduces **MCP Apps** (servers ship sandboxed interactive HTML UIs that hosts prefetch and security-review) plus a **Tasks** extension for long-running work, alongside a formal deprecation policy. _Why it matters:_ this is the update that makes MCP enterprise-ready — stateless scaling and real OAuth remove the two biggest blockers to running MCP servers in production. [https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/) (context: [WorkOS](https://workos.com/blog/mcp-2026-spec-agent-authentication))

**Microsoft launches Frontier Company, a $2.5B bet that "the next battle is deployment, not models."** Announced July 2, Microsoft Frontier Company is a new operating business embedding ~6,000 industry and engineering experts inside customer organizations to co-design, deploy, and continuously improve AI systems against measurable business outcomes — explicitly going "beyond Forward Deployed Engineering." It is led by new president Rodrigo Kede Lima and is deliberately **model-diverse** (OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft AI, open-source, or specialized models per scenario). _Why it matters:_ mirrors AWS's $1B Forward Deployed Engineering unit (June 30) — the hyperscalers are racing to own enterprise AI *delivery*, betting the bottleneck is now integration and change management, not raw model capability. [https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/07/02/microsoft-frontier-company-ai-engineering-that-amplifies-and-protects-your-intelligence/](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/07/02/microsoft-frontier-company-ai-engineering-that-amplifies-and-protects-your-intelligence/) (context: [BigDATAwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/2026/07/06/microsoft-launches-new-2-5b-ai-initiative-with-6000-experts-to-help-enterprises-deploy-a/))

**Snowflake opens Cortex Sense to private preview — a runtime knowledge layer for agents.** Entering private preview in mid-July, Cortex Sense is a runtime layer that automatically assembles data, business definitions, and operational knowledge for AI agents *at query time*. Snowflake reports an 86% accuracy benchmark on structured questions when agents have full business context, versus 24% with generic frontier models alone. _Why it matters:_ this is the retrieval/knowledge-infra frontier — enterprises are moving past vector-only RAG toward runtime context assembly, and the data-cloud vendors want to own that grounding layer. [https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/enterprise-ai-agents-grounded-context/](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/enterprise-ai-agents-grounded-context/)

**The non-GPU inference layer keeps shifting: Nvidia's Groq 3 LPU nears customer shipment; Cerebras trades above $40B.** The first product of Nvidia's ~$20B non-exclusive license of Groq's LPU architecture — the Nvidia Groq 3 LPU, on a Samsung 4nm process — is slated to reach customers in Q3 2026. Meanwhile Cerebras, public since its ~$5.6B May IPO, trades above a $40B market cap and is powering an AWS Bedrock inference solution; SambaNova says its new chips hit 600–700 tokens/sec versus ~250 for GPUs. _Why it matters:_ specialized inference silicon is consolidating (Nvidia co-opting Groq) while wafer-scale and dataflow challengers find hyperscaler homes — the serving layer beneath the agent boom is being repriced. [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/05/nvidia-groq-deal-ai-chip-startups-in-play/) (context: [SDxCentral](https://www.sdxcentral.com/analysis/cerebras-spins-nvidias-groq-tieup-as-proof-its-waferscale-bet-was-right/)) _(vendor permalink not found — aggregator sourcing)_
