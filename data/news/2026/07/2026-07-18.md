## Major announcements & releases

**Thinking Machines Lab ships Inkling — the leading U.S. open-weights model** — Mira Murati's lab released Inkling (Jul 15), its first in-house model: a multimodal Mixture-of-Experts transformer with 975B total / 41B active parameters, a 1M-token context window, and controllable "thinking effort," pretrained on 45T tokens of text/image/audio/video. Weights ship on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0 and the model is fine-tunable on Thinking Machines' Tinker platform; a lighter Inkling-Small (276B/12B active) preview accompanies it. It debuts at ~41 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index — the strongest open-weights release from a U.S. lab. _Why it matters:_ A credible U.S. counterweight to the Chinese open-weight surge, built explicitly as a customization base rather than a benchmark leader. [https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/introducing-inkling/]

**Moonshot AI releases Kimi K3, the largest open-weight model ever at 2.8T parameters** — China's Moonshot AI announced Kimi K3 (Jul 16), a 2.8-trillion-parameter MoE with a 1M-token context and native multimodal input, calling it the first "open 3T-class" model and taking the size crown from DeepSeek's V4. It debuted at #1 on the Frontend Code Arena (1679 Elo, past Claude Fable 5) and leads five of seven launch benchmarks; the API is live at $3/$15 per million tokens, with open weights promised by Jul 27. _Why it matters:_ Frontier-scale open weights out of China keep compressing the gap to closed U.S. labs despite export controls. [https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/moonshot-releases-2-8-trillion-parameter-kimi-k3] (no vendor permalink surfaced; also Simon Willison: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/16/kimi-k3/)

**TSMC posts record Q2 on AI-chip demand, lifts full-year outlook past 40%** — TSMC reported (Jul 16) Q2 net income of NT$706.56B (~$22.4B), up 77% YoY, on revenue of $40.2B (+36%), beating estimates for a fifth straight record quarter. High-performance computing (which includes AI accelerators) drove 66% of revenue; TSMC raised 2026 revenue growth guidance to >40%, hiked capex to $60–64B, and added $100B to its Arizona build-out. _Why it matters:_ The clearest hard-numbers signal that AI infrastructure demand is still accelerating, not plateauing. [https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20260716VL223/tsmc-revenue-profit-demand-2nm.html] (also Yahoo Finance: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/tsmc-q2-2026-earnings-record-112109987.html)

**Red Hat ships OpenShift 4.22 with expanded AI/agent tooling** — Red Hat made OpenShift 4.22 (Kubernetes 1.35, CRI-O 1.35) generally available (Jul 14), focused on cutting cloud infra cost, simplifying virtualized-workload operations, and securing sensitive data; the July migration toolkit 8.2 adds cross-cluster migration for stateless apps. It slots into the broader Red Hat AI Enterprise "metal-to-agents" stack announced earlier in 2026. _Why it matters:_ OpenShift is the hybrid-cloud substrate many enterprises actually run agents on, so its release cadence shapes real deployments. [https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2026/07/14/whats-new-developers-red-hat-openshift-4-22] (also Friday Five: https://www.redhat.com/en/blog/friday-five-july-17-2026-red-hat)

**Databricks and NVIDIA expand their agentic-era enterprise partnership** — Databricks and NVIDIA broadened their collaboration into an end-to-end platform for training, inference, and agentic AI on governed enterprise data, adding multinode training in AI Runtime, GPU support in Databricks Free Edition, Model Serving enhancements, and native support for the NVIDIA Agent Toolkit. _Why it matters:_ Tightens the lakehouse-plus-accelerated-compute stack that enterprises use to move agents from pilots to production on their own data. [https://www.databricks.com/blog/databricks-and-nvidia-building-agentic-era]

**EU AI Act: Commission enforcement powers over general-purpose AI activate Aug 2** — From 2 August 2026 the Commission's supervision and enforcement toolkit for general-purpose AI (GPAI) model providers comes into force — information requests, model access, and recall powers — and the European AI Office gains full penalty authority (up to the greater of €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover). Providers have been subject to GPAI obligations since Aug 2025, but could not be fined until now; a July Commission action plan on Cybersecurity and AI accompanies the shift. _Why it matters:_ The compliance clock for every frontier and enterprise model provider serving the EU runs out in two weeks. [https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-gpai-providers] (enforcement detail: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/enforcement-of-chapter-v-under-the-eu-ai-act/)

**The RAG-to-"runtime context" shift accelerates in enterprise retrieval** — A cluster of coverage this week argues that classic vector-RAG is giving way to compilation-stage knowledge layers and runtime/agent memory as agent workloads expose retrieval gaps; Redis positioned its new Iris context-and-memory platform (which auto-generates MCP tools from business data models and runs agent memory on Redis Flex) as an example, echoing Anthropic's earlier removal of vector search from Claude Code. _Why it matters:_ Retrieval budgets are shifting from embeddings/vector DBs toward context architecture — a structural change for the RAG tooling market. [https://venturebeat.com/data/the-rag-era-is-ending-for-agentic-ai-a-new-compilation-stage-knowledge-layer-is-what-comes-next] (also: https://venturebeat.com/data/context-architecture-is-replacing-rag-as-agentic-ai-pushes-enterprise-retrieval-to-its-limits)
