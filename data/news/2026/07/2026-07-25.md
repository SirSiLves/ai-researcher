## Major announcements & releases

**Bipartisan "AI Kill Switch Act" would let Washington shut down an advanced model mid-crisis** — Representatives Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran are preparing a bill that would give the Department of Homeland Security authority to order a large AI company to shut down, suspend, or throttle access to an advanced system during a severe loss-of-control incident. It applies to firms above set revenue/compute thresholds, requires them to keep a technical mechanism capable of limiting their systems and to report serious safety incidents, and allows emergency action when a system causes or is likely to cause deaths, >$100M in damage, or resists being disabled — with penalties up to $20M per day for refusing an order.
_Why it matters:_ This is the clearest sign yet that US AI policy is shifting from transparency requirements toward direct operational intervention in privately run models — landing the same week the EU AI Act's GPAI enforcement powers switch on (2 Aug). [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/969939/lawmakers-ai-kill-switch-proposal) (via [Tech Startups daily](https://techstartups.com/2026/07/24/top-tech-news-today-july-24-2026-amd-apple-google-intel-oracle-samsung-spacex-more/))

**White House commits >$5B to the "Genesis Mission," a whole-of-government AI-for-science push** — OSTP Director Michael Kratsios unveiled more than $5 billion in federal commitments expanding the Genesis Mission, with 278 projects selected and 15+ agencies (DOE, HHS/NIH, NASA, NSF, NIST, VA, DOW/NNSA, USDA, DOI) contributing datasets, compute, and facilities. Work runs on DOE's "American Science and Security Platform" and spans National Science and Technology Challenges in chronic-disease root causes, pediatric-cancer models, grid digital twins, microelectronics co-design, autonomous laboratories, and fault-tolerant quantum.
_Why it matters:_ It is the largest concrete US government bet to date on AI as scientific infrastructure, and it explicitly ties frontier compute to national-security and biomedical missions — a template other governments will benchmark against. [White House release](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/07/45502/) · [DOE Genesis Mission](https://www.energy.gov/undersecretaryforscience/genesis-mission/genesis-mission)

**Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta and 20+ tech giants urge Trump to protect open-weight AI** — A coalition of more than 20 major technology companies publicly pressed the administration to back open-weight models and warned against "premature restrictions" that would hand ground to overseas open-weight efforts. The letter lands as Chinese open-weight releases (DeepSeek V4, Kimi K3) keep resetting the cost/performance frontier and as US policymakers debate emergency-shutdown authority.
_Why it matters:_ Open-vs-closed has become an explicit Washington policy fight, not just a research-community debate — the outcome shapes whether the strongest freely downloadable models keep coming from US or Chinese labs. [Tech Startups](https://techstartups.com/2026/07/24/nvidia-microsoft-meta-and-20-tech-giants-urge-trump-to-back-open-weight-ai-warn-against-premature-restrictions-on-open-weight-models/)

**Kimi K3's full open weights land 27 July — the largest open-weight model yet** — Moonshot AI's 2.8-trillion-parameter, 1M-context multimodal reasoning model (live via API since 16 Jul) drops its full weights under a Modified MIT license on 27 July; independent testing places K3 fourth among all frontier models, trailing only Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol and edging past Claude Opus 4.8. The catch is operational: the download is reportedly ~1.4TB, so "open-weight" does not mean cheaply self-hostable.
_Why it matters:_ It is the biggest open-weight escalation to date and the second half of this week's China open-weight double-header (after DeepSeek V4 going stable) — arriving precisely as US firms lobby Washington to keep open weights unrestricted. [TECHi](https://www.techi.com/kimi-k3-open-weights-inference-economics/) · [Interconnects (N. Lambert)](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/kimi-k3-the-open-weights-escalation) · [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/16/kimi-k3/)

**AMD partners with Cerebras to split inference across mixed silicon** — AMD struck a deal with Cerebras Systems that lets customers divide AI inference workloads between AMD and Cerebras hardware; Cerebras will fold AMD's Helios rack-scale systems into its data centers and offer the combined stack via Cerebras Cloud later this year. It extends AMD's inference push (Instinct accelerators, Helios, an existing Anthropic relationship) into a multi-vendor serving story aimed squarely at Nvidia's dominance.
_Why it matters:_ Inference is now the industry's largest and fastest-growing compute cost, and the deal signals the next stage of the chip race is heterogeneous — mixing GPUs, wafer-scale, and specialized systems by cost/latency rather than betting on one processor. [Axios](https://www.axios.com), via [Tech Startups daily](https://techstartups.com/2026/07/24/top-tech-news-today-july-24-2026-amd-apple-google-intel-oracle-samsung-spacex-more/)

**Google posts its first negative free cash flow as AI capex balloons to ~$200B** — Alphabet reported roughly negative $5.8B quarterly free cash flow — a first — as capital spending on data centers and chips outran ~$39.1B of operating cash flow; Google raised its 2026 capex guidance to $195–205B. Cloud revenue rose sharply, showing the same buildout is generating real business, but investors are now pressing on how fast the spend converts to returns.
_Why it matters:_ Even the most profitable ad business in tech is being reshaped into a capital-intensive infrastructure company by the AI race — the clearest financial marker yet of how expensive frontier-scale serving has become. [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com), via [Tech Startups daily](https://techstartups.com/2026/07/24/top-tech-news-today-july-24-2026-amd-apple-google-intel-oracle-samsung-spacex-more/)

**Intel raises its outlook as AI data centers soak up CPUs, not just GPUs** — Intel beat estimates, guided Q3 above consensus, and lifted 2026 capex from $18B to $20B, citing demand for general-purpose data-center CPUs that coordinate large AI clusters; management says demand at points this year outran capacity. It is fresh evidence the AI buildout is reaching suppliers beyond Nvidia's accelerators.
_Why it matters:_ Every GPU cluster still needs CPUs to orchestrate it, so Intel's rebound is a signal that AI-infra spend is broadening across the silicon stack — with knock-on implications for US domestic-fab ambitions. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com), via [Tech Startups daily](https://techstartups.com/2026/07/24/top-tech-news-today-july-24-2026-amd-apple-google-intel-oracle-samsung-spacex-more/)

**ITC Infotech becomes "customer zero" for Google Cloud's Gemini Enterprise** — ITC Infotech announced a strategic partnership with Google Cloud to re-tool itself into an "agentic-first" enterprise, deploying and scaling Gemini Enterprise internally first before packaging implementation blueprints for its own global clients. It is a concrete adoption datapoint for Google's enterprise-agent platform among large services/SI firms.
_Why it matters:_ Systems integrators reselling their own internal agentic transformation is becoming the go-to-market motion for enterprise agent platforms — the channel through which Gemini Enterprise, Agentforce, and Joule actually reach the Global 2000. [AIwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2026/07/24/itc-infotech-and-google-cloud-partner-on-enterprise-agentic-ai/)

**Norm AI raises $120M Series C to turn regulations into autonomous compliance agents** — Norm AI closed a $120M round led by Khosla Ventures (with Blackstone, Vanguard, Bain Capital Ventures, Coatue, Craft) to expand agents that convert complex regulatory rules directly into software that monitors and enforces compliance inside regulated industries. It sits at the intersection of the enterprise-agent boom and the tightening governance environment.
_Why it matters:_ As shutdown laws and the EU AI Act raise compliance stakes, "governance-as-agents" is emerging as a fundable enterprise category — and AI-agent startups captured ~58% of July's $1.8B+ agent funding, skewed hard to B2B. [Crescendo AI funding tracker](https://www.crescendo.ai/news/latest-vc-investment-deals-in-ai-startups)

**Enterprise clouds race to offer Claude Opus 5 same-day** — A day after Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5 (24 Jul), the model is already live across enterprise platforms: Snowflake made it available in public preview on Cortex AI as a same-day launch partner (usable from Cortex Agents, AI Functions, Inference, and CoWork), alongside same-day GPT-5.6 availability. The near-instant multi-cloud availability, not just the model launch, is the enterprise story.
_Why it matters:_ Same-day frontier-model availability across Snowflake/Bedrock/Cortex is now the expectation, collapsing the old gap between a lab's release and enterprise deployability — and pushing Opus 5's half-price-of-frontier positioning straight into governed enterprise data stacks. [Snowflake — Claude Opus 5 on Cortex](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/claude-opus-5-snowflake-cortex-ai/) · [Snowflake — GPT-5.6 on Cortex](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/openai-gpt-5-6-snowflake-cortex-ai/)
