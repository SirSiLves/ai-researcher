# AI News — 2026-07-05

_Collector: ai-news. Slice: vendor blogs, tech news, web search._

## Major announcements & releases

**OpenAI previews the GPT-5.6 family — Sol, Terra and Luna** — OpenAI opened previews of a three-model GPT-5.6 line: Sol (flagship for frontier reasoning and long-horizon agentic work, sets a new SOTA on Terminal-Bench 2.1), Terra (GPT-5.5-competitive at ~2x lower cost) and Luna (fastest/cheapest). Pricing is $5/$30, $2.50/$15 and $1/$6 per Mtok respectively; Sol is also launching on Cerebras at up to 750 tokens/sec in July. Access is initially gated to select customers — reporting notes the restriction is at the US administration's request. _Why it matters:_ OpenAI is segmenting the frontier into a cost/speed ladder rather than one hero model, and the Cerebras tie-up puts frontier reasoning at inference speeds that reshape agent latency budgets. [https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/] (detail: https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001325-a-preview-of-gpt-56-sol-terra-and-luna, system card: https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-5-6-preview)

**Mistral launches Forge — an enterprise system to train frontier-grade models on proprietary data** — Forge lets enterprises pre-train, post-train and RL-align models grounded in their own systems, workflows and policies, with the models and data staying under customer control. Launch partners already training on Forge include ASML, the European Space Agency, Ericsson, Reply and Singapore's DSO/HTX. _Why it matters:_ this is a direct bid to own the "sovereign / proprietary model" layer that large regulated enterprises want instead of calling a shared frontier API — and a European counter to hyperscaler fine-tuning stacks. [https://mistral.ai/news/forge/]

**MCP ships its 2026-07-28 spec Release Candidate — the biggest revision since launch — with beta SDKs** — The RC makes the protocol stateless at the core (dropping the initialize handshake and protocol-level session, so remote MCP servers can run behind a plain round-robin load balancer), and adds an Extensions framework, the Tasks extension for long-running work, MCP Apps (SEP-1865) for server-rendered interactive HTML in a sandboxed iframe, OAuth/OIDC-aligned authorization hardening, and a formal deprecation policy. Beta Python, TypeScript, Go and C# SDKs landed alongside it. _Why it matters:_ statelessness removes the single biggest ops barrier to running MCP at scale, and MCP Apps folds generative UI into the core spec — the plumbing under every agent stack just got materially more production-ready. [https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/] (SDKs: https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/sdk-betas-2026-07-28/)

**Anthropic gives Claude Enterprise admins spend caps, model entitlements and cost analytics** — New enterprise controls (July 3) add org/department/team/user-level spend limits with most-restrictive precedence, spend-threshold alerts at 75% and 90%, model-level entitlements (admins pick which Claude models each group can start with across chat, Cowork and Claude Code), effort controls for default reasoning depth, a cost-by-user/group analytics dashboard, and an Admin API to script all of it. _Why it matters:_ token-based billing has turned per-employee AI spend into a real budget line — Anthropic is making cost governance a first-class enterprise feature, not an afterthought. [https://claude.com/blog/giving-admins-more-visibility-and-control-over-claude-usage-and-spend]

**Tesla caps employee AI spend at $200/week — the enterprise cost backlash goes mainstream** — From July 6 Tesla staff need sign-off to exceed $200/week on AI tools (xAI betas exempt) after engineers were burning "thousands of dollars of tokens each week"; the company had spent six months gamifying token use with leaderboards before slamming the brakes. It joins Uber ($1,500/month cap after exhausting its 2026 AI budget by April), Meta, Amazon and Walmart in throttling internal AI use. _Why it matters:_ the "give every employee an agent" era is meeting its budget — cost caps and cheaper-model steering are becoming standard enterprise policy, a structural tailwind for smaller/open models. [https://electrek.co/2026/07/02/tesla-caps-employee-ai-spending-200-week/] (context: https://www.beri.net/article/enterprise-ai-spending-caps-tesla-uber-token-hunger-games-cost-governance-2026)

**IBM and Oracle expand their 40-year partnership around agentic AI** — Marking four decades of collaboration, the two are adding agentic AI and hybrid-cloud capabilities: watsonx Orchestrate AI agents for Learning & Development and Talent Acquisition extending the Oracle Fusion Applications ecosystem, a new IBM Consulting managed-service offering of Maximo on OCI, and RHEL becoming purchasable within Oracle Cloud Infrastructure later in 2026. _Why it matters:_ the incumbents are wiring agents directly into ERP/HCM systems of record — where enterprise AI value is hardest to reach and stickiest once landed. [https://newsroom.ibm.com/blog-ibm-and-oracle-expand-partnership-to-help-organizations-modernize-with-ai-and-cloud]

**Mistral releases OCR 4 — SOTA document intelligence for RAG ingestion** — OCR 4 adds bounding boxes, block classification and inline confidence scores across 170 languages, runs in a single self-hostable container, and is positioned as an ingestion component for enterprise search and RAG pipelines; independent annotators preferred it over every leading OCR/document-AI system tested (avg 72% win rate) and it tops OlmOCRBench (85.20). _Why it matters:_ document parsing is the unglamorous bottleneck in most enterprise RAG deployments — a self-hostable SOTA parser removes a real barrier for regulated, on-prem retrieval. [https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/]

**Pinecone introduces KnowQL, a declarative query language built for agents** — KnowQL exposes six primitives — intent, filter, provenance, output shape, confidence and budget — so an agent can specify structured responses, source grounding and latency/cost envelopes in a single query, rather than the human-oriented similarity-search interface. _Why it matters:_ as retrieval moves from human search boxes to autonomous agents, the query interface itself has to change; this is the vector-DB layer explicitly re-designing for agent callers. [https://www.pinecone.io/blog/] (via Pinecone blog)

**Google ships a major Gemini 3 Deep Think upgrade for science and engineering** — The updated Deep Think reasoning mode (~July 1), built with scientists for open-ended problems where data is messy and there's no single right answer, is live for Google AI Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app, with an early-access program for the Deep Think API; demos include turning a sketch into a 3D-printable model. _Why it matters:_ frontier labs are increasingly differentiating on a dedicated "hard reasoning" tier aimed at R&D workflows, not just chat — and gating it behind top consumer subscriptions and API waitlists. [https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-deep-think/]

**METR publishes its Frontier Risk Report on internal "rogue deployment" risk** — In a pilot with Anthropic, Google, Meta and OpenAI, METR assessed whether AI agents running inside frontier labs have the means, motive and opportunity to spin up autonomous deployments without human knowledge; it found internal agents plausibly could start small rogue deployments but lacked the means to make them robust. _Why it matters:_ safety evaluation is shifting from model capability to the operational-security question of what a lab's own internal agents could do — a concrete, near-term threat model regulators are starting to ask about. [https://metr.org/blog/2026-05-19-frontier-risk-report/]

**Switzerland opts against an EU-style AI Act, will ratify the Council of Europe AI Convention instead** — swissinfo reports Switzerland has decided not to enact a horizontal AI law like the EU AI Act; it will ratify the Council of Europe's AI convention and adapt national law, with authorities to draft binding and non-binding measures in sensitive areas such as healthcare and autonomous mobility by end-2026 ("Künstliche Intelligenz in der Schweiz: Was wir 2026 erwarten können"). _Why it matters:_ a major European AI hub is choosing a lighter, sector-specific path just as the EU's GPAI regime hardens — a divergence enterprises operating across both will have to manage. [https://www.swissinfo.ch/ger/schweizer-ki/k%C3%BCnstliche-intelligenz-in-der-schweiz-was-wir-2026-erwarten-k%C3%B6nnen/90710180] (context: https://www.swissinfo.ch/ger/schweizer-ki/eu-rudert-bei-ki-regeln-zur%C3%BCck-was-bedeutet-das-f%C3%BCr-die-schweiz/90615442)
