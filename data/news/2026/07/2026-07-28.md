## Major announcements & releases

**MCP 2026-07-28 ships — the protocol goes stateless, and Claude rolls it out the same day** — The fifth Model Context Protocol spec release is live, and it is the largest revision since launch: the initialization handshake and sessions are gone, so any server instance can serve any request; Extensions become first-class (MCP Apps for server-rendered UIs, Tasks for long-running work); authorization is hardened around OAuth 2.1 and OpenID Connect; Roots, Sampling and Logging are deprecated; and tools now use full JSON Schema 2020-12. There is finally a formal deprecation policy — deprecated features keep working for at least 12 months. The practical payoff is deployment-shaped: a remote MCP server that needed sticky sessions, a shared session store and deep packet inspection at the gateway can now sit behind a plain round-robin load balancer, route on an `Mcp-Method` header, and let clients cache `tools/list` for as long as the server's `ttlMs` allows. Anthropic published its rollout post the same morning, noting MCP recently passed 400M monthly SDK installs. _Why it matters:_ this is a genuine breaking change — servers on 2026-07-28 may not talk to older clients and vice versa — so every team running a remote MCP server now owns a migration, but in exchange MCP finally behaves like ordinary HTTP infrastructure instead of a stateful special case. [Specification — modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2026-07-28) · [Anthropic — Bringing MCP 2026-07-28 to Claude](https://claude.com/blog/bringing-mcp-2026-07-28-to-claude) · [MCP blog — the release candidate write-up](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/2026-07-28-release-candidate/)

**Snowflake launches Cortex AI Gateway at Black Hat — a centralized MCP gateway wired into the data plane** — Snowflake is folding Natoma, a centralized MCP gateway that enforces identity, policy and audit at the tool-call level, into its ecosystem as the Cortex AI Gateway. The pitch is aimed squarely at the mess that MCP adoption has created inside enterprises: decentralized adoption produces unmanaged server sprawl, fractured UX, unvetted servers, tool hijacking and data-exfiltration exposure, with no visibility into what agents are actually doing and AI costs running away. Snowflake cites the Linux Foundation's 2026 State of Tech Talent Report, where AI security concerns jumped from 17% in 2024 to 48% in 2026 while 57% of organizations report a capacity gap in security and risk management. _Why it matters:_ it lands on the same day as the MCP spec that makes servers trivially horizontally scalable — the two together define the shape of the next enterprise procurement cycle, where the gateway, not the server, is the control point. [Snowflake](https://www.snowflake.com/en/blog/enterprise-ai-security-agentic-mcp-governance/)

**NVIDIA is negotiating a $250 billion guarantee to underwrite OpenAI's Ohio data centre** — Reported today, NVIDIA would provide a $250B backstop giving OpenAI access to SoftBank's planned 10-gigawatt data centre in Ohio, effectively guaranteeing the debt behind the buildout. _Why it matters:_ NVIDIA is no longer just selling the accelerators — it is underwriting the credit that buys them, which folds vendor financing into the AI capex cycle at a scale where the circularity becomes a systemic question rather than an accounting footnote. [heise (German; "Nvidia negotiating $250 billion guarantee for OpenAI")](https://www.heise.de/news/Nvidia-verhandelt-ueber-250-Milliarden-Dollar-Buergschaft-fuer-OpenAI-11380911.html) · [Golem (German)](https://www.golem.de/news/250-milliarden-us-dollar-nvidia-will-kredite-fuer-openai-rechenzentrum-absichern-2607-211373.html) · [t3n (German)](https://t3n.de/news/nvidia-buergschaft-openai-1755241)

**Microsoft ships MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, its first purpose-built cyber-defence model — and the routing is the real product** — The model is a sparse-MoE transformer with 137B total parameters, 5B active, 256k context, text-only, fine-tuned from MAI-Code-1-Flash (the agentic coding model already inside GitHub Copilot and VS Code) out of the MAI-Thinking-1 lineage. It does not ship as a standalone endpoint: it runs inside MDASH, Microsoft's multi-model agentic scanning harness that orchestrates 100+ specialized agents across five stages (Prepare, Scan, Validate, Dedupe, Prove), using debater-agent disagreement as an exploitability signal. MDASH running MAI-Cyber-1-Flash alongside GPT-5.4 scores 95.95% on CyberGym's 1,507 real-world vulnerability-reproduction tasks, up from 88.45% in May, with the small model absorbing up to 90% of tasks and escalating only the hardest 10%. _Why it matters:_ the headline number comes from swapping 80% of the harness's models for a cheap specialist, not from a bigger frontier model — the clearest production example yet that cost-aware routing beats raw capability on long-horizon agentic work. [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/28/microsoft-ai-releases-mai-cyber-1-flash-a-5b-active-parameter-cyber-model-that-pushes-mdash-to-95-95-on-cybergym/) · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/07/microsoft-unveils-ai-security-tools-it-says-outperform-competing-platforms/) · [heise (German; "Microsoft presents its own AI model for cybersecurity")](https://www.heise.de/news/Microsoft-stellt-eigenes-KI-Modell-fuer-Cybersecurity-vor-11379956.html)

**Dario Amodei: Anthropic has never advocated banning open-weights models** — Responding to reports that US officials are weighing a ban on Chinese open-weights models — and to accusations that Anthropic wants such a ban to protect its business — Amodei states flatly that open-weights models without dangerous capabilities are a public good, and that protectionist bans would not address his actual concerns. Those he names as two: authoritarian governments building models more powerful than the US's and using them for permanent military superiority or deep domestic repression (a risk he argues is orthogonal to whether weights are open — "the most dangerous model may be one that is trained in secret and handed only to the People's Liberation Army"), and misuse of powerful models for cyber or biological attack. _Why it matters:_ the frontier-lab consensus on open weights has been the year's most consequential policy fight, and the CEO most often cast as the restrictionist has just publicly ruled out the restriction everyone assumed he wanted. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/position-open-weights-models) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/27/anthropics-dario-amodei-responds-doesnt-oppose-open-weight-models-but-fears-chinese-ai/)

**OpenAI publishes a field report on coding agents rebuilding scientific software** — Eight agent-assisted scientific-computing projects, primarily in the life sciences: five using Codex alone, three combining Codex with Claude Code. The work ranges from routine maintenance and targeted optimization through large-scale language migrations to GPU-native redesigns of genomics tooling. Contributors report that agents let small academic teams take on work that would otherwise have needed far more time or specialist engineering, while flagging the persistent problem the report is honest about: long-term stewardship of the resulting code. _Why it matters:_ the interesting detail is methodological, not promotional — OpenAI ran three of its eight case studies on a competitor's agent, which is a more credible signal about where agentic coding actually works than any benchmark released this month. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/scientific-computing-agentic-ai/)

**America's largest grid will start cutting power to data centres** — After a capacity auction fell short, PJM Interconnection has said it will curtail supply to data centres and other large users during shortages. Curtailment starts June 2027 and applies only to sites of 50 MW or more; affected customers are compensated, as in long-standing demand-response programmes, and typically get 30 minutes to a few days of notice. Data centres are projected to use 4x more electricity by 2035. _Why it matters:_ interruptibility has just become a design constraint for large AI sites in the PJM footprint, which pushes operators toward on-site generation or diesel backup — and turns "how much power can you actually get, firm" into a harder procurement question than GPU allocation. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/data-centers-may-face-temporary-power-cuts-to-prevent-blackouts-on-largest-us-grid/)

**Recursive Superintelligence commits $410M of its $650M to AWS compute** — Richard Socher's company, out of stealth since May, has signed a multiyear compute deal with Amazon Web Services worth $410 million — the bulk of everything it has raised. Socher told TechCrunch he expects it to be the first of several. The company works on open-ended self-improving systems, an approach that is compute-hungry by construction. _Why it matters:_ a lab spending roughly two-thirds of its total funding on a single cloud contract before shipping a product is the clearest illustration yet of how much of frontier-lab venture money is really a pre-purchase of capacity. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/28/recursive-superintelligence-signs-400-compute-deal-with-amazon/)

**AWS launches the GuardDuty Investigation Agent to automate threat triage** — Amazon has added an agentic investigation layer on top of GuardDuty findings, automating the triage step that security teams normally do by hand. _Why it matters:_ it lands in the same week as Microsoft's MAI-Cyber-1-Flash and NVIDIA's Open Secure AI Alliance — security operations is turning into the first enterprise function where agents are being sold as the default rather than the pilot. [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/07/guardduty-investigation-agent/)

**Perplexity's Personal Computer turns Windows PCs into AI agents** — Perplexity has extended its agent push from the browser to the desktop, with a product that drives a Windows machine directly. _Why it matters:_ computer-use agents keep converging on the same delivery model — the OS as the tool surface — and the competitive question is shifting from model quality to who holds the endpoint. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/971750/perplexity-personal-computer-windows-ai-agents)

**Codeberg bans predominantly AI-generated projects** — At its 2026 general assembly, the Berlin non-profit Codeberg e.V. resolved that projects written primarily by generative AI are no longer permitted on the platform, changing its terms of service accordingly. _Why it matters:_ the first significant code-hosting platform to make provenance a membership condition rather than a labelling requirement — and a direct test of whether "predominantly AI-generated" is a rule anyone can actually enforce. [t3n (German; "No vibe coding: Codeberg bans AI-generated software on its platform")](https://t3n.de/news/codeberg-verbietet-ki-generierten-code-agb-1755360)

**EU Commission issues Cyber Resilience Act guidance clarifying the open-source position** — A Brussels guidance document fleshes out the Cyber Resilience Regulation, giving manufacturers and open-source projects legal certainty ahead of the reporting obligations taking effect. _Why it matters:_ the CRA's open-source carve-outs have been the main source of compliance anxiety for European maintainers shipping AI tooling, and this is the first concrete answer on where steward obligations begin. [heise (German; "Cyber Resilience Act: EU Commission creates more clarity for open source")](https://www.heise.de/news/Cyber-Resilience-Act-EU-Kommission-schafft-mehr-Klarheit-fuer-Open-Source-11381011.html)
