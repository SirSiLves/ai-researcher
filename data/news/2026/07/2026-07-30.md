## Major announcements & releases

**OpenAI cuts GPT-5.6 Luna by 80% and Terra by 20%, and replaces Priority Processing with Fast mode** — The efficiency gains OpenAI described yesterday — GPT-5.6 helping make itself cheaper to serve — are now passed to customers as list-price cuts on the two non-flagship tiers, and the same rates apply to how usage counts against Codex and ChatGPT Work subscriptions. Sol keeps its price but gains Fast mode in the API: up to 2.5× Standard speed at twice the price with no change in intelligence, backward-compatible with requests already tagged `priority`. OpenAI's own framing of what the cut buys: Luna now delivers roughly year-ago frontier-class performance at about six cents on the dollar per task and nearly nine times the speed, and on Agents' Last Exam it outperforms Fable 5 at an estimated cost per task nearly 99% lower. _Why it matters:_ an 80% cut on the volume tier is the clearest signal yet that the competitive axis has moved from capability to cost-per-completed-task — the same shift Fireworks Nexus and Cursor Router were arbitraging last week, except now the frontier lab is doing it to its own price list rather than letting a router do it for them. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/advancing-the-price-performance-frontier-with-gpt-5-6/) · [companion: how GPT-5.6 fuses frontier intelligence with frontier efficiency (29 Jul)](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6-frontier-intelligence-efficiency/)

**Google DeepMind ships Gemini Robotics ER 2 — an embodied-reasoning brain that watches video, orchestrates tools, and coordinates multiple robots** — ER 2 is explicitly a high-level planner, not a controller: it handles real-time spatial reasoning and multi-step task planning, then hands motor execution to whatever vision-language-action model you already run, which developers declare as tools alongside navigation APIs. The upgrade over ER 1.6 is continuous video: robots can now track their own progress against a plan, notice when something went wrong, self-correct, and know when a step is actually finished. Multi-robot collaboration lets several machines share a workspace on a workflow no single robot could complete. It is publicly available today via the Gemini API and Google AI Studio, and in private preview on the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform. It lands two days after Gemini Robotics 2 brought whole-body control, making three physical-AI models in three days. _Why it matters:_ the interesting part is architectural, not robotic — DeepMind has factored embodied agents into a reasoning layer and a swappable actuation layer, with video as the feedback channel that closes the loop. That is the same orchestrator/executor split enterprise software agents are converging on, and shipping it as an API means the planning layer is now a product you rent rather than a system you build. [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-er-2-powering-robotics-with-video-understanding-task-orchestration-and-multi-robot-collaboration/) · [Gemini Robotics 2, whole-body intelligence (28 Jul)](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-brings-whole-body-intelligence-to-robots/) · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/07/google-reveals-gemini-robotics-2-0-promising-improved-dexterity-and-safety/) · [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/30/google-deepmind-gemini-robotics-2-whole-body-control-dexterity-multi-robot-collaboration/)

**Qualcomm completes its acquisition of Modular — Mojo, MAX and Modular Cloud survive as products** — Closed 29 July. Qualcomm gets an AI-native software platform for deploying generative and agentic workloads across heterogeneous compute, and pairs it with its own silicon across devices, data centre, edge and industrial AI. Modular says the open-ecosystem mission continues and that Mojo, MAX and Modular Cloud continue as products and brands. _Why it matters:_ Modular's whole thesis was a portable compilation and serving layer that breaks the CUDA lock-in; it now sits inside a chip vendor with a direct interest in one particular non-NVIDIA target. The commitment to heterogeneity is on the record, which makes the next twelve months of Mojo's backend support a readable test of whether an acquired abstraction layer stays neutral. [Modular / Qualcomm](https://www.modular.com/blog/qualcomm-completes-acquisition-of-modular)

**The EU opens the AI Gigafactories tender: €30B, four consortia, and a 75,000-H100 yardstick that is already two generations old** — A year after von der Leyen announced them, the Commission has launched the formal call. Financing is a public-private partnership of up to €10B public and €20B private, with EU and "like-minded country" investors expected to carry the private share. The first lot funds four consortia to build datacentres reaching the compute of 75,000 NVIDIA H100 accelerators; a second lot adds three more. EU money starts at €100M per project and rises to €400M; winners have 18 months from award to standing facility, and EuroHPC has explicitly ruled out splintering: "we don't want a hundred small datacentres." Denmark, Czechia, Finland, France and Poland are in for round one with eight more member states interested; Italy, Spain and Germany are waiting for round two. Non-EU firms may participate but consortium control must sit in the EU and the facilities must be physically on Union territory. The forthcoming Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA) will set the sovereignty and usage criteria. _Why it matters:_ this is Europe's actual compute-sovereignty bet, and the benchmark chosen is a four-year-old GPU two generations behind Rubin — a fair proxy for the gap between the timeline European industrial policy runs on and the one the hardware runs on. Note also who the participants concede they still depend on: NVIDIA, Qualcomm, AMD. [heise (German; "AI Gigafactories: EU Commission launches tender")](https://www.heise.de/news/AI-Gigafactorys-EU-Kommission-startet-Ausschreibung-11387125.html) · [Golem (German; "Delayed: EU Commission launches gigafactory tender after eighteen months")](https://www.golem.de/news/verspaetet-eu-kommission-startet-nach-anderthalb-jahren-ausschreibung-fuer-ki-gigafactories-2607-211462.html)

**Nscale buys Anyscale for $1.65B to own the software layer above its own GPUs** — British neocloud Nscale is acquiring the company built by the team behind Ray, adding workload management, orchestration and observability to business lines that already span energy, datacentres and orchestration software. Anyscale was last valued at $1.38B in 2022 and says revenue grew 70% quarter-on-quarter; Nscale raised $2B in March at a $14.6B valuation with NVIDIA, Nokia, Blue Owl, Dell and Aker on the cap table. Anyscale's stated rationale is co-design: "neither company could do [this] as effectively by optimizing its layer alone." _Why it matters:_ the neoclouds have worked out that renting bare GPUs is a commodity business with a hyperscaler-shaped ceiling, and are buying their way up the stack toward the workload. For anyone standardising on Ray, the distributed-computing framework underneath a large share of LLM training and serving pipelines just acquired a specific hardware allegiance. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/30/nscale-buys-anyscale-as-it-seeks-to-own-more-of-the-ai-compute-stack/)

**Okta buys Permiso for just under $200M — the second nine-figure bet on agent identity in three days** — An almost all-cash deal, closing in Q3 of Okta's fiscal 2027. Permiso, founded by former FireEye executives Paul Nguyen and Jason Martin, detects attacks that move laterally through cloud infrastructure using stolen or compromised identities, and has extended that to monitoring AI agents and other machine identities; its SandyClaw platform, launched in April, analyses agent *skills* in a sandbox to catch malicious behaviour before deployment. _Why it matters:_ Cyera paid $1B for Oasis Security two days ago for the same problem. Identity vendors are converging on the conclusion that login-time verification is worthless against an agent that behaves legitimately at authentication and then does something unexpected for four days — which is a precise description of the OpenAI incident the entire industry has spent this week reading. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/30/okta-buys-ai-security-startup-permiso-source-says-for-about-200m/)

**Kimi K3 becomes broadly deployable: Together AI signs a launch-platform deal with Moonshot, and AWS publishes the reference deployment** — Together AI announced a strategic partnership making it a launch platform for every open-weights model Moonshot ships, starting with the 2.8-trillion-parameter K3 — day-zero access on US-hosted infrastructure with zero data retention and the option to post-train on your own data. AWS followed with a deployment guide on SageMaker HyperPod and EKS that makes the operational cost concrete: 896 experts with 16 active per token (~104B active parameters), MXFP4 weights, and a hard requirement for `ml.p6-b300.48xlarge` — eight B300 Blackwell Ultra GPUs — served through a day-0 vLLM container not yet merged upstream. _Why it matters:_ three days after the weights landed, a Chinese frontier open-weight model has a US-hosted commercial serving path with a zero-retention contract and a named hyperscaler runbook. That combination is what actually determines whether a European or US enterprise can put K3 in front of a compliance team, and it arrives while Washington is still arguing about whether to restrict these models at all. [Together AI](https://www.together.ai/blog/together-ai-announces-strategic-partnership-with-moonshot-ai-to-natively-serve-kimi-models) · [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/deploying-kimi-k3-on-aws/)

**Microsoft Research open-sources Echoverse — and reports that shallow training worlds actively make computer-use agents worse** — Twelve simulated environments for training computer-use agents: ten deep domain worlds that reproduce an application's real behaviour with seeded realistic data and state that stays coherent across screens and users, plus two capability worlds that drill a single stubborn control — date pickers, nested filters — in many forms. The findings are the point. Trained on shallow and deep builds of the same sites, the model *regressed* on the shallow ones and improved on the deep ones. Drilling awkward controls in varied forms transferred to domains never seen in training. Co-evolving the model, the world and the verifier improved all three, and RL against a grounded verifier lifted held-out performance while teaching the agent to reach goals in fewer steps. Four worlds ship with code, data and graders on GitHub and Hugging Face. _Why it matters:_ the industry's default answer to weak agents has been more environments; this says fidelity beats count and that cheap synthetic UIs are net-negative training data. If that holds, the moat for computer-use agents is faithful simulators of real applications, which is a much harder and much less scalable asset than a benchmark suite. [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/echoverse-deep-evolving-environments-for-computer-use-agents/) · [same day: EvoLib, turning experience into evolving knowledge](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/evolib-turning-experience-into-evolving-knowledge/)

**Germany's cartel office clears SAP — customers can pull their data out, and process mining doesn't require Signavio** — The Bundeskartellamt concluded its preliminary inquiries and will not open abuse proceedings. Its findings, which SAP is happy to quote: customers and partners have sufficient and permissible technical means to extract data from SAP systems for use in third-party solutions, and the SAP API Policy does not restrict that; on process mining specifically, SAP offers competition-compliant licensing including options that do not require SAP Signavio. Handelsblatt frames it as the end of the Celonis complaint. _Why it matters:_ the single biggest practical question in European enterprise AI is whether you can get your ERP data into a model or an agent that SAP does not sell you. A regulator has now examined that question and said yes — which removes the antitrust lever, and makes data egress from SAP a commercial and engineering problem rather than a legal one. [SAP](https://news.sap.com/2026/07/statement-on-the-decision-of-the-german-federal-cartel-office-not-to-initiate-antitrust-proceedings-against-sap/) · [Handelsblatt (German; "Federal Cartel Office does not target SAP in Celonis dispute")](https://www.handelsblatt.com/technik/it-internet/ermittlungen-bundeskartellamt-nimmt-im-streit-mit-celonis-sap-nicht-ins-visier/100243978.html)

**Tencent open-sources AngelSpec — two specialised speculative-decoding drafters instead of one averaged compromise** — A torch-native training framework for draft models covering both autoregressive multi-token prediction and the block-parallel DFlash family on Hy3 models. The design argument is that most speculative-decoding work optimises one drafter against an averaged benchmark mixture that resembles no real traffic. Acceptance rates decay fast in high-entropy open-ended chat, where a short MTP proposal wins; code and mathematical reasoning constrain future tokens far more, producing long predictable spans that block-parallel drafting amortises well. So AngelSpec ships both, trained on different data. It also fixes a train/inference mismatch in the original Hy3 MTP layer with a shared-parameter multi-depth scheme that unrolls one physical block autoregressively during training, following EAGLE-3's Training-Time Test principle. _Why it matters:_ speculative decoding is lossless free throughput and most teams leave it on the table because the drafter never matched their traffic. Treating workload heterogeneity as a first-class design constraint — rather than a benchmark to average over — is the right framing, and it is now open source. [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/30/tencent-open-sources-angelspec-a-unified-training-framework-for-mtp-and-block-parallel-speculative-decoding-on-hy3-models/)

It arrives in the same week Microsoft booked $3.2B from its Anthropic investment, shipped MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, and was reported to be competing with OpenAI and Anthropic more openly than at any point in the partnership. _Why it matters:_ the consolidation matters less as a product story than as a positioning one — Microsoft is building a first-party agent surface that does not depend on which lab's model is underneath, while simultaneously holding equity in two of them and shipping its own. [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/972927/microsoft-copilot-super-app-confirmed) · [Microsoft is openly competing with OpenAI and Anthropic more than ever](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/29/microsoft-is-openly-competing-with-openai-anthropic-more-than-ever/)

**Token Saver: an open-source MCP extension that cuts Claude PDF token costs 90–99% with local hybrid RAG** — Rather than pushing whole documents through the context window, the extension indexes PDFs locally and serves only the retrieved spans over MCP. _Why it matters:_ the most common enterprise RAG failure mode right now is not bad retrieval, it is teams disabling retrieval entirely because long-context models made it feel unnecessary — and then paying for it per token on every document-heavy call. A drop-in MCP layer that restores the retrieval step without changing the client is the pragmatic middle. [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/07/30/token-saver-an-open-source-mcp-extension-using-local-hybrid-rag/)
