## Major announcements & releases

**Anthropic ships Claude Sonnet 5 — most agentic Sonnet yet, priced to run agents cheaply** — Announced June 30, Sonnet 5 becomes the default model on Free and Pro and lands close to Opus 4.8 on reasoning, tool use, coding, and long-horizon agentic work (driving browsers, terminals, running unattended for long stretches). Introductory pricing is $2/M input and $10/M output through Aug 31 (then $3/$15), roughly half of Opus 4.8's $5/$25. Note: a new tokenizer can map the same text to up to ~1.35x more tokens, partially offsetting the headline savings. _Why it matters:_ A near-frontier model at mid-tier pricing resets the cost floor for production agent fleets — the biggest lever on agentic deployment economics this quarter. [https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-sonnet-5] (coverage: TechCrunch, VentureBeat)

**OpenAI previews the GPT-5.6 series (Sol, Terra, Luna)** — On June 26 OpenAI opened a limited preview of GPT-5.6: Sol (flagship), Terra (balanced everyday model), and Luna (fast/affordable). GA is promised "in the coming weeks," and OpenAI plans to serve GPT-5.6 Sol on Cerebras at up to 750 tokens/sec in July. Sol ships with OpenAI's most robust safety stack to date. _Why it matters:_ Keeps the frontier cadence tight against Anthropic's Sonnet 5 and Google's Gemini 3.5, with the Cerebras tie-up signaling a serving-speed front in the frontier race. [https://openai.com/index/previewing-gpt-5-6-sol/]

**Google rolls out Gemini 3.5 Flash and Gemini Omni** — Gemini 3.5 Flash leads the new series (Terminal-Bench 2.1 76.2%, GDPval-AA 1656 Elo, MCP Atlas 83.6%, CharXiv 84.2%), with 3.5 Pro already in internal use and rolling out next month. Google also launched Gemini Omni (any-input generation, starting with video) and Managed Agents in the Gemini API — one API call provisions a remote sandboxed Linux environment where an agent can reason, run code, manage files, and browse. _Why it matters:_ Managed Agents pushes the "agent runtime as a service" model directly into the API layer, competing with Anthropic/OpenAI agent SDKs on infrastructure rather than just weights. [https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/next-generation-gemini-deep-research/]

**Qualcomm closes $3.9B all-stock acquisition of Modular** — Confirmed June 24, Qualcomm is buying Modular, whose software runs AI models across CPU/GPU/NPU/custom silicon without code rewrites, to power its data-center push. The deal positions a vendor-neutral software layer directly against NVIDIA's CUDA moat; close expected in H2 2026. _Why it matters:_ A serious full-stack challenge to CUDA lock-in — if Modular's portability layer scales inside Qualcomm, it lowers the switching cost off NVIDIA for enterprise inference. [https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-24/qualcomm-confirms-buying-modular-to-help-ai-market-push]

**SAP invests in n8n at $5.2B, embeds it in Joule Studio** — SAP's ~$60M strategic investment doubled n8n's valuation (from $2.5B in Oct 2025) and makes it Germany's most valuable AI company. n8n's visual multi-agent workflow canvas gets embedded inside Joule Studio on the SAP Business AI Platform, GA targeted Q3 2026. _Why it matters:_ The workflow/orchestration layer is becoming the battleground for enterprise agentic AI — SAP is buying its way into the automation canvas rather than building it. [https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-05-12/sap-invests-in-ai-automation-startup-n8n-at-5-2-billion-value]

**ServiceNow extends Autonomous Workforce across every business function** — ServiceNow brought its Autonomous Workforce to all major business functions and introduced ServiceNow Otto, unifying conversational AI, autonomous workflows, and enterprise search into one experience. IT AI specialists reach GA around June; security/risk AI specialists preview June, GA September. _Why it matters:_ Positions ServiceNow as the governance "control tower" spine for enterprise agent sprawl — governed, auditable autonomous work is its explicit pitch. [https://newsroom.servicenow.com/press-releases/details/2026/ServiceNow-brings-Autonomous-Workforce-to-every-major-business-function/default.aspx]

**Mistral OCR 4 and Search Toolkit expand the retrieval stack** — Mistral OCR 4 (June 23) adds bounding boxes, block classification, and inline confidence scores to document intelligence; the Search Toolkit entered public preview as a composable framework for building production search pipelines for AI apps. Both sit alongside Mistral Medium 3.5 powering its Vibe remote agents. _Why it matters:_ Document-grounded retrieval with confidence scoring is exactly what enterprises need for RAG they can audit — Mistral is packaging the retrieval plumbing, not just the model. [https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/]

**MCP June spec + Linux Foundation governance mature the agent-protocol layer** — The June 2026 MCP spec targets server-as-agent capabilities (MCP servers connecting to other MCP servers for recursive composition), with fine-grained authorization to follow. MCP is now governed by the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation with 18,000+ indexed servers and ~97M+ monthly SDK downloads; the two-layer MCP (vertical tool integration) + A2A (horizontal agent coordination) stack is now the enterprise default, both LF projects with a joint interoperability effort. _Why it matters:_ Neutral-foundation governance plus recursive server composition is what turns MCP from a connector spec into durable agent infrastructure. [https://zylos.ai/research/2026-03-26-agent-interoperability-protocols-mcp-a2a-acp-convergence/]

**EU AI Act: high-risk enforcement clock ticks toward Aug 2, 2026 amid Digital Omnibus delay push** — Full applicability lands Aug 2, 2026, but a provisional Digital Omnibus agreement (early May) proposes pushing high-risk obligations to Dec 2, 2027 (standalone) and Aug 2, 2028 (embedded) — treat as proposed, not adopted. Colorado's AI Act implementation date hit June 30, 2026, and the UK signaled a Frontier AI Bill to give its AI Security Institute statutory pre-deployment testing powers. Germany is standing up its own AI Security Institute. _Why it matters:_ The regulatory calendar is now the binding constraint for EU deployments, and the delay proposal creates real planning uncertainty for compliance teams. [https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/]
