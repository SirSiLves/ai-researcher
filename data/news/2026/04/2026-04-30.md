## Major announcements & releases

**Anthropic launches Claude Security public beta + CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, SentinelOne, Trend Micro and Wiz embed Opus 4.7** — Anthropic on 30 April opened Claude Security to public beta for Claude Enterprise customers, a dedicated defensive product powered by Opus 4.7 that reasons over entire codebases the way a security researcher does — tracing data flows, reading source, and modelling cross-file interactions. The private preview surfaced 500 production vulnerabilities in two months, including bugs that had remained invisible to expert reviews for years. Same day, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, SentinelOne, Trend Micro (TrendAI) and Wiz announced integrations putting Opus 4.7 across their platforms; CrowdStrike's Project QuiltWorks lands Opus 4.7 directly in Falcon for AI-powered vulnerability discovery and remediation. _Why it matters:_ This is Anthropic's direct counter to OpenAI's TAC cyber-defender plan (29 April) and a much broader vendor footprint than Mythos Preview had a month ago. Five vendor-platform integrations in one announcement is the most concrete answer to the "but where do enterprises actually run frontier-defensive models?" question of the cycle. **IBM releases Granite 4.1 family (3B, 8B, 30B dense, Apache 2.0) ahead of Think 2026** — IBM Research dropped Granite 4.1 on 30 April: a new generation of dense, decoder-only language models in 3B, 8B and 30B sizes, trained on ~15T tokens with progressive annealing toward technical, scientific and mathematical instruction-following data. The family targets two enterprise primitives — instruction following and tool calling — and IBM claims competitive results against same-class Gemma and Qwen open models with thinking disabled. Available on watsonx, Hugging Face, OpenRouter, and Ollama under Apache 2.0; companion Granite Speech 4.1 2B models (autoregressive ASR with translation + non-autoregressive editing for fast inference) released the same day. _Why it matters:_ Five days before Think 2026, IBM seeds the conference with an open-weight enterprise-tooling stack — the 8B/30B sizes are the sweet spot for watsonx Orchestrate and Granite agents. The dual-track speech release (autoregressive + non-autoregressive editing) is the most interesting architectural choice in the 30 April batch. **xAI completes Grok 4.3 API rollout — 1M context, native video, 207 tok/s, 20% cheaper than Grok 4.20** — xAI quietly completed the full Grok 4.3 API rollout on 30 April after a soft-launch on 17 April. The model goes GA on the xAI API and on Oracle's OCI Enterprise AI; pricing settles at $1.25 input / $2.50 output per million tokens with a 1M-token context window and 207 tok/s output. Grok 4.3 scores 53 on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index — above Claude Sonnet 4.6, behind GPT-5.5 (60), Claude Opus 4.7 and Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview — and is ~20% cheaper to run the index than the previous Grok 4.20 v2. Largest single-benchmark jump is GDPval-AA: ELO 1500 vs 1179 for the prior model. _Why it matters:_ This is xAI's clearest agentic-tool-calling position to date and a deliberately quiet launch — no livestream, no Elon thread, just an API push. The price drop matters more than the benchmark: at $1.25/$2.50 it undercuts Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Gemini 3.1 Pro on agentic loops where Grok 4.3 has parity. [Artificial Analysis — Grok 4.3 agentic + pricing](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/xai-launches-grok-4-3-with-improved-agentic-performance-and-lower-pricing) · [VentureBeat — aggressively low price + voice cloning](https://venturebeat.com/technology/xai-launches-grok-4-3-at-an-aggressively-low-price-and-a-new-fast-powerful-voice-cloning-suite) · [xAI release notes](https://docs.x.ai/developers/release-notes)

**EU AI Act Digital Omnibus trilogue collapses at dawn 28–29 April; next round 13 May; 2 August deadline back in play** — Reporting solidified on 30 April that the second political trilogue on the Digital Omnibus on AI broke up at dawn on 29 April after ~12 hours of talks. The point of failure was not the headline (postponing the 2 August high-risk obligations) but Annex I conformity-assessment architecture for AI embedded in products under existing EU sectoral safety law. The Cypriot Council Presidency now has 13 May targeted for a third trilogue and is racing to close the file before its term ends 30 June. _Why it matters:_ With the August deadline legally still in force and no political agreement, providers of high-risk AI systems can no longer assume the postponement will arrive in time. The Annex I dispute is the more consequential one for industry — it determines whether AI inside cars, medical devices and machinery gets fast-tracked or routed through full third-party conformity assessment. **Legora hits $5.6 B at Series D close; NVIDIA NVentures takes first legal-AI position** — Swedish legal-AI startup Legora confirmed on 30 April that it has reached a $5.6 B valuation, with NVIDIA's corporate VC fund NVentures backing the company in what is reportedly NVentures' first legal-AI investment. The round extends the March Series D and sharpens Legora's positioning against U.S. incumbent Harvey, which is itself in a fresh round. _Why it matters:_ NVIDIA backing a legal-AI vertical is the strongest signal yet that Jensen views the SI/services layer as part of the compute flywheel — same logic as Sierra's $950 M round a few days later and the OpenAI Deployment Company in May. Legora is the first European applied-agent company to clear $5 B. [TechCrunch — Legora $5.6 B](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/30/legal-ai-startup-legora-hits-5-6-valuation-and-its-battle-with-harvey-just-got-hotter/) · [Crunchbase — April billion-dollar rounds](https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/global-startup-funding-april-2026-anthropic-jeff-bezos-project-prometheus-biggest-deals/)

**GPT-5.5 GA arrives in Microsoft Foundry the day after MSFT-OpenAI restructure** — GPT-5.5 (and the GPT-5.5 Pro parallel-compute variant) reached general availability inside Microsoft Foundry on 30 April, one day after the 29 April Microsoft Q3 print and the 27 April restructure. The Foundry availability matters more than the timing: with OpenAI no longer exclusive to Azure, Foundry remains the first place GPT-5.5 ships at enterprise grade — but Microsoft is also wiring up Anthropic Claude as a default in Copilot for Excel/PowerPoint (1 May Agent 365 GA hands a multi-vendor agent stack to IT admins). _Why it matters:_ The "Foundry-first" model continues to hold despite the non-exclusivity — but the gap is days, not quarters. The post-restructure Foundry-on-day-1 cadence is what makes the multi-cloud OpenAI strategy survivable for both sides.
