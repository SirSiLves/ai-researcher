## Major announcements & releases

**Anthropic weighs $50 B round at ≥$900 B valuation — would topple OpenAI as the world's most valuable AI startup** — Bloomberg, CNBC and TechCrunch reported on 29 April that Anthropic is entertaining preemptive funding offers in the $850–900 B valuation range, potentially raising as much as $50 B in fresh capital. The company has not accepted any offers; sources tell TechCrunch the round could close within two weeks. Context: OpenAI was valued at $852 B in late March after a record-breaking $122 B round; Anthropic's last raise was in February at a $380 B valuation. The 27 April Microsoft–OpenAI restructure and 24 April Google $40 B (up to) Anthropic commitment have repriced the entire frontier-lab cap stack. _Why it matters:_ If Anthropic clears $900 B with this round, it inverts the OpenAI-on-top narrative that has defined the cycle. The 80x Q1 revenue growth Anthropic cited internally — plus the Google compute commitment, AWS Trainium 5GW capacity and Claude Code/Cowork momentum — is the underlying story; the funding mark is the visible signal. **Mistral launches Vibe Remote Agents and Mistral Medium 3.5 (128 B dense, 256 k context, 77.6% SWE-Bench Verified)** — Mistral on 29 April released Mistral Medium 3.5 — a 128 B dense model with a 256 k context window — as the new default in Mistral Vibe (its coding-agent platform) and Le Chat. The model unifies instruction-following, reasoning and coding in a single set of weights; it scores 77.6% on SWE-Bench Verified, beating Devstral 2 and Qwen3.5-397B-A17B. Alongside, Mistral introduced Remote Agents in Vibe: cloud-resident coding sessions that run asynchronously and in parallel from the Vibe CLI or directly in Le Chat, with local sessions teleportable to the cloud without losing state. Open weights on Hugging Face under a modified MIT licence; API pricing $1.50/$7.50 per M tokens; Le Chat Work mode (parallel multi-step agentic tasks across email/calendar/docs/Jira/Slack) ships on Pro, Team and Enterprise. _Why it matters:_ This is Mistral's clearest coding-agent move — and the first time the company has fielded a model at the SWE-Bench frontier (77.6% is in the same band as Claude Opus and GPT-5.5). Vibe Remote Agents is positioned directly against Codex on Bedrock (launched yesterday) and Claude Code's cloud-execution path. **Microsoft Q3 FY26 earnings: $82.9 B revenue (+18% YoY), Azure +40%, Copilot >20 M paid seats, AI run-rate >$37 B** — Microsoft reported Q3 FY26 results after market close on 29 April: revenue $82.9 B (+18% YoY), Azure and other cloud services +40% (39% constant-currency). The company disclosed >20 M paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats, with Copilot for M365 paid seats up 75% sequentially and reaching ~15 M users across 80% of the Fortune 500. CFO Amy Hood said AI business annual revenue run-rate grew 123% YoY past $37 B. CY26 capex guidance: ~$190 B, including ~$25 B from component-pricing headwinds. _Why it matters:_ Two days after the OpenAI exclusivity unwind, Microsoft's number proves the Azure-AI flywheel survives the restructure. Copilot's 75% sequential acceleration is the most important enterprise-adoption data point of the quarter; the $190 B capex line is what every hyperscaler will be benchmarked against. **OpenAI publishes cyber-defender Action Plan and confirms GPT-5.5-Cyber rollout** — OpenAI on 29 April published an Action Plan detailing how it will arm cyber defenders ahead of an Anthropic-Mythos-style frontier security model. Sam Altman confirmed GPT-5.5-Cyber would start rolling out to "critical cyber defenders" within days. The plan extends the Trusted Access for Cyber (TAC) program with tiered access by trust level and mission; the broader push covers democratising cyber defence, coordinating across government and industry, strengthening security around frontier cyber capabilities, preserving visibility/control in deployment, and enabling self-defence at the user level. _Why it matters:_ This is OpenAI's direct counter to Anthropic's Mythos Preview (announced 8 April under Project Glasswing). The "time to keep up with AI-driven attacks is narrowing" framing is the clearest acknowledgement yet from a frontier lab that offensive AI cyber capability is shipping and defenders need parity. **Meta Q1 2026 earnings: $56.3 B revenue (+33%), 2026 capex raised to $125–145 B, ~8,000 layoffs framed as "AI bill"** — Meta reported Q1 results on 29 April: revenue $56.3 B (+33% YoY), 41% operating margin, EPS $10.44. The stock dropped ~9% post-print on the earnings miss and capex hike. Meta raised CY26 capex guidance to $125–145 B citing higher component pricing and additional data-centre costs; the company added a $107 B step-up in contractual commitments for cloud and infrastructure in a single quarter. CFO Susan Li confirmed Meta will reduce employee headcount in May — ~8,000 jobs, with cuts expected to begin 20 May and additional rounds later in the year. Zuckerberg held a town hall framing the layoffs as a line-item against the AI bill: "We are seeing more and more examples where one or two people are building something in a week that would have previously taken dozens of people months." _Why it matters:_ This is the first time a Magnificent-7 CEO has explicitly framed layoffs as an offset to AI capex on an earnings day. The $107 B contractual step-up validates the Graviton-CPU thesis from 27 April: Meta's agentic-AI compute footprint is rising even faster than its capex line implies. **SAP API policy v4/2026 quietly blocks external AI agents from SAP APIs** — Techzine reported on 29 April that SAP's updated API Policy v4/2026 (Section 2.2.2) explicitly prohibits the use of SAP APIs by AI systems that "independently schedule or execute calls." Salesforce and ServiceNow are taking the opposite posture — opening their MCP servers and API surfaces to external agents. _Why it matters:_ With Sapphire 2026 two weeks away, SAP is fencing off its surface to keep customers inside Joule and Joule Studio rather than letting Claude/Copilot/Otto orchestrate over SAP transactions. The split with Salesforce/ServiceNow is the most consequential enterprise-platform divergence of the agentic era — it defines whether customers can build their own multi-vendor agent stack or have to buy the OEM's.
