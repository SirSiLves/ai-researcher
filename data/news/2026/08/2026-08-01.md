## Major announcements & releases

**OpenAI publishes ten decade-open mathematics results produced by "Astra" — every argument shipped with a Lean certificate, for about $2,000 of tokens** — The flagship item of the day and the first time OpenAI has named its next major model in a research publication. An internal version of **Astra** produced new results on ten problems that had seen no progress on their main result for at least a decade, spanning high-dimensional geometry, coding theory, arithmetic circuit complexity, group theory, operator algebras, quantum complexity, lattice cryptography and extremal combinatorics. The named results include new upper bounds on sphere-packing density down to the Cohn–Elkies threshold, exponentially improved bounds on binary and spherical codes, a construction establishing the existence of **non-sofic groups**, a **disproof of Connes's rigidity conjecture**, an arithmetic-formula lower bound of order n⁴/log n for the permanent, an exponential parallel repetition theorem for two-player quantum games, polynomial-factor hardness of approximation for the closest vector problem, Ehrhart's volume conjecture, and resolutions of Erdős problems 146, 180 and 183. Humans prepared the arguments into manuscripts using the same model; the model then formalised each one as a **Lean certificate**, and OpenAI released a narration of the model's reasoning for each solution. The stated compute: the tokens needed to *find* all ten solutions would cost roughly **$2,000 at Sol API rates**. _Why it matters:_ two numbers carry this. The Lean formalisation is the strongest verification bar any AI-produced mathematics has cleared at this scale — it converts "the model claims a proof" into "a machine checked the proof," which is exactly the property the field has been demanding since the Erdős unit-distance disproof in May. And $2,000 puts decade-open problems inside the discretionary budget of any funded research group, which reframes the frontier-model value proposition from assistance to production of novel results. Independent community review is still pending, and the Lean certificates are what will make that review fast. [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/) · [Developers Digest breakdown](https://www.developersdigest.tech/blog/openai-ten-advances-mathematics-lean-2026)

**DeepSeek ships V4-Flash-0731 as the official release and moves the API into public beta — Responses API native, adapted for Codex** — Published on Hugging Face on 31 July, with the model card explicit that this supersedes the preview. Architecture and size are unchanged: the gains come from **re-post-training, not a new design**, with the checkpoint shipping the DSpark speculative-decoding module attached (Hugging Face reports 304B parameters for the repo — the 284B base plus the draft module). On the API side `deepseek-v4-flash` now natively supports the **Responses API** format and is adapted for Codex. The caveat worth carrying: the 0731 build exists only behind the API — the downloadable weights still present the earlier preview, with April evaluation tables and no 0731 entry. _Why it matters:_ the headline gains are agentic and coding, and they were bought with post-training on a frozen architecture — which is the cheapest kind of capability jump to replicate and the hardest to see coming from a model-size watchlist. The Responses-API-native and Codex-adapted framing is the more strategic move: DeepSeek is making itself a drop-in behind harnesses built for OpenAI, so the substitution cost for a coding-agent stack drops to a base-URL change. **MiniMax H3 collapses the whole video stack into one omni-modal model with native stereo audio** — Launched 31 July, live in the platform API as `MiniMax-H3` and in the consumer Hailuo app. MiniMax is explicit that this is not text-to-video with add-ons: it reads text, images, video and audio **as one unified context** and returns 2K video with native stereo sound at 4–15 second integer durations. Where previous stacks split text-to-video, image-to-video, first-and-last-frame, subject reference, motion reference and video editing into separate expert models, H3 folds all of them into one pretraining paradigm where the reference and editing relationships are expressed **in natural language** — MiniMax's own example prompt asks it to take the camera movement from Video 1, have the character in Image 2 sing, and match the vocals to Audio 3. One endpoint, asynchronous create/poll/download, up to 9 reference images, 3 reference video clips and 3 audio clips (audio cannot be sent without an accompanying image or video), 12 files total, 7,000-character prompts. Not runnable on your own hardware today. _Why it matters:_ the interesting part is the interface, not the pixels. Replacing a routing layer over six expert models with a single context window addressed in prose is the same consolidation that happened to text models three years ago, and it removes the per-mode integration work that made video pipelines expensive to maintain. For anyone evaluating generative media for product or marketing workflows, the unit of comparison just changed from "which model for which task" to "one prompt, all modalities." [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/01/minimax-releases-minimax-h3-an-omni-modal-video-model-that-generates-15-second-2k-clips-with-native-stereo-audio/)

**Supabase open-sources Evals — a benchmark that runs Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode against a real Supabase stack and scores what they actually did** — Apache-2.0, published 31 July. Scenarios are grounded in real support tickets, bug reports and GitHub issues — building a schema, debugging a failed Edge Function, fixing a broken RLS policy — and each one runs against a **real Supabase environment in containers**, so agents invoke the actual MCP server and CLI rather than a mock. Two suites: *benchmark* scenarios chosen for breadth across product areas, cross-cutting topics and builder-journey stages, run against several harness configurations and published publicly; *regression* scenarios chosen for depth against known failure modes, refreshed daily and deliberately excluded from published scores so bug triage doesn't skew the leaderboard. Scoring mixes **deterministic checks** (can a user reach this data, does the Edge Function return the expected result) with LLM-as-a-judge only where semantic judgment is unavoidable, and agents get one retry before grading to suppress false negatives. _Why it matters:_ this is the most transferable eval design published this month, and the transferable part is the split. A public benchmark you cannot tune against, plus a private daily regression suite you *can* — that separation is what keeps a vendor-run leaderboard honest, and almost nobody publishing agent benchmarks currently maintains it. The second lesson is architectural: Supabase measured the agent experience across CLI, MCP server, agent skills and docs *together*, because that is the surface an agent actually meets. Vendors shipping an MCP server without measuring it alongside their docs are measuring a fraction of their own product. [Supabase](https://supabase.com/blog/introducing-supabase-evals) · [MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/01/supabase-releases-evals-an-open-source-benchmark-that-scores-claude-code-codex-and-opencode-on-real-supabase-tasks/)

**GEMA beats Suno in Munich — training on protected works, not just output, is covered by the injunction** — The Landgericht München I ruled on **31 July** that Suno reproduced protected musical works without permission for its AI music generator, and must stop. Six tracks were at issue, including "Atemlos," "Big in Japan" and "Daddy Cool." Per Tagesschau's report of the ruling, the prohibition extends to **use of the works in AI training**, not only to generated output. Suno argued that the works are not stored in the model — only mathematical parameters derived from large datasets — invoked US fair use, and disputed the German court's jurisdiction; all three arguments failed. GEMA's evidentiary approach was blunt: it fed the generator only the lyrics, title and style of the six songs, withholding melody and arrangement, and reported that the outputs "strongly resembled" the originals. This follows GEMA's November 2025 win against OpenAI at the same court over memorised lyrics. _Why it matters:_ two German rulings in nine months have now rejected the "it's only parameters" defence, and this one reaches training rather than stopping at output. That is the distinction European licensing negotiations have been waiting on, and it lands with a US defendant that argued fair use and jurisdiction and lost on both — which makes the Munich court an attractive venue for the next rightsholder. Anyone fine-tuning on catalogue content for the EU market should treat the training-stage exposure as live. **AWS ships an Agentic Catalog Experience in Amazon Quick — semantic metadata flows from the catalog into the AI answer instead of being retyped** — Preview for AWS Glue Data Catalog and Databricks Unity Catalog. The problem statement is the sharpest articulation of the enterprise context gap this week: data teams have already invested in upstream catalogs — Glue, Unity Catalog, Snowflake Horizon, Collibra, dbt — where table descriptions, column semantics, primary/foreign key relationships, glossary terms and metric definitions are meticulously defined; none of that reaches the Text2SQL product that business users actually touch. AWS names three compounding failures: **limited discoverability** across thousands of tables, **semantic fragmentation** forcing curators to manually recreate descriptions and reconcile definitions (does "revenue" mean gross or net; is an "active customer" 30 days or 90), and a resulting **time-to-insight measured in weeks**, which then decays into semantic drift as upstream definitions change and manually copied ones go stale. The new experience lets curators describe what they need in natural language, discovers the upstream assets, and auto-creates Datasets and Topics with **inherited semantics**. _Why it matters:_ this is the enterprise RAG problem stated correctly for once. The failure is not retrieval quality, it is that the governed meaning of the data lives in a system the AI product never reads — and the second-order failure, semantic drift after a manual copy, is what erodes trust in dashboards months after the pilot succeeded. Inheriting semantics rather than copying them is the right architecture, and it is the question to put to every vendor selling natural-language analytics. [AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/announcing-the-agentic-catalog-experience-in-amazon-quick/)

**NVIDIA publishes an attention co-design checklist for long-context agentic inference — with the number that explains the bill** — Attention is no longer a fixed cost you optimise around: NVIDIA's measurements show DeepSeek-R1's prefill attention share rising from **18% at 4K context to 85% at 128K**, which means how attention is *designed* — not merely implemented — now determines a model's inference economics. The post distils GEMM-shape arithmetic plus measured FP8 prefill and decode kernels into four guidelines: keep **group size** (query heads per KV head) high because decode efficiency scales with it; use head dimensions of **128 or 256** to match GPU tile and memory alignment; minimise effective KV state via cache compression, sparse or sliding-window attention, or hybrid architectures; and let KV-head count dictate parallelism — tensor parallelism must never exceed KV heads or you duplicate the KV cache, and models with few KV heads should use Attention Data Parallelism, KV Parallelism, or hybrids like Wide EP and Helix Parallelism in TensorRT-LLM. _Why it matters:_ the 18%→85% curve is the single most useful number for anyone budgeting agentic workloads, because agent harnesses grow context monotonically by construction. It says the cost of a long-running agent is dominated by a term most capacity models do not have a line for, and it converts "our agents got slower as sessions got longer" from a mystery into an arithmetic result. [NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/co-designing-ai-model-attention-for-fast-interactive-long-context-inference/)

**AI fatigue: nearly three quarters of German knowledge workers want the pre-ChatGPT era back, and 41% would abolish generative AI outright** — An Adaptavist survey of 2,500 knowledge workers across several countries, 500 of them in Germany. Among German respondents, **~75% look back longingly at work before generative AI** and 41% would prefer to abolish it entirely. The stated reasons are about quality, not fear of replacement: **45%** say dealing with substandard AI output makes their work feel less meaningful and more repetitive, **37%** report lower overall motivation, and **25%** feel constrained in their creativity. t3n reads it against OpenAI's own study of 800,000+ user messages, which found employees — especially at smaller companies — increasingly performing tasks outside their actual job description, most visibly in customer service and design but also HR, marketing and legal, a shift visible in usage data before it shows up in job postings. _Why it matters:_ the enterprise counterweight to a week of abundance narratives, and the mechanism is specific enough to act on. The dissatisfaction is driven by **rework** — humans cleaning up low-quality generated output — which is the same failure that OpenAI's own cost argument identified from the vendor side: a cheap model that needs retries and human repair is not cheap. Deployments that measure token spend but not the human repair cost downstream are measuring the wrong half, and this survey is what that shows up as on an engagement score.
