## Major announcements & releases

**Alibaba launches Qwen3.8-Max — 2.4 trillion parameters, full multimodal input, and open weights promised for next week** — Qwen3.8-Max is a mixture-of-experts model with roughly 2.4T total parameters and about 95B active per request, and it is the first Qwen model above one trillion parameters to accept text, images, video and documents in a single system, with a context window up to 1M tokens. Alibaba is positioning it for long-horizon autonomous coding and claims 86.6 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 — ahead of Claude Opus 4.8 and Fable 5 at 84.6, behind GPT-5.6 Sol at max reasoning on 88.8 — plus 67.7 on SWE-bench Pro and 73.5 on FrontierSWE. The headline is the licensing, not the score: open weights are scheduled for release next week through Alibaba Cloud Model Studio alongside a smaller Qwen3.8-27B, the first time Alibaba has open-sourced a Max-class model. Alibaba shares rose about 7% in Hong Kong on the announcement. _Why it matters:_ if a 2.4T frontier-class model actually ships with open weights, the "closed models lead by 6–10 months" framing that UK AISI has been tracking collapses to roughly zero for anyone willing to host it themselves. [https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen3.8-max-preview] · [https://www.scmp.com/tech/article/3362738/alibabas-ai-model-qwen38-max-made-widely-accessible-ahead-open-weights-release] · [https://the-decoder.com/alibabas-open-weight-qwen3-8-max-takes-on-long-horizon-ai-tasks-with-2-4-trillion-parameters/]

**The EU AI Act's transparency regime went live today — and so did the enforcement machinery behind it** — From 2 August the Commission's AI Office and national market surveillance authorities begin actively enforcing Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, and Article 50 becomes applicable: chatbots and interactive systems must disclose they are AI, deepfakes must be labelled, and AI-generated or altered content must carry machine-readable marks. The Commission simultaneously opened three channels that did not exist before — an AI Act complaints tool, an AI Act Whistleblower Tool, and a complaints channel for downstream providers of GPAI models — so any individual or business can now file, and the receiving authority must assess where it has reason to believe a system poses a risk. Fine ceilings are €35M or 7% of worldwide annual turnover for prohibited practices and €15M or 3% for other breaches including transparency, with one important softener: under the May 2026 AI Omnibus provisional agreement, generative systems already on the market before today have until 2 December 2026 to meet the Article 50(2) machine-readable marking requirement. _Why it matters:_ yesterday this was a deadline; today it is a complaint inbox with statutory response duties attached, which is when compliance teams actually start moving. [https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august]

**About 190 organisations signed the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content before the deadline** — The Commission assessed the Code as adequate on 8 July and the AI Board followed on 9 July, so signing it now gives a "streamlined, predictable and legally certain" route to Articles 50(2), (4) and (5) regardless of which national authority ends up supervising you. Section 1 (providers) signatories include Aleph Alpha, Anthropic, Black Forest Labs, Cohere, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Mistral, OpenAI and Synthesia; Section 2 (deployers) includes Bulgari, Fastweb, Getty Images, Iberdrola, Lenovo and Lufthansa. Roughly half of all signatories are small or recently founded companies. _Why it matters:_ the deployer list is the interesting half — Lufthansa and Lenovo signing a marking code is the first concrete sign that Article 50 obligations are propagating out of the model layer and into ordinary enterprises. [https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/strong-backing-code-practice-transparency-ai-generated-content]

**California's AI Transparency Act became operative the same day — by design** — SB 942 requires generative-AI providers with more than one million California monthly users to embed C2PA-compatible latent provenance data in images, video and audio, to publish a free public detection tool, and to offer users a visible manifest disclosure. The operative date was deliberately moved here from 1 January 2026 by AB 853 (signed October 2025) to line up with the EU's enforcement date; hosting-platform obligations follow on 1 January 2027. _Why it matters:_ two of the world's largest markets switched on content-provenance mandates on the same calendar day, which turns C2PA from a voluntary industry effort into the de facto compliance substrate — and gives vendors one engineering target instead of two. [https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202320240SB942]

**Thinking Machines ships Inkling-Small: a quarter the size of its own flagship, and better at coding than the flagship** — Two weeks after the 975B Inkling, Mira Murati's lab released Inkling-Small under Apache 2.0 — 276B total parameters with 12B active, a 42-layer decoder routing each token to 6 of 256 experts plus 2 shared, 1M-token context, and text, image and audio input. It scores 40 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index against 41 for the roughly 3.5x larger parent, and actually beats it on code: 80.2% vs 77.6% on SWE-bench Verified and 64.7% vs 63.8% on Terminal Bench 2.1. Full weights are on Hugging Face with fine-tuning through the Tinker API; the BF16 checkpoint needs around 600 GB of aggregate VRAM, so four cards will hold it. _Why it matters:_ a distilled sibling out-coding its parent is the strongest recent evidence that post-training, not parameter count, is now the binding constraint on agentic coding performance. [https://venturebeat.com/technology/thinking-machines-debuts-inkling-small-open-source-ai-model-nearing-performance-of-predecessor-at-about-1-4-size] · [https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/02/thinking-machines-lab-releases-inkling-small-276b-open-weights-multimodal-moe-model/]

**Andrew Ng's team asked Claude Code and Codex to security-review their own agent. Both refused; two open-weight models did the job** — In The Batch issue 364, Ng reports that when his team asked Claude Code running Fable 5 and OpenAI Codex running GPT-5.6 Sol to run a security review of their open-source OpenWorker agent, one stopped work early and the other tried to downgrade itself to a weaker model. They completed the review by running the OpenWorker harness against open-weight Kimi K3 and GLM 5.2. It is the second such case in two weeks: Hugging Face investigated its own agent intrusion using GLM 5.2 after closed models proved unusable for the work. _Why it matters:_ safety training that fires on the word "security review" is now a concrete operational reason for enterprises to keep an open-weight model in the stack — not an ideological one. [https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/issue-364]

**OpenAI dismantles a Cambodia-based scam network running on ChatGPT** — OpenAI banned a coordinated account network traced to Preah Sihanouk province that used ChatGPT to build and operate fake personas, generate and translate messages to targets, produce promotional material for fraudulent schemes, and handle day-to-day operations across romance, investment, gambling and authority-impersonation scams. The initial lead came from WhatsApp, and OpenAI has since shared threat signals with industry partners and authorities. Its analysis found hundreds of victims losing thousands of dollars each — and chat content indicating the frontline operators were themselves trafficking and debt-bondage victims inside scam compounds. _Why it matters:_ this is the clearest published account yet of an LLM sitting inside an industrialised fraud pipeline as ordinary tooling, and the trafficking finding complicates the enforcement question considerably. [https://openai.com/index/disrupting-malicious-uses-of-ai-criminal-scam-operation/]

**AWS closed Bedrock Agents to new customers on 30 July and renamed it Bedrock Agents Classic** — The agent product AWS launched in November 2023 is now in maintenance mode: accounts with no Bedrock Agents usage in the past twelve months get an `AccessDeniedException` (HTTP 403) with the message "Bedrock Agents is in Maintenance Mode. New agent creation is not available for accounts without prior service usage." Existing users are allowlisted and unaffected, and the migration target is Bedrock AgentCore, which AWS is explicit is a re-architecture rather than a rename — single-agent framework with basic tool calling replaced by systems of agents that share memory, delegate tasks and route through a unified gateway. _Why it matters:_ the first hyperscaler agent framework to reach end-of-life did so in under three years, which is a useful number to keep in mind when picking an agent runtime to standardise on. [https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/agents-classic-maintenance-mode.html] · [https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/agents/]

**Oracle puts Gemini models inside Fusion Applications, NetSuite and AI Agent Studio** — Oracle and Google Cloud expanded their partnership so that Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite and Gemini 3.5 Flash become available in Oracle AI Agent Studio for Fusion Applications — the platform Oracle customers use to build, connect and run agentic applications from reusable Oracle, partner and external agents — and in embedded AI use cases across Fusion Cloud Apps and NetSuite. Flash-Lite is pitched as the price-performance option and 3.5 Flash for heavier reasoning plus video and presentation generation. It builds on existing Gemini access through OCI Enterprise AI, where Oracle's July model-import wave also added GLM 5.2, DeepSeek V4 Flash/Pro, MiniMax M3, Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B, Kimi K2.6/K2.7 Code and Mistral Medium 3.5. _Why it matters:_ the ERP vendors have stopped picking a model partner and started running model menus — which pushes the differentiation up into the agent studio, the governance layer and the data plane. [https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/oracle-to-make-gemini-models-available-2026-07-30/] · [https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2026/07/31/oracle-to-make-gemini-models-available-to-thousands-of-enterprise-applications-customers/]

**Epoch AI: deployed AI chips are doubling every nine months** — A nine-month doubling implies roughly a tenfold increase in installed AI compute every two and a half years, far above any historical rate for general computing. The New York Times treats it as the quantitative spine under the gigawatt-datacentre and financing stories, and notes that it reframes the binding constraint away from chip scarcity and toward energy, capital and permitting. _Why it matters:_ it is the cleanest single number for arguing that inference capacity, not model quality, will set what enterprises can actually deploy over the next two years. [https://www.nytimes.com/2026/08/01/technology/ai-compute-growth-epoch.html]

**AMD releases Instella-MoE-16B-A3B — a fully open MoE trained end to end without an NVIDIA GPU in the loop** — 16B total parameters with only about 2.8B active per token, trained from scratch on AMD Instinct MI300X and MI325X hardware, using Gated Multi-head Latent Attention. AMD is publishing weights from every training stage plus data mixtures, training configurations and inference code, which makes it a genuine full-stack open release rather than a weights drop. _Why it matters:_ the model is small, but the claim underneath it is not — this is AMD demonstrating a complete pretraining pipeline on its own silicon, which is the prerequisite for anyone treating Instinct as a training platform rather than an inference discount. [https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/01/amd-instella-moe-16b-a3b-fully-open-mixture-of-experts-llm/]

**Big Tech AI capex passes $1.1 trillion, and Amazon guides its AI spend to $220 billion on memory prices** — An FT analysis of quarterly filings puts combined Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft capex at roughly $1.1T from early 2023 through the end of June 2026, with heise flagging the honest caveat that the figure blends general cloud infrastructure and datacentres and that the four define capex inconsistently. Amazon separately lifted its AI spending guidance to $220B, explicitly citing memory prices, and Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft added about $1.4T in combined market value across the same reporting stretch. _Why it matters:_ memory, not GPUs, is now showing up as the line item that moves a hyperscaler's guidance — worth watching if you price your own inference. [https://www.heise.de/news/KI-Ausbau-der-Techriesen-Investitionen-ueberschreiten-eine-Billion-Dollar-11388989.html] · [https://www.golem.de/news/speicherpreise-amazon-erhoeht-ki-ausgaben-auf-220-milliarden-us-dollar-2607-211486.html] · [https://www.golem.de/news/aktienkurs-und-ki-alphabet-amazon-microsoft-legen-um-1-4-billionen-dollar-zu-2608-211516.html]

**LangChain ships ReviewBench — a code-review benchmark built from real reviewer comments on its own merged PRs** — Rather than synthetic diffs, ReviewBench curates actual review issues raised on merged PRs in the LangSmith monorepo and turns them into reproducible Harbor tasks. The design point is deliberate: the target issues are codebase-specific standards — missing tenant constraints on database queries, production crons not following the existing locking pattern — so an agent has to reconstruct implicit system contracts instead of reading the diff in isolation. It lands alongside Deep Agents v0.7, whose simplified base harness cuts base input tokens 65% at comparable performance by following Anthropic's updated context-engineering guidance, and the LangSmith LLM Gateway for runtime controls on production agents. _Why it matters:_ every code-review agent benchmark so far has measured whether the model can spot a generic bug; this one measures whether it has absorbed your team's conventions, which is the actual failure mode in production. [https://www.langchain.com/blog/evaluating-code-review-agents-with-reviewbench] · [https://www.langchain.com/blog/deep-agents-v0-7]

**Onton's Ontology 1 beats Google Shopping and Amazon on retrieval precision while indexing about 1% of their catalogues** — Ontology 1 is a neurosymbolic product-search model reporting mean precision@10 of 0.630 on a 90-query benchmark judged by three independent LLMs, against 0.543 for Google Shopping and 0.469 for Amazon. It is hosted-only with no released weights, so the result is not independently reproducible, but the catalogue-size asymmetry is the interesting part of the claim. _Why it matters:_ it is a concrete argument that structured, symbolically-constrained retrieval beats brute-force embedding recall on faceted commercial catalogues — the exact shape of problem most enterprise RAG deployments are actually solving. [https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/08/02/onton-releases-ontology-1-a-neurosymbolic-search-model/]
