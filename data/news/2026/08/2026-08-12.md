## Major announcements & releases

**Google puts Gemini at the centre of Made by Google '26 — Pixel 11, Tensor G6 and background agents** — At the 12 Aug event Google unveiled the Pixel 11 ($899, 256 GB base), Pixel 11 Pro / Pro XL ($1,099), the Pixel 11 Pro Fold (October), Pixel Watch 5 and a first Pixel Tag tracker — but spent most of the keynote on Gemini. The Tensor G6 is the first major Android SoC on TSMC's 2 nm process and runs Gemini Nano on-device; a new Titan M3 security chip adopts NIST post-quantum cryptography standards. New Gemini features include Magic Capture (picks the best frames out of hundreds shot between start and stop), Rambler voice input that tolerates run-on speech and filler words, Circle to Search directly inside the Pixel Camera, and agentic actions for US users — ordering groceries, booking rides, buying coffee — executed in the background. _Why it matters:_ This is the first flagship launch where the differentiator is an agent that acts rather than a model that answers, and it lands the agentic loop on 2 nm consumer silicon rather than in a datacentre. The transactional actions are also the first mass-market test of an assistant spending the user's money. [https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/made-by-google-2026/](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/made-by-google-2026/) (coverage: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/google-unveils-pixel-11-lineup-new-airtag-rival-and-gemini-features-at-made-by-google-2026/), [TechCrunch — hardware vs Gemini split](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/pixel-11-has-few-hardware-changes-and-more-gemini/), [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/12/made-by-google-2026-announcements/), [Pixel 11 Pro post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/pixel/google-pixel-11-pro-xl/))

**Google DeepMind ships sign-language AI into Live Transcribe** — Announced alongside the Pixel launch, DeepMind's sign-language recognition work moves from research into a shipping accessibility feature: Live Transcribe now translates American Sign Language captured through the Pixel Camera into text, giving signers a path that doesn't route through typing. _Why it matters:_ It is a rare case of a frontier lab's research reaching general-population hardware in the same announcement cycle, and it puts a continuous-video model on-device rather than behind an API — the harder engineering problem, and the one that determines whether accessibility features actually get used. [https://deepmind.google/blog/putting-sign-language-ai-into-users-hands/](https://deepmind.google/blog/putting-sign-language-ai-into-users-hands/)

**OpenAI slows Astra's release: it cannot rule out Critical cyber capability under its own Preparedness Framework** — Internal evaluations run over the days before 11 Aug showed significant advances in agentic coding and cybersecurity for Astra — the model that on 1 Aug was reported to have closed ten open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science. OpenAI concluded it cannot rule out that Astra has crossed the Critical threshold of its Preparedness Framework, and is holding the release while it prioritises alignment training, expanded monitoring and additional security controls. For context, GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.6-Cyber were both assessed High, but below Critical. _Why it matters:_ This is the first time a frontier lab has publicly deferred a flagship release on the strength of its own capability evaluation rather than an external demand — the Preparedness Framework is being used as a brake in production, not as a document. Anyone tracking model-release risk policy now has a live precedent for what "Critical" triggers in practice. [https://openai.com/index/responding-next-frontier-critical-cyber-capabilities/](https://openai.com/index/responding-next-frontier-critical-cyber-capabilities/)

**OpenAI ships GPT-5.6-Cyber and expands Daybreak to vetted defenders** — Published 11 Aug, the Daybreak expansion gives approved security professionals frontier-model access for vulnerability research, code review, incident response and security testing through two tracks, Daybreak Blue and Daybreak Red. The dedicated GPT-5.6-Cyber model is in the API and has already been used internally to study and harden real projects including V8, Chrome's JavaScript engine, with disclosures running through Daybreak partners and the open-source community on mobile OS, database and kernel bugs. All individual Daybreak accounts must adopt hardware security keys from 1 September 2026. _Why it matters:_ Capability gating has become an access-control product: the same lab is simultaneously withholding Astra and selectively arming defenders, and the hardware-key mandate signals that model access itself is now treated as a credential worth stealing. [https://openai.com/index/expanding-daybreak-as-the-cyber-defense-window-narrows/](https://openai.com/index/expanding-daybreak-as-the-cyber-defense-window-narrows/) (model card: [GPT-5.6 Cyber](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-cyber), programme: [Daybreak](https://openai.com/daybreak/))

**Anthropic will watermark all Claude-generated text — worldwide, not just in the EU** — Announced 11 Aug: new Claude models embed an imperceptible, machine-readable watermark woven into the words themselves rather than into metadata, so it survives copy-paste and some editing. Coverage spans Claude, the Claude API, Claude Code, Claude Cowork and Claude Tag; generated files carry digitally signed C2PA metadata instead. The obligation comes from Article 50 of the EU AI Act, which applies to models launched in the EU on or after 2 August — Anthropic is applying it everywhere Claude is offered and is working to retrofit pre-2 August models during the transition period. It will publish detection details, while cautioning that short or heavily edited snippets may not resolve. _Why it matters:_ A single lab has just turned an EU transparency obligation into a global product default, which is the clearest instance yet of the Brussels effect operating on model behaviour rather than on paperwork. It also gives platforms and universities a detector that is not a statistical guess — and gives everyone else a reason to check whether their content pipeline strips it. [https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/anthropic-says-it-will-watermark-text-generated-by-its-ai-models/](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/11/anthropic-says-it-will-watermark-text-generated-by-its-ai-models/) `(vendor permalink not found — no anthropic.com/news or claude.com/blog post surfaced in search)` (coverage: [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/08/12/anthropic-claude-watermarks-ai-detection), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/11/anthropic-claude-watermark-ai-text-police-ai-slop/), [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/11/anthropic-start-watermarking-claude-generated-text-images/), [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2026/08/11/eu-compliance-delivered-globally-anthropic-to-watermark-claudes-output-worldwide))

**Snowflake Summit 2026: SAP zero-copy goes GA, Datastream enters preview, Open Semantic Interchange passes 50 vendors** — The Summit recap lands this week with 25+ announcements. Zero-Copy Integration to SAP is now generally available, wiring SAP data into Snowflake without duplication; the Salesforce integration (GA for two years) gets a reimagined connector. Snowflake Datastream enters preview as a fully managed, Apache Kafka-compatible streaming service native to the platform. The Open Semantic Interchange standard now counts 50+ participating vendors including Databricks, and governance centralises on Apache Polaris inside Horizon Catalog. _Why it matters:_ The agentic-AI fight between Snowflake and Databricks is being fought on semantics and zero-copy plumbing, not on models — whoever owns the semantic layer decides what an enterprise agent is allowed to believe is true. A 50-vendor interchange standard is the first sign that layer might not be proprietary. **ServiceNow's AI Control Tower reaches general availability this month** — The expanded Control Tower announced at Knowledge 2026 hits GA in August 2026. It discovers, observes, governs, secures and measures AI deployed across any system in the enterprise — 30 new integrations scan beyond ServiceNow into AWS, Google Cloud and Azure plus SAP, Oracle and Workday, detecting agents and connected devices across OT and IT. If an agent goes rogue or exceeds its permissions, the Control Tower can shut it down in real time. _Why it matters:_ Real-time agent kill-switch plus cross-cloud discovery is the concrete shape of the "agent control plane" category that Snowflake's Cortex AI Gateway and SAP's agent-sprawl argument have been circling for a fortnight. The differentiator is inventory: you cannot govern agents you have not found. **Oracle's AI datacentre bet is now visibly exposed to OpenAI's changing plans** — German coverage on 12 Aug frames Oracle's hundreds of billions in AI datacentre commitments as structurally dependent on a single tenant. Oracle contracted last year to build 4.5 GW of capacity for OpenAI under Stargate; that headline number remains on track, but the two have abandoned the expansion of the flagship Abilene, Texas site after financing talks stalled and OpenAI's requirements shifted — opening the door for Meta to lease the site instead — and Oracle has reportedly delayed several other OpenAI datacentres by up to a year on material and labour shortages. _Why it matters:_ Compare with Anthropic's Theseus structure from two days ago, where an infrastructure fund and a sovereign wealth fund absorb the capex risk and the lab merely signs a lease. Oracle took the opposite side of that trade with its own balance sheet, and this week is the first clear look at what that costs when the anchor tenant reprices. **Berlin switches on AI behaviour recognition in public space this month** — Berlin police begin AI video analysis at Kottbusser Tor in Kreuzberg from August 2026, with further districts to follow. The system flags anomalous behaviour patterns automatically and explicitly forgoes biometric facial recognition — passers-by are rendered as schematic stick figures. The CCC and netzpolitik.org have both published warnings about automatic behaviour recognition, and civil-society guidance on contesting the scanners is already circulating. _Why it matters:_ This is a live EU AI Act stress test in a member state capital: a system deliberately engineered to sit below the biometric-identification prohibition while still doing automated real-time inference on people in public. Wherever that line is finally drawn, it will be drawn on cases like this one. [https://netzpolitik.org/2026/ki-gestuetzte-videoueberwachung-ccc-warnt-berlin-vor-automatischer-verhaltenserkennung/](https://netzpolitik.org/2026/ki-gestuetzte-videoueberwachung-ccc-warnt-berlin-vor-automatischer-verhaltenserkennung/) — DE ("AI-supported video surveillance: CCC warns Berlin about automatic behaviour recognition") (context: [ad-hoc-news](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/wirtschaft/ki-videoueberwachung-berlin-startet-pilotprojekt-am-kottbusser-tor/69914016), [CILIP](https://www.cilip.de/2026/03/24/mehr-verhaltenserkennung-mit-ki-in-deutschen-staedten/))

**Controlled trial with 7,700 subjects: AI personalisation triples phishing success rates** — Reported by German tech press on 12 Aug, an experiment across 7,700 people found that LLM-personalised phishing emails converted at roughly three times the rate of generic ones. Personalisation now happens per-second — salutation, pretext, context and register adapt across thousands of recipients simultaneously, producing linguistically flawless, contextually grounded messages at negligible marginal cost. _Why it matters:_ It quantifies the offence side of the same capability curve that OpenAI is gating Astra over, and with a controlled n large enough to be actionable. Security-awareness training built around spotting bad grammar is now measurably obsolete. [https://www.cloud-computing-koeln.de/it-sicherheit-studie-zeigt-hohe-klickraten-durch-ki-spear-phishing/](https://www.cloud-computing-koeln.de/it-sicherheit-studie-zeigt-hohe-klickraten-durch-ki-spear-phishing/) — DE ("IT security: study shows high click rates from AI spear phishing") (context: [Washington Post op-ed](https://www.washingtonpost.com/opinions/2026/07/28/ai-is-gaining-ability-personalize-cyberattacks-enabling-phishing-scale/))
