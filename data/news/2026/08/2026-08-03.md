## Major announcements & releases

**Brussels' day two: the Commission has opened discussions with OpenAI and Anthropic over cyberattacks linked to their models** — Twenty-four hours after Article 50 became enforceable, the shape of actual enforcement got clearer, and it is not what most compliance teams prepared for. Sidley Austin partner Elisabetta Righini's read is that GPAI liability "isn't limited to substantive breaches — refusing an information request, giving misleading answers, or blocking a model evaluation is fineable on its own," which makes procedural non-cooperation an independent offence rather than an aggravating factor. Providers not headquartered in the EU must designate a local representative as the regulator's contact point. Separately, the Commission has opened discussions with OpenAI and Anthropic about cyber attacks connected to their models — following Anthropic granting ENISA access to Mythos and OpenAI's earlier offer of GPT-5.5-Cyber access. The Commission's own 2 August explainer adds the enforcement split: national market surveillance authorities, the AI Office for systems under its supervision, and the EDPS where an EU institution is the provider or deployer, with a separate €750k ceiling for EU bodies against €15M/3% for companies. _Why it matters:_ the first enforcement conversations are about model-linked cyber incidents, not deepfake labels — so the compliance surface that matters first is your incident-disclosure and evaluation-access posture, not your watermarking pipeline. [https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/03/eu-ai-act-enforcement-powers.html] · [https://qz.com/eu-ai-act-enforcement-fines-openai-anthropic-google-080326] · [https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/safer-and-more-transparent-ai-2026-08-02_en]

**The White House finished its frontier-AI evaluation framework and won't say what's in it** — The administration confirmed it met the 1 August deadline set by the June 2 executive order for a voluntary frontier-model evaluation framework, but declined to disclose the contents, who has seen it, or when labs would begin using it. One official would say only that the voluntary cyber tests measure the hacking capability of the most advanced US models. OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and other frontier labs have been invited to the White House tomorrow, Tuesday 4 August, to review it; the process is run out of the Office of the National Cyber Director and would establish a voluntary pre-release model submission channel. _Why it matters:_ this is the US answer to the AI Act landing the same week, and the contrast is the story — Brussels published a regulation with fine ceilings and a complaints inbox, Washington produced an unpublished voluntary framework negotiated with the companies it covers. **Microsoft Agent Framework crosses from SDK to supported runtime — and takes Semantic Kernel and AutoGen off the board** — The Agent Harness and Foundry Hosted Agents are now generally available, shipping as a single binary that runs identically in local development, in a container, and as a hosted deployment. The runtime handles function invocation, per-call history persistence, context compaction and a built-in todo mechanism, and Build 2026 stabilised connectors for the GitHub Copilot SDK and the Claude Agent SDK alongside multi-agent orchestration patterns. Semantic Kernel and AutoGen — the two frameworks Microsoft spent three years telling enterprises to build on — are both now in maintenance mode. _Why it matters:_ that is the second hyperscaler agent framework generation to be retired in a week, after AWS moved Bedrock Agents to Classic. Anyone standardising an agent runtime should now be pricing in a roughly three-year support horizon and choosing on migration cost, not features. [https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/08/agent-framework-harness-ga/]

Superblocks has 50 employees and $60M raised; AWS has no comparable business-user vibe-coding agent of its own, since Kiro is developer-facing. _Why it matters:_ the hyperscalers have worked out that the way to kill shadow AI is not to block it but to host it — expect Azure and Google Cloud to sign equivalents, and expect "runs in your own VPC" to become the default enterprise ask for every agent product. **Embabel 1.0: Rod Johnson brings goal-oriented planning agents to Spring and the JVM** — The creator of Spring shipped 1.0 GA of a Java/Kotlin agent framework where agents are defined as typed domain objects — goals, actions, preconditions — and a Goal-Oriented Action Planning engine replans at runtime when tool calls fail.
