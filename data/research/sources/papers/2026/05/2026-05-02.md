## Top picks

- **Feedback-Normalized Developer Memory for Reinforcement-Learning Coding Agents: A Safety-Gated MCP Architecture** — Mehmet Iscan (sole author). _Takeaway:_ MCP-architected memory layer for RL coding agents that normalizes developer feedback signals through safety-gated controls before they enter the policy update loop. Reports ~80% decision accuracy with robust hard-negative suppression on the proposed benchmark. Sits at the intersection of `mcp-adoption` (uses MCP as the substrate for agent-side memory operations) and `agent-safety` (gated writes prevent reward poisoning) — a concrete instantiation of "safety-gated memory" that the radar's been tracking. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.01567 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01567)

- **Automated Interpretability and Feature Discovery in Language Models with Agents** — Arnau Marin-Llobet, Javier Ferrando. _Takeaway:_ Autonomous multi-agent framework for mechanistic interpretability with two coupled loops — explanation refinement (one agent proposes competing hypotheses) and feature discovery (another generates prompt sets and constructs activation-space graphs). Important data point on whether research agents can do real internal-mechanism science rather than reformulating known features; pairs with the `eval-and-observability` and `agent-as-researcher` lines. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.01555 (cs.AI/cs.LG). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01555](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01555)

- **ControBench: An Interaction-Aware Benchmark for Controversial Discourse Analysis on Social Networks** — Ta Thanh Thuy, Jiaqi Zhu (Fri 1 May rollover). _Takeaway:_ Benchmark dataset combining social interaction graphs with textual content for analyzing controversial discussions across ideological divides on Reddit. Useful for evaluating LLM-as-judge and content-moderation systems on content where context (who is replying to whom) matters as much as text — relevant to safety/eval stack. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.00513 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00513)

- **Rethinking LLM Ensembling from the Perspective of Mixture Models** — Jiale Fu, Yuchu Jiang (Fri 1 May rollover). _Takeaway:_ Recasts LLM ensembling as a mixture-of-experts inference problem rather than the prevailing logit-/output-aggregation approach. Reports a faster, more principled ensembler that recovers what ad-hoc ensemble heuristics approximate — useful primitive for inference platforms running multiple frontier models for routing or quality lifts. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.00419 (cs.CL/cs.LG). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00419)

- **Synthetic Computers at Scale for Long-Horizon Productivity Simulation** — Tao Ge, Baolin Peng (Microsoft, late-April submission surfaced for the May 1–2 window). _Takeaway:_ Constructs 1,000 synthetic computer environments to train computer-use / productivity agents on realistic long-horizon tasks; reports significant in-domain and out-of-domain gains. Concrete throughput contribution to the CUA / browser-agent line where data scarcity for full-day workflows is the bottleneck. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.28181 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28181)

- **Claw-Eval-Live: A Live Agent Benchmark for Evolving Real-World Workflows** — Chenxin Li, Zhengyang Tang (late-April submission surfaced for the May 1–2 window). _Takeaway:_ Live benchmark for evolving real-world agent workflows — even with deterministic grading and structured LLM-judge scoring, the strongest model only hits 66.7% task completion. Sharp eval gap that makes "agents are productionizable" claims testable; relevant to the `agent-eval` and `production-readiness` threads. _Category:_ arXiv:2604.28139 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28139)

## Other interesting items

- **[FlashRT: Towards Computationally and Memory Efficient Red-Teaming for Prompt Injection and Knowledge Corruption](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28157)** — Yanting Wang, Chenlong Yin (late-April rollover). 2-7x speedup and 2-4x memory reduction for prompt-injection / RAG-corruption red-teaming — concrete operational win for safety teams running these evals at scale.
- **[Safety Drift After Fine-Tuning: Evidence from High-Stakes Domains](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24902)** — Emaan Bilal Khan, Amy Winecoff (late-April rollover). Empirical evidence that domain fine-tuning measurably erodes safety properties even when base-model evals look clean — direct input to the post-deployment safety-drift governance debate.
- **[The Last Human-Written Paper: Agent-Native Research Artifacts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24658)** — Jiachen Liu, Jiaxin Pei (late-April rollover). Argues for replacing the static PDF with machine-executable research packages so AI agents can re-run, fact-check, and extend results. Adjacent to the `auto-research` thread.
