## Top picks

- **AIP: Agent Identity Protocol for Verifiable Delegation Across MCP and A2A** — Sunil Prakash. _Takeaway:_ Introduces Invocation-Bound Capability Tokens (IBCTs) — issued in compact JWT and chained Biscuit forms — that carry verifiable identity and attenuated delegation across MCP and A2A boundaries, after a scan of ~2,000 MCP servers found all of them lacked authentication. Direct input to the `agent-identity` and `mcp-adoption` radar threads. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.24775. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24775)
- **AgentRFC: Security Design Principles and Conformance Testing for Agent Protocols** — Shenghan Zheng, Qifan Zhang. _Takeaway:_ First systematic security framework for AI-agent protocols (MCP, A2A, ANP, ACP) with a formal architectural model and a tool that detects gaps both within a single protocol and across protocol compositions on shared infra. Reads as the protocol-conformance counterpart to AIP's identity layer. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.23801. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23801)
- **Mind the Gap: How the Technical Mechanisms of Agentic AI Outpace Global Legal Frameworks** — Marcel Osmond, Thomas Jego. _Takeaway:_ Comparative legal analysis showing that current statutes conflate model capability with system architecture and miss the actual control points (system prompts, API permissions, sandboxing) where agentic behavior is governed. Direct ammunition for the EU-AI-Act-readiness debate. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.27075. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27075](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27075)
- **Cryptographic Runtime Governance for Autonomous AI Systems: The Aegis Architecture for Verifiable Policy Enforcement** — Adam Massimo Mazzocchetti. _Takeaway:_ Treats policy and legal constraints as cryptographically sealed *execution* conditions rather than advisory guidelines, with autonomous shutdown when violations occur. One of the first concrete designs for a hardware-rooted compliance boundary on autonomous agents. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.16938. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16938)
- **Runtime Governance for AI Agents: Policies on Paths** — Maurits Kaptein, Vassilis-Javed Khan. _Takeaway:_ Formalises compliance policies as deterministic functions over (agent identity, partial path, proposed next action, organizational state) → violation probability — i.e. real-time path evaluation rather than design-time constraints. Pairs with Aegis as the policy-language sibling to its enforcement mechanism. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.16586. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16586)
- **Risk-Adjusted Harm Scoring for Automated Red Teaming for LLMs in Financial Services** — Fabrizio Dimino, Bhaskarjit Sarmah. _Takeaway:_ Domain-aware harm-scoring framework that weights red-team findings by financial-regulatory risk, rather than treating every successful jailbreak as equivalent. Pragmatic eval primitive for any team operating LLMs under sectoral regulation. _Category:_ arXiv:2603.10807. [https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10807)

## Other interesting items

- **[Contrastive Reasoning Alignment: Reinforcement Learning from Hidden Representations (CRAFT)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17305)** — Luo, Wang. Aligns reasoning models against jailbreaks at the hidden-representation level rather than the output level; ~4.7% accuracy improvement on R1-Distill / Qwen3-Thinking with smaller safety-vs-reasoning trade-off than output-level defenses.
- **[An Onto-Relational-Sophic Framework for Governing Synthetic Minds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18633)** — Ning, Ding. Philosophy-grade governance framework that proposes a graded spectrum of digital personhood as a complement to capability-based AI policy. Useful as the conceptual end of the governance reading list.
- **[Mending the Holes: Mitigating Reward Hacking in RL for Multilingual Translation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.13045)** — Liu, Ouyang. WALAR uses word and language alignment to plug the QE-model holes that RL otherwise amplifies; concrete reward-hacking mitigation pattern that generalises beyond translation.
- **[AgenticCyOps: Securing Multi-Agentic AI Integration in Enterprise Cyber Operations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09134)** — Mitra, Patel. Earlier in the week but central to this slice: tool orchestration and memory management are the two dominant MAS attack surfaces, with five defensive principles tied to compliance standards.
