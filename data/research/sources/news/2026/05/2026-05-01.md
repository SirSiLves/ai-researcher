## Major announcements & releases

**Microsoft Agent 365 + Microsoft 365 E7 "Frontier Suite" reach general availability** — Microsoft on 1 May made Agent 365 and the Microsoft 365 E7 Frontier Suite generally available. E7 ($99/user/month) bundles M365 E5, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Agent 365 in a single SKU; Agent 365 standalone ($15/user/month) delivers the control plane to observe, govern and secure agents the same way IT manages people today. The 1 May GA adds Agent 365 registry sync with AWS Bedrock and Google Cloud in public preview — letting IT auto-discover, inventory and govern agents across hyperscalers — plus runtime alerting and Intune/Defender controls in June preview. Same day, Microsoft decommissioned the Anthropic independent-processor (IP) setting, completing the in-Azure data-flow path for Anthropic models in Copilot. _Why it matters:_ This is the single most consequential enterprise-AI control-plane launch of the quarter. Agent 365 in GA — with cross-cloud registry sync and observability — is Microsoft's direct answer to ServiceNow's AI Control Tower and SAP's API-fencing posture, and the price ($15/user) makes it a default purchase rather than a deliberate one. **Pentagon signs Impact-Level-6/7 AI deals with 8 vendors — and freezes out Anthropic** — On 1 May the Department of Defense announced classified AI deployment contracts with eight technology companies: OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, AWS, NVIDIA, SpaceX, Oracle, and Reflection AI. The deals cover Impact Level 6 and Impact Level 7 environments — the highest classified data tiers in DoD. Anthropic was deliberately excluded after refusing to remove its usage-policy guardrails for fully autonomous weapons and mass domestic surveillance; Secretary Hegseth formalized a "supply chain risk" designation against Anthropic in March, and although a federal judge in California blocked the designation, the Pentagon proceeded with awards to the other eight. Pentagon officials publicly said the department will "never again" rely on a single AI provider. _Why it matters:_ This is the largest single defense-AI procurement event of the cycle and the cleanest test of whether a frontier lab can hold a usage policy against federal pressure. Anthropic's exclusion — combined with the >$900 B funding round reporting and the Goldman/Blackstone/H&F joint venture — defines a two-track frontier-lab market: defense-aligned vs commercially-positioned. **Meta acquires Assured Robot Intelligence to seed humanoid foundation-model stack** — Meta on 1 May closed its acquisition of Assured Robot Intelligence (ARI), a humanoid-AI startup co-founded a year ago by former Fauna co-founder Lerrel Pinto and former NVIDIA researcher Xiaolong Wang. Wang, Pinto, Xuxin Cheng and the rest of the ARI team join Meta Superintelligence Labs to train physical agents that learn from human-experience data. Meta's framing: be the Android of humanoid robots — develop sensors, software and AI models, license them to manufacturers Meta does not own. _Why it matters:_ This is Meta's clearest "physical AGI" bet to date and the cleanest signal that Meta Superintelligence Labs is the focal point for capex post-Q1 earnings. The Android-of-humanoids framing is the same play Google made for mobile in 2008 — pricing it as a moat against Tesla Optimus and Figure AI rather than against Apple. **Microsoft Copilot adds Claude Opus 4.7 in Excel/PowerPoint/Word default rollout starts** — Coinciding with Agent 365 GA, Microsoft began the staged rollout that makes Anthropic Claude the default model for Microsoft 365 Copilot in Excel and PowerPoint (with full default flip dated 4 May for Excel/PowerPoint, opt-in for UK tenants by 12 May). Word and Outlook beta extend the same Anthropic surface, and Claude Opus 4.7 lands as the lead reasoning model. _Why it matters:_ Microsoft's M365 distribution is now openly multi-model, with Anthropic getting the workhorse Office surfaces. Combined with the OpenAI restructure on 27 April, this is the most explicit "Foundry-first but model-agnostic" stance Microsoft has taken — and pre-empts Anthropic's own developer conference next week. [Cloudswitched — Copilot defaults to Anthropic + UK opt-in](https://www.cloudswitched.com/news/microsoft-365-copilot-anthropic-default-may-2026-uk-business-opt-in-guide) · [Microsoft Learn — Anthropic as M365 subprocessor](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/microsoft-365/connect-to-ai-subprocessor) · [Microsoft Learn — Copilot in M365 apps with Anthropic models](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/copilot-anthropic-apps)

**Salesforce Headless 360 lights up: 60+ MCP tools wired to Claude Code, Cursor and any MCP runtime** — Salesforce confirmed Headless 360 — its agentic API architecture published in late April — is live across Agentforce, with more than 60 new MCP tools accessible directly from Claude Code, Cursor, or any MCP-compatible runtime. Headless 360 is the deliberate opposite of SAP's API Policy v4/2026 (which blocks external AI agents): every Salesforce data object, workflow and action is reachable via API, MCP tool or CLI without requiring a Salesforce-owned AI interface as the entry point. _Why it matters:_ This is the most important agentic-platform divergence of the enterprise era. Salesforce + ServiceNow are betting that customers want to compose their own multi-vendor agent stacks; SAP is betting they want the OEM's. Headless 360's MCP-tool count (60+) is the largest enterprise-software MCP exposure to date. [Techzine — SAP blocks external agents; Salesforce + ServiceNow don't](https://www.techzine.eu/blogs/applications/141323/sap-blocks-external-ai-agents-salesforce-and-servicenow-dont/)

**OpenAI's NVIDIA-funded GPU buildout briefed to investors: $1.4 T multi-year compute footprint** — OpenAI updated investors on 1 May about the >$1 T multi-year GPU buildout undergirding GPT-5.5 / GPT-Realtime / agentic-OS workloads, with NVIDIA among the financing counterparts. The brief follows the 27 April Microsoft-OpenAI restructure (which removed exclusivity) and frames the multi-cloud OpenAI compute graph for Foundry, AWS, Oracle and Google Cloud. _Why it matters:_ The number isn't new but the formal investor framing is — and it sets a $190 B-per-year capex run-rate (Microsoft's own CY26 number) as the floor, not the ceiling, for the AI hyperscaler era. [Reuters / press wire framing via CNBC + Bloomberg coverage of OpenAI's restructured partnership context](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/29/anthropic-weighs-raising-funds-at-900b-valuation-topping-openai.html)
