## Major announcements & releases

**Fortune breaks Anthropic "Mythos" / "Capybara" data leak — step-change model revealed in unsecured CMS** — Fortune published the exclusive on Thursday 26 March: a draft Anthropic blog post and nearly 3,000 unpublished assets were left publicly accessible in a misconfigured content-management system. The draft described a new Anthropic model — named internally as Capybara, branded externally as Claude Mythos — as a "step change" in AI performance and "the most capable we've built to date." Capybara is described as a new tier above Opus: dramatically higher scores on software coding, academic reasoning and cybersecurity than Claude Opus 4.6, with the blog post warning of "unprecedented cybersecurity risks." Security researchers Roy Paz (LayerX Security) and Alexandre Pauwels (University of Cambridge) discovered the exposed data store. Anthropic confirmed the leak ("human error" in CMS configuration), removed public search, and acknowledged Mythos is in early-access customer testing. _Why it matters:_ Easily the disclosure of the quarter. Three immediate consequences: (1) the cybersecurity-risk framing pre-stages the Claude FreeBSD kernel-RCE writeup on 1 April (CVE-2026-4747, Carlini's MAD Bugs project) and the Cohere/Aleph Alpha "sovereign AI" rationale; (2) it's the second Anthropic packaging mistake in the same window — five days before the Claude Code source-code leak via the missing `.npmignore` on 31 March; (3) it forces Anthropic's Mythos/Glasswing roll-out roadmap into the public eye six days before the company had planned. **Anthropic Claude Code: caching-optimisation bug ships, degrading quality across the user base** — A caching optimisation shipped on 26 March in a Claude Code release contained a critical bug: instead of clearing thinking history once after an hour of inactivity, it cleared the history on every subsequent turn, causing the model to lose short-term memory and become repetitive or forgetful. The fix lands in v2.1.116 ahead of the formal post-mortem on 23 April. _Why it matters:_ Second documented Claude Code quality regression in March (the first being the 4 March default-reasoning-effort change). Anthropic's eventual postmortem identifies this date as a key inflection in user-reported degradation — the same month the Mythos disclosure and the source-code leak landed. The harness-vs-model distinction the engineering team had been making becomes very public, very fast. [Anthropic — engineering post-mortem on Claude Code quality](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/april-23-postmortem) · [VentureBeat — Claude harness changes likely caused degradation](https://venturebeat.com/technology/mystery-solved-anthropic-reveals-changes-to-claudes-harnesses-and-operating-instructions-likely-caused-degradation)

**EU AI Act trilogue Council general approach (March 13) hardens into Parliament negotiating mandate (March 26)** — The European Parliament adopted its negotiating mandate on the Digital Omnibus on AI on 26 March, 569 votes to 45, completing the institutional set-up for the trilogue cycle (Council general approach was adopted 13 March; Commission proposal already in place). Convergence point: backstop deadlines of 2 December 2027 (Annex III high-risk) and 2 August 2028 (Annex I). _Why it matters:_ The compliance calendar firms up. The eventual 7 May political deal pushes the AI regulatory sandbox deadline from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Aug 2027 and shortens the GP-AI transparency grace period from six to three months. Today's Parliament mandate is the necessary precondition for that 7 May deal. [Europarl — Digital Omnibus on AI legislative train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/package-digital-package/file-digital-omnibus-on-ai) · [Tech Law Ireland — EU AI Act timeline update](https://www.techlaw.ie/2026/03/articles/artificial-intelligence/eu-ai-act-timeline-update/) · [Modulos — is the EU AI Act delayed? 2026 status check](https://www.modulos.ai/blog/is-the-eu-ai-act-delayed/)

**Mistral releases Voxtral TTS open-weights — 4 B parameters, nine languages, ~3-second voice cloning** — Mistral formally launched Voxtral TTS on Thursday, an open-weights text-to-speech model targeting nine languages (English, French, German, Spanish, Dutch, Portuguese, Italian, Hindi, Arabic). 4 B parameters runnable on consumer hardware; voice cloning from a 3-second reference; available open-weights on Hugging Face under CC BY NC 4.0; API priced at $0.016 per 1,000 characters, undercutting ElevenLabs at parity quality. _Why it matters:_ The first open-weights TTS at credible-vs-ElevenLabs quality — and the most consequential European-sovereign voice-stack release of 2026 to date. Threads Voxtral into Le Chat's voice mode and undercuts the OpenAI Realtime / ElevenLabs voice-agent economics by ~10×.
