**[Kimi-K3 on HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3)** — 1054 pts · 429 comments · 11 hours ago · `huggingface.co` · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49065752)

_What this is:_ Moonshot AI released the weights for Kimi-K3, a ~3T-parameter MXFP4-native open-weight model, on HuggingFace. The single biggest HN story of the day by a wide margin.

_Comment signal:_
- @NitpickLawyer: At mxfp4 it needs ~1.5TB of VRAM — right at the edge of 8×B200, realistically 16× for usable context/throughput; third-party pricing will finally reveal the true $/MTok of serving a 3T model and whether labs are subsidising API tokens.
- @minraws: Tested it — skeptical of the "60-70% cheaper than Moonshot's own API" claims; estimates Moonshot already runs a healthy 40-50% margin, so third-party discounts will be marginal unless they gut throughput.
- @gorgmah: Counterpoint from the GLM 5.2 precedent — competition drove prices down ~45% in six weeks and hasn't settled yet.
- @KronisLV: The hardware to run these is "shaped wrong for individuals" — either 5-10 tok/s on unified memory or kilowatt datacentre cards; no prosumer 128-256GB VRAM option exists.
- _Notable critique:_ @dilliwal and @fahrradflucht both surfaced the licence: any Model-as-a-Service operator above $20M revenue over 12 consecutive months (or 100M MAU) must sign a separate commercial agreement with Moonshot. "Open weights" with a commercial ceiling attached.
- _Notable:_ @fnordpiglet urged people to archive and seed copies now — "the man is coming to lock these down, like they tried to do with encryption algorithms." Export-control anxiety is now a mainstream HN reflex on model releases.

**[AI companies are shredding rare books](https://twitter.com/HedgieMarkets/status/2081534588485296565)** — 662 pts · 412 comments · 5 hours ago · `twitter.com` · [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49068738)

_Comment signal:_
- @ACCount37: The publishers "got analog hole'd" — they sued over shadow-library training hoping to force paid content deals, and instead made $5 book + $25 destructive scan the cheaper path. Also disputes the "rare, precious books" framing: labs want in-copyright books, and old ≠ valuable.
- @squidbeak: Limited sympathy for publishers who sit on out-of-print works until copyright expires; also a broadside at modern publication quality vs 17th-18th century binding.
- @est31: The whole thing is a copyright-law problem, not an AI problem — scanning books you own should be legal, and nothing here touches genuinely public-domain 18th-century texts.
- @lousken: Direct line drawn to the Internet Archive lawsuit — "publishers should be more careful what they wish for."
- @JumpCrisscross: Constructive proposal — let labs do the digitising but require the scans be made public after a delay, reversing the damage from _Authors Guild v. Google_.
- _Notable:_ Thread sentiment is markedly less anti-AI than the headline implies; the dominant read is that copyright maximalism produced this outcome.

_Comment signal:_

- @throwatdem12311: Sharp frustration — why is a system prompt needed at all, why does the best coding model still invent nonexistent APIs, and why does benchmark improvement keep coinciding with perceived output degradation.

- _Notable critique:_ @fl0ki points out the guidance contradicts the tooling — most people generate CLAUDE.md via `/init`, which fills it with exactly the superficial repo facts the post says to omit.
- _Notable:_ @espeed flagged that Claude Code context history is deleted on a ~30-45 day retention timer, calling local-device data deletion "nuts."

_What this is:_ Stanford SIEPR policy brief arguing measured AI labour-market effects are far smaller than the discourse implies.

_Comment signal:_
- @simonw: The core methodological objection — coding agents only started working well in late November (effectively January for most), and general agents later still; a study centred on 2022-2025 may miss the entire meaningful period.
- @chewbacha: Productivity gains concentrate in less-experienced engineers and go flat-to-negative for highly experienced ones — "LLMs move your baseline towards the mean." Also reports worse memory retention for LLM-assisted work.
- @cjbgkagh: Exactly the opposite — AI is a sharper tool that widens the Pareto gap, turning 80:20 into 90:10 and beyond.
- @zahlman: Names the contradiction directly — the first two top-level comments in the thread flatly contradict each other on who benefits.
- @bloaf: "The impacts are here, they're just not evenly distributed yet" — Fortune 500s still have internal AI bans while his own team is dropping software subscriptions a non-programmer vibe-coded a replacement for in a week.
- _Notable critique:_ @dahart audits the charts — unemployment rises for a year pre-COVID contrary to BLS data, and the "AI exposure" quintiles for 2015 really measure computer use, not AI.
