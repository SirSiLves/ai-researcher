- **Agent identity & authorization** — Agents & infrastructure → Agent security & containment. Agent identity reached GA as a security control (Snowflake) and as least privilege for non-human identities (just-in-time MFA per agent), not as a protocol extension

**Sector births / deaths:** spawned **Agent security & containment** (4 topics, 48 orgs active). None dissolved — *Agents & infrastructure* keeps its name, its 2026-04-22 first_seen and 11 topics, so this is a split, not a rename. Sector count 4 → 5, still under the max of 6.

**Stage movements:** **Agent execution control** — emerging → consolidating. Its slow EMA crossed the floor (29.5 → 30.5) on day 7 of sustained signal with 5 source types, both gates met. Three more are queued within 1.5 of the door: `agent-skills-wave` (29.6), `content-provenance-copyright` (28.8) and `agent-sdks` (28.6).

**The shape of the day.** Containment is the answer the whole industry gave at once. Black Hat's product wave, Mistral's 3B Apache-2.0 guardrail classifier, Snowflake's MCP enforcement point at the warehouse boundary, a trajectory-assurance position paper, two MCP-security Show HNs and the day's #1 GitHub repo (a security skill-router, +2,400 stars) all bound what a compromised agent can reach rather than trying to make it trustworthy — which is why the sector split today rather than last week. The second thread is that agent programmes are finally being judged on evidence, and the evidence is arriving late: Amazon burned $1.8M of Claude budget on a menial job for five months before anyone noticed, and LangChain's production write-up is almost entirely negative results found after launch. The governance thread sharpens both — Article 50 is live, and the liability sits with whoever *deploys* the assistant, not whoever built it.
