## The month in 90 seconds

**The month the frontier-lab race stopped being decided by benchmarks and started being decided on the cap table — and the enterprise agent layer became a real, revenue-bearing business.**

May 2026 resolved the two questions April left open: who leads the frontier, and whether agent revenue is real. Both were answered with numbers rather than model launches. Anthropic closed the month with a $65B Series H at a $965B post-money valuation, overtaking OpenAI as the most valuable AI startup in the world — telegraphed all month (the $1.5B Wall Street services JV on May 4, Google's up-to-$40B commitment on May 5, a projected first-ever profitable quarter at $10.9B Q2 revenue / $559M operating profit by May 21, a $30B+ round "queued" by May 24) before landing hard. OpenAI answered by confidentially filing a draft S-1 with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley targeting a Q3/Q4 listing above $1T; SpaceX's own S-1 accidentally exposed Anthropic paying ~$1.25B/month (~$45B through 2029) for Colossus compute. The "frontier labs lose money through 2028" thesis died on the third week, and "$50B run-rate" became the new floor for a credible frontier-lab story.

Underneath the capital story, enterprise agent revenue stopped being a debate. Salesforce printed $1.2B in Agentforce ARR (+205% YoY, 28.6T tokens) — the first clean Tier-1-SaaS data point past $1B — and reported its own engineering org on Claude Code at +79% PRs/developer and a 33-endpoint migration done ~18× faster. Anthropic verticalized across finance, legal, SMB, SAP, M365 and the Big Four (PwC 30,000 staff, KPMG 276,000, EY/Microsoft) and took the Ramp AI Index lead from OpenAI (34.4% vs 32.3%) — the first US business-AI-adoption lead change of the cycle. Every major enterprise platform shipped its agent control plane in the same window (SAP Sapphire's Autonomous Enterprise with Claude as the default reasoning engine and n8n embedded at a doubled $5.2B valuation; ServiceNow's cross-vendor AI Control Tower; Salesforce Agent Fabric; AWS Bedrock AgentCore Payments; IBM watsonx Orchestrate). MCP and A2A graduated from emerging protocols to procurement-grade substrate — all four hyperscalers now ship first-party MCP servers, and the MCP 2026-07-28 release candidate (stateless core, Tasks, MCP Apps, OAuth-aligned auth) entered its ten-week implementer validation window.

Two structural shifts ran the full month. AI security pivoted from finding bugs to fixing them: Anthropic's Project Glasswing flagged 10,000+ critical/high vulnerabilities across 1,000+ open-source projects in ~30 days (a 27-year OpenBSD bug, a 16-year FFmpeg flaw), while a Compliance API, IBM/Red Hat's $5B "Project Lightwell" bank-backed remediation clearinghouse, and Google's AI Threat Defense made cyberdefense the most contested frontier-lab go-to-market vertical — with maintainer/patch capacity, not detection, now the binding constraint. And the cheap-and-fast open-weight cost story moved from skeptic's footnote to the foreground: Artificial Analysis benchmarked a standard workload at $4,811 on Claude versus $544 on Zhipu GLM (~9×), OpenRouter's Chinese-model share crossed 60%, DeepSeek made its 75%-off rate permanent, and by month-end the cost ceiling surfaced *unprompted* inside Anthropic's own Opus 4.8 launch threads. Cohere Command A+ shipped frontier-class under full Apache 2.0; Tencent open-sourced Hunyuan Hy3.

Policy hardened in Europe while Washington drifted. The Trump AI cybersecurity executive order collapsed on signing day, leaving the federal coordinating layer a structural gap through Q3. Europe filled the vacuum: FINMA classified Mythos-class capability as "immediate systemic risk" (the highest-jurisdiction Mythos designation in the global stack), Germany unveiled its Hightech-Agenda, and the EU AI Act Omnibus formalized (Annex III deferred to Dec 2027) with the Commission's Article 6 draft treating a compound/multi-agent system as a *single* high-risk system assessed end-to-end. Sentiment, meanwhile, tipped from launch-hype to fatigue — "AI psychosis" held #1 on Hacker News two days running mid-month, vendor-specific receipts piled up by W21 (Antigravity bait-and-switch, Microsoft canceling internal Claude Code, Stenberg contesting Glasswing), and a broad consumer AI-fatigue cluster ("I'm Tired of Talking to AI," 1,717 pts) closed the month — even as OpenAI's reasoning model disproving Erdős's 1946 unit-distance conjecture supplied the strongest single capability data point of the year.

## Defining themes

**The frontier-lab order gets decided on financing, not a model release** — The capital drumbeat that defined April (OpenAI's $122B/$852B close, Anthropic's $45B+/8.5GW capacity stack) resolved into a financing race in May. Anthropic stacked validation across the month — a $1.5B Wall Street enterprise-services JV (Blackstone/H&F/Goldman/Apollo/GIC/Sequoia, May 4), Google's up-to-$40B cash-plus-compute commitment (May 5), a projected first-ever profitable quarter ($10.9B Q2 revenue / $559M operating profit, May 21), a $30B+ round "queued" (May 24) — then closed a **$65B Series H at $965B** (May 29–31), overtaking OpenAI as the world's most valuable AI startup against a ~$47B run-rate driven heavily by Claude Code, widely read as the last private raise before an IPO. OpenAI answered with a **confidential S-1** (Goldman/Morgan Stanley, Q3/Q4 >$1T); SpaceX's own S-1 exposed Anthropic's **~$1.25B/month / ~$45B-through-2029 Colossus contract**; Coinbase markets priced 85% odds OpenAI lists first. _Why it matters:_ For the first time this cycle the pecking order is being set on cap tables rather than benchmarks; "$50B run-rate" is the new credibility floor; whoever's S-1 lands first prices every other AI valuation; and the cost-vs-valuation tension (a $1T mark against models doing the same job for 1/9 the price) is now a paragraph every prospectus must address.

## Research highlights

- **Plant, Persist, Trigger: Sleeper Attack on LLM Agents** ([arXiv:2605.28201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28201)) — Formalizes dormant injections planted via tool returns/MCP context that activate on a later benign query; existing single-turn defenses miss the class entirely. Maps directly to the MCP 2026-07-28 RC threat model.
- **FutureSim: Replaying World Events to Evaluate Adaptive Agents** ([arXiv:2605.15188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15188)) — Replays real-world news chronologically so agents are scored on adapting to post-training-cutoff information; direct attack on the static-eval problem and the month's clearest successor to ARC-AGI for agent-benchmark integrity.
- **Harness-Bench / Harness effects across models** ([arXiv:2605.27922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27922)) — 106 sandboxed tasks varying harness config across model backends; the empirical backbone of the month's "model is commodity, harness is moat" thesis (echoed by "Is Grep All You Need?" [2605.15184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184) and "Code as Agent Harness" [2605.18747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18747)).
- **An OpenAI reasoning model disproves Erdős's 1946 planar unit-distance conjecture** — Independently verified by Alon, Wood, Bloom; refined by Sawin. First time a general-purpose (not math-specialized) frontier model produced a verified novel result against a major open problem; Gowers: "a milestone in AI mathematics." [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/model-disproves-discrete-geometry-conjecture/)
- **Agent-memory observability cluster — STALE / MemGuard / MemCog / MemTrace / EvolveMem** ([2605.06527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527), [2605.28009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28009), [2605.13941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13941)) — Memory-validity benchmarks + heterogeneous-contamination/per-operation error attribution (up to 28% reliability gain); the tooling that must exist before memory-augmented agents run under SLAs.
- **OmniRetrieval: Unified Retrieval across Heterogeneous Knowledge Sources** ([arXiv:2605.29250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29250)) — A single retriever over text, tables, knowledge graphs and property graphs without a lossy shared space; the cleanest RAG-over-mixed-enterprise-knowledge read of the final week, paired with the month's "post-RAG / compilation-stage knowledge layer" vendor language (Pinecone Nexus + KnowQL).
- **How Well Do Models Follow Their Constitutions?** ([arXiv:2605.24229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24229)) — Decomposes Anthropic's constitution into 205 tenets and OpenAI's Model Spec into 197; Sonnet 4 15.0% → Sonnet 4.6 2.0% violations. Most concrete alignment-with-public-spec benchmark yet.
- **A Governance Horizon for Open-Weight AI Models** ([arXiv:2605.24383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24383)) — Audits 2.14M Hugging Face repos; restriction evidence decays with a 1.31-derivation-step half-life, ≥80% of descendants lack public governance evidence past seven generations. A hard data point for the open-weight licensing debate.
- **Multi-agent reality checks — Single-Agent vs MAS under equal budgets / Coordination as an architectural layer** ([2604.02460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02460), [2605.03310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03310)) — Normalizing for compute, much of the multi-agent edge vanishes; 41–87% of MAS production failures are coordination defects, not model capability. Sobering counterweight to multi-agent hype, alongside Sakana's 7B Conductor router beating its frontier-model pool.

May produced the first hard *positive* numbers — Salesforce's $1.2B Agentforce ARR and its +79%-PRs/18×-migration internal data — flipping the narrative from "which incumbents lose" to "agent revenue is real and being captured."
