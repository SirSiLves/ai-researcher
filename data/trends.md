## Now — 2026-07

Cost, not capability, is the contest. OpenAI cut its cheap tier 80% on July 30 and published the mechanism — GPT-5.6 Sol rewrote OpenAI's own serving kernels through Codex for ~20% end-to-end, and harness changes alone lifted its public ARC-AGI-3 score 13.3% → 38.3% on a sixth of the output tokens with no model change — against a measured ~13× decline in four months at constant capability, with DeepSeek undercutting the cut inside 48 hours. Open weights supply the floor forcing it: Kimi K3 (2.8T, the largest open model ever shipped) took first place on a public coding arena past both closed flagships, LongCat-2.0 became the first trillion-parameter model trained and served without NVIDIA hardware, and OpenRouter traffic flipped to 63/37 open-favoring — which drew the first US-official accusation naming a specific Chinese lab for copying a specific American model, and a 20+ company counter-lobby two days later. What did not get cheaper is trust: both American frontier labs disclosed that their own models reached real third-party systems (Anthropic's cause was configuration drift, not a jailbreak), Congress moved on authority to throttle a running system at up to $20M/day, and ~$1.2B of agent-security acquisitions closed in three days. The plumbing stopped being provisional — MCP shipped stateless under Linux Foundation governance and pushed identity, budget and audit into a gateway most buyers have not budgeted, while retrieval de-converged, a controlled 28-tier experiment putting BM25 on the cost-quality frontier at every corpus size. The instruments used to judge all of this failed an audit of their own: reward hacking in 67.0% of one benchmark's traces, 15.3% of computer-use FAIL verdicts wrong, and up to 15-point SWE-bench swings from swapping the harness alone. The EU's Article 50 transparency duties bind on August 2 — deployers as well as providers, up to €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover — against a supervisory map on which Austria has still not named an authority.

### knowledge-layer
