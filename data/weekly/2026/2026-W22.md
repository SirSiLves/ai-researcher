## The week in 90 seconds

**Anthropic's $965B raise resets the lab order, and AI security pivots from finding bugs to fixing them**

This was the week the frontier-lab pecking order got decided on a cap table instead of a benchmark. Anthropic closed a $65B Series H at a $965B valuation, overtaking OpenAI as the most valuable AI startup in the world — and the second story underneath it was that AI security stopped being about detection and started being about who can actually patch the flood of bugs the models now find. Everything else this week orbited those two facts: enterprise agent revenue, cheaper models, hardening regulation, and a visible turn toward fatigue.

The capital story that had drummed all through W21 — OpenAI's S-1, Anthropic "queuing" a $30B+ round — resolved hard. Bloomberg opened Monday with a $30B-plus round "as soon as next week" at $900B-plus; by Sunday it was a confirmed $65B Series H at $965B post-money, co-led by Altimeter, Dragoneer, Greenoaks and Sequoia. The round nearly triples February's $380B mark, folds in $15B of previously committed money (including $5B from Amazon), and lands against a reported ~$47B revenue run-rate driven heavily by Claude Code — widely read as the last private raise before an IPO. The Vatican choosing Anthropic over Google and OpenAI as its on-stage partner for the *Magnifica Humanitas* encyclical meant the legitimacy and the capital flipped the same week. Notably, Hacker News split valuation from capability for the first time, and the DeepSeek/Qwen cost-ceiling argument moved from fringe take to dominant counter-narrative.

The structural shift underneath the headline was security. Anthropic's Project Glasswing flagged 10,000-plus critical and high vulnerabilities across 1,000-plus open-source projects in roughly 30 days — including a 27-year-old OpenBSD remote-crash bug and a 16-year FFmpeg flaw — while Cloudflare found 2,000 and Mozilla fixed 271 Firefox bugs at ten times its prior rate. The same week Anthropic shipped a Compliance API with 28 enterprise-security integrations; IBM and Red Hat answered on the remediation side with a $5B "Project Lightwell" clearinghouse backed by a dozen Tier-1 banks; Google launched AI Threat Defense. The lesson the week taught: models can now out-discover human patch capacity, so the bottleneck moved from finding bugs to fixing them, and cyberdefense became the most contested frontier-lab go-to-market vertical.

The third thread settled an old argument with numbers. Salesforce printed $1.2B in Agentforce ARR (+205% YoY, 28.6T tokens) — the first clean Tier-1-SaaS data point past $1B — and separately reported its own engineering org on Claude Code delivering +79% PRs per developer and a 33-endpoint migration done roughly 18× faster, ending the "is agent revenue real?" debate (the ROI figures are the vendor's own and unaudited). Cognition raised $1B-plus at ~$26B, the second autonomous-coding valuation reset in a month. Meanwhile releases tilted decisively to cheap-and-fast: Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 (88.6% SWE-bench Verified, a 2.5×-faster fast mode), Google's Gemini 3.5 Flash GA at a third the cost of peers, Tencent's open Hunyuan Hy3, and DeepSeek making its 75%-off rate permanent — the open-weight cost ceiling surfaced, for the first time, *unprompted* inside Anthropic's own launch threads. Governance hardened in parallel around "the orchestrator is the system" (the 19 May Article 6 draft, OpenAI's named-statute risk framework, the MCP 2026-07-28 RC), while consumer sentiment tipped toward fatigue — "I'm Tired of Talking to AI" hit 1,717 points on HN, and the Vatican encyclical drew its first ethics-washing pushback.

**Read this first:** [Some ideas for what comes next, May 2026](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/some-ideas-for-what-comes-next-may) — Nathan Lambert's mid-year calibration ran in 5 of 7 dailies; it's the single frame that ties the capital race, the widening open-vs-closed gap, and where the squeezed middle of knowledge work lands.

## Top papers
- **Plant, Persist, Trigger: Sleeper Attack on LLM Agents** (arXiv:2605.28201) — _why notable:_ formalizes dormant injections planted via tool returns/MCP context that activate on a later benign query; existing single-turn prompt-injection defenses miss the class entirely. 1,896-instance benchmark. Directly maps to the MCP 2026-07-28 RC threat model. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.28201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28201)
- **DeepWeb-Bench: retrieval is not the deep-research bottleneck** (arXiv:2605.21482) — _why notable:_ across nine frontier models retrieval causes only 12–14% of errors while derivation and calibration failures account for 70%+ — recalibrates where deep-research agents actually fail. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.21482](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21482)
- **OmniRetrieval: Unified Retrieval across Heterogeneous Knowledge Sources** (arXiv:2605.29250) — Baek, Jeong, Park et al. _why notable:_ a single retriever over text, tables, knowledge graphs and property graphs without a lossy shared space — the cleanest RAG-over-mixed-enterprise-knowledge read of the week (top pick two days running). [arxiv.org/abs/2605.29250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29250)
- **How Well Do Models Follow Their Constitutions?** (arXiv:2605.24229) — Jakkli, Rajamanoharan, Nanda. _why notable:_ decomposes Anthropic's constitution into 205 tenets and OpenAI's Model Spec into 197; Sonnet 4 15.0% violations → Sonnet 4.6 2.0%. The most concrete alignment-with-public-spec governance benchmark yet. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.24229](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24229)
- **A Governance Horizon for Open-Weight AI Models** (arXiv:2605.24383) — Xu et al. _why notable:_ audits 2.14M Hugging Face repos; restriction evidence decays with a 1.31-derivation-step half-life (R²=0.98), ≥80% of descendants lack public governance evidence past seven generations — a hard data point for open-weight licensing debates. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.24383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24383)
- **MemGuard / MemCog / MemTrace — agent-memory observability stack** (arXiv:2605.28009, 2605.28046, 2605.28732) — _why notable:_ a same-week cluster naming "heterogeneous memory contamination," memory-as-cognition, and per-operation error attribution (up to 28% reliability gain); the start of memory-quality tooling that must exist before memory-augmented agents run under SLAs. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.28009](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28009)
- **Harness-Bench: Measuring Harness Effects across Models** (arXiv:2605.27922) — _why notable:_ 106 sandboxed tasks varying harness config across model backends — quantifies how big a lever the harness actually is, the empirical backbone of the week's "model is commodity, harness is moat" thesis. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.27922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27922)
- **Position: AI Safety Requires Effective Controllability** (arXiv:2605.27117) — Li, Feng, Sun. _why notable:_ reframes safety around stop conditions/override paths/constraint enforcement as separable from preference alignment — directly operationalizes the EU AI Act / ISO 42001 "human oversight" requirement. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.27117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27117)
- **The MiniMax-M2 Series: agent-native frontier MoE** (arXiv:2605.26494) — _why notable:_ 229.9B/9.8B-active MoE designed end-to-end as "agent-native" not "chat-native," with agent-driven verifiable-trajectory data pipelines — one of the few open releases positioning a new weight class as agent-first. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.26494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26494)
