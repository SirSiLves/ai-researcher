## The week in 90 seconds

**Anthropic closes a $965B raise to overtake OpenAI — just as the cheap-open-model counter-argument goes mainstream**

The defining story of W22 was a capital event with a built-in skeptic. Anthropic became the most valuable AI startup on the planet, and in the same seven days the case against that valuation moved from the fringe to the front page. The rest of the week filled in the proof points: enterprise agent revenue stopped being a debate, cybersecurity hardened into the most contested frontier-lab vertical, and the protocol everyone is building on graduated to enterprise infrastructure exactly as researchers named the threat class that will test it. Five threads, one through-line — the field is now being priced, and the price is being argued.

The headline number bookended the week. Monday carried Bloomberg's confirmation of a $30B-plus round at a $900B-plus valuation; Friday closed a $65B Series H at a $965B post-money mark, led by Altimeter, Dragoneer, Greenoaks and Sequoia, folding in $15B of prior commitments including Amazon's $5B, against a reported $47B run-rate. The read is unambiguous: this is widely taken as the last private raise before an IPO, and it explicitly puts Anthropic ahead of OpenAI in the cap-table race. What changed this week was the reception. On Friday's Hacker News funding thread, the argument that DeepSeek, Qwen-on-Ascend and now Tencent's newly open-sourced Hunyuan Hy3 deliver most of the capability at a fraction of the price was, for the first time, the dominant counter-narrative rather than a contrarian aside. A roughly $1T valuation is now colliding in public with a $0.06-per-million-token open-weight price floor, and that collision is the story to watch into June.

Underneath the financing, the product and revenue stories both crossed thresholds the same day. Claude Opus 4.8 shipped Thursday at an unchanged $5/$25 per-MTok, posting SWE-bench Verified 88.6 percent and SWE-Bench Pro 69.2 percent, ahead of GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro — but the real shift was architectural. The model now writes its own orchestration scripts, spinning up tens to hundreds of parallel subagents in a single session, a step from externally-scripted agent harnesses toward in-model long-horizon autonomy, with Jarred Sumner's 11-day, 750k-line port of Bun from Zig to Rust as the proof point. Hours later, Salesforce printed the number the industry had been waiting for: Agentforce alone at $1.2B ARR, up 205 percent year over year, atop $3.4B combined AI and Data ARR — the first clean Tier-1-SaaS data point past the billion-dollar line. The "is agent revenue real?" question ended on a P&L.

The week's remaining threads were defensive and structural. Cybersecurity completed its turn into the vertical every frontier lab now contests at once: Anthropic's Project Glasswing flagged 10,000-plus critical vulnerabilities (with patch capacity, not detection, now the bottleneck) and shipped a Compliance API with 28 enterprise-security integrations; Google Cloud rolled out AI Threat Defense; and IBM and Red Hat committed $5B to Project Lightwell with 20,000-plus engineers and a Tier-1 banking roster. Meanwhile the agent-valuation curve reset again — Cognition raised $1B-plus at roughly $26B, 2.5 times its mark of eight months earlier — and the AI supply chain split along geopolitical lines as NVIDIA committed $150B a year to Taiwan and Beijing eased overseas-travel rules for its top AI talent. Governance hardened in lockstep: the MCP 2026-07-28 release candidate locked the same day a new paper formalized the "Sleeper Attack" dormant-injection class, while OpenAI became the first lab to map its risk framework to named California and EU statutes.

## Top papers
- **[Plant, Persist, Trigger: Sleeper Attack on LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28201)** — _why notable:_ formalizes dormant injections (via tool returns, web pages, MCP context) that activate on a later benign query; 1,896-instance benchmark — existing single-turn prompt-injection defenses miss the class entirely. The most operational agent-security paper of the week, directly tied to the MCP 2026-07-28 RC threat model.
- **[OmniRetrieval: Unified Retrieval across Heterogeneous Knowledge Sources](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29250)** — Baek, Jeong, Park et al. _why notable:_ a single retriever over text, tables, knowledge graphs and property graphs without a lossy shared space — the most directly useful read for RAG over genuinely mixed enterprise knowledge.
- **[MemGuard / MemCog / MemTrace — the agent-memory observability stack](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28009)** — UIUC/Columbia/Amazon et al. _why notable:_ four memory papers from different groups landed in one drop, naming "heterogeneous memory contamination" (up to 28% reliability gain) and building executable memory-evolution graphs for error attribution — the field is converging on memory as an active cognitive process with its own observability layer.
- **[Harness-Bench: Measuring Harness Effects across Models in Realistic Agent Workflows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27922)** — _why notable:_ 106 sandboxed tasks varying harness config across model backends; quantifies how big a lever harness choice actually is — the empirical companion to the "Agent = Model + Harness" thesis.
- **[Relevance as a Vulnerability: How Web Retrieval Degrades Safety Alignment in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29224)** — Nawal, Baser, Gurusamy. _why notable:_ AgentREVEAL shows enabling web retrieval weakens agent safety alignment and raises compliance with harmful requests — a retrieval-specific attack surface for agentic RAG.
- **[MiniMax-M2: Mini Activations Unleashing Max Real-World Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26494)** — MiniMax (~200 authors). _why notable:_ flagship 229.9B-total/9.8B-active MoE designed end-to-end as "agent-native" rather than "chat-native," on agent-driven data pipelines producing verifiable trajectories.
- **[How Well Do Models Follow Their Constitutions?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24229)** — Jakkli, Rajamanoharan, Nanda. _why notable:_ decomposes Anthropic's constitution (205 tenets) and OpenAI's Model Spec (197) into atomic tenets with adversarial scenario generation; Sonnet 4 15.0% violations → Sonnet 4.6 2.0% — the most concrete alignment-with-public-spec benchmark seen.
- **[A Governance Horizon for Open-Weight AI Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24383)** — Xu et al. _why notable:_ audits 2.14M Hugging Face repos; restriction evidence decays with a half-life of 1.31 derivation steps (R²=0.98) — a quantitative data point for open-weight licensing debates (pairs with CMU's "Fine-Tuning Defenses Susceptible to Simple Attacks").
- **[Governing Technical Debt in Agentic AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29129)** — Hydari, Iqbal, Ramasubbu. _why notable:_ defines "Agentic Technical Debt" accumulating in prompts, memory, tool schemas, orchestration graphs and control policies — a vocabulary for the orchestration-layer maintenance problem.
- **[LACUNA: Safe Agents as Recursive Program Holes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28617)** — Odersky group (EPFL). _why notable:_ a programming model where each agent action is a typed `agent[T](task)` call type-checked against the surrounding program before running — the most rigorous safety-via-types take on agent runtimes so far.
