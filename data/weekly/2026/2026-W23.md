## The week in 90 seconds

**Anthropic files to go public as the AI fight turns from capability to cost**

The week the most valuable AI startup started the IPO clock was also the week the industry's center of gravity shifted from "what can the model do?" to "what does it cost, and who collects the toll?" Anthropic's confidential S-1 sits atop a ~$965B valuation; meanwhile coding agents went metered, enterprise agents got a shared runtime, and Microsoft quietly stopped being a customer and started being a competitor.

On June 1 Anthropic became the first pure-play frontier lab to formally begin going public, confidentially filing a draft S-1 with the SEC days after closing a $65B Series H at a roughly $965B post-money valuation — up from $380B in February, and now ahead of OpenAI, which is reported weeks behind with a September target above $1 trillion. A confidential filing keeps the prospectus private for 60 to 90 days, so the public document lands in late summer. The reported ~$47B revenue run-rate, leaning heavily on Claude Code, hasn't been independently verified, but that number is now the thing the public market will price. For the first time the lab pecking order gets decided on a cap table rather than a benchmark — the first real test of whether frontier-lab economics justify trillion-dollar math.

Underneath the IPO headline ran a harder structural story: cost. GitHub Copilot moved every plan to metered "AI Credits" at a penny each on June 1, the clearest sign yet that the flat-rate coding subscription is dead. By midweek the loudest signal on Hacker News wasn't capability but the bill — Uber capping engineers at $1,500 a month, a "$1,500 to hack an app" experiment making the rounds, and Sam Altman conceding that token costs are "an issue." Escalating inference cost is dragging the whole category onto consumption pricing, and developer sentiment has pivoted from hype to economics and over-constraint frustration.

The enterprise-agent war told the same toll-collection story at a larger scale. The week opened with rival gateways — SAP routing external agents through its Joule gateway under a hard June 9 enforcement deadline, Salesforce going the opposite way with open, MCP-first "Headless 360." By June 5 the framing flipped: NVIDIA's enterprise Agent Toolkit launched with 17 platform adopters — Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow, Red Hat, Palantir, Adobe, Siemens, CrowdStrike, Atlassian and more — all agreeing to run agents on one governed runtime. The rivals fighting over who owns the data got knitted onto NVIDIA's substrate. Around it, a three-lab release wall landed: Claude Opus 4.8 (88.6% SWE-bench Verified) became the default across Claude tiers, Google made Gemini 3.5 Flash GA while cutting AI Ultra from $250 to $200, and Microsoft used Build 2026 to ship seven first-party MAI models, put its own Project Polaris coding model on Maia 200 silicon to replace GPT-4 in Copilot by August, and open-source WAF 1.0 — a quiet, structural renegotiation of the OpenAI partnership. On the open flank, Mistral's Apache-2.0 Small 4 and an open Search Toolkit, plus Cohere–Aleph Alpha merger talks, gave Nathan Lambert's "two different exponentials" thesis a concrete European identity.

## Top papers
- **Capability Advertisement as a Market for Lemons: A Trust Layer for Heterogeneous Agent Networks** (arXiv:2606.03034) — Gaurav Naresh Mittal. _why notable:_ argues MCP/A2A registries naively treat advertised agent capabilities as static and truthful, creating an adverse-selection problem; the sharpest theoretical counterpoint to "just open the MCP server" enthusiasm. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.03034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03034)
- **Proof-Carrying Agent Actions (PCAA)** (arXiv:2606.04104) — _why notable:_ model-agnostic runtime governance that attaches verifiable proofs to agent actions so policies enforce at runtime across mixed stacks — a concrete agent-governance / runtime-standards primitive. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.04104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04104)
- **Toward Pre-Deployment Assurance for Enterprise AI Agents** (arXiv:2606.04037) — Thanh Luong Tuan, Abhijit Sanyal. _why notable:_ ontology-grounded verification with an Agent Operational Envelope and machine-verifiable Trust Certificate; ontology-driven scenario generation beats persona baselines on regulatory coverage (48.3% vs 33.1%) across four regulated industries. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.04037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04037)
- **The Evaluation Blind Spot: A Stereological Theory of Benchmark Coverage for LLMs** (arXiv:2606.05169) — Jason Z Wang. _why notable:_ LLM leaderboards have low effective dimensionality (2.9–4.8); 92% of random visible/held-out splits flip the top-1 ranking, with a 4-benchmark stable core identified via submodular greedy. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.05169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05169)
- **Cascading Hallucination in Agentic RAG: The CHARM Framework** (arXiv:2606.04435) — Saroj Mishra et al. _why notable:_ characterizes how a single retrieval hallucination propagates and amplifies across multi-step agentic RAG, with a detection-plus-mitigation framework to break the cascade. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.04435](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04435)
- **Memory is Reconstructed, Not Retrieved: Graph Memory for LLM Agents** (arXiv:2606.06036) — Shuo Ji, Yibo Li, Bryan Hooi et al. _why notable:_ argues agent memory should be reconstructed from a graph rather than retrieved verbatim, improving long-horizon recall and coherence. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.06036](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06036)
- **Agent Planning Benchmark (APB)** (arXiv:2606.04874) — _why notable:_ 4,209 multimodal planning cases across 22 domains covering holistic and feedback-conditioned step-wise planning plus robustness under broken tools and unsolvable tasks — a fine-grained planning diagnostic distinct from end-to-end agent benchmarks. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.04874](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04874)
- **Agent Skills for Large Language Models: Architecture, Acquisition, Security, and the Path Forward** (arXiv:2602.12430) — _why notable:_ revised June 2; frames the shift from monolithic models to modular skill-equipped agents (composable instructions+code+resources) — directly relevant to the "skills" wave dominating GitHub trending. [arxiv.org/abs/2602.12430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12430)
- **ColBERTSaR: Sparsified ColBERT Index via Product Quantization** (arXiv:2606.05568) — Eugene Yang et al. _why notable:_ turns a ColBERT index into a true inverted index via embedding quantization, proving equivalence to learned-sparse retrieval and shrinking the index 50–70% below one-bit PLAID. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.05568](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05568)
