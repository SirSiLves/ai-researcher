## The week in 90 seconds

**Anthropic starts the frontier-lab IPO clock as AI-coding goes pay-per-use**

The week opened with the first frontier lab moving toward public markets and the flat-rate coding subscription quietly dying. Only Monday's data is in so far, but two threads already define W23: who gets to go public on AI economics, and who pays the metered bill underneath them.

The headline arrived on day one. Anthropic confidentially filed a draft S-1 with the SEC on June 1, becoming the first frontier lab to formally start the IPO clock — days after closing a $65B Series H at a $965B post-money valuation, up from $380B in February. A confidential filing keeps the prospectus private for roughly 60 to 90 days, so a public document wouldn't surface until late summer, and OpenAI is reported just weeks behind with a September target above $1T. This is the first genuine public-market test of whether frontier-lab revenue and margins can justify near-trillion-dollar valuations, and the question that has hovered over the sector since 2023 finally gets a real answer. Hacker News corroborated the news at 215 points and 145 comments.

Underneath the listing story, the economics of building with AI shifted to the buyer's invoice. GitHub Copilot moved every plan to metered "AI Credits" on June 1, where one credit equals one cent and agentic sessions now bill by consumption — Pro+ bundles $39 a month, Enterprise runs $39 plus $39 in credits, while completions and next-edit stay unlimited. It is the clearest signal yet that escalating agentic-session inference cost is forcing the entire AI-coding category off flat subscriptions. The same metering logic is hardening across the enterprise stack: SAP gatekeeps external agents through its Joule layer under A2A, Salesforce takes the contrarian MCP-first open route, and ServiceNow, SAP, and Workday now charge outright for third-party agent access. Whoever owns the system-of-record data decides which agent stack you run — and bills for the traffic.

The week's quieter counterweight was security, which refused to stay solved. PromptArmor demonstrated a working prompt-injection exploit that exfiltrates entire workbooks through a ChatGPT-for-Google-Sheets integration, and it became the day's highest-engagement HN AI item at 311 points and 116 comments — outpacing even the Anthropic IPO on comment volume. Capital kept pouring into the retrieval and world-model layer in parallel: Decart raised $300M at roughly $4B, and Exa raised $250M at $2.2B for agent-facing search. With six dailies still to land, the open question is whether net-new frontier model releases — quiet on Monday — pick up before the EU AI Act's enforcement powers activate August 2.
