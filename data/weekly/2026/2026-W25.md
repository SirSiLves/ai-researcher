## The week in 90 seconds

**A government kept two frontier models dark for a week — and the capital, the open weights, and the doubt all flowed around the hole.**

The defining fact of the week is what didn't happen: Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 never came back. Under the June 12 US Commerce directive ordering foreign-national access blocked, Anthropic disabled both models for everyone and they stayed dark all seven days. Around that frozen center, everything moved — money rushed into the layers below the model, open weights claimed a named cost-adjusted lead, and the public soured on the whole pitch.

The freeze stopped being news and became precedent. Early in the week it was still acute crisis — a roughly 90-minute compliance window, a failed June 15 meeting between Anthropic leadership and the administration, the company asking AWS to revoke Bedrock access because it couldn't gate by nationality. By midweek it had hardened into the first sustained case of a state pulling a shipped frontier model, with Lawfare tracing the trigger to Amazon CEO Andy Jassy's Treasury calls and noting the cited "read a codebase, fix the flaws" jailbreak is widely available, including on GPT-5.5. By Sunday it was reshaping commerce: Anthropic opened a Seoul office, signed Korea's tech giants, and bolted government-ID verification onto its top models even as they stayed offline. Fable 5, the model now frozen, had scored 80.3% on SWE-Bench Pro.

While the frontier sat behind a government wall, capital poured into the layers underneath it. SpaceX confirmed a roughly $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Anysphere, maker of Cursor — the largest deal in AI developer-tooling history — folding the coding-agent layer in just after its record SPCX/xAI IPO. Beneath that, inference provider Baseten neared a $1.5 billion raise at a $13 billion valuation, up about 160% in five months, on the bet that open-weight serving economics, not closed training, is where the next margin lives. Vercel shipped Eve, a TypeScript-native agent framework; Supabase took $500 million at $10.5 billion; Qualcomm opened advanced talks to buy Jim Keller's Tenstorrent for $8–10 billion. The strategic action has moved decisively from the model to the agent, the framework, the serving layer, and now the silicon.

And the export curbs may be backfiring on their own goal. GLM-5.2 (Z.ai, 753B MoE, 1M context, MIT-licensed) took the top open-weights spot and ranked #2 on Code Arena WebDev behind only Fable 5 — at a fraction of GPT-5.5's price — with Simon Willison calling it "probably the most powerful text-only open-weights LLM." NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra landed on Hugging Face the same week. By Sunday, Hacker News developers were openly framing the restrictions as a tailwind, legitimizing non-US weights that "didn't make sense to invest in two weeks ago." A field where the best model can be switched off by directive is one where a freely downloadable second-best becomes very interesting, very fast.

Two more threads cut against the noise. Enterprise incumbents rotated from features to metered tollbooths — ServiceNow, SAP, and Workday all stood up pay-per-operation gateways for external agents, eroding per-seat pricing, while Salesforce shipped Summer '26 with Multi-Agent Orchestration GA. And the public pushed back: two Hacker News sentiment surveys topped the front page, with only 16% of Americans expecting AI to improve society and 60% saying "AI" branding is an active turnoff. Grounded evals agreed — OpenAI's own PhD-authored LifeSciBench saw its best model pass just 36.1%.

**Read this first:** [Welcome to the AGI era of AI governance](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/welcome-to-the-agi-era-of-ai-governance) — Nathan Lambert's argument that the Fable/Mythos freeze is the starting gun for a regime where export-control authority, not legislation, becomes the lever on frontier models.

Six weeks out, enterprises still cannot say with certainty which calendar governs them.

## Top papers
- **Nemotron 3 Ultra** (arXiv:2606.15007) — NVIDIA. _why notable:_ a 550B-total / 55B-active open MoE hybrid Mamba-Transformer trained on 20T tokens with 1M context and agentic-reasoning post-training — the week's headline open frontier-scale release. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.15007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15007)
- **DeLM: Decentralized Multi-Agent Systems with Shared Context** (arXiv:2606.10662) — Mao & Mirhoseini. _why notable:_ replaces the central-orchestrator bottleneck with parallel agents that asynchronously claim subtasks from a queue over a shared verified context — recurred across two dailies as the cleanest multi-agent-scaling result. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.10662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10662)
- **NRT-Bench: Multi-Turn Red-Teaming of LLM Agents in Safety-Critical Systems** (arXiv:2606.20408) — _why notable:_ benchmarks frontier "operator" models in a simulated nuclear control room under sustained adversarial pressure; multi-turn attacks breach a safety limit 8.7–12.1% of the time — a sharper robustness test than single-turn jailbreaks. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.20408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20408)
- **DeFAb: A Verifiable Benchmark for Defeasible Abduction** (arXiv:2606.18557) — Cooper, Velasquez. _why notable:_ a logic solver hits 100% while the best frontier model reaches only 65% (dropping to 23.5% under rendering-robust evaluation) — a sharp, defendable capabilities-and-robustness gap. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.18557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18557)
- **CEO-Bench: Can Agents Play the Long Game?** (arXiv:2606.18543) — Chen, Narasimhan, Liu. _why notable:_ a long-horizon benchmark simulating 500 days of running a startup (pricing, marketing, budgeting under uncertainty), probing capabilities short-horizon SWE/customer-service benchmarks miss. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.18543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18543)
- **WorkBench Revisited: Workplace Agents Two Years On** (arXiv:2606.13715) — Olly Styles. _why notable:_ the cleanest two-year progress snapshot — best office agent jumps from GPT-4's 43% task completion (26% harmful actions) to Claude Opus 4.8's 89% (harmful down to 2.5%). [arxiv.org/abs/2606.13715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13715)
- **ProvenanceGuard: Source-Aware Factuality Verification for MCP-Based LLM Agents** (arXiv:2606.18037) — Alvarez et al. _why notable:_ a provenance-sensitive factuality metric catching the "supported somewhere but not by the right source" failure mode in MCP tool-call pipelines — recurred across two dailies. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.18037](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18037)
- **TokenPilot: Cache-Efficient Context Management for LLM Agents** (arXiv:2606.17016) — Xu et al. _why notable:_ prunes/evicts tokens without breaking KV-cache layouts, cutting long-horizon agent inference cost while keeping reuse high — directly applicable to production agent design. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.17016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17016)
- **How Inference Compute Shapes Frontier LLM Evaluation** (arXiv:2606.17930) — _why notable:_ eval rankings are highly sensitive to test-time compute budget; single-budget reporting can misrank models on hard agentic tasks — the methodological caution under the week's leaderboard debates. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.17930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17930)
- **Self-Aware Vector Embeddings for RAG (SmartVector)** (arXiv:2604.20598) — _why notable:_ a neuroscience-inspired framework adding temporal awareness and confidence-weighted retrieval, lifting version-sensitive QA accuracy from ~58% (standard RAG) to ~90% — the anchor of the week's retrieval crop. [arxiv.org/html/2604.20598v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20598v1)
