## The week in 90 seconds

**The government pulled the best model off the market, and the field reorganized around the gap it left**

This was the week a single export-control directive became the axis everything else turned on. Washington compelled Anthropic to withdraw a shipped frontier model; by Thursday that order had hardened into precedent and set off a chain of strategic, financial, and open-weight reactions across the whole industry. If you read one thing, read how the suspension reframed AI governance — releases are now gated on executive-branch judgment, not just lab discretion.

The spine of the week is the Fable 5 / Mythos 5 episode. A Commerce Department directive issued late Friday ordered Anthropic to block foreign-national access to both Mythos-class models; unable to enforce by nationality, the company disabled them worldwide and asked AWS to revoke Bedrock access, reportedly inside a roughly 90-minute window. This was no minor SKU — Fable 5 had scored 80.3% on SWE-Bench Pro, well above Opus 4.8 at 69.2% and GPT-5.5 at 58.6%. A June 15 White House meeting ended without resolution. By Thursday the arc had matured from live crisis into settled precedent and fallout: Simon Willison's instrumented timeline of his own access being cut, New York Times reporting on employees who allege they were targeted, and Anthropic walking back its silent research-degradation policy with a blunt "we made the wrong tradeoff." It is the first time a state has forced a frontier lab to retract a deployed model.

The counterweight is that open weights chose this exact moment to close the cost-adjusted gap and say so. As the most capable model vanished by government order, a freely downloadable second-best looked suddenly compelling. GLM-5.2 took the top open-weights slot on Artificial Analysis — read on Hacker News as relief, "near-Opus-4.7 quality at stupid prices," rather than hype. NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra (550B-total, 55B-active, 1M context) landed as the headline open frontier-scale release, and a 1,217-point HN thread reported Qwen 3.6 and Gemma now handling roughly 90% of daily coding on 128GB Macs. A model nobody can switch off changes both procurement math and the geopolitics of who gates what.

Underneath, the capital structure shifted. OpenAI filed a confidential S-1 at a roughly $852B mark, about a week behind Anthropic's ~$965B Series H, while a newly public SPCX (which folded in xAI/Grok) is reportedly buying Cursor-maker Anysphere for ~$60B — the week's most-mocked M&A. A multi-state attorneys-general coalition opened a joint OpenAI probe days ahead of its filing. Meanwhile the enterprise stack rotated from features to tollbooths: ServiceNow, SAP, and Workday are erecting metered, pay-per-operation gateways for external agents, eroding two decades of per-seat pricing, even as Salesforce shipped Summer '26 and SAP doubled n8n to $5.2B. And two reality checks cut against the noise — only 16% of Americans expect AI to improve society, 60% call "AI" branding a turnoff, and OpenAI's LifeSciBench saw its best model pass just 36.1% of 750 PhD-authored tasks. With three days left in the week, watch whether the export-control precedent draws a formal legal challenge or a copycat order.

**Read this first:** [Welcome to the AGI era of AI governance](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/welcome-to-the-agi-era-of-ai-governance) — Nathan Lambert on why the suspension is the "starting gun" of a regime where releases hinge on executive-branch judgment, with open-model advocates next in line.

## Top papers
- **[Nemotron 3 Ultra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15007)** — NVIDIA. _why notable:_ 550B-total / 55B-active open MoE hybrid Mamba-Transformer, 20T tokens, 1M context, multi-teacher distillation for agentic reasoning — the week's headline open frontier-scale release.
- **[WorkBench Revisited: Workplace Agents Two Years On](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13715)** — Olly Styles. _why notable:_ best office agent jumps from GPT-4's 43% completion (26% harmful) to Claude Opus 4.8's 89% (2.5% harmful) — a concrete two-year capability-and-safety snapshot.
- **[CEO-Bench: Can Agents Play the Long Game?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18543)** — Chen, Narasimhan, Liu. _why notable:_ simulates 500 days of running a startup under uncertainty, probing long-horizon capabilities that short-horizon SWE/customer-service benchmarks miss.
- **[DeFAb: A Verifiable Benchmark for Defeasible Abduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18557)** — Cooper, Velasquez. _why notable:_ a logic solver hits 100% but the best frontier model reaches only 65% (23.5% under robustness eval) — a sharp capabilities-and-robustness gap.
- **[How Inference Compute Shapes Frontier LLM Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17930)** — _why notable:_ eval rankings are highly sensitive to test-time compute budget; single-budget reporting can misrank models on hard agentic tasks — the empirical backbone of the "open gap is overstated" argument.
- **[ToolChain-CRC: Conformal Risk Control for Agentic AI Under Drift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18467)** — Opoku, Banahene. _why notable:_ treats each agent run as a trajectory with calibrated step-level risk scores, catching cases where the final answer looks fine but retrieval/tool output was wrong.
- **[The Coin Flip Judge? Reliability and Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13685)** — _why notable:_ quantifies run-to-run instability of LLM-as-a-Judge across 29 tasks — how unreliable judge-based leaderboards and reward signals can be.
- **[Control-Plane Placement Shapes Forgetting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15903)** — _why notable:_ architectural study of agent memory across 13 configs / 385 adversarial cases; where the LLM sits relative to recall and control planes determines which forgetting failures it recovers from.
- **[SAE Interventions are Unreliable](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18322)** — _why notable:_ sparse-autoencoder feature interventions used for steering/safety can be silently reversed downstream — a caution for SAE-based control and alignment auditing.
- **[PseudoBench: How Agentic Auto-Research Fuels Pseudoscience](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18060)** — _why notable:_ adversarial benchmark on whether autonomous research agents resist generating plausible-but-misleading studies.
