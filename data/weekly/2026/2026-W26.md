## The week in 90 seconds

**The week the government became the gatekeeper of frontier AI — and open weights became the escape hatch.**

What started Monday as a single company's quirky access requirement ended the week as standing US policy: the state, not the lab, now decides who may use the most capable models. Everything else this week — the rush to open weights, the scramble for silicon and talent, the repricing of the inference layer — bent around that gravity.

The spine of the week was government pre-clearance over frontier models. It opened Monday with Anthropic's gov-ID requirement (840 points on Hacker News) and hardened by Saturday into an explicit per-organization regime: the US government will individually approve who may use OpenAI's GPT-5.6, and has cleared Anthropic's Mythos only for vetted domestic organizations. Friday's GPT-5.6 Sol/Terra/Luna preview was itself gated to trusted partners at the government's request. Developer reaction peaked at 1,128 points, reading the whole thing as regulatory capture and openly forecasting a flight to Chinese and open models — the "PostgreSQL versus Oracle" framing. Crucially, that prediction already has receipts: TechCrunch reported Asian startups shipping Mythos-like models within a day, and the week's top research item on HN was a DeepSeek inference paper, upvoted partly because DeepSeek still publishes.

That access friction is what kept recruiting for open weights all week. Z.ai's MIT-licensed GLM-5.2 was the persistent anchor — Nathan Lambert called it the first open model that "feels right" inside coding harnesses as a general agent, roughly 204 days behind Opus 4.5, with Simon Willison's hands-on and an Unsloth run-locally guide (598 points) corroborating. Krea 2's 12B open image weights, Baidu's open OCR, and Reflection AI's compute deal for open-weight training all fed one narrative: in a field where the best model can be switched off by directive, a freely downloadable second-best becomes strategically central, fast.

Underneath the policy noise, the frontier labs reached up the stack — for silicon, runtimes, and people. OpenAI and Broadcom unveiled "Jalapeño," OpenAI's first in-house inference ASIC, taped out in about nine months and bound for gigawatt-scale deployment via Microsoft from late 2026; SpaceX leased up to roughly $6.3B of GB300 capacity to open-weight lab Reflection AI. The talent war went public, with Nobel laureate John Jumper leaving DeepMind for Anthropic days after Noam Shazeer left Google for OpenAI — Alphabet fell as much as 7.2% intraday. Meanwhile the managed agent runtime became a first-party feature everywhere at once: Google Managed Agents and GA Gemini 3.5 Flash, Microsoft Build's governed agent stack, Anthropic's scoped Claude Tag Slack teammate, and ServiceNow's Action Fabric opening to any external agent. Capital followed the same logic downward, repricing the serving layer over training: Baseten closed $1.5B at up to $13B (around $600M ARR, 1B+ inference requests a day), Groq raised $650M for its LPU cloud, and Qualcomm is reportedly in talks to buy Tenstorrent for $8–10B. Gartner put a number on the prize: roughly $206.5B in AI-agent software spend in 2026, up 139% year over year.

The quieter through-line: the durable engineering this week happened in document ingestion and context architecture, not orchestration. Mistral OCR 4 — self-hostable, topping OlmOCRBench at 85.20, with bounding boxes, typed blocks and per-word confidence across 170 languages at $4 per 1,000 pages — shipped the same day as Baidu's open OCR, and by Friday the retrieval conversation had reframed from "RAG pipeline" to agent-native context (Redis Iris, Pinecone KnowQL). Running counter to all of it was a steady skeptic's chorus: red-teamers hitting near-100% attack success on models that ace static injection benchmarks, "AI's Affordability Crisis," and the "Will It Mythos?" debunking of the vuln-finding leaderboard.

**Read this first:** [Welcome to the AGI era of AI governance](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/welcome-to-the-agi-era-of-ai-governance) — Lambert called the "starting gun" of state-controlled frontier release a week before Saturday's GPT-5.6 access news proved him right.

## Top papers
- **NRT-Bench: Multi-Turn Red-Teaming of LLM Agents in Safety-Critical Systems** (arXiv:2606.20408) — Hanwool Lee et al. _why notable:_ a nuclear control-room sim with a five-role LLM operator team; adaptive multi-turn attacks breach a hard safety limit in 8.7–12.1% of runs across four frontier models, harm measured objectively rather than LLM-judged. The week's sharpest robustness read. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.20408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20408)
- **Prompt Injection as Role Confusion** (role-confusion.github.io) — _why notable:_ reframes injection as the model "forgetting who it is," triggered by writing style not `<think>` tags; human red-teamers hit ~100% attack success on models that ace static injection benchmarks — meaning those benchmarks badly overstate agent robustness. [role-confusion.github.io](https://role-confusion.github.io)
- **Is GraphRAG Needed?** (arXiv:2606.25656) — Long Chen et al. _why notable:_ a standardized head-to-head of regular/Graph/Modular/Agentic RAG across 9 implemented scenarios on semi-structured KBs — practical guidance on when each variant actually pays off. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.25656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25656)
- **The Verification Horizon: No Silver Bullet for Coding Agent Rewards** (arXiv:2606.26300) — Wang, Liu, Chen et al. _why notable:_ the "verifying is easier than producing" intuition empirically *inverts* for modern coding agents — direct consequences for RL reward design. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.26300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26300)
- **AgentRiskBOM: A Risk-Scoping Security Bill of Materials for Agentic AI Systems** (arXiv:2606.21877) — Dutta, Moharir. _why notable:_ extends the bill-of-materials concept to agents that retrieve private context, call tools and act without human approval — a concrete governance artifact for the week's agent-security thread. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.21877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21877)
- **Constraint Tax in Open-Weight LLMs** (arXiv:2606.25605) — Fangzheng Li et al. _why notable:_ a reproducible production failure — open-weight models stop invoking tools when tool-calling and JSON-schema constraints are enabled together; directly relevant to agent reliability. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.25605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25605)
- **Externalization in LLM Agents: A Unified Review of Memory, Skills, Protocols and Harness Engineering** (arXiv:2604.08224) — _why notable:_ frames agent capability gains as externalization across memory, skills, interop protocols (MCP/A2A) and harness engineering rather than weights — the clearest synthesis of the "harness era" thesis dominating practitioner discussion. [arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08224](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08224)
- **Improved Large Language Diffusion Models (iLLaDA)** (arXiv:2606.25331) — Shen Nie et al. _why notable:_ an 8B masked-diffusion LM with full bidirectional attention, 12T tokens and variable-length generation — a credible non-autoregressive alternative to causal LLMs. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.25331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25331)
- **Agent Skills for LLMs: Architecture, Acquisition, Security, and the Path Forward** (arXiv:2602.12430) — Renjun Xu, Yang Yan. _why notable:_ composable on-demand skill packages as an alternative to retraining — directly relevant to the agent-skills wave dominating GitHub trending all week. [arxiv.org/abs/2602.12430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12430)
