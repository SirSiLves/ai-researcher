## The week in 90 seconds

**The week the frontier race tilted from benchmarks to S-1s, and the agent layer split into open versus consolidated camps.**

W21 stopped settling the "which lab is on top" question on capability and started settling it on cap tables. Inside seven trading days OpenAI confidentially filed a draft S-1 with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley targeting a Q3/Q4 listing above $1T (current private mark ~$852B); Anthropic projected its first-ever profitable quarter at $10.9B Q2 revenue and $559M operating profit, told investors it expects a $50B annualized run-rate by end of June, and queued a $30B+ round at $900B+ co-led by Sequoia, Dragoneer, Altimeter and Greenoaks; SpaceX's own S-1 accidentally exposed Anthropic paying $1.25B/month — roughly $45B through 2029 — for the full output of Colossus 1 plus the Colossus 2 GB200 ramp. Coinbase markets now price 85% odds OpenAI lists before Anthropic. The "frontier labs lose money through 2028" thesis died on Wednesday, and whichever S-1 lands first sets the multiple for everything else.

The agent-interop layer broke apart in a single working day. Monday Anthropic acquired Stainless for roughly $300M — the codegen pipeline behind every official Anthropic SDK and the SDKs for OpenAI, Google, Cloudflare and Meta — and began winding down all hosted Stainless products; the top Hacker News comment read "we just bought OpenAI's front door and we're EOLing it." Hours later Microsoft used Open Source Summit NA to open-source the Agent Framework SDK, runtime and Governance Toolkit under the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation. Tuesday Claude Managed Agents shipped self-hosted sandboxes and MCP tunnels — the missing enterprise-deployability story for regulated buyers. Saturday the MCP 2026-07-28 release candidate entered its ten-week implementer validation window with stateless core, a Tasks extension, MCP Apps and OAuth/OIDC authorization; AWS, ServiceNow, Snowflake and Weaviate are already shipping against it. Governance data hit the same week: 53% of production MCP servers still use long-lived static secrets, only 8.5% OAuth.

Google used Tuesday's I/O keynote to answer six months of model-narrative losses in one stage show. Gemini 3.5 Flash became the default across Google services (claimed 4× faster at less than half the cost of comparable frontier models, beating 3.1 Pro on coding and agentic benchmarks); Omni Flash unified any-input-to-video; Gemini Spark shipped as a 24/7 personal agent on dedicated Cloud VMs. The structural play was the Agent Payments Protocol with 60+ launch partners — Mastercard, Amex, JCB, UnionPay, PayPal, Coinbase, Salesforce, ServiceNow — plus Universal Cart in Search, Gemini, YouTube and Gmail this summer. Gemini is now at 900M+ monthly users across 230 countries. Wednesday added a $5B Blackstone TPU joint venture (500MW by 2027, up to $25B leveraged), the largest single PE commitment to non-NVIDIA AI compute on record. The counter-current showed up Thursday: Flash 3.5 ships at 3× the price of 3.0 Flash and 6× 3.1 Flash-Lite — the first time a Flash-tier model has gotten more expensive across generations.

Washington went the other way. Thursday's Trump AI cybersecurity executive order signing ceremony was scrapped on signing day after the president pulled the order with "I didn't like certain aspects of it"; Friday and Saturday confirmed no new date, leaving the federal coordinating instrument for the Mythos-triggered FRB/OCC bank-exam pause a structural gap through at least Q3. Europe filled the vacuum: FINMA classified Mythos-class capability as "immediate systemic risk," giving Switzerland the highest-jurisdiction formal Mythos designation in the global stack; Germany's Research Minister Dorothee Bär unveiled the Hightech-Agenda (4,000 AI startups, sovereign chips, AI gigafactory) — the first numerical federal AI-industrial-policy target since 2018; the Commission opened Article 6 high-risk classification guidelines for consultation through June 23. Enterprise stacks moved in coordination Tuesday: Dell Tech World, SAP Sapphire (Claude as primary reasoning engine, n8n embedded at a doubled $5.2B valuation as the orchestration layer), and ServiceNow Knowledge all shipped within hours. Saturday OpenAI launched a Deployment Company with $4B+ from a 19-firm consortium and the Tomoro acquisition seeding 150 forward-deployed engineers — the corporate-vehicle answer to Anthropic's KPMG and PwC lockups.

Three threads sat underneath everything. Andrej Karpathy joined Anthropic Monday to lead a pre-training team — the highest-signal talent move of 2026, though Hacker News reception was unusually skeptical at 1,389 points. Cohere shipped Command A+ Saturday at 218B sparse MoE / 25B active under full Apache 2.0 — the first frontier-class enterprise model without the commercial-use tax. And the Chinese-model cost story moved from background to foreground: Artificial Analysis benchmarked a standard 10-evaluation workload at $4,811 on Claude versus $544 on Zhipu GLM, roughly 9×; OpenRouter share for Chinese models climbed from ~1% in 2024 to over 60% in May 2026; Anthropic's own May policy paper conceded US models are only "several months ahead." Sunday DeepSeek V4 Pro made its 75% promo discount permanent, and HN finally surfaced data-privacy and geopolitical concerns about Chinese-hosted models as the top counter-argument rather than a buried one. Trust-in-vendor is now a primary axis.
