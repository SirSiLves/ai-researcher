## The week in 90 seconds

**The week enterprise-agent consolidation peaked and the bubble counter-narrative hardened in the same seven days.**

W20 was the operational consolidation everyone in the field has been building toward for nine months — every major platform vendor, every frontier lab, the year's largest IPO, the first US business-AI-adoption lead change, and the first sustained Hacker News pushback against the procurement frenzy underwriting all of it. The two stories define each other; reading the week without holding both at once misses what shifted.

The consolidation story is SAP Sapphire and what it pulled into orbit. Tuesday's Autonomous Enterprise keynote unified 50+ Joule Assistants over 200+ specialized agents and took a strategic stake in n8n that doubled the German workflow company's valuation to $5.2B. Thursday's Day 3 made Claude the default reasoning engine across SAP's Business AI Platform; Joule Studio 2.0 shipped with 224 agents on NVIDIA OpenShell. The same week, ServiceNow Knowledge 2026 made its MCP Server GA across every Now Assist SKU and extended the AI Control Tower into Microsoft Agent 365; Salesforce Summer '26 shipped Agentforce Multi-Agent Orchestration and Tableau MCP; AWS Bedrock AgentCore Payments preview launched with Coinbase and Stripe — the first managed payment capability for autonomous agents. Anthropic verticalized across finance, legal, SMB, M365 add-ins, SAP, and a 30,000-staff PwC training rollout, then took the Ramp AI Index lead from OpenAI on Saturday (34.4% vs 32.3%) — the first US business-AI-adoption lead change of the enterprise race. Sunday's reporting put the Anthropic run-rate above $30B from $9B a year earlier.

The capital side validated the consolidation with public-market money. Wednesday Cerebras priced at $185 a share (~$56B fully-diluted, 20x oversubscribed); Thursday it opened at $350 and closed at $311 on a ~$70B first-day market cap — the largest US tech IPO of 2026 and a +25% re-rating of the inference thesis over the priced book on day one. DeepSeek moved toward its first VC round at $45B with China's "Big Fund" leading; Sierra closed $950M at $15.8B; Cohere bought Aleph Alpha to assemble a ~$20B transatlantic AI champion with Schwarz Group plus Canadian and German government backing; Cisco lifted its AI-orders forecast from $5B to $9B and cut 4,000 jobs to refund the capex.

The counterweight arrived the same weekend. Mitchell Hashimoto's "I believe there are entire companies right now under AI psychosis" tweet took #1 on Hacker News at 1,727 points on Saturday, climbed to 2,061 points and 1,210 comments on Sunday — the first time the procurement-outrunning-deployability framing has held the top slot two days running. Pair-reads clustered around it: Amazon workers making up tasks to meet AI-usage quotas, Ontario auditors finding that ~60% of audited AI medical-scribe systems mix up prescribed drugs, arXiv banning hallucinated references for a year, Turso retiring its bug-bounty under AI-spam load, Sunday's SANA-WM "open without weights" backlash. The Stanford AI Index 2026, also published this week, supplied the macro frame: $581.7B in 2025 corporate AI investment (+130% YoY), 23% of the US public expecting AI to positively affect their work versus 73% of AI experts — the widest expert-public gap the index has ever recorded.

A few facts the body adds and the lead does not pad. Sakana's 7B Conductor router reportedly beat every individual worker in its pool of GPT-5, Claude Sonnet 4, and Gemini 2.5 Pro at 83.9% LiveCodeBench and 87.5% GPQA-Diamond — first credible "small router on top of big models" pattern with numbers. The Five Eyes published their first multi-government agentic-AI doctrine. UK AISI extended its Microsoft partnership into societal-impact research — emotional dependency, mental-health interaction, professional-trust erosion. FutureSim, the Sunday paper that replays real-world news chronologically against agent leaderboards, reads as the natural successor to ARC-AGI for benchmark integrity. The EU AI Act Omnibus VII held its 7 May trilogue deal — Annex III deferred to December 2027, GPAI grace cut to three months.

**Read this first:** ["Inside the AI Index — 12 Takeaways from the 2026 Report"](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/inside-the-ai-index-12-takeaways-from-the-2026-report) — Stanford HAI's twelve-point macro is the single document that lets you hold consolidation peak and bear case in the same head: $581.7B in corporate AI investment, the FMTI dropping from 58 to 40, and a 23%-vs-73% public-expert sentiment gap on workplace impact.

## Top papers
- **FutureSim: Replaying World Events to Evaluate Adaptive Agents** — Goel, Chandak, Arun, Prabhu, Staab, Hardt et al. _Why notable:_ Replays real-world news chronologically so agents are evaluated on whether they actually adapt to post-training-cutoff information; direct attack on the static-eval problem of current agent leaderboards. [arXiv:2605.03310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03310)
- **Tool Calling is Linearly Readable and Steerable in Language Models** — Wu, Wang, Cho et al. (UCL / Holistic AI). _Why notable:_ Tool a model is about to call is encoded in a single linear direction in hidden state across 12 instruction-tuned models — cheap pre-execution probe. [arXiv:2605.07990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07990)
- **ComplexMCP: Evaluation of LLM Agents in Dynamic, Interdependent, and Large-Scale Tool Sandbox** — anon. _Why notable:_ First MCP-native eval that models tool interdependence + dynamic state — bridges toy MCP demos and production. [arXiv:2605.10787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10787)
- **MCP-Cosmos: World Model-Augmented Agents for Complex Task Execution in MCP Environments** — anon. _Why notable:_ "Bring Your Own World Model" — MCP client simulates state transitions in latent space before executing tool calls. [arXiv:2605.09131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09131)
- **STALE: Can LLM Agents Know When Their Memories Are No Longer Valid?** — Chao, Bai, Sheng, Li, Sun. _Why notable:_ 400-scenario benchmark for "implicit conflict" — new observations invalidating earlier memory without explicit negation; current agents fail to revise stored beliefs. [arXiv:2605.06527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527)
- **EvolveMem: Self-Evolving Memory Architecture via AutoResearch for LLM Agents** — Liu, Ye, Xia, Zheng, Xie, Ding et al. _Why notable:_ Co-evolves stored content AND retrieval mechanism; exposes the full retrieval config as an action space optimized by an LLM diagnosis module — the strongest concrete RAG-architecture proposal of the week. [arXiv:2605.13941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13941)
- **Authorization Propagation in Multi-Agent AI Systems: Identity Governance as Infrastructure** — anon. _Why notable:_ Authorization as first-class infra concern across A2A and MCP handoffs; how to maintain authz invariants for non-human principals. [arXiv:2605.05440](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05440)
- **Is Grep All You Need? How Agent Harnesses Reshape Agentic Search** — anon. _Why notable:_ Lexical (grep) vs semantic vs hybrid retrieval across tool-equipped LLM agent harnesses; harness design changes which retrieval primitive wins — undermines one-size-fits-all RAG. [arXiv:2605.15184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15184)
- **GraphBit: A Graph-based Agentic Framework for Non-Linear Agent Orchestration** — Sarker, Ullah, Molla, Joty. _Why notable:_ Engine-orchestrated DAG-based agentic framework (Rust core) that replaces prompted routing with typed function-style agents — fixes hallucinated routing, infinite loops, reproducibility. [arXiv:2605.13848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13848)

[arXiv:2605.15034](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15034)
