## The week in 90 seconds

**The week Azure exclusivity ended, the Pentagon picked sides, and the enterprise agent control plane split three ways.**

W18 is the week the contractual scaffolding of the frontier-lab era was rewritten in public. Three threads converge: the Microsoft-OpenAI restructure unwound the Azure-exclusive partnership that had defined hyperscaler-LLM economics since 2023; the Pentagon awarded eight classified-AI vendors and froze Anthropic out for refusing autonomous-weapons and mass-surveillance guardrails; and Microsoft, Salesforce and SAP each picked a different architectural answer to the enterprise agent control plane in the same five-day window.

The lead story is the Monday-Tuesday sequence. On April 27 Microsoft and OpenAI formally amended their partnership — a non-exclusive IP license through 2032, revenue-share capped and ending in 2030, Azure as "first-look" but explicitly not exclusive, and the AGI clause retired. Twenty-four hours later, at AWS What's Next, OpenAI landed on Bedrock — GPT-5.5/5.4 plus Codex plus Bedrock Managed Agents powered by OpenAI in limited preview, with Altman appearing by recorded video while Matt Garman ran the keynote. Amazon Quick and Bedrock AgentCore CLI shipped as companion pieces, repositioning Bedrock from model catalogue to agent operating environment. On Friday OpenAI's $1.4 trillion multi-year GPU buildout was briefed with NVIDIA as financing counterpart. Microsoft's Q3 print landed mid-week with Copilot for M365 paid seats up 75 percent sequentially to more than 20 million and AI run-rate above $37 billion — the Azure-AI flywheel survived the non-exclusivity, but the gap to other clouds is now measured in days, not quarters.

The second thread is Anthropic's. On April 29 Bloomberg, CNBC and TechCrunch reported preemptive offers in the $850-900 billion band for a $50 billion round that could close within two weeks — past OpenAI's $852 billion mark. Forty-eight hours later the Pentagon awarded IL-6/7 classified-AI contracts to OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, AWS, NVIDIA, SpaceX, Oracle and Reflection AI, and explicitly excluded Anthropic after the lab refused to drop usage-policy guardrails. Hegseth's March supply-chain risk designation had been blocked by a federal judge; the DoD proceeded anyway. "We will never again rely on a single AI provider." It is the first time a frontier lab has held a public usage policy against direct federal pressure and accepted being designated supply-chain risk for it — the cleanest test of the values-aligned commercial lab versus federally-aligned compute frame the field has produced.

The third thread is structural rather than political. Friday May 1 was the day the enterprise agent control plane forked. Microsoft Agent 365 and the M365 E7 Frontier Suite went GA — Agent 365 standalone at $15 per user per month, E7 at $99 bundling M365 E5 plus Copilot plus Agent 365, cross-cloud registry sync with AWS Bedrock and Google Cloud in public preview, Anthropic IP setting decommissioned and Claude defaulted as the M365 Copilot subprocessor for Excel and PowerPoint. The same day Salesforce Headless 360 lit up with 60-plus MCP tools wired to Claude Code, Cursor and any MCP runtime — the deliberate opposite of SAP's API Policy v4/2026, which had quietly blocked external AI agents from independently scheduling or executing calls earlier in the week. Microsoft governance plane, Salesforce open-MCP everywhere, SAP walled garden — three architectural positions enterprises must now pick between, all live on the same week.

A few facts the body adds that the lead does not pad: Mistral Medium 3.5 shipped at 77.6 percent SWE-Bench Verified with open weights and a $1.50/$7.50 per million-token rate card; IBM Granite 4.1 (3B/8B/30B Apache-2.0) and xAI Grok 4.3 (1M context, 207 tokens/sec, 20 percent cheaper at $1.25/$2.50) followed inside 48 hours. Anthropic Claude Security shipped public beta with Opus 4.7 having surfaced 500 production vulnerabilities in two months of preview, integrated with CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, SentinelOne, Trend Micro and Wiz the same day. UK AISI confirmed GPT-5.5's cyber capabilities at Mythos-comparable but publicly available — the "no more gating" reality the EU AI Act trilogue, which collapsed twelve hours after the OpenAI-AWS launch, must now absorb. Meta acquired Assured Robot Intelligence on Friday, the "Android of humanoid robots" framing, and closed the week with Sakana KAME — the cleanest voice-AI architectural innovation since GPT-Realtime split.
