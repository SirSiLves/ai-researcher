## The week in 90 seconds

**The government turned Fable 5 back on — and developers turned on Anthropic.**

The first state-directed suspension of a frontier model completed its full arc this week: switched off in June, conditioned on new safeguards, and switched back on worldwide on July 1. The lab that won its model back then spent the same five days losing developer trust in public, while open-weight models and enterprise token bills quietly reset the economics underneath everyone.

The week opened with Mythos 5 and Fable 5 partially restored to roughly 100 "trusted" organizations. Within days Commerce lifted the export controls outright — the price being new classifiers that block a wider range of cyber tasks, with the June 30 Commerce letter itself posted to Hacker News at 880 points — and on July 1 Fable 5 came back globally across the API, the apps, and Claude Code, at $10/$50 with a million-token context window. Mythos 5 stays walled off for vetted cyberdefense partners. Anthropic closed the week publishing the model's cyber safeguards and proposing CJS, a cross-vendor jailbreak-severity scale, the same day heise reported researchers had already jailbroken the redeployed model. OpenAI's GPT-5.6 remains gated to about 20 government-cleared partners ahead of a mid-July GA — and by Friday OpenAI was floating a roughly 5% equity stake for the US government. Every future frontier release inherits this template: state review on the way in, concessions as the price of coming back.

The counterweight is a developer-trust inversion aimed at the lab that just got its model back. Claude Sonnet 5 launched June 30 and went from launch to verdict within a day: community benchmarks put it near GLM-5.2 level at about twice the price, its own system card shows it weaker than Sonnet 4.6 at vulnerability discovery — deliberate cyber suppression is now a measurable product tradeoff — and Simon Willison's analysis found the new tokenizer emits roughly 30% more tokens for the same text, an effective 1.3–1.4x price increase behind unchanged rates. Then the grievances stacked. A claim that Claude Code hides hostname-keyed fingerprints in requests topped Hacker News two days running, at 2,327 and then 1,299 points; Claude Science was caught silently disabling citation verification on Pro plans; and a single-source Reuters report said Alibaba will ban Claude Code internally. For the first time in this cycle, "you can't trust the big labs" ran as the majority take.

Underneath both stories, open weights set the floor. GLM 5.2 appeared in all five dailies, carried by Semgrep's claim that it finds IDOR-class security bugs at about $0.17 apiece — beating Claude Code — a post that climbed to 1,093 points while Qwen 3.6 topped the front page beside it. NVIDIA shipped Nemotron 3 Ultra, a genuinely open 550-billion-parameter reasoning model released with its training data and RL recipes, and Friday's top story put Kimi K2.7 Code into GitHub Copilot as generally available — distribution, not just benchmark wins. Nathan Lambert's estimate that open models now sit about 6.8 months behind the closed frontier became the week's most-cited frame, and Sonnet 5's cool reception shows the mechanism: every closed launch is now priced against an open baseline.

The money explains why that baseline bites. Citi is rationing frontier access, Uber's 2026 AI budget was gone by April, Atlassian runs near $15 million a month, and per-employee token budgets of $250 to $2,000-plus are the new norm — so the week's top GitHub gainer was caveman, a Claude Code skill that cuts about 65% of tokens. Vendors are repricing around the same fact: Salesforce's help agent charges a flat $2 only when it fully resolves a case, Microsoft stood up a $2.5 billion deployment company, and SAP is funding Joule through austerity while paying some $60 million to double n8n's valuation to $5.2 billion. Two days remain before this snapshot is finalized on Sunday. Watch the July 28 MCP spec — its largest-ever revision froze as a release candidate this week — the EU's GPAI enforcement date now about 30 days out on August 2, and GPT-5.6's expected mid-July GA.

**Read this first:** [GLM-5.2 is the step change for open agents](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/glm-52-is-the-step-change-for-open) — Nathan Lambert's essay is the week's most-cited frame and the fastest way to understand why a closed-model launch now gets judged against the open-weight floor.

## Top papers
- **Governance Gaps in Agent Interoperability Protocols: What MCP, A2A, and ACP Cannot Express** — Kang & Diponegoro. _Why notable:_ systematic map showing enterprise governance constraints fall through the gaps of the agent-interop stack — names the ceiling on the MCP-everywhere narrative. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.31498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31498)
- **AutoMem: Automated Learning of Memory as a Cognitive Skill** — Wu, Zhu, Zhang, Wang, Yeung-Levy. _Why notable:_ memory management as a trainable skill lifts long-horizon performance ~2–4x and makes a 32B open model competitive with frontier closed models. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.01224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01224)
- **One Retrieval to Cover Them All: Session-Level RAG** — Ratnakar, Zhu, Cheng, Vijayakumar. _Why notable:_ a single retrieval covers only 41% of a session-level need; argues session coverage should replace single-query recall as the enterprise RAG metric — recurred in two dailies. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.31156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31156)
- **Jailbreaking Function-Calling LLMs via Simulated Moderation Traces** — Liu, Wang, Luo, Jia. _Why notable:_ injected fake moderation traces jailbreak tool-calling LLMs — a structural vulnerability of stateful function-calling runtimes, directly relevant to MCP security. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.00481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00481)
- **Behind the Refusal: Determining Guardrail Activation via Behavioral Monitoring** — Hackett & Garraghan. _Why notable:_ guardrails can be fingerprinted from refusal behavior alone — a blind spot for AI security stacks, landing the week AI-security agents entered the radar. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.02121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02121)
- **Safeguarding LLM Agents from Misalignment through Provenance Analysis** — She, Liang, Kang. _Why notable:_ provenance over tool invocations catches actions deviating from user intent before harm — runtime alignment checking for tool-using agents. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.01236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01236)
- **Scaling the Horizon, Not the Parameters** — _Why notable:_ argues extending the agentic reasoning horizon can match trillion-parameter performance with a 35B agent — the counterpoint to parameter-scaling orthodoxy. [huggingface.co/papers/2606.30616](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.30616)
- **OSWorld2.0: Benchmarking Computer-Use Agents on Long-Horizon Tasks** — _Why notable:_ refreshed computer-use benchmark stressing realistic long-horizon desktop workflows, as cloud-running agents become the labs' declared next trend. [huggingface.co/papers/2606.29537](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.29537)
- **Evaluating Chunking Strategies for RAG on Academic Texts** — Kreileder et al. _Why notable:_ semantic chunking did NOT beat fixed-size/recursive, and RAGAs faithfulness proved unreliable — a useful negative result for pipeline design. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.01852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01852)
- **Supersede: Diagnosing and Training the Memory-Update Gap in LLM Agents** — _Why notable:_ isolates a distinct multi-session agent failure (using superseded facts) and trains for it — pairs with the week's agent-memory backlash on HN. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.27472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27472)
