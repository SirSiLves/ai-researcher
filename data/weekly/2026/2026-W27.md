## The week in 90 seconds

**Anthropic launches into a buzzsaw as the state starts dictating what models may do**

Three days into the week, the story isn't a product — it's a mood. Anthropic shipped Claude Sonnet 5 and Claude Science on Tuesday, but by Wednesday the reception had curdled into the first cycle where "you can't trust the big labs" ran as an upvoted majority take on Hacker News, aimed squarely at the field's safety-forward lab. Underneath the sentiment, Commerce turned export controls into a bargaining chip: lift the ban, add cyber-blocking classifiers, ship a weaker model.

The week opened Monday with the government-as-gatekeeper arc going live. Commerce partially restored Anthropic's Mythos 5 and Fable 5 to roughly 100 "trusted" companies and federal agencies — the first full take-down-then-reinstate cycle of a frontier model. By the June 30 letter surfacing Wednesday, controls on both were lifted entirely, but only after Anthropic agreed to add classifiers that block more cybersecurity tasks, with commenters flagging that Fable 5 reportedly can no longer be used for coding under them. In parallel, OpenAI's GPT-5.6 firmed up as the first OpenAI flagship gated behind US frontier-model review, narrowed to around 20 government-approved partners. The terms of state approval now shape what a commercial model can do, not merely who gets to use it.

Then Anthropic launched into it. Claude Sonnet 5 landed Tuesday — default on Free and Pro, intro pricing of $2/$10 through August 31 against Opus 4.8's $5/$25 — caught on HN /newest before the vendor blog even propagated. But independent benchmarks pegged it at roughly GLM-5.2-level capability at about twice the cost, and its own system card showed it less capable than Sonnet 4.6 at vulnerability discovery, the deliberate cyber suppression now a measurable product tradeoff. A new tokenizer quietly maps the same text to about 1.35x more tokens, eating the headline price cut. The sharper turn was sentiment: a thereallo.dev steganography and fingerprinting claim topped HN at 2,327 points, and distrust of Anthropic specifically crystallized into a majority view for the first time this cycle — a reputational variable enterprises now have to price alongside capability.

The counter-current ran on open Chinese weights, then faded. Semgrep's claim that GLM 5.2 beats Claude Code at finding IDOR-class bugs climbed from 748 to 1,093 points; Qwen 3.6 27B topped the same page at 1,095 simultaneously, so two open Chinese models sat atop a Claude-comparison feed at once. By Wednesday the surge receded as the front page consolidated back around closed-lab trust and policy — a faster sentiment cycle than last week's sustained open-weights momentum. NVIDIA leaned in with the fully-open 550B Nemotron 3 Ultra; the honest thread-level counterweight ("Claude Code is a harness, not an LLM," "what hardware runs 753B locally?") kept the "own it" story grounded.

The quieter but more durable move was capital and product converging on the orchestration layer, not the model. SAP's roughly $60M strategic investment doubled n8n's valuation to $5.2B, making it Germany's most valuable AI company and embedding its multi-agent workflow canvas into Joule Studio; ServiceNow extended Autonomous Workforce and shipped Otto with a GA MCP Server; Google turned agent runtimes into an API primitive with Managed Agents. Beneath that, money kept repricing inference silicon against CUDA — Qualcomm's ~$3.9B Modular buy, Cognition's $1B+ at $25B, Reflection AI's ~$2B on a SpaceX/Colossus GB300 lease. The enterprise battleground is shifting from smartest model to who owns the governed automation canvas.

**Read this first:** [Governance Gaps in Agent Interoperability Protocols](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31498) — the week's sharpest paper maps what MCP, A2A, and ACP structurally cannot express, naming the first real ceiling on the MCP-everywhere narrative.

## Top papers
- **[Governance Gaps in Agent Interoperability Protocols: What MCP, A2A, and ACP Cannot Express](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31498)** — Kang & Diponegoro. _why notable:_ the week's highest-signal paper — a systematic map of what today's agent-interop stack structurally *cannot* express; identity/discovery/messaging are covered, but enterprise governance constraints fall through the gaps. Directly names the ceiling on the MCP-everywhere narrative dominating LinkedIn and blogs. _cs.MA._
- **[PixelRAG: Web Screenshots Beat Text for RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28344)** — Zaharia / Gonzalez / Min et al. _why notable:_ retrieves and reads websites in native visual form, preserving layout that HTML-linearizing pipelines discard — a retrieval-quality lever tied to the week's document-ingestion theme.
- **[CORTEX: Token-Level Hallucination Detection in RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31033)** — _why notable:_ localizes hallucinated spans inside long-form RAG outputs at token granularity via comparative internal representations, rather than flagging whole responses. _cs.CL._
- **[Scaling the Horizon, Not the Parameters](https://huggingface.co/papers/2606.30616)** — _why notable:_ argues extending the agentic reasoning horizon, rather than scaling parameters, can match trillion-parameter performance with a 35B agent — the "harness era" thesis in benchmark form.
- **[A Systems-Level Analysis of Sensitivity, Robustness and Stability in RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28337)** — _why notable:_ controlled 56-run study isolating where RAG actually fails (chunking, top-k, reranking, generation) rather than scoring only final answers.
- **[ComplianceGate: Classifier-Gated Multi-Tier LLM Routing for Regulated Industries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31163)** — _why notable:_ a classifier gate routes queries across model tiers to enforce PII/compliance constraints before requests reach endpoints — timely against the week's governance/enforcement drumbeat. _cs.LG._
- **[Always-On Agents: A Survey of Persistent Memory, State and Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30306)** — _why notable:_ frames always-on agents as persistent-state systems (memories, ledgers, permissions, audit records) and the governance primitives they demand.
- **[Recursive Self-Evolving Agents via Held-Out Selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28374)** — _why notable:_ apples-to-apples re-evaluation of weight-free self-improving agents, introducing held-out selection to avoid single-benchmark overfitting.
- **[Addressing Over-Refusal in LLMs with Competing Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31748)** — Kim & Kumar. _why notable:_ competing reward signals cut safety-induced over-refusal without weakening refusal of genuinely harmful prompts. _cs.LG._
