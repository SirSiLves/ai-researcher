## The week in 90 seconds

**The week a frontier lab refused to ship — and got Treasury, the Fed and the Bank of England to agree it was right.**

W15 is the week the AI policy conversation moved from working papers to convened bank CEOs. On Tuesday 2026-04-07 Anthropic disclosed Project Glasswing and Claude Mythos Preview, the first capability-gated frontier model, alongside pre-release evidence that the model had autonomously identified and exploited thousands of zero-days in testing — including a 27-year-old OpenBSD bug and a 16-year-old FFmpeg flaw — at better than 80% reproduction. Forrester, Arctic Wolf and CETaS treated the gated rollout as the new procurement baseline the same day. Within 24 hours the WEF, ITECS and the Internet Governance Project had promoted it to a governance template. By Friday Treasury Secretary Bessent and Fed Chair Powell convened Fraser, Pick, Moynihan, Scharf and Solomon at Treasury HQ — the first time U.S. financial regulators have organised the bank-CEO class around a single named frontier model. Saturday the Bank of England's FPC scheduled CMORG and a CMORG AI Taskforce session with HMT, FCA and NCSC, with Anthropic to brief UK banks the following week. Sunday Bloomberg Opinion previewed its Monday "Anthropic Sets the Right AI Standard with Mythos" framing. The full transatlantic regulator chain closed inside 96 hours.

The counterweight is the revenue narrative arriving the same Tuesday. Anthropic disclosed an Annual Revenue Run-Rate of about $30B, leapfrogging OpenAI's $25B on the same day, on a trajectory that ran $87M in January 2024 to $1B by December 2024 to $30B now. Customers paying more than $1M a year roughly doubled from around 500 to over 1,000 in two months. OpenAI is disputing approximately $8B on gross-revenue accounting, and that dispute remained unresolved through the week; even so it does not erase the gap. By Friday CoreWeave had signed $28B of capacity in 48 hours — $21B to expand Meta toward roughly $35B of backlog through 2032 and the first Vera Rubin deployments on Thursday, then $6.8B for Claude production on Friday. Nine of the top ten frontier providers now run on CoreWeave; the word "neocloud" landed in mainstream market commentary the same week.

Underneath both stories the open-weight map redrew. On Wednesday Meta debuted Muse Spark from its Superintelligence Labs as a closed-weights model, the first Meta flagship since Llama not on public weights, with 2026 capital expenditure guided to $115-135B. On Tuesday Z.ai's GLM-5.1, a 744B MoE under an MIT licence, had already taken the top of SWE-Bench Pro at 58.4 and built a Linux desktop unattended over roughly eight hours. MiniMax shipped M2.7 weights on Saturday and the agent-to-agent MMX-CLI on Sunday, both designed to be invoked from inside Cursor, Claude Code and OpenCode harnesses. Hugging Face's Spring 2026 numbers — 41% Chinese share of Q1 downloads, robotics datasets up twenty-three-fold — anchored Nathan Lambert's "inevitable need for an open model consortium" essay as the U.S. policy answer. Apache-2.0 is now the minority licence among the most-downloaded weights, with modified-MIT and OpenRAIL splitting the rest. The frame the week settled on is blunt: Anthropic gates, China opens, and the U.S. open-weight anchor just left the table.

What to watch into W16: Bloomberg Opinion's Monday publication of the Mythos-standard framing, the follow-through from the BoE CMORG AI Taskforce sessions, and whether any second frontier lab follows Anthropic's gating template before Cloud Next '26 opens on April 22.

**Read this first:** [Anthropic's Project Glasswing — restricting Claude Mythos to security researchers sounds necessary to me](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Apr/7/project-glasswing/) — Simon Willison's 1,296-word case for the gated-release model became the most-cited public defence of W15's policy template, and is the cleanest 5-minute read for understanding why the regulator chain that followed treated Glasswing as the new floor rather than the alarm.

## Top papers
- **GrandCode: Achieving Grandmaster Level in Competitive Programming via Agentic RL** — anon. _Why notable:_ Multi-agent RL ("agentic GRPO") reports grandmaster-level Codeforces; counter-data to "more agents = better" corrective. [arXiv:2604.02721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02721)
- **Adam's Law: Textual Frequency Law on Large Language Models** — anon. _Why notable:_ Power-law data-frequency-vs-performance with operational distillation + curriculum. [arXiv:2604.02176](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02176)
- **MCP-DPT: Defense-Placement Taxonomy and Coverage Analysis for Model Context Protocol Security** — Rostamzadeh, Narula. _Why notable:_ Layer-aligned taxonomy across MCP's six layers; first defense-placement view, not attack-catalog. [arXiv:2604.07551](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07551)
- **RAGEN-2: Reasoning Collapse in Agentic RL** — anon. _Why notable:_ Identifies "template collapse" — agent outputs become structurally similar while entropy looks healthy. Sharpest agentic-RL diagnostic of the week. [arXiv:2604.06268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06268)
- **Externalization in LLM Agents: A Unified Review** — Zhou, Chai. _Why notable:_ Cleanest taxonomy of agent platform stack as memory + skills + protocols + harness. [arXiv:2604.08224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08224)
- **The Master Key Hypothesis: Cross-Model Capability Transfer via Linear Subspace Alignment** — anon. _Why notable:_ Post-trained capability lives in low-dimensional latent subspaces; transferable across scales by linear alignment — if it holds, changes post-training economics. [arXiv:2604.06377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06377)
- **MolmoWeb: Open Visual Web Agent and Open Data for the Open Web** — Gupta, Wolters. _Why notable:_ Open-weight multimodal web agents on screenshots only; state-of-the-art among open-weight web agents. [arXiv:2604.08516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08516)
- **Beyond Accuracy: Unveiling Inefficiency Patterns in Tool-Integrated Reasoning** — anon. _Why notable:_ PTE (Prefill Token Equivalents) — hardware-aware metric that correlates with wall-clock latency far better than raw token counts. [arXiv:2604.05404](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05404)
- **Personalized RewardBench** — anon. _Why notable:_ Individual user preference capture correlates with downstream PPO better than RM benchmarks. [arXiv:2604.07343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07343)
- **The Depth Ceiling: On the Limits of LLMs in Discovering Latent Planning Strategies** — anon. _Why notable:_ Discovery and execution dissociate — CoT-monitoring captures execution but not strategy selection. [arXiv:2604.06427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06427)
