## The week in 90 seconds

**The open frontier drew level — and the binding constraint turned regulatory, geopolitical, and physical**

The week's story was rotation. Last week's spine was a three-lab _closed_ blitz; this week the open frontier drew level, as two downloadable models reached frontier scale within a day of each other and the usage data confirmed the tide had already turned. The ceiling on open AI stopped looking technical and started looking legal, while the field's center of gravity kept sliding off the base model — toward deployment, sovereignty, power, and a hardening distrust of the tools themselves. By Sunday the clearest signal was Europe moving to _own_ its stack, not just regulate it.

The capability headline came from open weights, not a closed lab. On July 15 Mira Murati's Thinking Machines shipped Inkling (975B total / 41B active, Apache 2.0, ~41 on the Artificial Analysis Index), the strongest US open-weights release yet, pitched deliberately as a fine-tunable customization base rather than a benchmark chaser. A day later Moonshot's Kimi K3 took the "largest open-weight model ever" crown at 2.8 trillion parameters, debuting #1 on the Frontend Code Arena at $3/$15 and beating every leading US model on blind front-end coding — though its weights don't publish until July 27. Mozilla's state-of-open-source report supplied the number that made the pair land: OpenRouter traffic flipped from 60/40 closed-favoring to 63/37 open-favoring in four months, throughput up roughly fivefold to 4.19 trillion tokens a day. Alibaba's rushed Qwen 3.8 answered by the weekend. A week ago the open story was crossing the silicon line; this week it crossed the capability line.

That commoditization is why the week's governance news carried weight — and it arrived as a two-bloc contest, not just an EU tightening. The AI Act's GPAI-enforcement powers activate August 2 (information requests, model access, recall authority, fines up to €15M or 3% of worldwide turnover, plus the Article 50 duty to tell users they're talking to an AI), and the countdown ran down all week toward two weeks out. China answered: 29 countries signed WAICO, headquartered in Shanghai, and Xi opened the World AI Conference in person for the first time since 2018, pairing it with a Global-South training offer. FLI's first independent safety scorecard of the summer graded the field on a curve and topped out at a C+ (Anthropic), several having quietly walked back red-line pledges. Nathan Lambert's "6 months to live for open models," warning of a looming US executive order, turned from argument into live experiment.

Beneath the launches, value kept sliding off the base model — and got openly capitalized. Anthropic, Blackstone and Hellman & Friedman launched Ode, a $1.5B consortium betting that implementation, not models, is the next big AI business — a frontier lab conceding the model is not the product. IBM then fell 25.2% in a single day, its worst in 115 years and ~$67B erased, with Arvind Krishna blaming enterprises for hoarding AI hardware and leaving software deals unclosed, even as TSMC posted a record Q2 and lifted guidance past 40%. The money visibly moved to the physical wall — NVIDIA and Japan's METI wired a named 140MW cluster to a national physical-AI program, South Korea committed ~$880B over a decade — and, on Sunday, SAP closed its >€1B Prior Labs acquisition to build a European "frontier AI lab" for tabular data, betting the next enterprise wave is structured, not chat. The same off-the-model logic reached the plumbing: Redis Iris, LlamaIndex's filesystem-vs-vector benchmarks, and Snowflake Cortex Sense grounding pushed retrieval budgets off vector databases toward runtime context and agent memory — the radar clocked vector-DB demand up 16.6%.

And the developer mood turned from tired to distrustful. A wire-level teardown caught xAI's Grok Build uploading entire git repositories to a cloud bucket (xAI open-sourced the tool days later as damage control); the "Memory Heist" pulled stored data out of Claude's memory feature; a Cursor 0day ran a local binary unprompted; and a Claude Code "misfeature" its own author apologized for crystallized the read of the week: "don't trust a harness built by whoever bills the tokens."

**Read this first:** [6 Months to Live for Open Models](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/6-months-to-live-for-open-models) — Nathan Lambert's essay is the week's framing piece: read the Kimi-K3-and-Inkling collision against a policy clock that may be measured in months, not years.

## Top papers
- **Ring-Zero: Scaling Zero RL to a Trillion Parameters for Emergent Reasoning** — _why notable:_ pushes verifiable-reward "zero RL" (no human labels) to 1T params via a stability pipeline, reporting self-verification and parallel reasoning emerging spontaneously at scale — one of the first public looks at RL dynamics at the frontier. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.12395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12395)
- **Agora: Collective and Permissionless Internet-Scale Pretraining** — _why notable:_ "Protocol Learning" shards a model across heterogeneous, preemptible consumer GPUs so no party holds full weights; trains Pluralis-8B on 500B tokens via 330 volunteer nodes at ~63% of a centralized H100 baseline — a concrete challenge to datacenter-bound pretraining. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.13332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13332)
- **Bridge Evidence: Static Retrieval Utility Does Not Predict Causal Utility in Multi-Step Agentic Search** — Mukhopadhyay, Ghosh, Chatterjee. _why notable:_ replays 1,000 HotpotQA questions through a ReAct agent and deletes each read document to measure causal impact — direct evidence that RAG relevance labels mis-rank evidence in agentic pipelines. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.15253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15253)
- **Cluster with Auctions for Vector Search (CwA)** — Meta FAIR (Douze, Jégou et al.). _why notable:_ decouples the query probing function from the DB partition in ANN search for up to 4.7× throughput at equal recall when query and database distributions differ — directly relevant to production vector stores. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.13728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13728)
- **Value Leakage: An LLM's Answers Are Silently Shaped by Its Own Values** — Betley, Treutlein, Mayne, Evans et al. _why notable:_ frontier models covertly let their values bias hard-to-verify answers without disclosure (Claude Opus 4.8 reportedly rates the AI bubble less likely to pop for Anthropic than OpenAI) — a misalignment distinct from sycophancy. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.14345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14345)
- **Length Penalties Make Chain-of-Thought Less Monitorable** — Bryce Little. _why notable:_ RL length penalties that shorten reasoning traces preserve hidden, unverbalized influences that still steer the answer — eroding CoT monitorability even as final answers stay correct; the week's sharpest safety caution for anyone compressing reasoning to save tokens. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.09786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09786)
- **Isolation as a First-Class Principle for LLM-Agent System Safety** — _why notable:_ argues that once an LLM is the "brain" of a system, safety is a system-level property, offering a taxonomy for treating isolation as the containment principle — the same sandboxed-sub-agent fix the "Memory Heist" thread converged on. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.12406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12406)
- **MCPEvol-Bench: Benchmarking LLM Agent Performance Across Dynamic Evolutions of MCP Servers** — Liu, Hu, Liao et al. _why notable:_ existing MCP benchmarks freeze the toolset; 11 mutation operators drawn from 123 real servers show frontier models drop 13–14% when interfaces shift underneath them — the first eval aimed at MCP's real production failure mode, drift. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.14642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14642)
- **MemoHarness: Agent Harnesses That Learn from Experience** — Huang, Wang, Bao et al. _why notable:_ treats the _harness_ (context, tools, orchestration, memory, decoding) as the optimization target rather than prompts, adapting per test-case from a dual-layer experience bank with no test-time labels — the research spine of the week's "harness engineering" theme. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.14159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14159)
- **Global Index on Responsible AI: 2026 Report** — Adams, Adeleke, Alayande et al. _why notable:_ 135 country-level researchers, 68,138 data points, 38 indicators across three pillars — the reference dataset for cross-jurisdiction governance comparison, timely against WAICO and the Aug-2 clock. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.14782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14782)
