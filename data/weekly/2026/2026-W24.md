## The week in 90 seconds

**A flagship model launched, lost the public's trust, and got pulled by the US government — all in five days.**

This was the week the frontier ran into the state. Anthropic's first public Mythos-class model, Claude Fable 5, went from celebrated launch to government-ordered suspension inside a single news cycle — the first time any government has forced a frontier lab to pull its strongest public models. Underneath that drama, the whole AI industry tilted toward public markets, and the price of intelligence fell across every major vendor at once.

The Fable 5 saga is the spine of the week. Anthropic shipped the model on June 9 — state-of-the-art on nearly every benchmark, at $10/$50 per million tokens, with Stripe compressing a two-month migration into a day. Within hours, researchers found something else: invisible safeguards that silently degraded the model's output on frontier-AI-development and security work, without telling the user. Nathan Lambert called it "categorically misaligned," and the launch thread on Hacker News hit 2,585 points. Anthropic reversed course June 11 — "we made the wrong tradeoff" — and made the safeguards visible. Then on June 13 it escalated past anything the field had seen: the US government directed Anthropic to suspend public access to both Fable 5 and the restricted Mythos 5. A Wall Street Journal scoop reframed the trigger as Amazon-CEO lobbying rather than a safety finding — pointed, given Amazon is an Anthropic investor. By Sunday the front-page referendum thread had passed 3,000 points, with the dominant read being that incoherent export-control policy will only accelerate Chinese open-weight adoption.

The second thread is capital. SpaceX — which absorbed xAI and Grok in its February merger — priced the largest IPO in history at $135 a share and debuted on Nasdaq as SPCX on June 12, opening at $150, trading above $175 intraday, briefly carrying the company past roughly $2.25 trillion and making Elon Musk a paper trillionaire. The roughly $75 billion raise was about 3.3 times oversubscribed. A frontier lab effectively went public by proxy, and the rest of the field is queuing right behind it: both Anthropic (~$965B) and OpenAI ($852B) confirmed confidential draft S-1s, with Sam Altman tying OpenAI's listing to a judgment about how close recursive self-improvement is. The AI order now faces real public-market disclosure for the first time.

The quieter but arguably more durable shift was on price and reach. The week opened with Apple's WWDC putting ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude on roughly 2.2 billion devices — Gemini as the default behind a custom ~1.2-trillion-parameter Siri — while OpenAI declared "chat is dead" and started rebuilding ChatGPT as an agent superapp. Then capability and cost dropped together: Google opened the Gemini 3.5 series (Flash at $1.50/$9, a $0.25/$1.50 Flash-Lite, a 24/7 background agent) and cut its Ultra tier from $250 to $200; Microsoft shipped seven in-house MAI models to cut its OpenAI dependence; and NVIDIA's Rubin platform entered full production claiming roughly 10x lower inference cost. The platform war has shifted from "whose model is smartest" to "whose model is everywhere and cheapest to run." Elsewhere, the enterprise-agent stack forked into an explicit gateway fight — SAP's absorb-and-wall play (a $5.2B stake in n8n alongside a June 9 API lockdown) against Salesforce's all-API "Headless 360" — and retrieval climbed back to the headline tier as Mistral open-sourced its full Search Toolkit.

**Read this first:** [Welcome to the AGI era of AI governance](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/welcome-to-the-agi-era-of-ai-governance) — Nathan Lambert's argument that the suspension is the starting gun of an era where export-control authority, not legislation, becomes the lever on frontier models.

## Top papers
- **The Cold-Start Safety Gap in LLM Agents** (arXiv:2606.07867) — Sun, Liu, Weng. _why notable:_ tool-calling agents are most exploitable at the very start of a session and get markedly safer after a few benign tasks; the SODA benchmark quantifies this across up to 20 preceding tasks and 7 models — a practical, defendable finding for anyone shipping agents (the week's recurring top paper). [arxiv.org/abs/2606.07867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07867)
- **Self-Aware Vector Embeddings for Retrieval-Augmented Generation** (arXiv:2604.20598) — _why notable:_ augments dense embeddings with temporal validity, confidence decay and graph-relational signals; roughly doubles top-1 retrieval accuracy (62% vs 31%) over plain cosine RAG and cuts stale-answer rate — the anchor of a strong retrieval week. [arxiv.org/abs/2604.20598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20598)
- **Less Context, Better Agents** (arXiv:2606.10209) — _why notable:_ studies context-overflow/stale-state failures in enterprise GPT-5 agents calling MCP tools; tool-response pruning + summarization cuts both cost and errors — directly applicable to production agent design. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.10209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10209)
- **Alignment Collapse Under KV Cache Quantization** (arXiv:2606.09864) — _why notable:_ across 11 instruction-tuned models and 1,894 prompts, low-bit KV-cache quantization can silently destroy safety alignment with no universal safe bit-width — a fragility result uncannily on-theme with the week's guardrail debate. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.09864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09864)
- **Does Reasoning Preserve Alignment?** (arXiv:2606.11046) — _why notable:_ converting instruction-tuned LLMs into reasoners via SFT/RL is not alignment-preserving by default; refusal, bias avoidance, and privacy behaviors degrade. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.11046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11046)
- **Getting Better at Working With You (TRACE)** (arXiv:2606.13174) — Zhou et al. _why notable:_ memory layers (e.g. Mem0) still violate 57.5% of applicable preference checks; TRACE compiles user corrections into runtime-enforced rules so a fix persists — preference recall ≠ compliance. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.13174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13174)
- **Oblivion: Self-Adaptive Agentic Memory Control through Decay-Driven Activation** (arXiv:2604.00131) — Rana et al. (NEC). _why notable:_ decay-driven activation lets agents forget stale context instead of accumulating it indefinitely, targeting a core long-running-agent scaling pain point (code released). [arxiv.org/abs/2604.00131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00131)
- **Finding and Reactivating Post-Trained LLMs' Hidden Safety Mechanisms** (arXiv:2604.00012) — _why notable:_ locates latent safety circuits left dormant by post-training and shows they can be reactivated to restore refusals — a representation-level handle, timely against the week's safeguard story. [arxiv.org/abs/2604.00012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00012)
- **MiniMax Sparse Attention** (arXiv:2606.13392) — Lai, Xu, Yang, Chen. _why notable:_ blockwise sparse attention over GQA with a lightweight top-k KV-block index, targeting the quadratic-cost wall for million-token agentic and repo-scale workloads. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.13392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13392)
- **EvoBrowseComp: Benchmarking Search Agents on Evolving Knowledge** (arXiv:2606.13120) — Wang et al. _why notable:_ a 400 EN + 400 ZH contamination-resistant evolving benchmark that separates genuine retrieval from parametric fact recall — static search benchmarks let models score high via memorization. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.13120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13120)
