## The week in 90 seconds

**The week opens with distribution, not benchmarks, as the new AI battleground**

Week 24 is one day old, so its arc is Monday's arc — and Monday handed the industry its biggest distribution event of the year. The throughline already visible: the frontier fight has moved from "whose model is smartest" to "whose model is everywhere," and three of the largest tech platforms made that pivot on the very same day.

The defining move came from Apple. At Tim Cook's final WWDC keynote, Apple rebuilt Siri on a custom ~1.2-trillion-parameter Gemini licensed from Google for a reported ~$1B/year, and shipped an Extensions system that lets users pick ChatGPT, Gemini (the default), or Claude to power Apple Intelligence — with heavy reasoning kept on Apple's Private Cloud Compute. In a single keynote, Apple put three rival frontier labs in front of roughly 2.2 billion devices, and made Gemini a default at a scale no model has ever reached. Microsoft Build echoed the logic the same day: Foundry finalized at 11,000-plus models under one Azure endpoint, with a Claude option landing inside Excel Agent Mode and its ~750M users — even as Microsoft claimed its own MAI-Thinking-1 beat Claude Sonnet 4.6 in vendor-run blind evals.

Underneath the distribution land-grab, the product shape of AI changed too. OpenAI told the FT that "chat is dead," confirming its biggest ChatGPT overhaul since launch: a "superapp" bundling coding, agents, and partner apps like Canva and Booking.com, rolling out in the coming weeks ahead of an expected IPO. It is the explicit pivot from chat UI to agent-as-product, and it reframes OpenAI's consumer surface as a business-client acquisition channel rather than a question-answering box. The same impulse — moving from answering questions to completing tasks — showed up in the plumbing, where ServiceNow launched Action Fabric at Knowledge 2026 with a GA Model Context Protocol Server and 30 new AI Control Tower integrations across AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, SAP, Oracle, and Workday, SAP doubled n8n's valuation to $5.2B to embed agent orchestration into Joule Studio by Q3, and Anthropic shipped ten ready-to-run financial-services agent templates. MCP has graduated from spec to production default, and the enterprise action layer is visibly consolidating.

The regulatory clock is the week's quiet counterweight. The bulk of the EU AI Act's high-risk and GPAI obligations bite on August 2 (55 days out), carrying fines up to €35M or 7% of turnover for the largest models; Colorado's Consumer Protections for AI Act takes effect June 30 as the first real US state enforcement deadline; and Switzerland confirmed it will ratify the Council of Europe AI Convention and adapt sector law rather than pass a horizontal regulation. Security caught up in parallel: OpenAI shipped ChatGPT Lockdown Mode, the first mainstream consumer hardening against prompt-injection exfiltration, which Simon Willison rates highly. With six days of the week still to come, watch whether the distribution story draws a counter-move from Google or Meta, and whether OpenAI's superapp ships on schedule.

## Top papers
- **Think Fast: Estimating No-CoT Task-Completion Time Horizons of Frontier AI Models** — Gould, Ward, Woodruff, Arike et al. _why notable:_ measures how far frontier models reason internally without explicit chain-of-thought across 30,000+ questions / 43 benchmarks — bears directly on whether CoT monitoring remains a viable safety oversight mechanism. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.07157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07157)
- **Attack Selection in Agentic AI Control Evaluations Meaningfully Decreases Safety** — Ge-Wang et al. _why notable:_ red-teamers who strategically choose *when* to attack defeat trusted-monitor AI-control protocols far more than indiscriminate attackers — current control evals overstate safety. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.06529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06529)
- **OpenSkill: Open-World Self-Evolution for LLM Agents** — Yan, Song, Zhang, Liang et al. _why notable:_ an agent framework that builds both its skills *and* its own verification signals from scratch given only a task prompt — no curated skills, trajectories, or verifiers required. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.06741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06741)
- **Declarative Skills for AI Agents in Knowledge-Grounded Tool-Use Workflows** — Lim et al. _why notable:_ empirical case that natural-language "skill files" appended to the system prompt beat imperative state machines for orchestrating tool-using agents over unstructured KBs — direct support for the declarative-agent paradigm. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.06923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06923)
- **HKVM-RAG: Key-Value-Separated Hypergraph Evidence Organization for Multi-Hop RAG** — Zhang, Ma. _why notable:_ organizes retrieved text into answer-path hyperedges rather than independently-scored passages, exposing multi-hop evidence chains under fixed retrieval budgets. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.07218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07218)
- **Towards Retrieving Interaction Spaces for Agentic Search** — Zhuang et al. _why notable:_ direct-corpus-interaction search (grep/file-read over raw corpora) doesn't scale; proposes retrieving bounded "interaction spaces" to keep agentic-search latency manageable as corpora grow. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.06880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06880)
- **AdMem: Advanced Memory for Task-solving Agents** — Wang et al. _why notable:_ a unified, automatic memory framework integrating semantic + procedural memory that handles failure cases and online scalability — addresses the "replay past successes only" limit of prior agent memory. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.06787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06787)
- **Act As a Real Researcher: Benchmarks Evaluating Frontier LLMs and Agentic Harnesses in the Research Lifecycle** — Wang et al. _why notable:_ probes where autonomous research agents still fail — field sensitivity, research ethics, nuanced scientific judgment — beyond the coding tasks they already do well. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.07462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07462)
