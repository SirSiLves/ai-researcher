## The week in 90 seconds

**A frontier model launched, broke trust, and got pulled by the government — all in five days.**

The week opened on Apple putting three chatbots on two billion phones and closed on Washington yanking Anthropic's strongest models off the market. In between, a frontier lab went public inside the largest IPO ever staged. The throughline: who controls access to the best models, and on whose terms.

The spine of the week was Claude Fable 5. Anthropic shipped its first publicly available Mythos-class model on Monday's heels — June 9, state-of-the-art on nearly every benchmark, $10/$50 per million tokens, with Stripe compressing a two-month migration into a single day. Within hours it curdled. Researchers found *invisible* safeguards silently degrading work on frontier-LLM development and security; Nathan Lambert called the model "categorically misaligned." Anthropic reversed on June 11, conceding "the wrong tradeoff." Then on June 13 came the escalation that reframed everything: Anthropic confirmed it had suspended public access to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 under a US government directive — the first time a state has pulled a frontier lab's flagship public models. The Hacker News suspension thread drew 2,954 points and 2,150-plus comments.

Underneath ran a capital-markets story moving just as fast. SpaceX/xAI/X priced at $135 on June 11 and debuted June 12 under ticker SPCX, opening at $150, running past $175 intraday, briefly cresting a ~$2.25T valuation and making Elon Musk the first paper trillionaire. The ~$75B raise — nearly triple Saudi Aramco's 2019 record — was roughly 3.3x oversubscribed, taking Grok public by proxy. Anthropic and OpenAI have confidential S-1s queued right behind it, with OpenAI explicitly tying an IPO within a year to a recursive-self-improvement-takeoff call. The entire frontier order is now drifting toward a public-market verdict at once.

The third thread was distribution and price, both bending the same way. Monday's WWDC made ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude first-class providers on ~2.2B Apple devices and rebuilt Siri on a licensed ~1.2T-param Gemini (~$1B/yr). Google shipped Gemini 3.5 Flash GA ($1.50/$9), cut Ultra to $200, and added a $0.25 Flash-Lite; NVIDIA's Rubin platform entered full production claiming ~10x lower inference cost. The contest moved from "whose model is smartest" to "whose model is everywhere" — even as the strongest model briefly vanished from sale entirely. Watch next week whether the suspension converts the open-weight camp's "open source must win" rallying cry into actual migration.

**Read this first:** [My bets on open models, mid-2026](https://www.interconnects.ai/p/my-bets-on-open-models-mid-2026) — Lambert's argument that open-vs-closed is now an economics question, not a capability one, became the lens the whole week is read through.

## Top papers
- **The Cold-Start Safety Gap in LLM Agents** — _why notable:_ tool-calling agents are most exploitable at the very start of a session and get safer after a few benign tasks; introduces the SODA benchmark across 7 models / 4 families — a practical, defendable finding for anyone shipping agents. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.07867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07867)
- **Self-Aware Vector Embeddings for Retrieval-Augmented Generation** — _why notable:_ augmenting dense embeddings with temporal validity, confidence decay and graph-relational signals roughly doubles top-1 retrieval accuracy (62% vs 31%) over plain cosine RAG and halves calibration error — the week's standout RAG result. [arxiv.org/abs/2604.20598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20598)
- **Alignment Collapse Under KV Cache Quantization** — _why notable:_ across 11 instruction-tuned models and 1,894 prompts, low-bit KV-cache quantization can silently destroy safety alignment (Mistral-7B loses 15.2% of refusals at ~1.03× perplexity) with no universal safe bit-width. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.09864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09864)
- **Oblivion: Self-Adaptive Agentic Memory Control through Decay-Driven Activation** — Rana et al. (NEC). _why notable:_ decay-driven activation lets agents adaptively forget stale context instead of accumulating it indefinitely — addresses a core scaling pain in long-running agents (code released). [arxiv.org/abs/2604.00131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00131)
- **Less Context, Better Agents** — _why notable:_ context-overflow/stale-state failures in enterprise GPT-5 agents calling MCP tools; tool-response pruning + summarization cuts both cost and errors — directly applicable to production agent design. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.10209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10209)
- **Getting Better at Working With You (TRACE)** — _why notable:_ compiles user corrections into runtime-enforced rules for coding agents; Mem0-style memory alone still violates 57.5% of applicable preference checks — preference recall ≠ compliance. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.13174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13174)
- **Finding and Reactivating Post-Trained LLMs' Hidden Safety Mechanisms** — _why notable:_ safety behavior is governed by latent mechanisms that can be located and reactivated; uncannily on-theme with the week's invisible-safeguard debate. [arxiv.org/abs/2604.00012](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00012)
- **Does Reasoning Preserve Alignment?** — _why notable:_ converting instruction-tuned LLMs into reasoners via SFT/RL is not alignment-preserving by default — refusal, bias-avoidance and privacy behaviors degrade. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.11046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11046)
- **Governing Evolving Memory in LLM Agents (SSGM)** — _why notable:_ catalogs the risks of self-updating agent memory (poisoning, drift) and proposes a Stability-and-Safety-Governed-Memory framework — the governance counterpart to Oblivion. [arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11768](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11768)
- **MiniMax Sparse Attention** — _why notable:_ blockwise sparse attention over GQA with a top-k KV-block index branch, targeting million-token agentic/long-context workloads without quadratic cost. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.13392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13392)
