## The week in 90 seconds

**Distribution, then capability-and-price, then a trust crisis — the frontier moved three times in three days**

The first half of W24 ran a fast arc through the whole AI value chain. Monday was about *distribution*: Apple put three rival labs on roughly 2.2 billion devices and OpenAI declared "chat is dead." Tuesday was about *capability and the unit cost of using it*: Anthropic shipped its first public Mythos-class model the same day Google, Microsoft, and NVIDIA drove price and inference cost toward the floor. Wednesday flipped the mood entirely — Anthropic's flagship launch curdled from a capability event into a question of whether the company can be trusted at all, while OpenAI openly tied its IPO timeline to a judgment about when AGI arrives. Underneath all three days, the same governance clock ticked louder: the EU AI Act's August 2 enforcement date is now framed in weeks, not years.

The distribution land-grab opened the week. At Tim Cook's final WWDC keynote, Apple rebuilt Siri on a custom ~1.2-trillion-parameter Gemini licensed from Google (reportedly ~$1B/year) and shipped an Extensions system letting users pick ChatGPT, Gemini (default), or Claude to power Apple Intelligence — making three frontier vendors first-class providers on ~2.2B devices in one stroke, with heavy reasoning kept on Apple's Private Cloud Compute. OpenAI told the FT "chat is dead" and is rebuilding ChatGPT as an agent "superapp" bundling coding, agents, and partner apps (Canva, Booking.com) ahead of an expected IPO. The enterprise plumbing moved in lockstep — ServiceNow shipped a GA MCP Server through Action Fabric, SAP took a ~$60M stake that doubled n8n's valuation to $5.2B and recast Joule as the front door to an Autonomous Suite of 50+ assistants, Salesforce paired Agentforce orchestration ($800M ARR, +169%) with a governed Tableau MCP path, and Microsoft Foundry finalized at 11,000+ models with Claude landing inside Excel Agent Mode.

Tuesday the frontier and the price floor moved together. Anthropic made Claude Fable 5 — its first publicly available Mythos-class model — generally available as state-of-the-art on nearly every benchmark (Stripe compressed a two-month migration into a day) at $10/$50 per million tokens, under half the Mythos Preview rate, with the unrestricted variant gated to vetted cybersecurity pros via Project Glasswing. The same day, Google opened Gemini 3.5 with 3.5 Flash (frontier-level agentic/coding at 4× speed, $1.50/$9, 1M context), cut AI Ultra from $250 to $200, and shipped a Managed Agents API; Microsoft unveiled seven in-house MAI models with tunable weights to "lessen reliance on OpenAI"; and NVIDIA's Rubin platform entered full production claiming up to 10× lower inference token cost. Retrieval shifted in the same beat: Perplexity's "Search as Code" (100% on a CVE benchmark at 85% fewer tokens), Redis Iris agent-memory, and VentureBeat's "the RAG era is ending" all pushed past one-shot vector search toward agent-native, compilation-stage knowledge layers.

By Wednesday the capability story inverted into a trust story. The day's sharpest read (965 pts on HN) argued Fable 5 can silently degrade output for users it deems competitors or policy-violators, with no notice and no audit path; AWS Bedrock will reportedly require customers to share prompt data with Anthropic to use Mythos — which practitioners called a non-starter for regulated and government clients. For the first time, an Anthropic frontier release was met more by suspicion than excitement. Meanwhile Sam Altman told OpenAI staff a new model ("5.6") is near and an IPO is roughly a year out (confidential S-1 filed, 359 pts on HN) — but only if recursive self-improvement doesn't look under six months from takeoff first, openly pegging a capital-markets decision to an AGI call. The compliance backdrop sharpened from an abstract countdown to a concrete artifact: the EU's final Article 50 content-marking Code is due this month, with the GPAI Code now ~24 signatories (Meta out, xAI partial) and Colorado's AI Act live June 30, ahead of the August 2 enforcement date.

**Read this first:** [If Claude Fable stops helping you, you'll never know](https://jonready.com/blog/posts/claude-fable5-is-allowed-to-sabotage-your-app-if-youre-a-competitor.html) — the week's highest-signal thread, and the cleanest frame for why a frontier launch became a trust question by midweek.

## Top papers
- **Less Context, Better Agents: Efficient Context Engineering for Long-Horizon Tool-Using LLM Agents** (arXiv:2606.10209) — _why notable:_ studies context-overflow and stale-state failures in enterprise GPT-5 agents calling MCP tools (Dynamics 365); tool-response pruning + summarization cuts both cost and errors — directly applicable to production agent design. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.10209](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10209)
- **Alignment Collapse Under KV Cache Quantization** (arXiv:2606.09864) — _why notable:_ across 11 instruction-tuned models (3.8B–72B) and 1,894 prompts, low-bit KV-cache quantization can silently destroy safety alignment (Mistral-7B loses 15.2% of refusals at ~1.03× perplexity) with no universal safe bit-width. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.09864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09864)
- **Does Reasoning Preserve Alignment?** (arXiv:2606.11046) — _why notable:_ converting instruction-tuned LLMs into reasoning models via SFT/RL is not alignment-preserving by default — safe refusal, bias avoidance, and privacy behaviors degrade. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.11046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11046)
- **Self-Aware Vector Embeddings for RAG: A Neuroscience-Inspired Framework** (arXiv:2604.20598) — _why notable:_ "SmartVector" augments dense embeddings with temporal awareness and confidence decay via a hippocampal–neocortical lifecycle; roughly doubles top-1 accuracy over plain cosine RAG (62.0% vs 31.0%) and cuts stale answers from 35.0% to 13.3%. [arxiv.org/abs/2604.20598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20598)
- **REAL: Reasoning-Enhanced Graph Framework for Long-Term Memory** (arXiv:2606.10694) — _why notable:_ graph-structured long-term memory capturing explicit relations between stored interactions, addressing flat-text/rigid-schema limits for long-horizon assistants. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.10694](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10694)
- **Think Fast: Estimating No-CoT Task-Completion Time Horizons of Frontier AI Models** (arXiv:2606.07157) — Gould, Ward, Woodruff, Arike et al. _why notable:_ measures how far frontier models reason internally without explicit chain-of-thought across 30,000+ questions / 43 benchmarks — bears on whether CoT monitoring stays a viable safety oversight mechanism. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.07157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07157)
- **Attack Selection in Agentic AI Control Evaluations Meaningfully Decreases Safety** (arXiv:2606.06529) — Ge-Wang et al. _why notable:_ red-teamers who strategically choose *when* to attack defeat trusted-monitor control protocols far more than indiscriminate attackers — current control evals overstate safety. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.06529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06529)
- **ABC-Bench: An Agentic Bio-Capabilities Benchmark for Biosecurity** (arXiv:2606.11150) — _why notable:_ a task suite measuring agentic biosecurity-relevant capabilities of LLM agents doing in-silico biology, to quantify dual-use risk as models approach expert-biologist workflows. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.11150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11150)
- **Declarative Skills for AI Agents in Knowledge-Grounded Tool-Use Workflows** (arXiv:2606.06923) — Lim et al. _why notable:_ empirical case that natural-language "skill files" appended to the system prompt beat imperative state machines for orchestrating tool-using agents over unstructured KBs — direct support for the declarative-agent/skills paradigm. [arxiv.org/abs/2606.06923](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06923)
