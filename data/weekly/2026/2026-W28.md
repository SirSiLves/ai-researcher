## The week in 90 seconds

**Six frontier models shipped in seven days — then the scoreboards deflated the launch pages**

The release queue emptied all at once, and independent benchmarks arrived a beat later to complicate every claim. The quieter, more durable signal ran underneath: value is migrating off the model and onto the orchestration layer that wraps it. And the Aug 2 EU AI Act cliff, now three weeks out, turned governance from advice into enforceable liability.

By Thursday afternoon three Western labs had put new public models out within hours of each other. GPT-5.6 — Sol, Terra and Luna — finished a 13-day, government-coordinated preview and went full GA across ChatGPT, the API and Codex; xAI shipped the "Opus-class" Grok 4.5 at $2/$6; OpenAI launched full-duplex GPT-Live voice; Meta released Muse Spark 1.1 with its own Muse Image and Video; and Cognition, Mistral, NVIDIA and Google's Gemma 4 all followed, with DeepSeek V4 reaching GA. Then the scoreboards landed and cooled the copy: Sol scored 64.6% on SWE-Bench Pro, behind Opus (69.2%) and Fable (80%), while Grok 4.5 took the top agentic-tool-use slot yet roughly doubled its hallucination rate to 54%. Hacker News answered with "release fatigue" and "LLM burnout," and "evaluation gap" became the radar's fastest-rising theme. A launch-day number is no longer something you can trust on sight.

The structural story ran the other way, and it came from the companies closest to enterprise budgets. ServiceNow began retiring its in-house Now LLM, making Azure OpenAI, AWS Claude and Google Gemini the default behind Now Assist — a large workflow vendor conceding that third-party frontier models beat its own. Snowflake's Cortex Sense grounding lifted agent accuracy from 24% to 86% while cutting per-query cost from $1.76 to $0.59; OpenAI folded Chat, Work and Codex into one agentic "super app"; Microsoft stood up a $2.5B Frontier Company; and SambaNova closed a $1B round with JPMorganChase as an on-prem inference partner. Open weights forced the same price war into the open — Z.ai's GLM-5.2 (62.1 on SWE-bench Pro at roughly a sixth of the cost) drove the week's loudest developer thread, "the coming AI margin collapse," while DeepSeek's peak-hour pricing and Meta's $1.25/$4.25 Muse Spark undercut everyone. The base model is commoditizing; the margin now accrues to whoever grounds, governs and distributes it.

Governance hardened from guidance into enforceable liability. The EU AI Act's Aug 2 obligations for high-risk and general-purpose models — fines up to €35M or 7% of turnover — are now roughly three weeks out, and the scaffolding filled in daily: an EU Cybersecurity-and-AI Action Plan, the Article 50 Transparency Code Opinion with signatory forms due July 22, and Germany handing the Bundesnetzagentur oversight of state and municipal AI. xAI simply deferred Grok 4.5's EU launch, citing conformity review. Alongside it, Amazon security chief Stephen Schmidt's "human in the loop is governance theater" — backed by 26,000 internal agents and more than 360,000 authorizations a week — rhymed with an Institutional Red-Teaming paper finding that deployment rules, not model choice, decide multi-agent safety, even as Claude Code flipped its default to manual permissions.

At the edges, the agent plumbing both hardened and showed its seams: MCP's Enterprise-Managed Authorization reached stable even as the GitLost disclosure tricked GitHub's AI agent into leaking private repositories. And the talent war reached court — Apple sued OpenAI over alleged trade-secret theft by ex-employees, the week's single biggest Hacker News thread. What to watch next: the final fortnight before Aug 2, and whether more launches follow Grok out of Europe.

**Read this first:** [The Art of Loop Engineering](https://www.langchain.com/blog/the-art-of-loop-engineering) — the clearest statement of the week's real theme: the leverage in agents now lives in the orchestration you build around the model, not in the model itself.

## Top papers
- **LLM-as-a-Verifier** — Kwok, Liu, Finn, Stoica, Mirhoseini et al. _Why notable:_ frames verification as a new scaling axis (continuous scores beat discrete LM-judge); SOTA on Terminal-Bench V2 (86.5%) and SWE-Bench Verified (78.2%), ships a Claude Code extension. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.05391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05391)
- **Gemma 4 Technical Report** — Google DeepMind. _Why notable:_ the week's biggest open-model drop — a natively-multimodal family (2.3B–31B, dense + MoE), encoder-free 12B ingesting raw audio/image patches. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.02770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02770)
- **Institutional Red-Teaming: Deployment Rules, Not Just Models, Causally Shape Multi-Agent AI Safety** — Yujiao Chen. _Why notable:_ holds agents/objectives fixed and varies one deployment rule across 228 contexts / 33,924 games — the governance layer drives collective safety. The research echo of the week's "governance theater" argument. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.07695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07695)
- **The Harness Effect: How Orchestration Design Sets the Token Economics of Enterprise Agentic AI** — Sayed Ali, Novik et al. _Why notable:_ isolates the orchestration harness as the decisive cost lever via a controlled swap (22 tasks, six models, same tasks / different harness) — pairs with the enterprise spend-cap news. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.06906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06906)
- **Context Graphs for Proactive Enterprise Agents** — Kumar. _Why notable:_ argues RAG/agent stacks stay reactive; a live relational graph of enterprise entities and state lets agents surface what a worker needs before they ask. The retrieval read of the week. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.07721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07721)
- **Token-Flow Firewall: Semantic Runtime Auditing for Persistent AI Agents** — Wang et al. _Why notable:_ persistent state + reusable skills + tool calls create a far larger semantic attack surface than chat; adds a runtime firewall that audits natural-language token flows. Direct match to the GitLost disclosure. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.08395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08395)
- **When the Judge Changes, So Does the Measurement: Auditing LLM-as-Judge Reliability** — Yang et al. _Why notable:_ an LLM-judge score can move with candidate responses held fixed, simply because the evaluator changed — a measurement-validity problem feeding the "evaluation gap" riser. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.08535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08535)
- **WebSwarm: Recursive Multi-Agent Orchestration for Deep-and-Wide Web Search** — Song, Zhang, Dong, Dou et al. _Why notable:_ progressive recursive delegation (each search node solves or spawns children) beats single- and multi-agent baselines on BrowseComp-Plus, WideSearch and GISA. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.08662](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08662)
- **From Solvers to Research: LLM-Driven Formal Mathematics at the Research Frontier** — Jiang, Liang, … Terence Tao, Wei Wang. _Why notable:_ position paper arguing AI4Math must move from predefined theorem "solvers" to formal research agents on open conjectures — a sober counterweight to the week's viral "AI proved a conjecture" claim. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.07779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07779)
- **The Effectiveness of Natural Language Tools (14-model replication)** — _Why notable:_ NL tools beat structured tool-calling by +14.9pp accuracy and cut critical errors 93% while saving 25% tokens; gains largest for smaller/reasoning models. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.03953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03953)

## Best blog reads
- **[The Art of Loop Engineering](https://www.langchain.com/blog/the-art-of-loop-engineering)** — Sydney Runkle, LangChain. The leverage in agents is the loops you stack around the model (base, verification, event-driven, hill-climbing) — pushing reliability into the harness, not the prompt.
- **[Harness Engineering for Self-Improvement](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/)** — Lilian Weng. Recursive self-improvement begins with the harness — the software deciding how a model thinks, which tools it calls, what it remembers — not with a model rewriting its weights.
- **[Evaluation Patterns for Deep Agents in Production](https://www.langchain.com/blog/evaluation-patterns-for-deep-agents-in-production)** — LangChain. Shipping four production deep agents broke single-shot evaluation; five concrete patterns (per-datapoint assertions, single-step, full-turn, multi-turn conditional, reproducible envs) replace it.
- **[The Pentagon Designates Anthropic as a Supply Chain Risk](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/lawfare-daily--the-pentagon-designates-anthropic-as-a-supply-chain-risk)** — Goodwin & Salem, Lawfare. Uses the DoD designation to argue how dependent government and industry have become on a handful of frontier vendors.
- **[Threat Modeling the Model Context Protocol](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/threat-modeling-model-context-protocol-brett-crawley-pmbff)** — Brett Crawley. The sharpest practitioner case that MCP security must be per-deployment and deterministic — bound impact with hard boundaries first, then layer probabilistic guardrails.
- **[Why AI Infrastructure Must Evolve for Agent Experience](https://www.latent.space/p/modal2026)** — Akshat Bubna (Modal CTO), Latent.Space. Serving infra built for human-facing request/response breaks for agent workloads; the new target is fast cold starts, cheap sandboxes, stateful long-horizon execution.
- **[MCP Deep Dive, Part 3: Build a Production-Grade MCP Server From Scratch](https://medium.com/@randhir.jassal/mcp-deep-dive-part-3-build-a-production-grade-mcp-server-from-scratch-1debcc955888)** — Randhir Jassal. Genuinely hands-on: a multi-tenant server (~85k req/day) with a clean tool-error vs protocol-error model and no stack-trace leaks.
- **[The Death of Prompt Engineering, and How Evals Are Rising in Its Place](https://medium.com/design-bootcamp/the-death-of-prompt-engineering-and-how-evals-are-rising-in-its-place-f8467871a815)** — Yahav M. The prompt-engineer role is being displaced by the eval engineer — defining what "good" means and measuring it at scale with version-controlled rigor.
