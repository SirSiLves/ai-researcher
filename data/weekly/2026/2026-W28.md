## The week in 90 seconds

**Three labs shipped inside 48 hours — then the benchmarks and the lawyers took the floor**

The frontier blitz that peaked on Thursday was the loudest capability moment of the summer: GPT-5.6, Grok 4.5, and Claude Cowork all landing inside a single 48-hour window. But by Sunday the story had inverted — independent scoreboards, an enterprise stampede onto one tool standard, and Apple's trade-secret suit against OpenAI turned the question from "which model is strongest" to "who can you trust, and where does the money actually land." The week that opened as a capability race closed as a referendum on cost, trust, and where value accrues.

The week opened almost quietly and then detonated midweek. Peaking Thursday 07-09, OpenAI moved GPT-5.6 (Sol/Terra/Luna) to general availability across ChatGPT, API and Codex; xAI shipped Grok 4.5 as an "Opus-class" model at $2/$6, co-trained on real Cursor sessions; and Anthropic brought Claude Cowork to web and mobile with background agents — flanked by Cognition's SWE-1.7, OpenAI's full-duplex GPT-Live voice, Google's Gemma 4 report and DeepSeek V4's GA. Then the independent numbers complicated every launch page: OpenAI's Sol landed 64.6% on SWE-Bench Pro against Opus at 69.2% and Fable at 80%, while Grok posted the board's best agentic tool-use even as its hallucination rate doubled from 25% to 54%. Capability had become table stakes; "which benchmark?" was the only honest answer to "is it any good?" — and the evaluation gap became the radar's fastest-rising theme of the week.

Underneath the launches, the week's most durable shift was that value moved off the base model. ServiceNow retired its in-house Now LLM, defaulting Now Assist to Azure OpenAI, AWS Claude and Google Gemini; Snowflake's Cortex Sense grounding layer lifted agent accuracy from 24.1% to 86.3% while cutting per-query cost from $1.76 to $0.59; OpenAI folded Chat, Work and Codex into a single desktop "super app." Retrieval reorganized around the same logic, shifting from vector-only RAG toward hybrid retrieval and compile-stage knowledge layers as "context architecture is replacing RAG" became a named market call. Microsoft ($2.5B Frontier Company), AWS and Salesforce ($1B in Switzerland) all stood up last-mile deployment businesses, while Alberta's government ran roughly 50 Claude Code agents across 3,400 repositories to scan 466 million lines of code in 20 hours. And the plumbing consolidated: by Sunday ServiceNow, Salesforce, SAP and Snowflake had all converged on MCP, whose Enterprise-Managed Authorization reached "stable" — the enterprise tool layer stopped debating whether to expose itself to outside agents and simply picked the standard.

The counterweight kept its footing, and then crossed a line. Open weights held the price floor all week — Z.ai's ZCode on GLM-5.2 drove the loudest Hacker News thread, "GLM 5.2 and the coming AI margin collapse," sharpened by a developer who cancelled Claude Pro for $20 a month of OpenRouter credits — before the week closed with Meituan open-sourcing LongCat-2.0, a 1.6-trillion-parameter model trained and served entirely on domestic Chinese chips, the first trillion-parameter system built without NVIDIA hardware. The mood turned with it. Governance hardened from guidance into liability as the EU AI Act's enforcement clock closed to three weeks; "human in the loop is governance theater" became the week's most-cited argument; and by Sunday the single loudest developer thread was not a launch but Apple's trade-secret suit against OpenAI at 1,622 points, read widely as the end of OpenAI's hardware ambitions. The radar closed the week as a broad cool-down, nearly every cluster fading off the launch-week spike.

## Top papers
- **Institutional Red-Teaming: Deployment Rules, Not Just Models, Causally Shape Multi-Agent AI Safety** — Yujiao Chen. _Why notable:_ holds agents/objectives fixed and varies exactly one deployment rule across 33,924 games; the governance layer, not the model, drives collective safety — the research spine of the week's "governance theater" argument. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.07695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07695)
- **LLM-as-a-Verifier** — Kwok, Liu, Finn, Stoica, Mirhoseini et al. _Why notable:_ reframes verification as a scaling axis (continuous scores beat discrete LM-judge); SOTA on Terminal-Bench V2 (86.5%) and SWE-Bench Verified (78.2%), ships a Claude Code extension. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.05391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05391)
- **Gemma 4 Technical Report** — Google DeepMind. _Why notable:_ the week's biggest open-model drop — a natively multimodal family (2.3B–31B, dense + MoE) with an encoder-free 12B ingesting raw audio/image patches. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.02770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02770)
- **The Effectiveness of Natural Language Tools (14-model replication)** — _Why notable:_ NL tools beat structured tool-calling by +14.9pp accuracy and cut critical errors 93% while saving 25% tokens — gains largest for smaller/reasoning models. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.03953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03953)
- **Unicode TAG-Block Concealment of Tool-Metadata Payloads in MCP** — Rashidi. _Why notable:_ exposes an "approval-view fidelity gap" — nothing forces the metadata shown in the one-time MCP approval dialog to match the bytes injected each turn. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.05744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05744)
- **Context Graphs for Proactive Enterprise Agents** — Kumar. _Why notable:_ argues RAG/agent stacks stay reactive; a live relational graph of enterprise entities and state lets agents surface what a worker needs before they ask — the retrieval read of the week. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.07721](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07721)
- **Token-Flow Firewall: Semantic Runtime Auditing for Persistent AI Agents** — Wang et al. _Why notable:_ since most security-critical agent interactions travel as natural-language token flows, it adds a runtime firewall that semantically audits them — a direct match to the GitLost disclosure. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.08395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08395)
- **When the Judge Changes, So Does the Measurement** — Yang et al. _Why notable:_ frames evaluator-replacement drift as a measurement-validity problem — judge upgrades aren't interchangeable — feeding the week's "evaluation gap" concern. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.08535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08535)
- **The Harness Effect: How Orchestration Design Sets the Token Economics of Enterprise Agentic AI** — Sayed Ali, Novik et al. _Why notable:_ names "token maxing" and isolates the orchestration harness as the decisive cost lever via a controlled swap (22 tasks, six models, same tasks / different harness). [arxiv.org/abs/2607.06906](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06906)
- **Do You Need a Frontier Model as a Citation Verifier?** — Leung et al. _Why notable:_ across 1,248 human-reviewed rubric decisions cheaper judges stay competitive (GPT-5-mini tops source-relevance F1 at 0.908), but scalar F1 hides a directional bias an RL loop would reinforce — calibrate the judge first. [arxiv.org/abs/2607.08700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08700)
