## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**Shadow MCP: The Governance Gap Already Inside Your Agent Stack** — Kranthi Manchikanti (lang: en). Eighteen months after MCP launched, enterprises run thousands of servers reachable from agents without inventory, ownership, or sub-minute revocation; the author lays out a four-layer fix — discovery/inventory with tool-list hashing, a signed private registry, Enterprise-Managed Authorization (the Nov-2025 Okta-derived spec extension) for scoped short-lived IdP-issued tokens, and tool-call observability with hash-diff alerting on description rug-pulls. _Why interesting:_ The governance gap is real (10k+ servers, 97M monthly SDK downloads) and EMA is the single most-misunderstood control — treating it as a complete answer is the failure mode to flag now. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/shadow-mcp-governance-gap-already-inside-your-agent-manchikanti-n52ce/](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/shadow-mcp-governance-gap-already-inside-your-agent-manchikanti-n52ce/)

**THE AI OMNIBUS: EUROPE POSTPONES THE DEADLINE TO SAVE THE APPLICATION OF THE AI REGULATION** — Mathieu Gitton (lang: en, written from a French Brussels-policy POV). Detailed walk-through of the May 6-7 2026 provisional Omnibus VII deal: Annex III high-risk obligations slip to 2 Dec 2027, Annex I (machinery, lifts, toys) to 2 Aug 2028; Machinery Regulation gets carved out via delegated acts; synthetic-content labelling still hits 2 Dec 2026; Article 5 ban on nudification/CSAM-generation tools added; SME/small mid-cap reliefs extended; sandbox setup deadline pushed to 2 Aug 2027. _Why interesting:_ Most concrete public summary of what actually got agreed at trilogue and the residual gap (medical devices remain in the horizontal regime) — directly relevant for any compliance roadmap due before August. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-omnibus-europe-postpones-deadline-save-application-mathieu-gitton-smkde/](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-omnibus-europe-postpones-deadline-save-application-mathieu-gitton-smkde/)

**ISO/IEC 42001 and the EU AI Act: Certification is not the same as compliance** — Matej Jambrich, Dentons Europe CTO (lang: en). Argues that ISO/IEC 42001 is a management-system standard governing the organisation while the AI Act regulates specific actors and specific systems; certification cannot grant the AI Act's presumption of conformity (only harmonised standards listed in the OJEU can). Proposes a five-component overlay: classification gateway, system-level evidence file, AI-aware supplier control model, change-control trigger, board reporting layer. Also gives the cleanest public timeline of where the Omnibus stood after the failed 28 April trilogue. _Why interesting:_ Direct answer to a question every CISO and risk-committee is asking right now — and a defensible board-level framing. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/isoiec-42001-eu-ai-act-certification-same-compliance-matej-jambrich-fdjnc/](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/isoiec-42001-eu-ai-act-certification-same-compliance-matej-jambrich-fdjnc/)

**Bottom Up braucht Top Down** — Paul Meyrat (lang: de-CH, "KI in der Verwaltung" / Swiss public-sector). Plädiert für ein Sandwich-Modell: zitiert die OpenAI/Bain "Move from AI Experimentation to AI Transformation"-These (Top-Down) gegen McAfee (Bottom-Up) und argumentiert, dass beide Seiten zusammen funktionieren müssen — Management liefert sichere Enterprise-Tools, geschützte Sandboxes und psychologische Sicherheit, während Mitarbeitende ("Secret Cyborgs" nach Mollick) ihre versteckten Workflows offenlegen. Stützt sich auf Stanford "Enterprise AI Playbook" und HBR. _Why interesting:_ Sehr konkrete Verwaltungs-/Schweiz-Perspektive auf das Governance/Enablement-Spannungsfeld, mit guter Quellenkette. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/bottom-up-braucht-top-down-paul-meyrat-iki0e/](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/bottom-up-braucht-top-down-paul-meyrat-iki0e/)

[https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/einf%C3%BChrung-agents-und-subagents-claude-code-ralph-hutter-fivqe/](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/einf%C3%BChrung-agents-und-subagents-claude-code-ralph-hutter-fivqe/)

**Agents Don't Replace Integration. They Change Where Integration Happens** — Steve Harris (lang: en). Frames data warehouse / API / MCP / A2A as four complementary integration patterns rather than competitors: warehouse for trusted business info, APIs for repeatable transactions, MCP for governed agent-to-tool access, A2A for cross-capability agent delegation (procurement agent asks legal agent for contract review). Includes a practical decision model and warns against "accidental integration" where agents scrape/query without monitoring. _Why interesting:_ Cleanest mental model so far for slotting MCP and A2A into existing enterprise architecture without throwing out APIs. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agents-dont-replace-integration-change-where-happens-steve-harris-yfskc/](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agents-dont-replace-integration-change-where-happens-steve-harris-yfskc/)

**The Orchestrator–Worker Pattern: How Anthropic Built the World's Best Research Agent** — Joon Nyip Koh (lang: en). Walks through Anthropic's published recipe — Opus 4 as orchestrator, Sonnet 4 as parallel workers — explains the ~90% quality lift vs single-agent setups and the ~15x token-cost trade-off, and highlights the underrated "agents rewrite their own tool descriptions" capability (~40% time reduction). Maps the pattern's emergence across CrewAI, Claude Code, and OpenClaw. _Why interesting:_ Strong, opinionated explanation of why orchestrator-worker is becoming the default production pattern and what the CFO conversation looks like. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/orchestratorworker-pattern-how-anthropic-built-worlds-joon-nyip-koh-t0nmc/](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/orchestratorworker-pattern-how-anthropic-built-worlds-joon-nyip-koh-t0nmc/)

**Context Engineering: The Skill Quietly Reshaping Strategy Work** — Matt Keane (lang: en). Reframes "vibe coding" / "agentic AI" as context engineering: senior leaders briefing AI systems with constraints, customer dynamics, and prior analyses produce strategic artifacts in 90 minutes that previously needed six-month consulting engagements. Argues the alignment tax (strategy → requirements → architecture → code handoffs) collapses because business leaders carry context to AI directly; engineering's center of gravity moves up the stack to hardening and productisation. _Why interesting:_ Names a shift the user has been seeing in practice ("context engineering" as the durable term) and explains why the leadership-craft gap is widening fast. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/context-engineering-skill-quietly-reshaping-strategy-work-matt-wxpxe/](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/context-engineering-skill-quietly-reshaping-strategy-work-matt-wxpxe/)

**System Design Deep Dive: How to Design an Enterprise RAG Assistant (like NotionAI)** — Avani Chaskar, JPMorganChase SWE3 (lang: en). End-to-end reference architecture: Kafka-fed CDC ingestion from Notion/Slack/Jira/Drive, hierarchical parent-child chunking (256/1024 tokens), single-stage ACL filtering at the vector DB level (metadata-payload approach with allowed_users / allowed_groups), hybrid dense+BM25 search with cross-encoder reranking, SSE streaming. Calls out two real failure modes: "ghost chunks" after deletes (mitigated via PostgreSQL document→chunk mapping) and "all-hands hot partitions" (Redis cache keyed by query+ACL signature). _Why interesting:_ Production-grade, opinionated, not generic — the ACL-at-the-index-level argument is the one most enterprise RAG designs get wrong. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/system-design-deep-dive-how-enterprise-rag-assistant-like-chaskar-guysc/](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/system-design-deep-dive-how-enterprise-rag-assistant-like-chaskar-guysc/)
