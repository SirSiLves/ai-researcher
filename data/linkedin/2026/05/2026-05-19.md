## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**Shadow MCP: The Governance Gap Already Inside Your Agent Stack** — Kranthi Manchikanti (May 15). Argues MCP became the load-bearing integration primitive in 18 months and most enterprises cannot list reachable servers; lays out a four-layer fix (discovery + tool-list hashing, signed private registry, Enterprise-Managed Authorization via the IdP, observability with hash-diff alerts). Calls out the rug-pull pattern where a server ships a benign tool description at install and silently swaps it for a malicious one, and warns EMA is not a complete answer — it doesn't catch hostile descriptions or cross-server collusion. _Why interesting:_ concrete this-quarter playbook anchored to real spec changes (Okta Cross-App-Access → EMA, Nov 2025). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/shadow-mcp-governance-gap-already-inside-your-agent-manchikanti-n52ce]

**Why Most Enterprise MCP Architectures Are Already Broken** — Prashanth Kurimella, MuleSoft (May 16). Six anti-patterns from production MCP deployments: ship-it-first connectivity, conflating UI access with agent access, inconsistent auth across servers, 403-error storms because agents see tools they can't actually call, dumping thousands of records into context instead of routing to a specialist agent, and Franken-stacks held together by Zapier + .env keys. Closes with a MuleSoft Agent Fabric pitch but the diagnosis is sharper than the product section. _Why interesting:_ same enterprise-MCP-governance topic as Manchikanti from a different angle — the failure modes are practitioner-observed, not theoretical. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-most-enterprise-mcp-architectures-already-broken-kurimella-slfkc]

**EU AI Act Conformity Assessment: What Providers Must Do Before August 2026** — Tijana Zunic Maric, Whisperly (May 18). Procedural walk-through of Article 43: which high-risk systems go through internal Annex VI self-assessment vs notified-body Annex VII (only biometric ID and providers who can't fully apply harmonised standards), what Annex IV technical documentation actually requires, when a "substantial modification" forces a fresh assessment, and the post-assessment chain — Article 47 declaration, CE marking, EU database registration. Flags the harmonised-standards gap (CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 deliverables still incomplete in early 2026) as the place most providers will silently fall short. _Why interesting:_ operational reading of the rules on the same August 2 cliff edge everyone is now writing about. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-act-conformity-assessment-what-providers-must-do-zunic-maric-0dfof]

**The Quiet Quarter Before the Storm: Why AI Governance Just Became a Board-Level Problem** — ALIGNMT AI (May 16). Three pressures converging at once: EU AI Act high-risk obligations going live in August (~50 fines, ~€250M issued already pre-cutover), UK FCA/BoE pulling frontier AI into the Senior Managers and Certification Regime so individual approvers carry personal accountability, and SEC making AI governance + AI-washing the top 2026 examination priority. Adds a second front rarely discussed: investors are starting to ask the same gap-between-policy-and-practice questions regulators are — Amazon excluded a shareholder proposal asking the board to audit whether its AI deployment matches its Responsible AI Approach. _Why interesting:_ ties the SEC AI-washing posture to investor proposals as one continuous disclosure exposure rather than two separate stories. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/quiet-quarter-before-storm-why-ai-governance-just-became-board-level-toiye]

**Bottom Up braucht Top Down** — Paul Meyrat (lang: de, May 17). Swiss public-administration framing: argues against the OpenAI/Bain top-down vs. McAfee bottom-up dichotomy and proposes a sandwich model — leadership provides safe enterprise tools, sandboxes and psychological-safety culture; the base provides the real workflow redesign. Cites Mollick's "secret cyborgs" (nearly half of employees hide their AI use from management) and the micro-productivity trap (faster email-writing doesn't move the org if the surrounding process still has manual handoffs). _Why interesting:_ Swiss-German voice on a debate that's mostly framed by US labs — and a useful counter to the "buy a platform and you're done" story. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/bottom-up-braucht-top-down-paul-meyrat-iki0e]

**Meta vor Gericht: Wer kontrolliert Wissen im KI-Zeitalter?** — Tamedia (lang: de, May 18). Newsletter-style roundup framed around the Elsevier/Hachette/Macmillan/Cengage/McGraw-Hill suit against Meta in New York over Llama training on copyrighted books; the more interesting strategic content is what European publishers are doing in response — Mediahuis joining the SPUR coalition (FT, Guardian, Telegraph, BBC, Sky News) to work out shared "pay-per-crawl" / "pay-per-inference" standards, and Thomson Reuters' explicit licensing posture (archive only, short terms, premium price). Also covers Google's first documented AI-developed zero-day exploit caught by Threat Intelligence Group. _Why interesting:_ Swiss-German media-house lens on the licensing fight, with named European coalition activity that doesn't get much English-language coverage. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/meta-vor-gericht-wer-kontrolliert-wissen-im-ki-zeitalter-tamedia-khkpe]

**Agents Don't Replace Integration. They Change Where Integration Happens** — Steve Harris (May 14). Frames MCP and A2A as additional layers in the integration landscape rather than replacements for warehouses + APIs. Useful four-pattern decision model: warehouse for trusted reporting, API for repeatable specific transactions, MCP for governed agent access to tools/context, A2A only when one agent genuinely needs to delegate to another specialist agent's domain. Warns against "accidental integration" where every PoC defines its own agent-tool-access path and the org ends up with ungovernable scraping/querying webs. _Why interesting:_ business-side framing that won't let agents become the integration architecture itself. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agents-dont-replace-integration-change-where-happens-steve-harris-yfskc]

**Building Production-Ready RAG with Hybrid Search and Re-Ranking** — Saurabh Anand (May 15). Mid-depth practitioner walk-through: why pure vector search misses exact-match terms (product names, error codes, API names, version numbers), the BM25 + dense retrieval hybrid pattern, where re-ranking (Cross-Encoders, Cohere ReRank, BGE Reranker) actually moves the needle, and what to instrument (retrieval accuracy, latency, hallucination rate, token usage). _Why interesting:_ paired with Shenoi's "Context Engineering: RAG" (https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/context-engineering-rag-rajesh-shenoi-g6ftc) these are the two cleanest re-statements of the production-RAG consensus from this week — chunking and retrieval quality, not bigger models. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/building-production-ready-rag-hybrid-search-re-ranking-saurabh-anand-gfdmc]
