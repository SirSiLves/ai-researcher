**The forcing function disappeared: what the AI Act Omnibus extension really did to enterprise AI governance** — Marko Markovic (lang: en). Argues most enterprise EU AI Act compliance work was never a control — it was a deadline with staff attached, and the 7 May Omnibus deal pushing high-risk obligations to 2 Aug 2028 just ran that test on every European enterprise simultaneously. Pattern-matches against Basel II, MiFID II, and GDPR slippages: programmes that survived were reframed from "comply by date X" to a control the institution would have built anyway. The orchestration boundary (Edition #6) is deadline-invariant because customers, courts, and risk functions will ask the accountability question on the date of the next disputed decision, not 2028. _Why interesting:_ a sharper diagnostic than the omnibus coverage in last week's pieces — gives governance leads a one-question test to run on their own programme this week. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/forcing-function-disappeared-what-ai-act-omnibus-did-marko-3nuve/]

**Email Got Its Own Protocol. Agents Deserve One Too.** — Chris Hood (lang: en). Argues the four things HTTP fails to give agents — agent-level identity (vs server-cert identity), negotiation as a wire primitive, scope-of-authority as a transport-layer header, transparency built into the substrate — are exactly what AGTP (Agent Transfer Protocol, IANA port 4480 registered April 2026, draft family at IETF) puts back in the wire. Status codes like 261 Negotiation-In-Progress / 263 Proposal-Approved / 463 Proposal-Rejected let infrastructure see what an agent conversation is doing without parsing JSON; Canonical Agent-ID is a 256-bit hash of the agent's signed Genesis document, so identity survives operator and domain changes. Reference implementation includes an unmodified MCP server running behind an AGTP gateway to show MCP/A2A layer on top, the same way TLS layers on HTTP. _Why interesting:_ companion piece to last week's "Narrow Waist" article (same author, AGTP angle) — fills in the SMTP-vs-HTTP analogy and the concrete IETF draft status. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/email-got-its-own-protocol-agents-deserve-one-too-chris-hood-0r1fc/]

**Enterprise Agentic Strategies Assume Infinite Access to Compute** — Michael Beygelman (lang: en). Counter-narrative argument: every enterprise software vendor's 12–24-month agentic roadmap assumes Anthropic/OpenAI/Google inference capacity will be there at reasonable price when these strategies hit production scale. The supply constraint is physical, not technical — power infrastructure, data-center construction times, semiconductor supply that doesn't expand on 12-month cycles. Aggregate token consumption of serious enterprise deployments is orders of magnitude bigger than what pilots imply. The rational response is not buying more compute but compression: smarter context management, inference-optimized model variants, better retrieval, token-efficient prompting — many of which run on infrastructure outside the supply-constrained GPU pool. Closes with the one question every agentic-strategy review should ask: "what does this cost when inference is three times more expensive than today, and is the architecture we're building designed to handle that?" _Why interesting:_ the supply-side framing that no vendor pitch deck includes; sharpens governance from compliance-conversation to financial-controls-conversation. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/enterprise-agentic-strategies-assume-infinite-access-beygelman-etn4f/]

**From Prompt to Context to Harness: The Three Eras of AI Engineering and Why Each One Ended** — Ahmed A. (lang: en). Heavily-cited synthesis arguing each transition was forced by structural technology shifts not a marketing rebrand: prompts (2020–22) were a workaround for 2k-token windows and weak instruction-following; context engineering (2023–25) emerged when windows hit 100k–2M and the unit became "what configuration of system prompt + retrieved docs + tool outputs + memory goes into the window at each step" (named in a 48-hour window in June 2025 by Lütke, Karpathy, Schmid, Chase, Yan); harness engineering (late 2025–26) named formally by OpenAI's February 2026 Lopopolo post once harness variance started exceeding model variance. Empirical evidence on the harness claim: Cursor 46→80% on identical model weights, SWE-bench Pro 23→45%+ with optimized scaffold, LangChain 52.8→66.5% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 with no model change, Endor Labs GPT-5.5 61.5% in Codex vs 87.2% in Cursor, Anthropic-internal Claude Opus 4.5 jumping 42→95% on CORE-Bench from eval-harness fixes alone. OpenAI retired SWE-bench Verified as a frontier eval in February 2026. Concrete operational thresholds (if harness Δ > model Δ on your task suite, allocate ≥70% of engineering headcount to the harness, not model integration). _Why interesting:_ the single best-cited piece in this space, with primary-source URLs to every claim and explicit caveats about the term still being in motion. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/from-prompt-context-harness-three-eras-ai-engineering-ahmed-albadri-wifgf/]

**Harness Engineering: The Next Evolution Beyond Prompt and Context Engineering** — Rishabh Software (lang: en). Companion field guide to the era-shift piece above. Frames the OpenAI 3-engineers / 5-months / 1M lines of code case study as a harness-engineering exemplar — what the engineers were actually doing was building guardrails, validation gates, role-separated agents, structured handoffs, and feedback loops in markdown documents the agent reads at the start of every session. Three-layer production stack: human (direction + approval) → orchestration layer (request → intent → plan → delegation → monitor) → agents constrained by role-definition files. Two rule types: norms (judgment principles like "verify from code before asking, push back on logical problems") vs guardrails (hard boundaries — "no implementation begins without an approved plan; restart fresh when asked to reconsider"). Six core components running in parallel: tool integration, layered memory, active context selection, planning, verification, modular swappable architecture. _Why interesting:_ practical "what does a harness actually contain" companion to Ahmed Al Badri's strategic framing. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/harness-engineering-next-evolution-beyond-prompt-context-32ljf/]

**A practitioner's guide to getting more value out of AI coding: agent quality & token optimization** — Maxim Salnikov, Microsoft/GitHub (lang: en). GitHub workshop write-up triggered by GitHub's shift from premium-requests to usage-based billing. Core reframe: stop asking "how do we cut token spend?" and start asking "how do we make every token count?" Compound-error math that should haunt anyone running multi-step agents — 99% accuracy/step over 50 steps = ~60% workflow success; 95% accuracy/step = ~8%. The two biggest levers vastly outweigh everything else: model choice (~24× cost gap between Claude Opus 4.7 and GPT-5.4 mini — reasoning models for planning, mid-tier for implementation, small models for refactors; Auto Mode arriving June) and relevant context (let the agent discover, don't stuff prompts). Walks the Research→Plan→Implement separation, the deterministic-guardrail antidote (Copilot CLI team ships ~500 PRs/week with ~53% of codebase as tests), and the full agent-config surface (CLAUDE.md, custom agents, skills, MCPs, subagents, scoped instructions, prompt files, Copilot Memory). Concrete five-actions-today checklist. _Why interesting:_ the most operationally specific token-economics guide of the week, written from a Microsoft AI-native-development position. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/practitioners-guide-getting-more-value-out-ai-coding-agent-salnikov-mhume/]

**KI im Schweizer Bewerbungsprozess: Was 6'000 Stellensuchende und 700 Unternehmen tatsächlich erleben** — Grass & Partner AG (lang: de-CH). Coverage of AMOSA's April 2026 study (Arbeitsmarktbeobachtung Ostschweiz, AG, ZG, ZH) — the largest Swiss dataset on AI in hiring. Headline asymmetry: 65% of Swiss job-seekers (83% of tertiary-educated) use AI in their search, but only 13% of Swiss companies use AI in recruiting at all and only 3.6% in screening/preselection. Regular AI-use correlates with 48% more interview invitations, but six-month follow-up shows no faster re-employment — AI works as a door-opener, not a deal-closer. From the employer side: companies estimate 39% of incoming applications (49% at large firms) are partially or fully AI-generated; 51% say it's harder to assess authenticity; 55% claim to spot AI-assisted applications but only 10% actively check; 7% have adjusted their selection process, mostly by leaning harder on references, additional assessments, diploma verification, and the probation period. Transparency wish-list is symmetric — 75% of candidates want firms to disclose AI use in hiring, 57% of firms want candidates to do the same. _Why interesting:_ definitive Swiss data on AI in hiring with concrete implications for executive-level job-seekers (the AI-screening fear is largely unfounded; polished documents are now table stakes; references and interview prep are where the residual value sits). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ki-im-schweizer-bewerbungsprozess-6000-stellensuchende-1ouqe/]
