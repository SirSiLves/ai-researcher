## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**The forcing function disappeared: what the AI Act Omnibus extension really did to enterprise AI governance** — Marko Markovic (lang: en). Argues the May 7 2026 AI Act Omnibus deal — which slipped the high-risk deadline to August 2028 — exposes that most enterprise AI governance was a deadline-with-staff, not a real control; the durable control is owning the model-agnostic "orchestration boundary" that records every decision. _Why interesting:_ Frames the Omnibus extension as a stress-test of which governance programmes were ever real, with a banking-regulation analogy (Basel II, MiFID II, GDPR). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/forcing-function-disappeared-what-ai-act-omnibus-did-marko-3nuve]

**The EU AI Act now treats multi-agent systems as one system** — Pieter van Schalkwyk (lang: en). Field note on the Commission's 19 May 2026 draft Article 6 guidelines, which classify compound/multi-agent systems serving a high-risk purpose as a single high-risk system assessed end-to-end; argues the real forcing functions are the recast Product Liability Directive, insurer AI exclusions, and procurement (ISO 42001 + AI BoM), not the deferred deadline. _Why interesting:_ Heavily sourced (arXiv, EUR-Lex, Five Eyes guidance) and ties the "orchestrator is the system" doctrine to five concrete Article-mapped engineering requirements. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-act-now-treats-multi-agent-systems-one-system-van-schalkwyk-ondmc]

**Preparing for the Upcoming Updates to the Model Context Protocol (MCP)** — amit singh (lang: en). Operator's breakdown of the 2026-07-28 MCP spec revision: stateless protocol core (session header removed), new ext-* extensions framework, MCP Apps (server-rendered UIs), a Tasks extension for long-running work, OAuth-aligned authorization hardening, and a deprecation policy. _Why interesting:_ Translates the spec changes into concrete per-team migration actions (stdio vs Streamable HTTP, the -32002→-32602 error-code break). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/preparing-upcoming-updates-model-context-protocol-mcp-amit-singh-zziye]

**What Building Ten MCP Servers Taught Me About Where the Protocol Bites** — Björn D. (lang: en). Practitioner notes: the MCP protocol is trivial; the pain is one layer up — the tool schema is really a prompt, a default value is a public contract, stdio is a shared channel that corrupts on stray output, and green unit tests can mask a broken cold-start path. _Why interesting:_ Concrete, hard-won engineering lessons rather than protocol overview; the kind of low-follower, high-substance post the focus prioritizes. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-building-ten-mcp-servers-taught-me-where-bites-bj%C3%B6rn-dijkhuizen-c0dwe]

**Which agentic AI use cases are getting budget in US enterprises** — The Leadership Board (lang: en). Market read arguing US enterprises fund bounded, governable agentic use cases (workflow automation, decision support, cyber/SOC ops, data interaction, support, predictive maintenance) rather than open-ended autonomy, and that budget follows a strong control story. _Why interesting:_ Maps where agentic AI money is actually flowing vs hype, with a clear "what buyers will not fund" list. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/which-agentic-ai-use-cases-getting-budget-us-enterprises-oggcf]

**RAG vs Long Context: How AI Remembers Things** — Imre Lóránt Dévai (lang: en). Argues RAG became an over-applied "golden hammer" with a black-box retrieval blind spot; for project-level work, large context windows plus a hand-structured Markdown "second brain" read via plain file/grep ("deterministic RAG", clearbox) beats vector-DB pipelines. _Why interesting:_ Articulates the RAG-vs-long-context debate from a practitioner angle and connects to the "LLM Wiki" pattern. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/rag-vs-long-context-how-ai-remembers-things-imre-l%C3%B3r%C3%A1nt-d%C3%A9vai-xh9sf]
