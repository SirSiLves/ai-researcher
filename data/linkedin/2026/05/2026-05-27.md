## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**Your Team Deployed MCP. You Still Don't Have a Governance Framework** — Ken Priore (lang: en). With 97M SDK downloads, 10k+ public MCP servers, and 53% still using long-lived static secrets while only 8.5% use OAuth, MCP has won as an integration standard but left a governance vacuum that Article 14 of the EU AI Act and the UK ICO already expect to be filled. The piece argues that custom-built integrations used to constrain agent scope as a side-effect — MCP removes that constraint, so authorization, audit, and vendor-contract scope have to be designed alongside the integration layer, not deferred to a phase two that rarely arrives. _Why interesting:_ in-house counsel framing of MCP-governance gap with concrete vendor-contract questions to ask now. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/your-team-deployed-mcp-you-still-dont-have-governance-ken-priore-hjktc/]

**The MCP Supply Chain Is on Fire — and Anthropic Says That's by Design** — Rashid Mahmood (lang: en). Walks through the April 2026 STDIO-RCE finding in MCP (9 of 11 public registries successfully poisoned in a controlled test, ~78% of production enterprise agents touching MCP), Anthropic's "sanitization is the developer's responsibility" stance, four attack families already seen in the wild, and connects it to EchoLeak (CVE-2025-32711) and Unit 42's twelve indirect-injection cases. Closes with a printable 10-item checklist across procurement, architecture, and detection. _Why interesting:_ the most actionable agent-security write-up of the week, with sandboxing/scoped-credential vendor names you can act on this week. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-supply-chain-fire-anthropic-says-thats-design-rashid-mahmood-islme/]

**The EU AI Act now treats multi-agent systems as one system** — Pieter van Schalkwyk (lang: en). Field note on the 19 May 2026 draft Commission guidelines under Article 6 (compound systems "where multiple AI subsystems or agents feed into a larger decision-making model" are assessed end-to-end as a single high-risk system), why the 7 May Digital Omnibus deferral to 2027 is operationally irrelevant once the recast Product Liability Directive (9 Dec 2026), insurer AI exclusions, and ISO/IEC 42001 procurement requirements are in scope, and the five engineering primitives any industrial agentic platform now needs (continuous risk per action, defensible data governance, parent-child agent trace IDs, orchestrator-level human oversight, privilege enforcement outside the LLM). _Why interesting:_ the single sharpest piece on what the May 19 Commission guidelines actually mean for compound/agentic systems, with primary citations through to arXiv:2604.04604 and CEN-CENELEC drafts. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-act-now-treats-multi-agent-systems-one-system-van-schalkwyk-ondmc/]

**ServiceNow and the EU AI Act: Five Structural Compliance Gaps** — Shardorn Wong A Ton (lang: en). Use-case classification (Annex III requires per-use-case conformity assessment, not one for "Now Assist"), prompt-level vs architecture-level privilege minimisation, automation-vs-oversight tension in Control Tower, behavioural drift and "substantial modification" under Article 3(23), and the Article 25/26 deployer-vs-provider boundary that current ServiceNow contracts don't address. Argues the same gaps exist across Salesforce, Workday, and comparable enterprise platforms. _Why interesting:_ rare technical-legal breakdown of a specific enterprise platform against the August 2026 high-risk deadline, with explicit remediation paths. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/servicenow-eu-ai-act-five-structural-compliance-gaps-wong-a-ton--tcace/]

**KI-Agenten als juristische Personen — eine Frage, die das Recht nicht mehr lange ignorieren kann** — Dominik Witz (lang: de). LegalTech founder responds to Harari's claim that AI agents will gain legal personhood within five years by walking through what Swiss law actually permits — ZGB knows only natural and juridical persons (tertium non datur), Art. 707 OR demands the Verwaltungsrat be made up of natural persons, and Melinda Lohmann's habilitation thesis frames machine declarations as normatively attributed to operators, not the machine. Concludes the bigger risk isn't premature recognition of AI personhood, it's the opposite: agents making more economic decisions while the law shrinks their role and lets accountability erode. _Why interesting:_ Swiss-law-specific governance angle the English-language press isn't running. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ki-agenten-als-juristische-personen-eine-frage-die-das-dominik-witz-u8axe/]

**The Next Enterprise Battle: Governing AI Agents Inside Operational Workflows** — Eduardo Poggi (lang: en). Argues the competitive battle has shifted from "who has the smartest model?" to "who controls the cognitive operational layer of the enterprise?" — citing Microsoft 365 copilots, Salesforce Agentforce, SAP Joule, ServiceNow agents, and Anthropic packaging Claude into prebuilt small-business workflows. Names the new governance layer that has to sit above DLP/IAM/PAM: agent identity, runtime authorization, operational permission governance, execution observability, decision tracing, lifecycle management. _Why interesting:_ clean strategic framing of where enterprise AI economics are heading, without product marketing. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/next-enterprise-battle-governing-ai-agents-inside-workflows-poggi-kozhf/]

**#22: DeepDive ACP vs MCP vs A2A: The Complete Guide to AI Agent Protocols** — NeosAlpha Technologies (lang: en). Three protocols sit at different layers: MCP (agent-to-tool, 97M downloads, production-ready), A2A (cross-vendor agent-to-agent, v0.3), ACP (REST-native intra-enterprise peer messaging, IBM-built because MCP doesn't support delta streaming or shared cross-server memory). Worked example: a procurement orchestrator using MCP to hit SAP and the supplier DB, ACP for the internal compliance peer agent, and A2A for the trading-partner supplier's agent. _Why interesting:_ cleanest side-by-side of the three-layer agent protocol stack, with concrete enterprise use cases for procurement, SOC, and financial reporting. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/22-deepdiveacp-vs-mcp-a2a-complete-guide-ai-agent-protocols-eiocc/]

**The Narrow Waist Architecture of the Agent Transfer Protocol** — Chris Hood (lang: en). Proposes AGTP as a transport-layer "narrow waist" beneath MCP/A2A so identity, authority scope, and audit move out of the application payload into transport headers. Components: cryptographic agent identity via X.509v3 + Ed25519 bound into TLS 1.3 mutual auth (one heavy verification per session, then constant-time checks), AGTP-Log where logs are signed by the governance platform's key rather than the agent's, and an Agent Name Service that combines a static trust-tier with a dynamic behavioural-trust score for discovery. _Why interesting:_ pushes the agent-governance conversation from application-layer policy down to network-layer architecture — analogous to how IP carries any payload. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/narrow-waist-architecture-agent-transfer-protocol-chris-hood-cedec/]
