## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**The MCP Spec Went Final on July 28. It Hardened Identity Again. Capability Didn't Move.** — Philipp Braun (lang: en)
The MCP specification left release-candidate status on 28 July; `2026-07-28` is now the locked version. Identity hardened on two axes — client credentials bound to their issuing authorization server (SEP-2352), Dynamic Client Registration formally deprecated in favour of Client ID Metadata Documents, and mandatory client-side `iss` validation (SEP-2468) closing the auth-server mix-up hole. Scope selection did not change: none of the six authorization SEPs touch it, and clients are still advised to request every scope a server supports.
_Why interesting:_ Cleanly separates a real identity improvement from the "enterprise-ready MCP authorization" claim vendors are now building marketing around — the per-call capability question stays with server-side policy.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-spec-went-final-july-28-hardened-identity-again-capability-braun-js0ee

**#56 Model Context Protocol Goes Stateless: Why This Architectural Shift Changes Everything** — Imdad Areeph (lang: en)
The same revision moves conversation state from the server into every request, making each request self-contained. Removes the need for sticky sessions or cross-server state synchronisation behind a load balancer.
_Why interesting:_ The practical deployment consequence of the spec release — horizontal scaling and serverless MCP hosting become straightforward, and losing a server no longer loses conversation state.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/model-context-protocol-goes-stateless-why-shift-changes-imdad-areeph-hx73f

_Why interesting:_ Rare hands-on governance account from regulated finance rather than a vendor — directly transferable to a Swiss banking context.

**Enforcement of the AI Act Starts Today** — Luiza Jarovsky, PhD (lang: en)
EU AI Act enforcement began 2 August. Enforcement splits three ways: the AI Office for GPAI providers and for systems shipped by the same provider as the underlying model, the 27 national competent authorities for other systems, and the EDPS for EU institutions. The Digital Omnibus pushed the Article 50.2 watermarking grace period to 2 December 2026, Annex III high-risk to 2 December 2027, and Annex I high-risk to 2 August 2028. The EU also launched a public Complaint Tool and a confidential Whistleblower Tool.
_Why interesting:_ The clearest mechanical breakdown of who actually enforces what, plus the new reporting channels — extraterritorial reach via Article 2 is called out explicitly.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/enforcement-ai-act-starts-today-luiza-jarovsky-phd-l0g7f

**The 2 August High-Risk Deadline Under the EU AI Act Just Moved to 2027. The Engineering Work Did Not.** — ViitorCloud Technologies (lang: en)
The Digital Omnibus on AI entered into force 27 July, deferring the heaviest high-risk obligations. What did *not* move: from 2 August the penalty regime is operative (up to EUR 35M or 7% of global turnover, Article 99), Article 50 human-facing disclosure applies, and market surveillance authorities gain investigatory powers. Notes notified bodies for conformity assessment remain scarce, with certification timelines put at 9–24 months.
_Why interesting:_ Frames the delay as a runway rather than a reprieve, and proposes the obligation → control → log → named owner mapping. Caveat: this is vendor content with a closing sales pitch, but the regulatory specifics are sourced and check out against the Jarovsky piece above.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/2-august-high-risk-deadline-under-eu-ai-act-just-moved-2027-a0wbf

**Agent Memory is the Attack Surface** — Mem0 (lang: en)
Memory poisoning now has its own category in OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications (ASI06) and MITRE ATLAS (AML.T0080). Core argument: a prompt injection buys one turn and dies with the context, while a single successful memory write persists and re-fires in sessions the attacker never joins. Identifies four write channels — direct injection, policy satisfaction, summary survival through compaction, and poisoned procedures — of which only the first looks adversarial. Cites Microsoft Defender's February 2026 "AI Recommendation Poisoning" finding (50+ attempts from 31 companies across 14 industries in 60 days), MemMorph at 85.9% attack success with three planted records, MINJA at 98.2% with query-only access, and 57–71% cross-user contamination measured with no attacker present at all.
_Why interesting:_ Unusually well-referenced for LinkedIn (full arXiv + vendor-doc bibliography), and the thesis — that sandboxing and output scanning guard the read path while the write path is the real trust boundary — is a genuinely useful reframe. Vendor-authored; the Mem0 product section is clearly marked and states its own limits.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agent-memory-attack-surface-mem0-s6qxf

**The Agentic AI Production Gap** — ganesh prasad bhandari (lang: en)
Opens on the failure mode that monitoring misses: an agent returns HTTP 200, completes every planned step, and still makes the wrong decision — infrastructure healthy, business outcome wrong. Argues enterprise agents need reliability engineering rather than better models.
_Why interesting:_ Names the observability gap between "the pipeline ran" and "the pipeline was right", which is where most agent pilots stall.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agentic-ai-production-gap-ganesh-prasad-bhandari-hxaye

**Vectorless RAG Explained** — Karthik Chakravarthy (lang: en)
Challenges the assumption that RAG requires a vector database. Where documents already carry invoice IDs, customer IDs, policy numbers or structured metadata, deterministic retrieval — SQL, BM25, metadata filtering, graph traversal — is cheaper, faster and explainable, with no re-indexing on document change. Semantic search still wins on vocabulary mismatch; the recommended landing point is intent-routed hybrid retrieval.
_Why interesting:_ The explainability argument ("matched policy number" versus an opaque similarity score) is the operative one for regulated industries and audit.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/vectorless-rag-explained-karthik-chakravarthy-m83dc
