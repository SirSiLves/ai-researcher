## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**Two stories dominate this week's long-form: the MCP spec going stateless (28 July) and the EU AI Act's 2 August deadline — which several authors say moved, and several say did not.**

### MCP spec revision (28 July)

**MCP Goes Stateless Today. Here's What That Means If You're Building AI Agents** — Daniele Stroppa (AWS), 28 July. The largest MCP revision since launch drops sessions entirely, going stateless at the transport layer. Written for retail/commerce agent builders: changes infrastructure cost math and introduces migration risk.
_Why interesting:_ The clearest practitioner account of what the transport change actually breaks.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-goes-stateless-today-heres-what-means-youre-building-stroppa-hpkue

**The MCP Spec Is Final. Now the Real Work Begins.** — solo.io, 30 July. Sessions gone; auth modernised around OAuth 2.1 and OpenID Connect; new Extensions, MCP Apps and an updated Tasks model. Argues the protocol debate is over but enterprise work is just starting.
_Why interesting:_ Best short inventory of what landed in the spec beyond statelessness.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-spec-final-now-real-work-begins-solo-io-fnkue

**When AI goes shopping: UCP, ACP and MCP explained** — Otto Group one.O, 28 July. Maps the three competing agent-commerce protocol camps and who controls which retailers appear when an agent shops.
_Why interesting:_ Rare comparative treatment of MCP against the commerce-specific protocols (UCP/ACP), from a European retailer.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-ai-goes-shopping-ucp-acp-mcp-explained-ottogroup-one-o-8ni1f

### EU AI Act — the 2 August dispute

**The 2 August High-Risk Deadline Under the EU AI Act Just Moved to 2027. The Engineering Work Did Not.** — ViitorCloud Technologies, 30 July. Reports the Digital Omnibus on AI entered into force 27 July, pushing the heaviest high-risk obligations to late 2027, and argues teams should still build the controls now.
_Why interesting:_ Names the specific instrument behind the deferral and reframes it as engineering, not paperwork.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/2-august-high-risk-deadline-under-eu-ai-act-just-moved-2027-a0wbf

**Enforcement of the AI Act Starts Today** — Luiza Jarovsky, PhD, 2 August. Newsletter edition #311; states non-compliance may now result in fines.
_Why interesting:_ Directly opposite framing to the ViitorCloud piece, same week. (Caveat: a large share of this edition is course/masterclass promotion.)
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/enforcement-ai-act-starts-today-luiza-jarovsky-phd-l0g7f

**The EU AI Act deadline everyone's still quoting is wrong** — Sooz Young, 3 August. Argues 2 August 2026 was the date the transparency rules came into force, and that businesses with customer-facing AI are already in scope.
_Why interesting:_ Reconciles the other two — transparency duties bit on 2 August, Annex III high-risk slipped to 2027. Worth checking against primary sources before repeating any of the three.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-act-deadline-everyones-still-quoting-wrong-sooz-young-ahece

**BaFin nimmt KI bei Versicherern ins Visier, BGH kippt Wohn-Riester-Gebühren & Finanzmarktaufsicht ermittelt gegen Zurich Insurance** — Versicherungsbote, 2 August (lang: de). Weekly German insurance-sector roundup whose lead item is the German regulator BaFin putting insurers' use of AI under supervisory scrutiny.
_Why interesting:_ DACH financial-services regulator moving on AI supervision; note this is a mixed roundup, most items are not AI.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/bafin-nimmt-ki-bei-versicherern-ins-visier-bgh-kippt-u1xbe

### Agent security

**Stored Prompt Injection: When the Payload Requires No Code** — Alex Levy (CISO), 30 July. Frames indirect prompt injection as the return of the injection class we spent twenty years closing, with stored XSS as the closest analogue — but no equivalent fix available.
_Why interesting:_ The security-engineering framing (channel separation) is sharper than the usual prompt-injection explainer.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/stored-prompt-injection-when-payload-requires-code-alex-levy-epnrc

**Agent Memory is the Attack Surface** — Mem0, 29 July. Distinguishes context-window injection (ends with the session) from memory-layer injection, which persists and is read back later as ordinary fact. Notes two standards bodies now classify this separately.
_Why interesting:_ Persistence is the part most agent-memory discussions skip; pairs directly with the Levy piece.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agent-memory-attack-surface-mem0-s6qxf

### RAG

**Agentic RAG in 2026: Why AI Agents Need a Brain, Not Just a Bigger Vector Database** — vThink Global Technologies, 29 July. Positions 2026 as the shift from retrieval access to systems that reason, plan, use tools and validate their own responses.
_Why interesting:_ Useful framing of why retrieval quality, not index size, is now the bottleneck.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agentic-rag-2026-why-ai-agents-need-brain-just-bigger-ceztc

**Vectorless RAG Explained** — Karthik Chakravarthy (Microsoft), 31 July. Challenges the assumption that every RAG system needs a vector database, for data whose structure already tells you where to look.
_Why interesting:_ Counter-current to the default embed-everything architecture.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/vectorless-rag-explained-karthik-chakravarthy-m83dc

### Enterprise adoption and governance

**The Agentic Infrastructure Gap Isn't Where Most Enterprises Are Looking** — Michael Leone, Moor Insights & Strategy, 29 July. Analyst read of Google Cloud's infrastructure survey of 1,402 senior IT leaders across twelve countries.
_Why interesting:_ Survey-grounded rather than anecdotal, and explicitly correlates stated priorities against vendor case studies.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agentic-infrastructure-gap-isnt-where-most-looking-michael-leone-bhwge

**From Checklists to Control Loops: Scaling AI Governance Beyond Questionnaires** — Dr. David R. Hardoon (ex-regulator), 29 July. Works the arithmetic of questionnaire-based governance — 100 questions per use case, ten thousand agents — to argue for continuous runtime control loops.
_Why interesting:_ Concrete argument for why static assessment cannot scale to agent fleets; relevant to any ISO 42001 programme.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/from-checklists-control-loops-scaling-ai-governance-beyond-hardoon-5yqnc
