## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

### Agent interoperability — MCP / A2A

**91.8% of Public MCP Servers Have No Authentication. Someone Finally Measured.** — Travis Lelle, 2026-08-04 (en)
First large-scale dynamic audit of internet-facing MCP servers: 380 of 414 tested had no OAuth at all, with hundreds exposing unauthenticated shell execution. Author also notes the audited servers churn away quickly, making the population hard to re-measure.
_Why interesting:_ Rare empirical number on MCP deployment hygiene rather than another explainer — directly relevant if you are standing up or consuming MCP servers.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/918-public-mcp-servers-have-authentication-someone-finally-lelle-0vobe

**We Test MCP Servers for Correctness. We Almost Never Test Them for Load.** — Shriram Vasudevan, 2026-08-01 (en)
Argues the MCP tooling stack covers spec conformance, tool-surface security audits and version diffing, but nothing answers the throughput question. Proposes load testing as the missing layer and ships a small tool for it.
_Why interesting:_ Names a concrete gap in the MCP ecosystem instead of restating what the protocol is.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/we-test-mcp-servers-correctness-almost-never-them-shriram-jkmcc

**Your Agent Runs Other People's Code: Securing the MCP Supply Chain with Docker** — Shriram Vasudevan, 2026-08-01 (en)
Reframes an MCP server as a software supply-chain artifact rather than a data endpoint, since using one grants execution trust. Walks through what Docker's existing controls cover and which second layer remains the operator's problem.
_Why interesting:_ The supply-chain framing is the right mental model for MCP risk and is still underused in practice.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/your-agent-runs-other-peoples-code-securing-mcp-chain-shriram-pecfc

**THE DIGITAL TOWER OF BABEL** — Mo Saker, 2026-08-04 (en)
Narrative case study of a supply-chain planner whose forecasting agent makes a correct, well-reasoned autonomous call that no downstream system or human process can validate. Uses it to argue that agent-to-agent coordination, not agent quality, is the bottleneck.
_Why interesting:_ Grounds the interoperability argument in an operational scenario rather than protocol diagrams.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/digital-tower-babel-mo-saker-ynypc

### AI governance / responsible AI

**Presence Isn't Oversight: What the EU AI Act, Article 14, Really Demands of Human-AI Systems** — Conor O'Neill (ServisBOT), 2026-08-06 (en)
Contrasts informal "human-in-the-loop" contact-centre practice with what Article 14 actually specifies for human oversight. Part 2 of a four-part hybrid-agentic series.
_Why interesting:_ Published today, and it targets the exact gap between HITL as a slogan and HITL as a legal obligation.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/presence-isnt-oversight-what-eu-ai-act-article-14-really-demands-g0age

**The EU AI Act's transparency rules are live: what applies since 2 August 2026** — BDF Experts, 2026-08-05 (en)
Walks Article 50 obligations that bind providers and deployers regardless of risk classification, so a company with no high-risk system is still in scope via a chatbot or generated imagery. References the Digital Omnibus Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 in force since 27 July.
_Why interesting:_ Cleanest read of the specific obligations that switched on four days ago, with the Omnibus interaction called out.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-live-what-applies-since-2-august-ddf3f

**The EU AI Act wants a record. Your traces are already most of one** — Jim Bennett, 2026-08-04 (en)
Responds to Angie Jones (Agentic AI Foundation) on agent record-keeping, arguing that existing observability traces already satisfy much of the Act's logging demand. Key point retained: autonomy does not shift responsibility from company to agent.
_Why interesting:_ Practical bridge between compliance logging and the telemetry teams already run — reframes the requirement as work you would do anyway.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-act-wants-record-your-traces-already-most-one-jim-bennett-kovpf

**"We Don't Need ISO 42001." Why That View Misses Its Real Value** — Vijay Reddy, 2026-08-05 (en)
Answers the "no customer is asking for it" objection by analogy to earlier ISO 9001 / CMMI adoption debates. Distinguishes treating a management system as a certificate from treating it as an operating discipline.
_Why interesting:_ Useful counter-argument to have ready when AI management-system investment gets challenged internally.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/we-dont-need-iso-42001-why-view-misses-its-real-value-vijay-reddy-grknc

**AI Act au 2 août 2026 : ce que les PME doivent réellement vérifier** — Philippe Contal, 2026-08-03 (fr)
_Summary (EN):_ Sets out what small and mid-size firms concretely owe since 2 August: identify your role, inform people in certain public interactions, and handle deepfakes and some generated text. Explicitly rejects the idea that every ChatGPT use must be declared or every AI-assisted output flagged.
_Why interesting:_ Scoped to SME reality and corrects a common over-reading of the transparency duties.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-act-au-2-ao%C3%BBt-2026-ce-que-les-pme-doivent-v%C3%A9rifier-philippe-contal-ovlae

**Le nouveau contrôle américain de l'IA ne s'appliquera pas aux modèles ouverts** — Emily Turrettini, 2026-08-05 (fr)
_Summary (EN):_ Reports that US federal pre-release security review will exclude open-weight models, concentrating on proprietary frontier labs. The framework, from a June executive order, would give the administration up to 30 days of early model access for cyber-risk evaluation.
_Why interesting:_ Sharp divergence from the EU approach, and it materially changes the compliance calculus between open-weight and closed deployment.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/le-nouveau-contr%C3%B4le-am%C3%A9ricain-de-lia-ne-sappliquera-pas-turrettini-rpdjf

### AI platforms / agent frameworks

**Designing an Enterprise Agentic System Is Really a Series of Decisions** — Rupesh Panda, 2026-08-06 (en)
Argues agentic design should start from ordered business and architectural decisions rather than models, frameworks, prompts, MCP or tools. Lays out the decision sequence he thinks teams should follow before any code.
_Why interesting:_ Published today; a decision-order framing that is more actionable than the usual reference-architecture diagram.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/designing-enterprise-agentic-system-really-series-decisions-panda-3xl3c

**Why Multi-Agent Systems Fail** — Fangzhu Chen, 2026-08-03 (en)
Traces a progression from prompt engineering through prompt chaining to multi-agent and platform architecture, then asks why MAS deployments fail. Focus is reasoning resilience, validation and cognitive error handling.
_Why interesting:_ Failure-mode analysis from a manufacturing architect, and it treats agentic AI as a software-architecture discipline rather than a prompting one.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-multi-agent-systems-fail-fangzhu-chen-psoxc

**Skills Are the Most Underrated Thing Anthropic Has Shipped** — Dextra Labs, 2026-08-01 (en)
Describes the "graveyard of system prompts" pattern where each team privately maintains its own unshared prompt assets. Positions Anthropic Skills as the packaging layer that makes those reusable, and calls slow adoption a knowledge gap rather than a tooling one.
_Why interesting:_ Direct commentary on Skills adoption in enterprises, with a recognisable diagnosis of why prompt assets do not get shared.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/skills-most-underrated-thing-anthropic-has-shipped-dextra-labs-icizf

**AI Observability: The Blind Spot Every Production AI System Eventually Exposes** — Navikenz, 2026-08-04 (en)
Distinguishes infrastructure monitoring (uptime, latency, error rates) from observing whether model decisions are still correct. Point retained: quality can degrade for months while every operations dashboard stays green.
_Why interesting:_ The "is it running vs is it still right" split is the cleanest one-line statement of why APM does not cover LLM systems.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-observability-blind-spot-every-production-system-eventually-qge7f

**Most companies have an architecture problem. OpenAI just reset the economics of AI agents** — Steve Nouri, 2026-07-31 (en)
Reports GPT-5.6 API price cuts (Luna −80% to $0.20/$1.20 per million in/out tokens; Terra −20% to $2/$12; Sol unchanged), attributed partly to ~20% lower end-to-end serving cost. Argues the real shift is agents cheap enough to run continuously in the background rather than only on user request.
_Why interesting:_ The always-on-agent framing is the load-bearing claim; the pricing numbers are worth checking against the vendor page.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/most-companies-have-architecture-problem-openai-just-reset-nouri-r8jfc

### RAG techniques

**Vectorless RAG Explained** — Karthik Chakravarthy, 2026-07-31 (en)
Questions the default assumption that every RAG system needs a vector database and embedding tuning. Argues that where data already carries enough structure to locate the right passage, semantic similarity is not required.
_Why interesting:_ Contrarian against the standard embed-everything architecture, and the structured-data case is common in enterprise settings.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/vectorless-rag-explained-karthik-chakravarthy-m83dc

**Why Enterprises Need RAG Evaluation Frameworks Before Deploying AI to Production** — Techment Technology, 2026-07-31 (en)
Argues evaluation gaps stay invisible until production: without reproducible baselines and structured evaluation, a system that passes spot-checks can still emit unsupported claims or retrieve incomplete documents.
_Why interesting:_ The reproducible-baseline requirement is the part most RAG pilots skip.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-enterprises-need-rag-evaluation-frameworks-before-3hhyc

**Building Production-Grade RAG Systems: Architecting Enterprise Document Intelligence with LangChain, FAISS, and Llama Models** — Saad Khan, 2026-08-05 (en)
Architecture walkthrough of a document-chunking, FAISS-indexed, context-injected pipeline over private unstructured data. Short but concrete on the component choices.
_Why interesting:_ A named, specific stack rather than a generic RAG diagram — useful as a reference point.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/building-production-grade-rag-systems-architecting-enterprise-khan-db3jf
