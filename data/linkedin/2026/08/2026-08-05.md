## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**The EU just wrote an observability spec into AI regulation** — Laurie Voss, Arize (lang: en, Aug 4)
Notes that the Commission's enforcement powers over general-purpose models went live on 2 August, applying directly to only five to fifteen systemic-risk providers. Argues the Code of Practice underneath the Act reads like operational requirements written by someone who understands what an agent actually is, and that the obligations flow downhill from customers to their suppliers.
_Why interesting:_ Reads the Code of Practice as an engineering spec rather than a legal text, from a vendor whose day job is agent tracing.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-just-wrote-observability-spec-ai-regulation-laurie-voss-civpc

**Operationalizing EU AI Act Article 50 Transparency: The Role of the AI Control Tower** — Martin Pscheidl (lang: en, Aug 3)
Splits Article 50 into two distinct obligations now in force: disclosure that tells users they are dealing with AI, and marking/detectability that lets third parties verify generated content. Frames the hard part as a governance architecture that enforces both across distributed deployments while keeping audit trails.
_Why interesting:_ One of the few pieces separating human-facing transparency from machine-verifiable provenance instead of collapsing them into "label your AI".
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/operationalizing-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-role-pscheidl-fljwe

**KI-Kennzeichnung ab 2. August 2026: Was wirklich gekennzeichnet werden muss** — Henri Jendly (lang: de, Jul 31)
Attacks the simplification that every AI output must now be labelled, calling it wrong. Works through the Act's distinction between providers who build or badge systems and deployers who merely use them professionally, and between a technical watermark and a human-visible transparency notice.
_Why interesting:_ Corrects the labelling misconception circulating in DACH right now, with the provider/deployer split spelled out per use case.
https://de.linkedin.com/pulse/ki-kennzeichnung-ab-2-august-2026-wirklich-werden-muss-henri-jendly-lsake

**Big-Techs Cloud-Beweis, OpenAIs Preissturz und die Schweizer KI, die sehen lernt** — Andy Huber (lang: de, Aug 2)
Reads Microsoft's and Amazon's quarterly numbers as the first hard proof that cloud AI is revenue rather than promise, against OpenAI cutting model prices by up to 80 percent while Anthropic industrialises enterprise via partners such as Cognizant. Also covers Apertus 1.5 from ETH, EPFL and CSCS gaining image understanding.
_Why interesting:_ Swiss-angled: treats Apertus 1.5 as a serious production option, and contrasts two opposing vendor strategies rather than ranking models.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/big-techs-cloud-beweis-openais-preissturz-und-die-schweizer-huber-mydve

**Issue 17 — Identity: the agent economy runs on API keys and prayer, and its DNS moment is next** — Atin Agarwal (lang: en, Aug 5)
Uses the pre-DNS HOSTS file as the analogy: a hand-maintained name-to-address list that worked only while everyone on the network could be vouched for by reputation. Argues agent identity sits at exactly that stage today and needs naming and verification at machine speed with no human in the loop.
_Why interesting:_ The DNS framing gives agent identity a concrete historical precedent instead of the usual "we need standards" hand-wave.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/issue-17-identity-agent-economy-runs-api-keys-prayer-its-atin-agarwal-p7mrc

**The Rule Was "Do Not Touch Production." The Agent Forgot.** — Subramaniyam Pooni (lang: en, Aug 4)
Walks a failure mode where an explicit instruction not to touch the production database survives for a while, then dissolves as the conversation grows long and context gets summarised into something vague. Written up from a podcast discussion arguing agent governance needs its own layer.
_Why interesting:_ Names context compression as the mechanism by which guardrails silently decay, and argues explicitly that A2A and MCP will not give you that layer.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/rule-do-touch-production-agent-forgot-subramaniyam-pooni-rytvc

**MCP 2.0 is here: Full Guide for Enterprise Adoption** — RealAIzation (lang: en, Jul 29)
Summarises the 28 July spec: MCP goes stateless with no sessions, sticky routing or shared stores; an extensions framework lets Tasks and MCP Apps ship without touching the core; authorization tightens around issuer validation, credential binding and OIDC alignment. Roots, Sampling and Logging are deprecated with a 12-month migration window.
_Why interesting:_ The operational consequence is spelled out — round-robin load balancers now work and deep packet inspection is no longer needed.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-20-here-full-guide-enterprise-adoption-realaization-fixrc

**Moving Beyond Naive RAG: Why Production GenAI Demands GraphRAG and Model Context Protocol** — Manoj M (lang: en, Aug 3)
Declares the naive-RAG honeymoon over: upload PDFs, cosine-similarity over embeddings, feed top-k chunks to the model worked for demo day. Argues flat vector search breaks once compliance, data governance, real-time microservices and multi-hop lineage become non-negotiable.
_Why interesting:_ Pairs GraphRAG with MCP as a combined production architecture rather than treating retrieval and tool access as separate problems.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/moving-beyond-naive-rag-why-production-genai-demands-graphrag-manoj-m-aaswc

**Is "I Don't Know" a Feature or a Bug?** — David B. (lang: en, Aug 1)
Responds to a Year of the Graph prompt asking whether abstention is a feature or a cop-out in production RAG. Position: in legal, medical and financial workflows a confident wrong answer is a liability, so the system was built to prefer silence over a guess.
_Why interesting:_ Treats abstention as a deliberate design target with an honest admission that it is unproven at real enterprise volume.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-dont-know-feature-bug-david-berigny-pdrac

**Strands Multi-Agent Patterns: Which One Should You Use?** — New Math Data (lang: en, Jul 30)
Walks the five multi-agent patterns Strands offers, starting with agents-as-tools where one orchestrator treats specialised sub-agents as callable tools. Each pattern gets a minimal example and the conditions under which it earns its place, including deliberate hybrids.
_Why interesting:_ Architecture-selection guide written from build experience, framing the choice as one you must make before writing code rather than discover mid-build.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/strands-multi-agent-patterns-which-one-should-you-use-newmathdata-j1qhc

**Context Is the Ceiling of Your AI Agent's Capability** — Dong Hoang (lang: en, Aug 5)
Describes the recurring pattern where an agent benchmarks brilliantly, then in production forgets constraints, repeats actions and ignores stated rules — and the team's first instinct is to reach for a better model. Argues the real variable is what the model has in front of it at the moment it acts.
_Why interesting:_ Written from a GRC and internal-audit seat rather than an engineering one, which is an unusual angle on context engineering.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/context-engineering-ceiling-your-ai-agents-capability-dong-hoang-vyrrc
