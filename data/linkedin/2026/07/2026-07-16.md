## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**What We Learned Building Enterprise MCP at Bloomberg** — Tony McManus / Bloomberg (en). Argues MCP is a genuinely important connection standard but that "connection" and "readiness" are not the same thing — financial institutions building AI strategies on early-stage MCP offerings inherit trade-offs their vendors haven't solved yet. Works through tool-granularity choices (coarse- vs fine-grained tools) and the enterprise-infrastructure rigor that rushed implementations still lack. _Why interesting:_ a major financial-data vendor's candid, buyer-side critique of how immature "enterprise MCP" actually is. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-we-learned-building-enterprise-mcp-bloomberg-tony-mcmanus-wjvnc]

**MCP Is Becoming the Infrastructure Layer for AI Agents** — John Cloud / belix.ai (en). Uses Reuters' 8 July 2026 launch of an MCP server (letting authorized agents search, retrieve and download subscribed Reuters content inside existing tools) as evidence that MCP is moving beyond developer tooling into production information systems. Frames the real shift as a standard way for AI apps to reach trusted data/tools/services, not model selection. _Why interesting:_ concrete example of a major information provider shipping a production MCP server. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-becoming-infrastructure-layer-ai-agents-john-cloud-buhbc]

**The New AI Trust Architecture: 5 Requirements for Agent-to-Agent Communication** — Silvio Savarese & Sabastian V. Niles / Salesforce (en). Opens with the 1832 London bank-clearing houses — competitors who didn't trust each other inventing a shared "trust architecture" to transact daily — and maps that onto agent-to-agent interaction, laying out five requirements for agents to act on each other's behalf reliably and verifiably. _Why interesting:_ Salesforce's chief scientist and chief legal officer jointly framing A2A as an identity/trust problem, not just a protocol. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/new-ai-trust-architecture-5-requirements-agent-to-agent-communication-jzb0c]

**Scaling Agent Systems with Apache Kafka and the Agent2Agent (A2A) Protocol** — Paul Brebner / Instaclustr by NetApp (en). Kickoff of a technical series connecting event-streaming (Kafka) with agentic AI and A2A, prompted by a panellist's claim that "Kafka is the last thing you need for AI." Sketches two directions: AI decisions over streaming data, and Kafka as the backbone for scaling multi-agent systems. _Why interesting:_ event-streaming-infrastructure angle on multi-agent scaling from an OSS evangelist (series opener; lighter on conclusions so far). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/scaling-agent-systems-apache-kafka-agent2agent-a2a-protocol-brebner-kyi8e]

**The multi-agent mess finally has a control plane** — Jothi Moorthy (en). A client with "agents everywhere and no clean way to govern them" is standing up a dedicated agent-governance team whose whole job is to observe every agent in the company and put real guardrails around it. Enumerates what that team must actually watch — beyond the headline risks of prompt injection and jailbreaking — to size the control-plane problem. _Why interesting:_ ground-level view of the emerging enterprise agent-governance / control-plane function (dated today). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/multi-agent-mess-finally-has-control-plane-jothi-moorthy-dqgwc]

**Your Agent Has Too Many Tools. Retrieval Is the Answer.** — Dr. Olav Laudy (en). At five tools a model can read the menu and pick; at 200 the menu becomes the problem. Building a single MCP server fronting 200+ tools (think SAP or a core-banking API surface), the fix was not a bigger model or context window but retrieving only the handful of tools that match a request — the "RAG-MCP" pattern (Gan & Sun, arXiv:2505.03275). _Why interesting:_ concrete, paper-backed pattern that ties RAG, MCP and agent scaling together. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/your-agent-has-too-many-tools-retrieval-answer-dr-olav-laudy-eyhnc]

**Review: "RAG from First Principles" (Packt, 2026)** — Mark Peters, PhD (en). Reviews Jia Huang's new book, framing RAG as the AI analogue of an intelligence analyst crafting queries to sift massive databases; the text builds from basic principles through code to advanced optimization and measurement, and is recommended for practitioners expanding core RAG knowledge. _Why interesting:_ a pointer to a new hands-on RAG text (book review — a lighter item, kept for the reading signal). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/review-rag-from-first-principles-packt-2026-mark-peters-phd-dss-mnsec]

**The $10,000 Loop: Why Agentic AI Demands a New Playbook for Business Leaders** — Eve Psalti / Microsoft AI (en). Describes the architectural shift from static GenAI (chatbots, copilots, knowledge assistants) to fully autonomous multi-agent systems that plan, reason, call APIs and execute workflows with no human in the loop — a win for operational efficiency that also introduces a new risk paradigm leaders need a fresh playbook for (the "$10,000 loop" being an agent looping unchecked). _Why interesting:_ senior Microsoft AI executive on why agentic autonomy changes the operational/risk model. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/10000-loop-why-agentic-ai-demands-new-playbook-business-eve-psalti-aak5c]

**Article 50(1) AI Act: "You Are Talking to an AI"** — Gayanthi Gunawardhana, LL.M. / Libra Sentinel (en). From 2 August 2026 one of the AI Act's most visible duties applies: providers of AI systems meant to interact with people must design them so users are informed they are dealing with AI, unless that would already be obvious to a reasonably well-informed person — information that is clear, distinguishable, accessible, and provided no later than the first interaction. _Why interesting:_ precise, timely breakdown of a concrete AI Act transparency obligation landing in ~2 weeks. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/article-501-ai-act-you-talking-gayanthi-gunawardhana-ll-m--up1nc]

**Anthropic just published something worth reading closely** — Jon-Rav G. Shende (en). Read through an AI-governance lens of declared controls versus observed behavior, he pulls out takeaways for CIOs, CISOs, security researchers and GRC leaders. _Why interesting:_ governance-lens reading of Anthropic's latest safety/jailbreak-scoring disclosure (dated today). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/anthropic-just-published-something-worth-reading-closely-shende-cawxc]
