_Why interesting:_ Concrete cross-vendor comparison of managed agent platforms rather than vendor marketing.

A practical field guide to Agent2Agent (A2A): Google built it, donated it to the Linux Foundation, v1.0 shipped in March with a well-designed wire format — but cross-agent interoperability was never the real enterprise bottleneck.
_Why interesting:_ Skeptical, grounded take on A2A hype from an enterprise-deployment angle.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/your-agents-can-talk-never-hard-part-dr-jerry-a-smith-awxfe

**Soon Every Agent Will Have a Verifiable Identity. Then Comes the Harder Part.** — Imran Siddique (en). Agent-identity work (cryptographic credentials, attestation grades, delegation chains) is maturing across A2A/MCP. But knowing what an agent is allowed to do is not the same as enforcing it or proving what it actually did.
_Why interesting:_ Pushes past agent-identity buzz to the enforcement/audit layer that protocols leave unsolved.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/soon-every-agent-have-verifiable-identity-comes-harder-imran-siddique-yfx0f

Argues RAG and agents are both retrieval, and stateless pipes can't make stateful decisions.
_Why interesting:_ Data-backed diagnosis of why enterprise RAG/agent pilots stall at scale.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/rag-agents-both-retrieval-stateless-pipes-cant-make-alok-srivastava-niqmc

**How RAG changes the economics of AI features in a SaaS product** — Mallow Technologies (en). SaaS AI features fail in production not from model quality but from information access. Positions RAG as the structural fix, framed at the product/economics level rather than as a technical tutorial.

https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-rag-changes-economics-ai-features-5fmec

Reads the Digital Omnibus on AI (Parliament 16 Jun, Council 29 Jun) together with a parallel development, arguing the two shift high-risk AI system (HRAIS) deadlines and should be planned as a single message to governance functions.

Uses FLI's Summer 2026 AI Safety Index to assess how far GPAI signatories live up to the EU Code of Practice — the main route to demonstrating Article 55 compliance — and how far non-signatories fall short.
_Why interesting:_ Independent scorecard of frontier labs against the EU GPAI Code from an EU-policy lead.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-top-ai-companies-fall-short-eu-gpai-code-risto-uuk-vtewf

**We're Teaching AI Agents Bad Security** — Steve Wilson, Exabeam / OWASP (en). An analysis of popular open-source agents finds weak defaults and missing controls. Because developers clone samples and tutorials, every reference agent acts as a de-facto security-architecture recommendation, propagating insecure patterns.
_Why interesting:_ Names a structural cause of agent insecurity — the sample-code ecosystem itself.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/were-teaching-ai-agents-bad-security-steve-wilson-dyobc

**When the Attacker Is the AI: What 13 Real Incidents Tell Us About Securing Agentic Systems** — Ashish Mahajan, Deloitte Australia (en). A pattern analysis of 13 named, dated AI security incidents (Jun 2025–Jul 2026) mapped to MITRE ATLAS, arguing they form a coherent pattern rather than unrelated bad luck, with lessons for anyone deploying agents.
_Why interesting:_ Incident-grounded, framework-mapped view of the emerging agentic threat landscape.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-attacker-ai-what-13-real-incidents-tell-us-securing-mahajan-sxqhc

**Die neue Rechnung der Arbeit: Warum KI Aufgaben verbilligt, Erfahrung verteuert und den Nachwuchs unter Druck setzt** — Roger Basler de Roca (de). (Translated) AI rarely replaces whole professions but cheapens individual recurring screen-tasks; it only pays off when four conditions hold at once — many identical cases, digital data, clear rules, controllable errors — and labor-market pressure hits entry-level roles first.
_Why interesting:_ Sober Swiss economic framing of AI's labor impact, against the automation hype.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/die-neue-rechnung-der-arbeit-warum-ki-aufgaben-und-basler-de-roca-7brce
