**MCP in the Microsoft AI Stack: Why It Matters for Copilot, Foundry, Azure, and Enterprise Agents** — Joel Plaut (en). ~3,000-word architect's deep-dive on MCP as the AI-facing tool layer across Copilot Studio, Azure AI Foundry, Azure MCP Server, API Management (turning an existing REST estate into governed agent tools) and API Center (registry/discovery). Heavy on security discipline: least-privilege, narrow business tools over a `run_sql` chainsaw, tool descriptions as part of the model's decision process, human approval for high-impact actions, and "MCP doesn't fix bad permissions/content." _Why interesting:_ one of the most complete, code-level treatments of enterprise MCP governance rather than abstract MCP advocacy. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-microsoft-ai-stack-why-matters-copilot-foundry-azure-joel-plaut-dpxpc]

**MCP vs WebMCP: They Sound Similar. They're Not.** — Bhavy Shekhaliya (en). Disambiguates two easily-confused standards: MCP is server-to-server (connects AI to backend APIs/DBs/tools), WebMCP is an emerging browser standard letting a website expose structured actions to in-browser agents. Argues they are complementary layers (backend vs on-screen) and most AI-native products will use both, not choose one. _Why interesting:_ crisp answer to the current "is WebMCP replacing MCP?" confusion. (Author runs an MCP startup.) [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-vs-webmcp-sound-similar-theyre-bhavy-shekhaliya-fqmye]

**HTTP was not designed for autonomous agents** — Jörg Veidt / IBM (en). Makes the case for an agent-native transport/governance layer (the emerging "Agent Transfer Protocol," AGTP): HTTP moves requests but can't see which agent acts, on whose authority, with what intent, or where authority must stop. Positions MCP (tool access) and A2A (agent-to-agent tasks) as application-layer, and AGTP / an "agent gateway" as the lower infrastructural layer for persistent agent identity, delegation chains, intent semantics and audit. _Why interesting:_ nuanced, self-skeptical framing (asks whether OAuth/OpenTelemetry/signed tokens already suffice) of "the next phase of agentic AI is about authority." [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/http-designed-autonomous-agents-j%C3%B6rg-veidt-ajyoe]

**The Missing Layer in Enterprise AI: A Semantic Web for the Agentic Era** — Kingsley Uyi Idehen / OpenLink (en). Argues the missing enterprise-AI layer isn't another model but a (private/consortium/public) Semantic Web: LLMs store statistical patterns, not knowledge graphs (no explicit `:Paris :capitalOf :France` triples), so meaning, identity, provenance and governance must live in Linked Data with globally unique identifiers. Proposes AI-agent Skills become identifiable Web resources with declared IO/auth/provenance, and reframes the LLM as a "Langulator" translating language ↔ structured semantics over a graph system-of-record. _Why interesting:_ coherent symbolic-vs-statistical architecture argument that ties MCP/A2A into identity-first interoperability. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/missing-layer-enterprise-ai-semantic-web-agentic-era-idehen-sa1fc]

**Why Enterprise Architects Are the Missing Piece in Enterprise RAG Adoption** — Rishi Kesh Kumar (en). Argues RAG projects fail on architecture/governance, not model quality ("poor retrieval quality is the #1 cause of failed AI projects"). Quantifies ungoverned sprawl (5 teams each picking their own vector DB/embedding model ≈ $300–500K avoidable spend; re-embedding 5M chunks ≈ $20K) plus data-residency/leakage and regulated-industry (model-risk-management, audit-trail, fiduciary) exposure. Prescribes 5 EA artifacts: reference architecture, governance/risk framework, build-vs-buy-vs-reuse, cost model, use-case assessment. _Why interesting:_ turns "RAG governance" into concrete cost/risk numbers a CFO understands. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-enterprise-architects-missing-piece-rag-adoption-rishi-kesh-kumar-cipvc]

**Compliance as code – AI will create a whole new SaaS layer to prevent violations, not just detect them** — Jan Lynn-Matern / Emerge (en). VC thesis (building on a16z's "Everything is Compliance") that the 2026 cohort is shifting from compliance-as-checklist to runtime enforcement: a "regulation-aware SaaS layer" that blocks non-compliant actions (especially by agents) before they happen, in three parts — regulation-monitoring as policy-as-code, build-time + runtime gating, and auditability. Notes the EU AI Act high-risk zone is largely work systems (hiring, performance, monitoring) with immature incumbents, plus market data (Vanta ~$300M ARR; EU issued 13,000+ regulations 2019–24; AI Act high-risk obligations deferred again). _Why interesting:_ sharp map of where agent-era compliance tooling is heading and why Europe builds it first. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/compliance-code-ai-create-whole-new-saas-layer-just-them-lynn-matern-u2gee]

**Agentic AI will not just automate the operating model, it will redraw it** — Chris Parsons / NHS England (en). Heavily-sourced (McKinsey, MIT Sloan/BCG, OECD, NIST AI RMF, UK AI Playbook) argument that agentic AI is an org-design problem, not a tech-roadmap item — adoption is outpacing redesign ("tidal wave of adoption, trickle of strategy"). Works through decision rights (what an agent may decide/recommend/escalate/never do), workforce recomposition, continuous embedded governance, and outcome-based service redesign in the NHS; recommends a federated model (central guardrails + local innovation) and autonomy graduated by risk. _Why interesting:_ rare public-sector, citation-backed treatment of designing human–AI operating models. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agentic-ai-just-automate-operating-model-redraw-chris-parsons-9zdre]
