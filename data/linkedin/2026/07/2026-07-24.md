### MCP & agent-interoperability protocols

**Model Context Protocol Is Becoming Boring. That's the Exciting Part.** — Ravi Sankar Vemuri (en), July 21.
Argues MCP has moved past the "magical demo" phase into the real production question, becoming standardized plumbing ("USB-C for AI") rather than a novelty. Frames the July 28, 2026 spec update as a planning date, not a panic date — the story now is maturity and stability, not disruption.
_Why interesting:_ Reads MCP's transition into "boring," dependable infrastructure as the actual milestone that unlocks enterprise adoption.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/model-context-protocol-becoming-boring-thats-exciting-vemuri-5phsc

### Agent platforms, harnesses & security

**The Agent Platform Bake-Off: Anthropic vs. Vercel vs. Google vs. AWS** — Ian Jones (en), July 20.
Declares the agent loop a commodity as of this month: Anthropic ships a managed harness, AWS a managed loop literally named "Harness," Google re-engineered Agent Engine into Agent Runtime, and Vercel wraps it in durable Workflows. The 2024 differentiator — orchestration, tool executor, retry logic — is now a vendor line item, so competition moves up the stack.
_Why interesting:_ A clear side-by-side of how the four major agent platforms diverge once the loop itself is free.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agent-platform-bake-off-anthropic-vs-vercel-google-aws-ian-jones-szebc

**Understanding the Claude Agentic AI Ecosystem** — Shyam Achuthan (en), July 20.
A from-first-principles mental model of Claude's agent stack, framed around the difference between asking someone a question and asking them to *handle* something. Walks through the Claude Agent SDK loop — plan, act, observe, adjust, recover — and running agents on your own infrastructure.
_Why interesting:_ A jargon-free map of where each Claude agent primitive fits; useful for orienting enterprise teams.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/understanding-claude-agentic-ai-ecosystem-shyam-achuthan-c6ufc

**We're Teaching AI Agents Bad Security** — Steve Wilson (en), July 20.
An analysis of popular open-source agents finds weak defaults and missing controls. Because developers start from samples, tutorials and SDK examples, every reference agent is effectively a security architectural recommendation — propagating where instructions live, how tools connect, and what permissions agents inherit.
_Why interesting:_ From an OWASP/Exabeam security leader — locates agent insecurity in the copied starter code, not just the runtime.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/were-teaching-ai-agents-bad-security-steve-wilson-dyobc

### RAG, retrieval & reasoning architecture

**Enterprise RAG Needs a Knowledge Authority Layer** — Guillaume Belisle (en), July 21.
Argues that wiring an assistant to SharePoint, Drive, Confluence and wikis gives it *content*, not *trusted knowledge*. Content only becomes knowledge once the business defines ownership, applicability and validity — the "knowledge authority" layer most RAG projects never build.
_Why interesting:_ Reframes RAG failure as a governance/authority gap rather than a retrieval-quality problem.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/enterprise-rag-needs-knowledge-authority-layer-guillaume-belisle-e1ruc

**The Reasoning Control Plane (RCP): The Missing Layer in Enterprise LLM Architecture** — Dr. Chesta Sofat (en), July 20.
Contends the next architectural layer is reasoning *allocation*, not model selection: enterprises have optimized retrieval, vector DBs, semantic caches, planning agents, memory and governance — but not how much reasoning each task actually receives. Proposes an RCP to coordinate that spend across the stack.
_Why interesting:_ Names an emerging control-plane concept for governing reasoning cost and quality across complex multi-agent systems.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/reasoning-control-plane-rcp-missing-layer-enterprise-llm-sofat-wif0f

### AI governance, EU AI Act & responsible AI

**The EU AI Act Deadline Panic: Why the Rule Everyone's Racing to Meet Already Changed** — Natasha Pomelova (en), July 23.
Walks the Digital Omnibus timeline that reworked the AI Act's deadlines — Commission proposal Nov 19 2025, provisional agreement May 7, Parliament June 16, Council June 29, signed July 8 2026 — and warns that teams are racing to meet obligations whose scope and timing already shifted.
_Why interesting:_ A precise, dated account of the Omnibus amendment for compliance planning, from a pharma/GxP governance practitioner.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-act-deadline-panic-why-rule-everyones-racing-meet-pomelova-lxihc

**Microsoft's Claude connector for Purview enters public preview** — Robbert Berghuis (en), July 20.
Corrects the assumption that "Purview can't govern Claude": Claude Enterprise now appears in Purview DSPM as a named Enterprise AI app alongside M365 Copilot and ChatGPT Enterprise. Breaks down the preview's capability table (what the connector can and cannot govern) and the tenancy model that shapes its reach.
_Why interesting:_ Concrete enterprise-governance detail on bringing Claude under Microsoft data-protection/compliance tooling — relevant to regulated/Swiss estates.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/microsofts-claude-connector-purview-enters-public-preview-berghuis-hjhoc

### German-language

**Die neue Rechnung der Arbeit: Warum KI Aufgaben verbilligt, Erfahrung verteuert und den Nachwuchs unter Druck setzt** — Roger Basler de Roca (de), July 20.
Summary (EN): AI rarely replaces a whole profession — it cheapens individual recurring screen tasks, and pays off only when four conditions hold together: many identical cases, digital data, clear rules, controllable errors. The side effect is that it devalues entry-level work and squeezes junior talent while making experience more valuable.
_Why interesting:_ A disciplined, hype-free German take on AI's real labor economics and its generational effect on the talent pipeline.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/die-neue-rechnung-der-arbeit-warum-ki-aufgaben-und-basler-de-roca-7brce

**Der Verwaltungsrat der Zukunft kontrolliert nicht – er schafft Orientierung** — Tanja Schiess (de), July 18.
Summary (EN): Argues a board's role in the AI era is not to give better answers but to ask better questions. Drawing on a governance program, it reframes board work from control toward providing orientation as digitalization and AI reshape the organization's decisions.
_Why interesting:_ Swiss healthcare-governance perspective on how AI shifts board responsibility; complements the enterprise-governance cluster.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/der-verwaltungsrat-zukunft-kontrolliert-nicht-er-schafft-schiess-tevie
