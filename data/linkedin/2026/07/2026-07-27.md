### EU AI Act — the Digital Omnibus enters into force today

**The EU AI Act Just Changed. This is What Governance Professionals Should Actually Do About It** — Rotimi Olatunde Fawumi (en, 27 Jul). Tracks the exact status of Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, the Digital Omnibus on AI: signed 8 July, published in the Official Journal 24 July, entering into force 27 July 2026. Argues the extra time addresses delayed technical standards and delayed national conformity-assessment bodies, not a reduction in obligations.
_Why interesting:_ Dated today and pins the three legal dates precisely — the cleanest primary-source anchor in the pipeline for a change that lands this week.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-act-just-changed-what-governance-professionals-should-fawumi-e6nhe

**What is new in the Digital Omnibus on AI - Regulation (EU) 2026/1744** — Tudor Galos (en, 24 Jul). Clause-level walkthrough of what the Omnibus actually amends in Regulation (EU) 2024/1689: when requirements apply, which product-related systems count as high-risk, how compliance can be demonstrated, how the AI Act interacts with GDPR, who enforces, plus new provisions on AI-generated sexual abuse material. Notes Annex III high-risk deadlines (employment, education, credit, biometrics, public services) are postponed.
_Why interesting:_ Explicitly pushes back on the "EU is deregulating AI" reading with article-by-article detail rather than commentary.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-new-digital-omnibus-ai-regulation-eu-20261744-tudor-galos-cf8tf

**Conformité à l'AI Act : par où commencer avant le 2 août 2026 ?** — Stéphanie Barge (fr, 22 Jul). Reminds readers that two AI Act obligations already bind every organisation with no headcount threshold — the Article 5 prohibited-practices ban and the Article 4 AI-literacy duty — both live since 2 February 2025. From 2 August 2026 national authorities (CNIL leading in France) gain supervision and sanction powers, and the Article 50 transparency obligation becomes effective.
_Why interesting:_ The French-market enforcement view, and a useful reminder that Articles 4 and 5 have been in force for 18 months already.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/conformit%C3%A9-%C3%A0-lai-act-par-o%C3%B9-commencer-avant-le-2-ao%C3%BBt-st%C3%A9phanie-barge-tlbhe

_Why interesting:_ The DACH-specific supervisory angle — German national enforcement machinery going live is not covered in the English-language sources. Note: the newsletter carries a compliance-product promotion alongside the news.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ki-news-f%C3%BCr-unternehmen-der-dach-region-kw-31-27072026-hilker-j5hze

### Agent governance, oversight and assurance

**Governing Agentic AI: A Framework for Risk, Oversight and Assurance** — Joseph Lam (en, 22 Jul). Sets out why agentic governance has to be enforced at the moment an agent plans, calls a tool, or hands off to another agent — not asserted in policy documents. Flags the layered-ecosystem problem: platform providers, tool developers, third-party SaaS agents and open-source frameworks all sit in one organisation's agent stack, each interface carrying its own risk.
_Why interesting:_ Runtime enforcement vs. paper policy is the distinction most governance frameworks still blur, and it maps onto the same "control plane" theme the pipeline has been tracking.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/governing-agentic-ai-framework-risk-oversight-joseph-lam-sky--5ntjc

**Your Bank Has AI Agents - Who Governs the Agents, and Who on Your Board Has Sufficient Background to Question Them?** — Jeff Lavine (en, 27 Jul). Applies model-risk-management discipline from banking supervision to AI agents, arguing failures come from absent monitoring and broken signal escalation rather than absent controls. Cites a 2026 survey finding 54% of boards have not placed AI governance in their top five priorities.
_Why interesting:_ Directly relevant to a Swiss banking context — treats agents as a model-risk category with existing supervisory machinery rather than a novel problem.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/your-bank-has-ai-agents-who-governs-board-question-them-jeff-lavine-f2wvc

**The Agent Handoff** — Ravi Shankar NRK (en, 21 Jul). Argues the agent-to-agent boundary is simultaneously the busiest and least governed crossing in an agentic system, and that closing it needs no new theory — only handoff validity, execution fidelity and ledger discipline applied at the smallest crossing.
_Why interesting:_ A governance-runtime treatment of A2A handoff that stays concrete about what is actually validated at the boundary.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agent-handoff-ravi-shankar-nrk-k7lof

### Agent security

**Self-State Attacks: Your AI Agent's Memory Files Are Its Biggest Vulnerability** — Travis Lelle (en, 21 Jul). Summarises a new KAUST paper formalising a threat class where agents are compromised through their own configuration and memory files. The core result: when an agent poisons its own memory the syscall, permissions and file path are identical to normal operation, so OS-level defences have a hard detection ceiling. The researchers ran 43 attack operations against a live agent.
_Why interesting:_ A proof of a defence ceiling, not just an attack demo — it says memory integrity has to be handled above the kernel, which changes where controls belong.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/self-state-attacks-your-ai-agents-memory-files-its-biggest-lelle-01xfe

**Beyond Prompt Guardrails: Why AI Agents Need Runtime Detection and Enforcement** — Timeplus (en, 24 Jul). Makes the case that prompt-level guardrails cannot answer the agent-era question: whether an agent is using an unexpected tool, touching sensitive data, following instructions hidden in retrieved content, or taking a risky action after several individually harmless steps.
_Why interesting:_ The multi-step-escalation framing — harmless steps composing into a risky action — is the failure mode that per-prompt filtering structurally cannot see.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/beyond-prompt-guardrails-why-ai-agents-need-runtime-detection-sen7c

Walks through the chunk-embed-vector-store pipeline, names its structural drawbacks, and argues an LLM-wiki approach (crediting an idea from Andrej Karpathy) may serve agents better than retrieval tuning.
_Why interesting:_ Part of a visible shift this week from "optimise retrieval" to "restructure the knowledge store" — pairs with the agent-wiki and enterprise-memory threads already in the pipeline.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/still-optimizing-your-rag-agents-well-think-again-llm-david-uhlmann-sfkce

**The Ground-Truth Stack: A 6-Layer Architecture to Make LLM Hallucinations Structurally Impossible.** — Manoj Mohan (en, 22 Jul). Layer-by-layer blueprint arguing enterprise hallucination is an access problem, not a model-capability problem: the fix is structured, verifiable access to internal data before generation, not a stronger model.
_Why interesting:_ Treats grounding as an architectural precondition rather than a post-hoc check — a concrete alternative to guardrail-and-eval approaches.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ground-truth-stack-6-layer-architecture-make-llm-impossible-mohan-odw4c

### Evaluation

**Agentic Evaluation** — Michael Lively (en, 23 Jul). Defines agentic evaluation as measuring reasoning, planning, tool selection, execution and adaptation across the whole trajectory, because small errors compound over multi-step workflows. Combines trace-based observability, deterministic checks, LLM-as-a-judge and human-verified datasets.
_Why interesting:_ Argues trajectory-level evaluation, not output-level scoring, is the unit of measurement for agents — with the four method families named.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agentic-evaluation-michael-lively-h27pc

### Adoption and operating model

**Dein bester KI-Mitarbeiter ist dein größtes Risiko. Und bremst dein ganzes Unternehmen aus** — Dr. Lukas Huber (de, 23 Jul). Argues most companies do not have an AI problem but a sharing problem and a joint-working problem. Describes three levels of organisational AI use and two breaking points: level 1 fails the moment you try to share, level 2 fails the moment sharing is not enough and people must act together.
_Why interesting:_ Locates the failure at the transitions between individual, shared and collective use rather than at tooling or licences — a diagnosis that explains stalled rollouts better than capability arguments.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/dein-bester-ki-mitarbeiter-ist-gr%C3%B6%C3%9Ftes-risiko-und-bremst-huber-gng8f
