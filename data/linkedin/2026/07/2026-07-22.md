## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**MCP becomes governed infrastructure inside SAP Integration Suite and SAP BTP** — Gaurav Singh (en, 2026-07-22)
The MCP Gateway in SAP Integration Suite reached general availability, and a wave of SAP guidance reframes Model Context Protocol as a governed integration surface — authentication, scoped tokens, rate limiting, audit — rather than an ungoverned agent shortcut. The author frames the week's story as architectural rather than a CVE.
_Why interesting:_ A major ERP vendor treating MCP as governed infrastructure is the strongest enterprise-adoption signal for the protocol so far.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-becomes-governed-infrastructure-inside-sap-suite-btp-gaurav-singh-kk96e

**Two gateways every secure Enterprise AI system needs** — Sukrit Goel (en, 2026-07-21)
Separates the reasoning path (application → LLM) from the execution path (agent → tool/data via MCP), arguing these carry different risks and need two distinct control planes: an LLM Gateway and an MCP Gateway.
_Why interesting:_ Clean architectural distinction that pairs directly with the SAP MCP Gateway GA above — controlling what agents may access is now as urgent as controlling model interactions.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/two-gateways-every-secure-enterprise-ai-system-needs-sukrit-goel-vzprc

**Soon Every Agent Will Have a Verifiable Identity. Then Comes the Harder Part.** — Imran Siddique, OPAQUE Systems (en, 2026-07-20)
Verifiable agent identity — cryptographic credentials, attestation grades, delegation chains — is advancing fast, but knowing what an agent is allowed to do is not the same as enforcing it or proving what it actually did.
_Why interesting:_ Names the enforcement and attestation gap that A2A and MCP explicitly leave out, from someone building that layer.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/soon-every-agent-have-verifiable-identity-comes-harder-imran-siddique-yfx0f

**The multi-agent mess finally has a control plane.** — Jothi Moorthy (en, 2026-07-16)
A client with agents everywhere and no clean way to govern them is standing up a dedicated governance team to observe every agent in the company. Enumerates the real surface beyond prompt injection and jailbreaking: what data each agent touches, what actions it may take, whether it leaks quietly.
_Why interesting:_ Field report on the org structure emerging around agent governance, not a vendor pitch.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/multi-agent-mess-finally-has-control-plane-jothi-moorthy-dqgwc

**Inside the Enterprise Retrieval Pipeline: How Query Rewriting, Hybrid Search, Reranking, and Context Assembly Improve LLM Accuracy** — advansappz (en, 2026-07-16)
Two AI applications can use the same language model, the same embedding model, and the same knowledge repository while producing significantly different results; the difference lies in how information is retrieved, filtered, ranked and assembled before reaching the model.
_Why interesting:_ At ~50k characters, the most substantial RAG engineering reference published on Pulse this week — a genuine pipeline walkthrough, not a listicle.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/inside-enterprise-retrieval-pipeline-how-query-rewriting-hybrid-tc1of

**Your RAG Pipeline Has a Parsing Problem. The Vector Database Isn't Going to Fix It.** — João Fernandes, DocDigitizer (en, 2026-07-19)
Teams spend weeks evaluating vector databases while their PDF parser quietly destroys the document structure that makes retrieval work. The parsing step decides more RAG outcomes than the vector database ever will.
_Why interesting:_ Contrarian and concrete — argues a mediocre embedding over well-structured chunks beats a state-of-the-art embedding over mangled text.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/your-rag-pipeline-has-parsing-problem-vector-database-jo%C3%A3o-fernandes-xhs2e

**How Top AI Companies Fall Short of the EU GPAI Code** — Risto Uuk, Future of Life Institute (en, 2026-07-22)
The EU's General-Purpose AI Code of Practice is currently the main route by which providers of GPAI models with systemic risk demonstrate Article 55 compliance. Assesses how well signatories live up to their commitments, drawing on FLI's AI Safety Index (Summer 2026 edition).
_Why interesting:_ From FLI's Head of European Policy; measures actual company behaviour against Article 55 rather than restating the law, eleven days before full enforcement.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-top-ai-companies-fall-short-eu-gpai-code-risto-uuk-vtewf

From 2 August 2026 the majority of EU AI Act regulatory requirements become fully enforceable, moving AI governance from an innovation initiative to a board-level responsibility.
_Why interesting:_ The deadline is eleven days out; the piece is aimed squarely at executives who have not yet inventoried their exposure.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/august-2-2026-eu-ai-act-enters-full-enforcement-while-vinic

**Der AI Spine: Wie Online KI vom Experiment zu messbaren Ergebnissen skaliert hat** — Balz Zuerrer (de, 2026-07-16)
Reports on "Create Generative AI Value at Scale" in MIT Sloan Management Review by Kevin Schmitt and Ivo Blohm (Universität St. Gallen) with Gregory Vial (HEC Montréal): durable AI success comes not from individual use cases but from an organisational structure — the "AI Spine" — that scales AI across core processes and business units. Based on a three-year study of 23 Swiss companies.
_Why interesting:_ Swiss-specific and evidence-based (23 Swiss firms, HSG research) rather than anecdotal — directly relevant to scaling AI inside a Swiss enterprise.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/der-ai-spine-wie-online-ki-vom-experiment-zu-skaliert-balz-zuerrer-x9vff

**Warum Unternehmen strukturierte Governance-Daten für LLMs und AI Agents benötigen** — Marcus Belke, 2B Advice (de, 2026-07-21)
A language model can process and phrase information but does not automatically know which data is current, approved, confidential, or accessible to a given user. Agents therefore need traceable enterprise context: roles, responsibilities, risks, approvals, relationships, versions, validity periods and documented decision histories, structured and interlinked.
_Why interesting:_ The longest German-language piece of the week; frames AI governance as a data-structure problem rather than a policy-document problem.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/warum-unternehmen-strukturierte-governance-daten-f%C3%BCr-llms-belke-tiple

**Wenn bei KI-Agenten die Leitplanken fehlen** — Christian Scharrer, Dell Technologies (de, 2026-07-22)
The first corporate AI agent now takes only weeks to build, and enthusiasm in business units is high — then the uncomfortable questions arrive: where does the data come from, why does the same query return different results, who actually controls what it decides. The technology outpaces the structures meant to operate it safely.
_Why interesting:_ German-language framing of agent guardrails from an infrastructure architect, published today.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/wenn-bei-ki-agenten-die-leitplanken-fehlen-christian-scharrer-i14re

**KI-Verordnung: Welche Pflichten Unternehmen je nach Rolle und Risiko prüfen müssen** — Nils Möllers, Keyed (de, 2026-07-16)
Which obligations apply depends on the company's role as provider or deployer, the intended purpose, and the risk class of the AI system. Companies should inventory their AI systems, assess risks and derive transparency, documentation and training duties; where personal data is processed, GDPR continues to apply alongside AI governance.
_Why interesting:_ Practical role-based obligation mapping instead of generic AI Act commentary — and it explicitly pushes back on the assumption that every AI use is high-risk.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ki-verordnung-welche-pflichten-unternehmen-je-nach-rolle-nils-m%C3%B6llers-9ysge
