**Le RAG n'a (presque) rien inventé** — Eric Blaudez (lang: fr). Long-form technical essay arguing RAG is not a 2023 rupture but the integration of 50 years of information retrieval (BM25, TREC, Rocchio 1971, Bates' berrypicking) into LLMs; the only genuine novelty is the *reasoning loop* (retrieve → reason → retrieve), so retrieval is becoming a commodity while value migrates to the orchestrator. Walks through agentic search (Claude Code dropping vector RAG for model-driven grep; Amazon AAAI 2026), context rot (Chroma), GraphRAG/LazyGraphRAG economics, agent memory (Mem0/Zep), Cartridges/Engram, and PoisonedRAG security — with hard numbers (17–33% legal-tool hallucination, 96% referral-traffic collapse, BrowseComp 51.5% vs <10%) and 22 academic references. _Why interesting:_ the single most substantive RAG piece of the week — a rigorous, citation-heavy corrective to the RAG hype cycle, dated today; exactly the topic-over-author signal that matters. **ISO 42001 and the EU AI Act: They're Not Competing. They Never Were.** — Fernando Moreno Alvarez (lang: en).
