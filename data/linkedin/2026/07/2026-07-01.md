## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**MCP in the Enterprise: From integration headache to agentic infrastructure** — Shriram Vasudevan (lang: en). Reviews MCP's 18-month arc from a developer convenience to enterprise plumbing: N×M connectors collapse to N+M, SDK downloads up ~1000×, governance moved to the Agentic AI Foundation. Finance/healthcare lead adoption; the four-layer model (host, client, gateway, servers) and a mandatory gateway are what separate production from pilot. _Why interesting:_ Grounds the MCP hype with concrete governance practices and Gartner's caution that 40%+ of agentic projects risk cancellation by 2027. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-enterprise-from-integration-headache-agentic-shriram-rzcrf](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-enterprise-from-integration-headache-agentic-shriram-rzcrf)

**Governing the Agentic Mesh: Why agentgateway and MCP Belong Together** — Shriram Vasudevan (lang: en). Separates the "language problem" (MCP: how agents discover/call tools) from the "control problem" (agentgateway: who may call, how often, provably). MCP is the protocol; agentgateway is the governed data plane that applies auth, rate limits, observability and audit at one chokepoint. _Why interesting:_ Clean architectural framing of the MCP + gateway pairing as the pragmatic open-source foundation for governed agent fleets. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/governing-agentic-mesh-why-agentgateway-mcp-belong-shriram-lmdxc](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/governing-agentic-mesh-why-agentgateway-mcp-belong-shriram-lmdxc)

**Agents Deserve Their Own Transport, and AGTP Refuses to Pretend Otherwise** — Chris Hood (lang: en). Argues HTTP was built for documents and present humans, not delegated agents acting at machine speed with authority granted earlier; proposes AGTP as a purpose-built transport substrate on which MCP and A2A become tenants rather than rivals. _Why interesting:_ A contrarian, foundational take on agent interoperability — questions whether the whole stack should inherit HTTP's assumptions at all. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agents-deserve-own-transport-agtp-refuses-pretend-otherwise-hood-cexjc](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agents-deserve-own-transport-agtp-refuses-pretend-otherwise-hood-cexjc)

**The Harness Is the Engineering: Why AI Agents Live or Die by Their Loop** — Pravesh Karn (Razorpay) (lang: en). Deep treatment of "the harness" (execution layer) as the real engineering surface: contrasts three philosophies (Ralph brute-force loop, Superpowers methodology-as-context, GStack opinionated-roles-plus-guardrails), citing LangChain's +13.7pt Terminal-Bench jump with the model held fixed. Verification-before-completion and bounded retries as backpressure. _Why interesting:_ Rigorous, sourced argument that agent reliability is distributed-systems engineering, not prompting — one of the strongest technical long-reads this week. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/harness-engineering-why-ai-agents-live-die-loop-pravesh-karn-rybpc](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/harness-engineering-why-ai-agents-live-die-loop-pravesh-karn-rybpc)

**Your RAG Pipeline Isn't a Retrieval Problem. It's a Grounding Problem.** — Rahul Jain (HCLTech) (lang: en). Names four production RAG failure modes (chunking mismatch, embedding gap, context flood, retrieval blindness) and fixes for each: semantic/hierarchical chunking, benchmarking embeddings on real queries, retrieve-more-pass-less with cross-encoder re-ranking, and retrieval observability. _Why interesting:_ Concrete, field-tested checklist that reframes RAG quality as an upstream grounding problem rather than a model choice. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/your-rag-pipeline-isnt-retrieval-problem-its-grounding-rahul-jain-syirc](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/your-rag-pipeline-isnt-retrieval-problem-its-grounding-rahul-jain-syirc)

**Why RAG is Failing: The Shift from Retrieval to AI Reasoning** — Mirko Peters (lang: en). Argues RAG hits a "latency wall" on liquid (real-time) context vs. static documents; proposes Microsoft's Work IQ, Agent 365 and per-agent Entra Agent IDs over shared service accounts, plus A2A workflows for governed agent collaboration. _Why interesting:_ Microsoft-ecosystem counterpoint to the RAG-is-enough view; the "service account trap" and per-agent identity argument are the useful governance nuggets. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-rag-failing-shift-from-retrieval-ai-reasoning-mirko-peters-bbtle](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/why-rag-failing-shift-from-retrieval-ai-reasoning-mirko-peters-bbtle)

**The EU AI Act's New Transparency Code: More Nuance, Less Panic** — Birgit J. Oberer (lang: en). Corrects the over-reading that every AI-generated image now needs a visible label: the new Code of Practice on Transparency is a voluntary framework helping comply with the legally binding Article 50; distinguishes provider vs. deployer duties, and notes optional (non-mandatory) icon sets. _Why interesting:_ Sober, accurate reading of a headline-distorted governance topic, with the provider/deployer split spelled out. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-acts-new-transparency-code-more-nuance-less-panic-oberer-gwddf](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-acts-new-transparency-code-more-nuance-less-panic-oberer-gwddf)

**KI-Governance im ISMS: ISO 42001 pragmatisch integrieren** — Swiss Infosec AG (lang: de-CH). Argues organisations with an existing ISO 27001 ISMS should extend it with ISO/IEC 42001 rather than build a parallel structure: add an AI inventory, fold AI-specific risks (bias, hallucination, model drift, manipulation) into existing risk management, extend dev/ops and incident-response processes, and note Swiss DSG duties (transparency, processor contracts, DPIA for high-risk). Proportional, risk-based approach for SMEs. _Why interesting:_ Swiss-specific, pragmatic governance playbook tying ISO 42001 to an existing ISMS and DSG obligations. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ki-governance-im-isms-iso-42001-pragmatisch-integrieren-5d3me](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ki-governance-im-isms-iso-42001-pragmatisch-integrieren-5d3me)
