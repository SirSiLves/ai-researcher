## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**MCP on AGTP: Adding the Layer MCP Was Missing** — Chris Hood (lang: en). Argues MCP deliberately scopes itself to the tool-call message and punts on identity, transport security, audit, scope/budget enforcement and discovery — and that an AGTP (Agent Transfer Protocol) gateway can wrap an *unmodified* MCP server to add cryptographic agent identity, Authority-Scope/Budget-Limit enforcement, signed attribution records, mTLS and a canonical `agtp://` address discoverable via an Agent Name Service. _Why interesting:_ Concrete, layered take on the MCP-vs-substrate debate — treats MCP, A2A, ACP as application protocols riding a shared governance transport, with a working verb-mapping gateway. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-agtp-adding-layer-missing-chris-hood-lq16c](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-agtp-adding-layer-missing-chris-hood-lq16c)

**Pick Your Agent Control Plane Before It Picks You** — Marc Kermisch (lang: en). Surveys ~60 days of enterprise announcements (late Mar–end May 2026) and argues the agent itself has been commoditized while the *governance/control plane* wrapped around proliferating agents has become the strategic battleground: Microsoft Agent 365, ServiceNow AI Control Tower, Salesforce Agent Fabric, SAP Joule Studio + Claude as reasoning engine, Google Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, AWS Bedrock AgentCore Policy. Also flags MCP RCE vulnerabilities (OX Security; CIS MCP Companion Guide) and runtime-vs-model lock-in. _Why interesting:_ Best single map this week of how every major enterprise vendor converged on agent governance in the same window; actionable buyer guidance. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/pick-your-agent-control-plane-before-picks-you-marc-kermisch-eoz9c](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/pick-your-agent-control-plane-before-picks-you-marc-kermisch-eoz9c)

**The forcing function disappeared: what the AI Act Omnibus extension really did to enterprise AI governance** — Marko Markovic (lang: en). On 7 May 2026 the Council presidency and Parliament reached political agreement on the AI Act Omnibus, slipping the high-risk compliance deadline (for AI in regulated products) to 2 August 2028. Argues most enterprise "AI governance" was a deadline with staff attached, not a real control, drawing parallels to Basel II / MiFID II / GDPR timeline slips, and that the orchestration/audit boundary is deadline-invariant because the accountability question arrives on the timetable of the next disputed decision. _Why interesting:_ Sharp governance angle on the Omnibus delay; reframes compliance as an operating capability rather than a calendar event. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/forcing-function-disappeared-what-ai-act-omnibus-did-marko-3nuve](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/forcing-function-disappeared-what-ai-act-omnibus-did-marko-3nuve)

**What Happens When AI Absorbs the Enterprise Layer?** — Mahmoud Dasser (lang: en). Traces the Q1–Q2 2026 repricing of enterprise software (S&P Software & Services Index down ~20%) to the rise of an "orchestration layer" sitting above foundation models and below applications, where agents/models/data are sequenced into business processes. Microsoft enters from above (Copilot), Anthropic/OpenAI from below (Claude deployed across US banks; Goldman embedding Anthropic engineers), incumbents (Salesforce, ServiceNow, SAP, Workday) respond, and small teams like Manus reach global scale. Frames orchestration control as an AI-sovereignty question for nations. _Why interesting:_ Connects market repricing, the agent stack, and sovereignty into one thesis; argues infrastructure was the wrong layer to fight on. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-happens-when-ai-absorbs-enterprise-layer-mahmoud-dasser-byyue](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-happens-when-ai-absorbs-enterprise-layer-mahmoud-dasser-byyue)

**MCP: The Quiet Protocol That's Rewriting What It Means to Build a Product** — Luciano Lykkebo (lang: en). Frames MCP as "USB-C for AI": donated to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation (Dec 2025) with Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Block backing, ~97M monthly SDK downloads, and shipping MCP servers from Stripe, Figma, Atlassian, Notion, MS Dynamics, PayPal. Argues product managers now define machine-readable capabilities for agents (what an agent can do/read, what needs human approval) and that products invisible to agents will vanish from agent workflows. _Why interesting:_ Product-strategy lens on MCP adoption rather than the usual dev framing; cites Figma Dev Mode MCP / Affirm rebuild as evidence. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-quiet-protocol-thats-rewriting-what-means-build-product-lykkebo-fq93e](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-quiet-protocol-thats-rewriting-what-means-build-product-lykkebo-fq93e)

**Zukunftsfähigkeit im Triadenwettbewerb – USA ● China ● Deutschland (Teil 2)** — Rainer E. Ulrich (lang: de). Long-form strategic analysis of the US AI economy: hyperscaler capex (~$427B in 2025, 2026 forecasts revised to $800–900B, possibly >$1T by 2027), Stargate, ~75% of global AI VC, NVIDIA/TSMC compute concentration and the Taiwan/Hormuz supply-chain risk, the America's AI Action Plan deregulation push, the energy bottleneck ($1.4T utility investment), and the gap between US investment leadership and weak enterprise adoption (rank 24, 28.3%). Heavily footnoted (Stanford HAI, Goldman, OECD, Brookings, Belfer). _Why interesting:_ Rare deeply-sourced German-language strategic survey; the capex-on-credit / debt-financed-infrastructure thread is the standout argument. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/zukunftsf%C3%A4higkeit-im-triadenwettbewerb-usa-china-teil-ulrich-jfjff](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/zukunftsf%C3%A4higkeit-im-triadenwettbewerb-usa-china-teil-ulrich-jfjff)
