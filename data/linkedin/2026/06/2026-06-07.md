**Designing MCP Servers That Don't Become a New Mess** — William Collins (en). Six design principles for production MCP servers: outcome-first tools (not endpoint wrappers), aggressive surface-area curation, bounded context resources, prompt-as-SOP discipline, deterministic guardrails, and failure handling. _Why interesting:_ Concrete, practitioner-level critique of the "expose every API as a tool" anti-pattern, grounded in real infra-ops deployments. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/designing-mcp-servers-dont-become-new-mess-william-collins-eenge](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/designing-mcp-servers-dont-become-new-mess-william-collins-eenge)

**MCP vs. Agent Skills: Why the "Either/Or" Crowd Is Missing the Point** — Csaba Tamas (en). Argues MCP (operational reach) and Skills (institutional judgment) are complementary, not competing; explains how Claude Code's Jan-2026 progressive tool discovery closed the token-cost gap that made Skills look like a replacement. _Why interesting:_ Clear, current synthesis of the MCP/Skills debate with hard token economics and the Linux Foundation governance angle. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-vs-agent-skills-why-eitheror-crowd-missing-point-csaba-tamas-l464f](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-vs-agent-skills-why-eitheror-crowd-missing-point-csaba-tamas-l464f)

**As AI agents evolve, we need to look past the RAG pipeline** — AlphaSignal / Ben Dickson (en). Introduces Direct Corpus Interaction (DCI) and the GrepSeek framework: giving agents terminal tools (grep/find/cat) over raw corpora instead of vector retrieval, with a hybrid semantic-then-DCI architecture for large corpora. _Why interesting:_ Names a concrete emerging alternative to embedding-based RAG for agentic/coding workloads, with benchmark and cost claims. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-agents-evolve-we-need-look-past-rag-pipeline-alphasignal-ei5of](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-agents-evolve-we-need-look-past-rag-pipeline-alphasignal-ei5of)

**From Paragraphs to Graphs: Architecting the Memory Layer for Autonomous Agents** — Norman Lim, VP @ DBS Bank (en). "Shape-native context engineering": match storage to data shape — Weaviate for prose, PostgreSQL/text-to-SQL for tables, Neo4j/Cypher for relational lineage, tree traversal for hierarchical docs — with a working reference repo. _Why interesting:_ Deep, code-level enterprise blueprint from a bank platform lead showing where flat vector RAG fails on tabular and graph data. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/from-paragraphs-graphs-architecting-memory-layer-autonomous-lim-dlg3c](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/from-paragraphs-graphs-architecting-memory-layer-autonomous-lim-dlg3c)

**When AI Agents Stop Speaking Human** — Rahmat Wibowo (en). Surveys the agent-communication stack — MCP (vertical/JSON-RPC), A2A (horizontal/Agent Cards, now LF AAIF-governed) — and the research frontier beyond JSON: emergent codes, latent-space communication, and Agora's self-organizing protocols; plus the observability/governance gap. _Why interesting:_ Unusually well-referenced map of where agent interoperability protocols are heading, with the audit/oversight implications spelled out. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-ai-agents-stop-speaking-human-rahmat-wibowo-eneic](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-ai-agents-stop-speaking-human-rahmat-wibowo-eneic)

**One Control Environment, Three Regulators: The Build Logic for Cross-Regime AI Governance** — Alena Elmer (en). Operating playbook for governing AI across US (model-risk carve-out, self-govern), EU (high-risk deferral to Dec 2027, Aug 2026 still legally live), and Swiss FINMA (principles-first): build to the strictest regime, treat carve-outs as obligations, separate the clock from the control. _Why interesting:_ Practitioner GRC view directly relevant to a Swiss financial-institution context; concrete cross-regime control design, not theory. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/one-control-environment-three-regulators-build-logic-ai-alena-elmer-6bvme](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/one-control-environment-three-regulators-build-logic-ai-alena-elmer-6bvme)

Post-Omnibus reading: the early-May agreement clarified high-risk deferral, SME exemptions and watermarking, but the text could still be reopened before the 2 Aug deadline; flags international fragmentation across 13 jurisdictions and the EU's heavy-handed posture. _Why interesting:_ Concise governance-watcher update on EU AI Act Omnibus status and the cross-border coordination problem.
