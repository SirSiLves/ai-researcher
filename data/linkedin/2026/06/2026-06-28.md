## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**MCP + A2A: The New Operating Layer for Enterprise Agentic AI** — Sanjay Kumar (en). Argues MCP (agent-to-tool) and A2A (agent-to-agent) are becoming the connective tissue of enterprise agentic AI; walks through the full production stack — orchestrator, specialist agents, Agent Cards, Contract-Net bidding, workload identity, least-privilege, audit, AgentOps. _Why interesting:_ unusually complete, vendor-neutral architecture map of where the agent stack is converging. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-a2a-new-operating-layer-enterprise-agentic-ai-sanjay--smdqc]

**Governing the Agentic Mesh: Why agentgateway and MCP Belong Together** — Shriram Vasudevan (en). MCP is the protocol (the contract); agentgateway is the data plane (the control point). Routing all agent traffic through one governed chokepoint gives auth, rate limits, observability, audit, and protocol normalisation across MCP/A2A/HTTP. _Why interesting:_ clean separation of the "language problem" vs "control problem" and a concrete incremental adoption path. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/governing-agentic-mesh-why-agentgateway-mcp-belong-shriram-lmdxc]

**What 'is' Agent Identity? Human? Workload? A new Layer?** — Christian Posta, Solo.io (en). Dissects whether agent identity is workload identity or a new layer; SPIFFE/kagent collapse them 1:1, but bursty/ephemeral actors (Google agent substrate, AWS AgentCore, Entra Agent ID, AAuth) need a registry-maintained identity layer on top. _Why interesting:_ rigorous treatment of class-vs-instance identity and the "permissions narrow, never widen" invariant. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-agent-identity-human-workload-new-layer-christian-posta-qdamc]

**The AI Agent Identity Landscape: Seven Lanes, 38 Players, One Question** — Chris Hood (en). Maps 38 products/protocols/standards bodies into seven lanes (Enterprise IAM, workload identity, CIAM auth, NHI security, decentralized/DID, protocols, governance bodies). Convergence on first-class agent identity, but fragmentation across lanes. _Why interesting:_ the missing layer he names — governing agent *conduct*, not just verifying the identifier. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-agent-identity-landscape-seven-lanes-38-players-one-chris-hood-4avqc]

**RAG Is Dead. Long Live RAG.** — Vipool Prajapati, EY (en). Naive single-vector RAG dies; long-context windows don't rescue you (cost, mid-context recall sag, freshness, defensibility). Real discipline is context engineering: router + hybrid dense/lexical/taxonomy retrieval + reranker + context graphs for relational queries + capturing "decision context". _Why interesting:_ concrete contract-clause worked example showing why three retrieval mechanisms are needed over one corpus. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/rag-dead-long-live-vipool-prajapati-0znbc]

**The U-Curve Trap: Why Bigger Context Windows Are Making AI Agents Dumber** — Rama Maddi (en). The U-curve attention effect (models attend to start/end, miss the middle) means dumping whole codebases degrades accuracy. Fixes: context engines, hierarchical summarization, knowledge graphs, an 80/20 reasoning/validation compute split, judge-node multi-agent review. _Why interesting:_ pairs well with the RAG piece — same context-budget thesis from the agent-engineering side. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/u-curve-trap-why-bigger-context-windows-making-ai-agents-rama-maddi-iluhc]

**Is ISO 42001 Really about AI "Governance"?** — Aron Lange, GRC Lab (en). Argues ISO 42001 is a *management* system (AIMS), not governance; using ISO 37000's direct/oversee/account framing, walks each clause sorting governance vs management. Genuine governance content sits only in Clause 5 + framing of Clause 6; the standard assumes a governing-body layer above the AIMS that it never provides. _Why interesting:_ corrects a pervasive market mislabel; useful for board-level AI accountability. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/iso-42001-really-ai-governance-aron-lange-knmce]

**When the EU Rewrites Its AI Law Before It Takes Effect** — Ivan Lebedev (en, Swiss Monday newsletter). On the 16 June 2026 Parliament vote (423–57) on the Digital Omnibus, postponing and rewriting high-risk AI Act obligations before they bite; "simplification" also changes *what*, not just *when* (registration/transparency narrowed, sensitive-data processing broadened). CSAM/NCII prohibition keeps a firm 2 Dec 2026 date. _Why interesting:_ Swiss-angle compliance-planning view — how to build against a flagship law still in motion; FADP applies to AI regardless. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-eu-rewrites-its-ai-law-before-takes-effect-ivan-dmuge]

**SAP and Anthropic: the prerequisite behind the AI hype** — Codestone Group (en). On SAP Sapphire's 200+ AI agents / 50+ Joule Assistants and Claude as primary reasoning layer via the SAP Business AI Platform (MCP for connectivity). The unspoken prerequisite: "clean core" — accurate data, standardised processes, minimal customisation — without which agents fail unpredictably. LC Waikiki cited at 70% efficiency gain. _Why interesting:_ reframes the SAP–Anthropic deal around data/process readiness rather than the model. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/sap-anthropic-prerequisite-behind-ai-hype-codestone-group-aryqe]

**Glitch Monday: Wie eine halbe Billion Dollar verdampfte (Projecter Weekly #25)** — Katja von der Burg (de, newsletter). German weekly recap: a "Glitch Monday" wiping ~half a trillion in tech market cap amid doubts on AI capex payback (Alphabet -6%/$256B); McKinsey "Rewiring Retail in Europe" pegs EUR 240–320B AI value potential; DeepMind's "AI Control Roadmap" (dual-pedal driving-instructor model for agents); OpenAI "Patch the Planet" / GPT-5.5-Cyber; Microsoft Copilot Cowork undercutting Claude Cowork 30–40% on price. _Why interesting:_ German-language pulse on AI market sentiment + several real product/governance items. [https://de.linkedin.com/pulse/glitch-monday-wie-eine-halbe-billion-dollar-projecter-von-der-burg-7ajic]
