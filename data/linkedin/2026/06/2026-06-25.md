## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**MCP in Production: Why Your AI Agents Need a Gateway Before They Hit the Real World** — Suraj Pillai (Kong), lang: en. Argues MCP solved connectivity, not governance, and walks through gateway-layer auth (Key Auth/OIDC/MCP OAuth2), per-tool ACLs, RFC 8693 token exchange for agent-to-agent delegation, and four Kong deployment patterns. _Why interesting:_ Concrete, vendor-grounded answer to the "MCP is great in the demo, now make it production-safe" gap, with a real production checklist. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-production-why-your-ai-agents-need-gateway-before-suraj-pillai-j8exf](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-production-why-your-ai-agents-need-gateway-before-suraj-pillai-j8exf)

**Security for Agentic AI in SAP** — Christoph Nagy (SecurityBridge), lang: en. Maps SAP's Sapphire 2026 agent stack (50+ Joule Assistants, 200+ agents, Agent Gateway, A2A, MCP, NVIDIA OpenShell sandboxing) against five concrete security gaps: SoD breaks down, audit logging unfit for autonomous actors, prompt injection via MCP ("Log4Shell-like blind spot"), the agent-to-agent assurance gap (SAP's agent↔tool/MCP enforcement point only planned H2/2026), and immature non-human identity lifecycle. _Why interesting:_ One of the most concrete agent-security pieces tied to a named enterprise roadmap; explicitly flags MCP as an under-inventoried dependency risk. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/security-agentic-ai-sap-christoph-nagy-94gle](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/security-agentic-ai-sap-christoph-nagy-94gle)

**5: Integrating Agents Is Still Integration** — Patryk Bandurski (MuleSoft Ambassador), lang: en. Field notes on MuleSoft Agent Fabric: A2A 0.3.0 strictly enforced (one missing `"kind":"message"` field broke his connector), Omni Gateway as governance layer, and the v1→v2 gap — v2 adds deterministic workflow steps *inside* the agent loop. _Why interesting:_ Practitioner's "integrating agents is still integration" thesis plus an honest v1 limitation (an agent reasoned past a stated rule, requiring a deterministic guard outside the loop). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/5-integrating-agents-still-integration-patryk-bandurski-gmfcf](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/5-integrating-agents-still-integration-patryk-bandurski-gmfcf)

**When the EU Rewrites Its AI Law Before It Takes Effect** — Ivan Lebedev (Swiss Monday newsletter), lang: en. Parliament's 16 June first-reading approval of the Digital Omnibus on AI (423-57-174) postpones *and* rewrites the AI Act before high-risk obligations bite; argues the change is to substance (bias-detection data processing, registration/transparency) not just timing, and frames the Swiss medtech compliance-planning dilemma against a moving reference law. _Why interesting:_ Swiss-anchored, legally careful take on planning compliance "against a rumor with a date on it"; notes Swiss FDPA already applies to AI regardless of any AI-specific statute. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-eu-rewrites-its-ai-law-before-takes-effect-ivan-dmuge](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/when-eu-rewrites-its-ai-law-before-takes-effect-ivan-dmuge)

**EU AI Act: High-Risk Draft Guidelines — The Key Watch Points** — Fiona Ghosh (Ashurst), lang: en. Breaks down the Commission's Article 6 draft classification guidelines: intended-purpose governs (disclaimers won't save you), Article 6(3) filter is narrow and never applies to GDPR profiling, and — notably — agentic/split architectures are assessed as a *single system*, so low-risk modules inside a high-risk system can't claim the filter. _Why interesting:_ Directly ties EU AI Act high-risk classification to multi-agent architecture decisions; consultation runs to 23 July. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-act-high-risk-draft-guidelines-key-watch-points-fiona-ghosh-azdue](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-act-high-risk-draft-guidelines-key-watch-points-fiona-ghosh-azdue)

**Stop Rebuilding the Same RAG Pipeline for Every Agent** — Jihane Lazrag (Avanade), lang: en. Makes the case for a shared, permission-aware knowledge layer (Microsoft Foundry IQ) over per-agent retrieval pipelines: agentic retrieval with query decomposition (claims +36% relevance on multi-hop), multi-source unification incl. MCP servers, and Entra ID / Purview ACL enforcement at query time. _Why interesting:_ Frames enterprise AI as moving from "build everything" to "compose from shared infrastructure"; concrete on the duplicated-pipeline cost most teams don't discuss. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/stop-rebuilding-same-rag-pipeline-every-agent-jihane-lazrag-camne](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/stop-rebuilding-same-rag-pipeline-every-agent-jihane-lazrag-camne)

**The Real Claude Tag Question Is Context Ownership** — AlphaSignal, lang: en. Analyzes Anthropic's 23 June Claude Tag (Slack-native agent, Opus 4.8, replaces Claude in Slack) — argues the differentiator isn't novelty (OpenClaw/Hermes/Viktor exist) but trustable enterprise packaging: service-account identities, per-thread hosted sandboxes, default-deny egress, Agent Proxy credential injection. Proposes a "Context Sovereignty Test" on memory portability and lock-in. _Why interesting:_ Sharp governance lens — "rent the agent, own the memory" — and flags the June 12 Fable 5/Mythos 5 suspension as a real model-access-risk precedent. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/real-claude-tag-question-context-ownership-alphasignal-rijje](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/real-claude-tag-question-context-ownership-alphasignal-rijje)
