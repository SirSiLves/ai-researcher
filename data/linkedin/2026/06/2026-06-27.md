## Pulse / long-form articles (priority layer)

**MCP Is the Missing Layer Your Enterprise RAG System Didn't Know It Needed** — Brudite Private Limited (lang: en). Argues enterprise RAG fails at the connector layer, not the model: hand-built connectors silently drift, fragment auth, and leak ACLs; MCP gives a standardized, observable connector contract (Resources/Tools/Prompts). _Why interesting:_ Concrete failure taxonomy + 5-step plan tying MCP directly to RAG reliability, exactly the focus-area intersection. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-missing-layer-your-enterprise-rag-system-didnt-know-needed-xzklf]

**Governing the Agentic Mesh: Why agentgateway and MCP Belong Together** — Shriram Kris Vasudevan (lang: en). Separates the "language problem" (MCP standardizes capability discovery/invocation) from the "control problem" (agentgateway as a governed data plane enforcing authn/z, rate limits, audit, A2A normalization). _Why interesting:_ Clear architectural framing of the MCP + governance/control-plane pairing for enterprise agent fleets. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/governing-agentic-mesh-why-agentgateway-mcp-belong-shriram-lmdxc]

**What 'is' Agent Identity? Human? Workload? A New Layer?** — Christian Posta, Solo.io (lang: en). Works through whether agent identity is workload identity (SPIFFE/kagent, Uber's agent-per-pod model) or a separate layer (Google agent substrate, AWS AgentCore, Microsoft Entra Agent ID, Dick Hardt's AAuth); collapses to workload identity only when 1:1 mapping, a runtime registry, and identity continuity hold. _Why interesting:_ Deep, vendor-spanning treatment of the under-discussed agent-identity/IAM problem for open-ecosystem agents. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/what-agent-identity-human-workload-new-layer-christian-posta-qdamc]

**Is ISO 42001 Really about AI "Governance"?** — Aron Lange, GRC Lab (lang: en). Uses ISO 37000's governance-vs-management split to show ISO 42001 (an AI *Management* System) only delivers real governance in Clause 5 and parts of Clause 6; the rest is management, and the standard assumes a governing-body layer above the AIMS that it never provides. _Why interesting:_ Sharp, standards-grounded correction to the common "ISO 42001 = AI governance" claim. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/iso-42001-really-ai-governance-aron-lange-knmce]

**EU AI Act for RegTech Vendors: What Needs to Be Built Before December 2027** — Dreamix / Digital Innovation Insights (lang: en). Translates Annex III high-risk obligations into six engineering workstreams (explainability layer, immutable audit trail, human-in-the-loop, model docs/versioning, bias detection, conformity assessment/CE) with a realistic 8–14 month build timeline and the €15M/3% penalty exposure. _Why interesting:_ Rare engineering-first read of EU AI Act compliance for AML/KYC/credit-risk AI, directly relevant to Swiss/EU financial services. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-ai-act-regtech-vendors-what-needs-built-before-december-2wkrf]

**The Governance Gap — The Evidence Gap** — Patrick Upmann, AIGN (lang: en). Issue #27 of the AI Governance Gap Brief: the next governance question is "can you prove control when AI acted?"; cites Deloitte/IBM/Stanford HAI/McKinsey data on the declaration-vs-evidence gap and proposes a seven-dimension "Defensible Trust" evidence architecture mapped to EU AI Act Articles 9/12/14/26 and ISO 42001. _Why interesting:_ Data-dense, regulation-anchored case that governance is shifting from principles to auditable operational evidence. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/governance-gap-evidence-patrick-upmann-g4bre]

**The U-Curve Trap: Why Bigger Context Windows Are Making AI Agents Dumber** — Rama Maddi (lang: en). Explains the lost-in-the-middle/"U-curve" effect, then prescribes context engineering (context engines, hierarchical summarization, knowledge graphs), an 80/20 reasoning-vs-validation compute split, and a multi-agent code-review loop with a judge node. _Why interesting:_ Practical context-engineering playbook for agentic systems beyond the "more tokens = better" myth. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/u-curve-trap-why-bigger-context-windows-making-ai-agents-rama-maddi-iluhc]

**RAG or Fine-Tuning: Which AI Strategy Pays Off on Your Own Data?** — Monterail (lang: en). RAG changes what the model *knows*, fine-tuning changes what it *is*; recommends starting with RAG (cheaper, current, citable) and adding fine-tuning for fixed style/narrow tasks, citing Chroma's "context rot" study across 18 frontier models and the RAFT / agentic-RAG direction. _Why interesting:_ Clear, sourced ROI framing of the RAG-vs-fine-tuning decision with current evidence. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/rag-fine-tuning-which-ai-strategy-pays-off-your-own-data-monterail-czjlf]

**Glitch Monday: Wie eine halbe Billion Dollar verdampfte (Projecter Weekly #25)** — Katja von der Burg, Obsidian Digital (lang: de). German weekly: a "Glitch Monday" wiped ~half a trillion dollars off big-tech as investors question when AI capex pays off; also covers the viral "Europa 2031" AI-sovereignty dystopia, Five Eyes' joint warning on AI cyberattacks, OpenAI's "Patch the Planet"/GPT-5.5-Cyber push, and Microsoft Copilot Cowork undercutting Claude Cowork on per-prompt cost. _Why interesting:_ German-language pulse on AI market sentiment, EU digital sovereignty, and the agent pricing war. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/glitch-monday-wie-eine-halbe-billion-dollar-projecter-von-der-burg-7ajic]
