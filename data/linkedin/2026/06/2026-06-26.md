**The AI Agent Identity Landscape: Seven Lanes, 38 Players, One Question** — Chris Hood ("Human+AI" newsletter), lang: en. Maps 38 products/protocols/standards bodies into seven lanes — Enterprise IAM/IGA (Entra Agent ID, Okta/Auth0, SailPoint, CyberArk, Palo Alto Idira…), Cloud/Workload Identity (Bedrock AgentCore, SPIFFE/SPIRE, Aembit, Vault), Developer/CIAM Auth (Descope, Stytch, WorkOS), Non-Human-Identity security (Astrix, Token, Oasis), Decentralized/DID, Protocols & Standards (MCP, **Google A2A signed Agent Cards**, AGTP/ANS, W3C DID+VC, AGNTCY), and Governance bodies (NIST, CoSAI, OWASP NHI). Core thesis: the field has converged on naming agents but built almost nothing to govern their *conduct* — the open ground is the behavioral/authority layer. _Why interesting:_ The clearest single survey of the agent-identity land rush; directly maps to MCP/A2A interoperability and the "who is this agent allowed to be" question. **Is ISO 42001 Really about AI "Governance"?** — Aron Lange (GRC Lab), lang: en. Uses ISO 37000's governance-vs-management distinction to walk ISO 42001 clause by clause: genuine governance content concentrates in Clause 5 (leadership, AI policy, roles) and the framing parts of Clause 6 (objectives/risk appetite); everything else (Clauses 7/8/10, most of Annex A) is management. Key point: 42001 is an AI *management* system (AIMS) that assigns top-of-house duties to "top management," but ISO 37000 puts a governing body *above* the AIMS — the layer 42001 assumes but doesn't provide (board ownership of AI risk appetite, independent assurance, stakeholder accountability). _Why interesting:_ Cuts through the "ISO 42001 = AI governance" buzzword conflation with a precise standards argument GRC/compliance leads can act on. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/iso-42001-really-ai-governance-aron-lange-knmce](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/iso-42001-really-ai-governance-aron-lange-knmce)
