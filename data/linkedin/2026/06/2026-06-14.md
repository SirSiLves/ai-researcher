**MCP is the new API — treat it as a do-over** — ReversingLabs / Chainmail Newsletter (lang: en), Jun 12. Argues MCP is repeating the early-API security trajectory — rapid adoption, no standard authn/authz, "left to the implementer" — but with higher stakes because an LLM, not a deterministic caller, selects tools; flags tool poisoning, rug-pull, and confused-deputy attacks that legacy SAST/DAST miss. _Why interesting:_ concrete, named-source security framing of the MCP threat surface, not hype. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-new-api-treat-do-over-reversinglabs-paxxe](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/mcp-new-api-treat-do-over-reversinglabs-paxxe)

**API Integration, A2A, and MCP: Which Protocol to Use and When?** — Sudesh Bhadouria (lang: en), Jun 8. Plain-language decision guide using a hospital analogy: fixed APIs for stable transactional calls, A2A for dynamic agent coordination/delegation, MCP as the governed permissions/audit layer for tool access — and how all three compose in a regulated lending workflow. _Why interesting:_ governance-first practitioner take from a regulated-industries (banking/FS/insurance) architect, with the audit-trail angle a regulator expects. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/api-integration-a2a-mcp-which-protocol-use-when-sudesh-bhadouria-sv4ae](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/api-integration-a2a-mcp-which-protocol-use-when-sudesh-bhadouria-sv4ae)

Frames a new "protocol layer" (MCP context, A2A collaboration, agent identity/trust) as the interoperability standard for multi-agent enterprises, mirroring the monolith→microservices shift; argues the next architectural challenge is interoperability, not model intelligence. _Why interesting:_ clear articulation of the agent-interoperability stack debate the user tracks (MCP/A2A/identity). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agentic-ais-missing-layer-why-mcp-a2a-agent-identity-become-baipalli-xgjdc](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/agentic-ais-missing-layer-why-mcp-a2a-agent-identity-become-baipalli-xgjdc)

**RAG Is Quietly Being Replaced. Here's What's Taking Its Place.** — Aashish Kasma (Lucent Innovation) (lang: en), Jun 9. Argues classic top-k RAG was built for one-shot human queries and breaks under agent traffic (reflexive, repeated retrievals → tripling cost, degrading quality); the discipline is shifting to "context engineering" — separating stable facts from live state, structuring agent memory, tracking cost-per-retrieval. _Why interesting:_ substantive production post-mortem on the RAG → context-architecture transition with a concrete data point (retrieval optimization overtook eval as top Q1-2026 AI investment). [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/rag-quietly-being-replaced-heres-whats-taking-its-place-aashish-kasma-ap7zc](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/rag-quietly-being-replaced-heres-whats-taking-its-place-aashish-kasma-ap7zc)

**Build vs buy for Agentic RAG: where each genuinely wins** — Jay Sanderson (Progress) (lang: en), Jun 9. Honest build-vs-buy breakdown: cloud primitives (Cloudflare Vectorize/Workers AI, Fabric + Azure AI Search) give you retrieval but not the production orchestration/eval/observability/governance layer; build when portability, cost-at-scale, or deep customization is the point, buy when RAG is a capability rather than your product. _Why interesting:_ balanced (despite vendor affiliation) treatment of where each side actually wins, with the "primitives aren't a platform" framing. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/build-vs-buy-agentic-rag-where-each-genuinely-wins-jay-sanderson-ocehc](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/build-vs-buy-agentic-rag-where-each-genuinely-wins-jay-sanderson-ocehc)

**EU releases draft guidelines on high-risk AI systems** — Tsaaro Consulting / Times of Privacy (lang: en), Jun 12. Summarizes the EU Commission's draft guidelines for classifying high-risk AI under the AI Act: two paths (safety component of regulated products vs. Annex III sensitive sectors), the Article 6(3) narrow-task exemptions, and the key carve-out that profiling in Annex III sectors stays high-risk; staged compliance from 2 Dec 2027 (biometrics/employment/etc.) and 2 Aug 2028 (embedded in regulated products). _Why interesting:_ concrete regulatory development on high-risk classification with dates and the profiling clarification that hits HR/credit-scoring tools. [https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-releases-draft-guidelines-high-risk-ai-systems-tsaaro-plodc](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/eu-releases-draft-guidelines-high-risk-ai-systems-tsaaro-plodc)
