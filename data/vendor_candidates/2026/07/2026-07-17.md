### 🔧 Data-quality fix applied this run

Hot-event detection had two false-positive generators, both now fixed in this sweep's matcher:

1. **Keyword matching was bare-substring** — `"Evaluations"` contains `valuation`, and `"hundreds-of-billions-parameter"` contains `billion`. Today's ETH *Apertus Engineer — Evaluations* job posting alone manufactured fake funding events for `ethz`, `epfl`, `apertus`, `cscs`, `swiss-ai-initiative`, `eth`, `vllm`, `switzerland` and `trains`. Keywords are now word-boundary anchored (`$1B`-style keywords keep their leading `$`).
2. **Co-occurrence unit was too coarse** — splitting on blank lines merges an entire `## Sources scanned` section (dozens of org names + search queries like `DeepSeek valuation $74 billion`) into one paragraph, so every org named there inherited a "funding event". Detection now runs at **line** granularity and skips `## Sources scanned` sections entirely.

Effect: orgs with a hot-event recorded today fell from **93 → 8**, and all 8 trace to one genuine paragraph (IBM's $67B wipeout + the $1.5B Ode consortium). This is also why `lakera` surfaced correctly as `promote` rather than being masked by a phantom `hot_event`. Historical `hot_events` entries in `discovered_orgs.json` from earlier runs still carry this noise; it is inert (Step C.5 rejects hint-less candidates) but it explains the persistently high `hot_event` tier count.
