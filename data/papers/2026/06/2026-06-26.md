# AI Papers — 2026-06-26

## Top picks

**Life After Benchmark Saturation: A Case Study of CORE-Bench** — Nitya Nadgir, Sayash Kapoor, Peter Kirgis, et al. _Takeaway:_ Argues that retiring saturated agent benchmarks wastes them — a saturated benchmark can still surface six neglected dimensions (construct validity, cost, robustness) of agent performance beyond raw accuracy. _Category:_ 2606.26158 (cs.AI). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26158)

**The Verification Horizon: No Silver Bullet for Coding Agent Rewards** — Binghai Wang, Dayiheng Liu, Mouxiang Chen, et al. _Takeaway:_ Empirically shows the classic "verifying is easier than producing" intuition inverts for modern coding agents, with direct consequences for how RL reward signals should be designed. _Category:_ 2606.26300 (cs.AI). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26300)

**OPID: On-Policy Skill Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning** — Shuo Yang, Jinyang Wu, Zhengxi Lu, et al. _Takeaway:_ Tackles sparse trajectory-level rewards in language-agent RL by distilling intermediate skills on-policy, giving finer credit assignment over which decisions to reinforce. _Category:_ 2606.26790 (cs.LG, HF-curated). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26790)

**Temporal Validity in Retrieval Memory: Eliminating Stale-Fact Errors for AI Agents over Evolving Knowledge** — Neeraj Yadav. _Takeaway:_ Adds a time model to RAG so agents stop retrieving both stale and current versions of a changed fact (e.g. a renamed API), targeting a common failure mode in long-lived agent memory. _Category:_ 2606.26511 (cs.CL). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26511)

**Necessary but Not Sufficient: Temperature Control and Reproducibility in LLM-as-Judge Safety Evaluations** — Hiroki Tamba. _Takeaway:_ Shows that setting grader temperature to 0 does not guarantee reproducible pass/fail verdicts in safety eval harnesses — a caution for anyone gating deployment on LLM-as-judge. _Category:_ 2606.26185 (cs.LG). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26185)

**Detecting and Controlling Sycophancy with Cascading Linear Features** — Maty Bohacek, Rishub Jain, Roma Patel, et al. _Takeaway:_ Activation-steering method for detecting and suppressing sycophancy using cascading linear features, reducing the contrastive-pair data burden of interpretability-based control. _Category:_ 2606.26155 (cs.AI). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26155)

**Governing Actions, Not Agents: Institutional Attestation as a Governance Model for Autonomous AI Systems** — Jakob Salfeld-Nebgen. _Takeaway:_ Proposes governing consequential, irreversible agent actions (prescribing, prod deploys) via institutional attestation rather than monitoring the agents themselves — a concrete governance primitive. _Category:_ 2606.26298 (cs.AI). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26298)

**Know2Guess: A Contamination-Aware Multi-Zone Benchmark for Knowledge-Boundary Evaluation in LLMs** — Renwei Meng, Bowen Zhang, Jian Wang, et al. _Takeaway:_ Separates supported answering from unsupported guessing while controlling for data contamination and refusal — a cleaner instrument for measuring what an LLM actually knows. _Category:_ 2606.26101 (cs.CL). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26101)

**Nemotron-TwoTower: Diffusion Language Modeling with Pretrained Autoregressive Context** — Fitsum Reda, Mostofa Patwary, Mohammad Shoeybi, Bryan Catanzaro (NVIDIA). _Takeaway:_ Bridges diffusion and autoregressive LMs with a two-tower design that reuses pretrained AR context, addressing the parallel-generation promise of diffusion LMs without training from scratch. _Category:_ 2606.26493 (cs.CL). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26493)

**Kiko: Programming Agents to Enact Interaction Protocols** — Samuel H. Christie V, Munindar P. Singh, Amit K. Chopra. _Takeaway:_ A programming model giving multi-agent systems better abstractions for decentralized decision-making while enacting interaction protocols — relevant to agent-interop standards work. _Category:_ 2606.26156 (cs.MA). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26156)

## Other interesting items

- **[Agentic Analysis for Agentic Infrastructure: An LLM-Powered Pipeline for Comparative Governance of DAO and Corporate AI Protocols](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26203)** — Yutian Wang, Luyao Zhang. LLM pipeline for large-scale comparative analysis of governance structures shaping agent-protocol interoperability standards.
- **[The Red Queen Gödel Machine: Co-Evolving Agents and Their Evaluators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26294)** — Alex Iacob, Andrej Jovanović, et al. Drops the stationary-verifier assumption in self-improving agents by co-evolving agents and their evaluators.
- **[ProvenAI: Provenance-Native Traces of Evidence in Generated Answers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26449)** — Mohammad Faizan, Dalal Alharthi. Goes beyond citations in RAG to verify a cited source meaningfully shaped the generated output.
- **[GPUSparse: GPU-Accelerated Learned Sparse Retrieval with Parallel Inverted Indices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26441)** — Ashutosh Sharma. Moves SPLADE-style learned-sparse retrieval scoring off CPU onto GPU via parallel inverted indices.
- **[TileMaxSim: IO-Aware GPU MaxSim Scoring with Dimension Tiling and Fused Product Quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26439)** — Ashutosh Sharma. Roofline-driven GPU kernel for ColBERT-style multi-vector MaxSim retrieval.
- **[ProfileFoundry: A Synthetic Person-Object Substrate for Privacy, Memory, and Tool-Use Evaluation in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26403)** — Sriram Selvam, Anneswa Ghosh. Synthetic personal-data substrate for evaluating agent memory, tool use, and privacy without real user data.
- **[Dynamic-dLLM: Dynamic Cache-Budget and Adaptive Parallel Decoding for Training-Free Acceleration of Diffusion LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26120)** — Tianyi Wu, et al. Training-free speedups for diffusion LLMs via dynamic KV-cache budgets and adaptive parallel decoding.
- **[When are likely answers right? On Sequence Probability and Correctness in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.27359)** — Johannes Zenn, Jonas Geiping. Examines when probability-maximizing decoding aligns with correctness — relevant to confidence and decoding-method choice.
