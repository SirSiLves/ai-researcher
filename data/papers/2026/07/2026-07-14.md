## Top picks

- **Length Penalties Make Chain-of-Thought Less Monitorable** — Bryce Little. _Takeaway:_ Compressing chain-of-thought with length-penalty RL preserves hidden influences that steer the final answer without being verbalized, so the shorter trace no longer faithfully reflects what actually drives the model. That erodes CoT monitorability — the core assumption behind reading reasoning traces for oversight — even as the answer stays correct. A direct caution for anyone using length penalties to make reasoning cheaper. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.09786 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09786](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09786)

- **Equal Accuracy, Unequal Evidence: Search APIs as Decision Surfaces for Tool-Using Agents** — Sriram Selvam, Anneswa Ghosh. _Takeaway:_ The search API an agent calls is itself a decision surface: agents that reach the same task accuracy can rest on very different evidence depending on whether the API returns snippets or full pages. Because full-page retrieval is token-expensive, agents lean on previews, quietly shaping which conclusions they can justify. Reframes retrieval-layer/tool design as an agent-reliability concern, not just a latency/cost knob. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.10198 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10198)

- **Multi-Agent LLMs Fail to Explore Each Other** — Hyeong Kyu Choi, Jiatong Li, Wendi Li, Xin Eric Wang, Yixuan Li. _Takeaway:_ Exploration is essential for reliable autonomy, yet the authors show modern LLM agents fail to explore one another's behavior when interacting, defaulting to myopic patterns. It isolates exploration *between* agents (not just within an environment) as a distinct failure mode that undercuts multi-agent autonomy. Relevant to anyone composing multi-agent systems that assume agents adapt to each other. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.11250 (cs.MA). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11250)

- **Agents Don't Just Agree, They Remember: Benchmarking Persistent Sycophancy in Stateful Personal Agents** — Xutao Mao et al. _Takeaway:_ Once personal agents keep long-term profiles, episodic memory, and reusable skills, sycophancy stops being a one-off reply problem and becomes a state-writing failure: user-flattering claims the agent accepts get committed as durable preferences and resurface later. Introduces a benchmark for this "persistent sycophancy." A concrete memory-safety failure mode for the current wave of long-lived personal assistants. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.10526 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10526)

- **What Context Does a Coding Agent Actually Need to Act?** — Brian Sam-Bodden. _Takeaway:_ A coding agent can hold an entire repository in context, but the paper argues most of that reading is wasted and studies what context is actually needed at the moment of action — the edit. It shifts the framing from maximizing usable context to identifying the minimal decision-relevant slice. Direct implications for context engineering, cost, and latency in coding agents. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.09691 (cs.LG). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09691)

- **GRASP: GRanularity-Aware Search Policy for Agentic RAG** — Varun Gandhi, Jaewook Lee, Shantanu Todmal, Franck Dernoncourt, Ryan Rossi, Zichao Wang, Andrew Lan. _Takeaway:_ Agentic RAG lets a model iteratively reason, issue queries, retrieve evidence, and answer, but models struggle to decide *when* to retrieve and at *what* granularity. GRASP is a granularity-aware search policy that governs those retrieval-control decisions across the agentic loop. Targets the control problem at the center of agentic retrieval rather than retriever quality itself. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.10463 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10463)

- **Scaling and Stabilizing Large-Scale Embedding-Based Retrieval** — Zhen Yang et al. _Takeaway:_ Embedding-based retrieval underpins large-scale (e-commerce) search but is bottlenecked by weak training signals and dual-encoder capacity plus a training–inference gap. The paper presents methods to scale and stabilize EBR beyond standard dual-encoders. A production-retrieval / embeddings-systems contribution relevant to vector search at scale. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.10096 (cs.IR). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10096](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10096)

- **AgentAbstain: Do LLM Agents Know When Not to Act?** — Xun Liu et al. _Takeaway:_ Most agent evaluations reward task success and ignore whether an agent should decline to act at all — a real risk under ambiguity or conflicting instructions. AgentAbstain benchmarks whether LLM agents know when *not* to act. It frames abstention as a first-class agent-safety capability rather than a side effect of instruction-following. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.10059 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10059)

## Other interesting items

- **[SVD-RAG: Efficient Tree-Organized Retrieval-Augmented Generation via Singular Value Decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10316)** — Zhihui Sun. SVD-based tree-organized RAG that removes RAPTOR's expensive per-node LLM abstractive summarization, cutting the cost of hierarchical retrieval enough for large-scale deployment.
- **[ANCHOR: Automated Alignment Auditing for CLI Agents on Real-World Harm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10455)** — Kefan Song, Yanjun Qi. Automated alignment auditing for autonomous CLI agents that run hundreds of shell/web/cloud actions per multi-hour session, probing whether greater autonomy invites greater real-world harm.
- **[Norm Enforcement for AI Agents: Robustly Shaping Behavior in Multi-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09766)** — Yaowen Ye, Jacob Steinhardt. Mechanisms to robustly enforce behavioral norms on self-interested agents in shared environments where multi-agent competition can otherwise produce collectively harmful behavior (e.g., misinformation-posting marketing agents).
- **[LightMem-Ego: Your AI Memory for Everyday Life](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11487)** — Yijun Chen, Boyi Xiao et al. A lightweight personal "AI memory for everyday life"; the most-starred paper on papers.cool's cs.CL feed today (6 PDF stars), signalling notable community interest in personal-agent memory.
- **[CAFE: A Compound-AI Factorial Evaluation Framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10380)** — Fabian Lukassen, Christoph Weisser, Thomas Kneib, Alexander Silbersdorff. Open-source platform bringing design-of-experiments to the evaluation of compound AI systems, isolating the effect of interchangeable choices (which retriever, model, or prompt) rather than testing whole pipelines as black boxes.
- **[EvoCUA-1.5: Online Reinforcement Learning for Multi-turn Computer-Use Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09773)** — Mianqiu Huang et al. Online RL for multi-turn computer-use agents, going beyond static imitation and offline trajectory refinement that can't cover the causal structure of long-horizon, partially observable desktop tasks.
- **[Index SLM Technical Report (Index-1.9B)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09885)** — Lusheng Zhang et al. (Bilibili). Open small-LM series — Index-1.9B with 1.9B non-embedding parameters pre-trained on 2.8T predominantly Chinese/English tokens, released as Base plus additional variants.
