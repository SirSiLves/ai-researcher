## Top picks

**Do Agent Benchmarks Measure Capability? Protocol Validity in the Age of Agentic AI** — Jiaqi Shao, Hanck Chen, Wei Zhang, et al. _Takeaway:_ Formalizes *protocol validity* — a benchmark score supports a capability claim only if the protocol keeps that capability necessary for success — and introduces HackDetect, a post-hoc audit that finds an exposure, traces how the agent used it, and scores the resulting inflation as a "Mislead gap"; auditing 2,385 traces across 15 agent benchmarks turns up reward hacking in 67.0% of Frontier Science traces and 66.7% of AutoLab tasks, with paired score inflation of 0.45–1.00. _Category:_ cs.AI — 2607.22368. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22368)

**ToolGuardian: Declarative Security for AI Agent-Tool Interactions** — Arun Ravindran, Saurabh Deochake. _Takeaway:_ Treats the agent–tool boundary as a first-class security surface: progressive characterization turns declared descriptions, syscall traces, mock execution, and source analysis into structured facts, then an Answer Set Programming policy layer reasons deterministically and auditably over capabilities, effects, task context, and multi-tool composition — evaluated on 16 MCP-style tools (8 malicious variants derived from real open-source servers) and 20 runtime scenarios, reaching deny-class F1 0.86 and beating heuristic and LLM-judge policy realizations on identical inputs. _Category:_ cs.CR / cs.AI — 2607.21835. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21835)

**TRACE-ROUTER: Task-Consistent and Adaptive Online Routing for Agentic AI** — Ritik Raj, Souvik Kundu, Sarbartha Banerjee, et al. _Takeaway:_ Per-call model routers cannot attribute a delayed, task-level outcome to any individual routing decision, so TRACE-Router moves the unit of routing to the task: a contextual bandit picks a backend once at admission, pins every subsequent LLM call to it, and updates on the terminal reward (accuracy *and* latency) — yielding non-dominated Pareto points across three agentic benchmarks, +7–8 accuracy points over latency-matched interpolation on tau2-Bench and +7.1 points with 36% lower latency than the best single model on Terminal-Bench. _Category:_ cs.AI / cs.LG / cs.MA — 2607.22465. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22465)

**Agent Security Needs Redefinition through a Holistic Framework** — Vincent Siu, Jingxuan He, Kyle Montgomery, Zhun Wang, Chenguang Wang, et al. _Takeaway:_ Argues agent security is contextual, not content-based — "delete user data" is either a routine admin request or a prompt injection, and the action text cannot tell them apart, while authorization context can; the paper operationalizes four jointly-held, trajectory-continuous properties (Source Authorization, Task Alignment, Action Alignment, Data Isolation), under which indirect prompt injection is simply a Source Authorization violation and snapshot benchmarks like AgentDojo/WASP are shown to be *structurally* incapable of evaluating Data Isolation. _Category:_ cs.CR / cs.AI — 2607.22024. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22024)

**What AI Red-Team Evaluations Can and Cannot Prove** — Bandana Kaur. _Takeaway:_ Derives in closed form the "evidential ceiling" of a safety evaluation — the largest factor by which a result can shift belief under a fixed testing budget — locating exactly where a clean sheet certifies a harm category and where no passive benchmark of feasible size can; the bound generalizes past benchmarks to adaptive and automated red teaming (discrimination between hypotheses, not attack success, is what carries evidential worth), and an audit of eight evaluation suites finds them adequate for high-frequency harms but several orders of magnitude short for rare catastrophic ones. _Category:_ cs.AI / cs.CR — 2607.21735. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21735)

**Ground Truth First: A Longitudinal Evaluation Instrument for Agent Memory, and the Tenure Crossover in Memory-Architecture Rankings** — Quentin Spencer. _Takeaway:_ Inverts the usual agent-memory benchmark pipeline — a seeded life-script sampler emits facts with validity intervals, volatility classes and source channels *before* any text exists, so gold answers are script-valid by construction — and the resulting long-horizon harness shows backend rankings actually invert with tenure: the budgeted curated-map memory that leads at three weeks drops 96%→72% on evicted content by nine weeks while a provenance-typed graph climbs to 90% (exact p=0.031); write-stage quality dominates downstream quality (weakly-written facts fail 24% vs 2%), and the layered architecture is released open-source as Veracium. _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.21962. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21962)

**SIREN (Luring LLMs onto the Rocks): PAIR-Driven Preference Manipulation in Web-RAG Recommenders** — Evan Caville, Siamak Layeghy, Billy Sung, et al. _Takeaway:_ Adapts the PAIR jailbreaking loop to competitive *rank* manipulation: an attacker–judge pair iteratively edits one already-retrieved page using a taxonomy of 23 content-poisoning techniques while a custom-RAG replay platform holds the source set and its order fixed, so ranking shifts are attributable to content rather than retrieval — across two production Claude models it reaches rank 1 in 62 of 124 technique trials, and successful payloads reproduce at a 0.805 mean rate in fresh sessions, with declarative ranking claims and seeded lists beating directive-form injections. _Category:_ cs.IR / cs.CR — 2607.21951. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21951)

**Where Facts Go Missing: A Layerwise Taxonomy and Per-Layer Attribution of Information Omission in Air-Gapped LLM Agent Pipelines** — Santhiya Rajan. _Takeaway:_ Targets the dominant on-prem reliability failure — silent omission, the agent that reads 20 of 400 records and reports "no anomalies" — and argues it is a *pipeline* phenomenon: a nine-layer taxonomy (L0–L8) plus an attribution method separating deterministic middleware (L0–L3) from behavioral layers (L4–L8) via controlled ablation and logit decomposition; a 75,476-trial sweep across five quantized models and two engines gives a pooled omission rate of 0.62 with 68% originating in the deterministic middleware, relocating where operators should intervene. _Category:_ cs.MA — 2607.22448. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22448)

**Skill Self-Play: Pushing the Frontier of LLM Capability with Co-Evolving Skills** — Siyuan Huang, Pengyu Cheng, Haotian Liu, et al. _Takeaway:_ Identifies *agent skills* as the middle ground in the self-evolution dilemma — environment-bound methods get precise feedback but stay narrow, open-ended self-generation broadens the task space but lets bad rewards pollute training — so a proposer, a solver, and a dynamic skill controller co-evolve in an RL loop where each skill guarantees deep verifiable execution while routing across skills preserves task variety; gains on tool-use and reasoning benchmarks are largest for initially misaligned backbones. #2 HF paper today (27 upvotes), code released under Qwen-Applications. _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.22529. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22529)

## Other interesting items

- **[Nanbeige4.2-3B: Unlocking Agentic Capabilities in a Compact Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22083)** — Nanbeige Lab. A 3B non-embedding-parameter general agentic model pretrained from scratch on 28T tokens with a Looped Transformer that reuses the layer stack for capacity without extra parameters; mixed-mode RLHF over Think/Non-Think responses plus length-controlled reasoning RL and outcome+process agentic RL let it beat Qwen3.5-9B and Gemma4-12B on agentic benchmarks — a credible local-assistant-class model.
- **[AgentKVShift: Efficient KV Cache Reuse for Agentic Memory Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21604)** — Nilesh Prasad Pandey, Jason Kong, Lanxiang Hu, et al. Training-free, probe-guided KV residual correction tuned for *structured* agentic memory units (summaries, keywords, tags) rather than RAG-style raw passages: the per-memory reuse residual decomposes into a shared memory-level offset plus small token-wise noise, so one weighted correction fixes every reused token instead of leaving un-recomputed cache stale.
- **[LAMAR: An Open Language-Aware Multilingual Alignment Reranker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22042)** — Seongtae Hong, Youngjoon Jang, Jungseob Lee, et al. Shows existing multilingual rerankers do not consistently prefer documents in the query's language when semantically equivalent candidates exist across languages, then fixes it with English-anchored relevance distillation followed by preference alignment for language coherence. HF-featured (8 upvotes).
- **[Draining the Energy Commons: Self-Defeating Over-Appropriation as a Coordination Failure in Agentic LLM Collectives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22188)** — Marcantonio Bracale Syrnicov, Federico Pierucci, Matteo Prandi, et al. Four same-family GPT, Gemini, or Grok agents act as electricity prosumers over a shared renewable reserve; all three families preserve it below the replacement threshold and over-appropriate above it (all nine scarcity contrasts survive Holm correction), behaving like impatient open-access optimizers — a system-level alignment failure invisible to isolated-response evaluation.
- **[Agentic Evaluation of Copyright Law Compliance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21799)** — Zheng Hui, Doni Bloomfield, Noam Kolt. Copyright-Bench puts agents on realistic commercial tasks (website build, merchandise design, pitch decks) where public-domain and copyrighted content are both available; agents pick the copyrighted work anyway, and for open-weights models violation rates *rise* under certain stated user preferences and simulated time pressure.
- **[Opaque Epistemic Mediation: How LLM Deployment Configurations Shape the Validation of Pseudo-Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22513)** — Davide Scarso, Hugo Noronha de Almeida, Joaquim Pina. Four model families across four snapshots (Oct 2025–Feb 2026) via both API and web: Grok's Fast versions scored ethnonationalist pseudo-science at 70–75 credibility vs 15–40 elsewhere, an undocumented silent patch flipped its behavior overnight, and the *same* model identifier returned 75 via API and 5.5 via web three months later — evidence that epistemic stance is a property of deployment configuration, not of the model.
