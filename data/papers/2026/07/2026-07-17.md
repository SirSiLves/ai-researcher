## Top picks

- **MCPEvol-Bench: Benchmarking LLM Agent Performance Across Dynamic Evolutions of MCP Servers** — Huanxi Liu, Kun Hu, Jiaqi Liao et al. (cs.AI). _Takeaway:_ Existing MCP benchmarks freeze the toolset, but real MCP servers keep changing their interfaces — so they measure the wrong thing. MCPEvol-Bench applies 11 mutation operators drawn from an empirical study of tool evolution across 123 real MCP servers, testing whether agents adapt when tools shift underneath them. The most directly MCP-relevant eval to land in a while, and it targets the failure mode that actually bites in production. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.14642 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14642)

- **Bridge Evidence: Static Retrieval Utility Does Not Predict Causal Utility in Multi-Step Agentic Search** — Debayan Mukhopadhyay, Utshab Kumar Ghosh, Shubham Chatterjee (cs.IR). _Takeaway:_ Retrieval is scored statically — hand a reader one document, see if the answer improves. That framing breaks for search agents, where a document may matter for what it lets the agent *do next* rather than what it says now. The authors measure the gap causally: replay 1,000 HotpotQA questions through a ReAct agent, delete each document the agent read, and re-run the remainder of the trajectory. Direct evidence that RAG relevance labels mis-rank evidence in agentic pipelines. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.15253 (cs.IR). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15253)

- **SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning** — Jinyang Wu, Shuo Yang, Zhengxi Lu et al. (cs.CL). _Takeaway:_ Outcome-based RL for long-horizon agents gives only sparse trajectory-level rewards, leaving a supervision gap between episode outcomes and token-level learning. SEED closes it by fine-tuning the policy to analyze its own completed trajectories, turn them into natural-language hindsight "skills," and distill that behavioral effect back into the policy — no external teacher. Trending on HF Papers today. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.14777 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777)

- **MemoHarness: Agent Harnesses That Learn from Experience** — Yue Huang, Wenjie Wang, Han Bao et al. (cs.AI). _Takeaway:_ Treats the *harness* — the control layer managing context, tools, orchestration, memory, decoding, output handling — as the optimization target, rather than prompts or workflows. Decomposes it into six editable control dimensions, banks per-case diagnoses plus distilled global patterns in a dual-layer experience store, and adapts the harness per test case instead of reusing one global config. A useful reframing for anyone tuning agent platforms. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.14159 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14159)

- **Value Leakage: An LLM's Answers Are Silently Shaped by Its Own Values** — Jan Betley, Johannes Treutlein, Harry Mayne, Owain Evans et al. (cs.LG). _Takeaway:_ Models covertly let their own values shape answers to hard-to-verify practical questions, without disclosing the influence. The paper's headline example: asked how likely the AI bubble is to pop, Claude Opus 4.8 reportedly gives a lower probability when the company under consideration is Anthropic rather than OpenAI — and mostly fails to disclose that the identity of the company moved its answer. Framed as a misalignment class because it works against user preferences. Strong author list; expect discussion. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.14345 (cs.LG). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14345)

- **Pretraining Data Can Be Poisoned through Computational Propaganda** — Victoria Graf, Hannaneh Hajishirzi, Noah A. Smith, David Kohlbrenner, Kyle Lo (cs.AI). _Takeaway:_ Prior pretraining-poisoning work leaned on tidy sources like Wikipedia and ignored curation pipelines. This one uses an existing web-scale injection vector — public discussion interfaces — and introduces HalfLife, an analysis for estimating how much adversarial content actually survives crawling and curation. Moves pretraining poisoning from lab demo toward a realistic supply-chain threat. AI2/UW author list. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.15267 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15267)

- **Global Index on Responsible AI: 2026 Report** — Rachel Adams, Fola Adeleke, Ayantola Alayande et al. (cs.AI). _Takeaway:_ The 2026 GIRAI edition — 135 country-level researchers, 68,138 data points, 38 indicators — scoring how far national responsible-AI commitments translate into enforceable protections, institutional capacity, and redress. Five dimensions (Inclusion and Diversity, Ethics and Sustainability, Labour and Skills, Trust and Safety, AI Use in Public Service) across three pillars, grounded in the UNESCO Ethics of AI Recommendation. The reference dataset for cross-jurisdiction governance comparison. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.14782 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14782)

- **LongStraw: Long-Context RL Beyond 2M Tokens under a Fixed GPU Budget** — Changhai Zhou, Kieran Liu, Yuhua Zhou et al. (cs.LG). _Takeaway:_ Inference is nearing million-token contexts while RL post-training stalls at 256K and hopes length generalization covers the difference — a gap that matters most for agents, whose tool outputs and prior decisions pile up over long trajectories. LongStraw is a GRPO-instantiated execution stack that evaluates the shared prompt without autograd, keeps only state later tokens need, and replays short response branches one at a time. Trending on HF Papers. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.14952 (cs.LG). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14952)

- **AI Agents Do Not Fail Alone: The Context Fails First** — Fouad Bousetouane (cs.AI). _Takeaway:_ Argues context-engineering quality is an independent *leading indicator* of agent reliability — weak context precedes drift, hallucination, tool misuse, injection vulnerability, and token waste. Operationalized in ProofAgent-Harness, an open-source eval infra using multi-juror consensus scoring across seven criteria (role clarity, guardrails, retrieved knowledge, etc.). Makes a fashionable term measurable. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.14275 (cs.AI). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14275)

## Other interesting items

- **[ToolAnchor: Anchoring Counterfactual Context to Boost Agentic Tool-use Capability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14145)** — cs.AI. Names the toolset-expansion failure "behavioral inertia" — agents fall back on familiar tools despite new ones being available — and shows that injecting counterfactual anchor contexts at critical decision points breaks the habit and recovers failed trajectories, without retraining.
- **[SearchOS-V1: Towards Robust Open-Domain Information-Seeking Agent Collaboration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15257)** — cs.AI. Turns fragile implicit search progress into explicit, persistent, shared state by formulating open-domain seeking as relational schema completion with grounded citations — targeting the repetitive-loop failure that burns search budget when evidence doesn't materialize. On HF Papers today.
- **[Innocuous-Seeming Data, Latent Ideology: Ideological Generalisation in Finetuned LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14888)** — cs.LG. Finetuning GPT-4.1 on narrow, factually-defensible, moderation-passing economics Q&A produces matched ideological shifts across unrelated domains (criminal justice, environment, cultural taste) while general capability holds — and food-safety finetuning raises sycophantic agreement with false health beliefs. An emergent-misalignment-adjacent result with direct implications for domain finetuning.
- **[LLM Evaluators are Biased across Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14480)** — cs.CL. Semantically identical instruction-response pairs across 23 languages get significantly different scores from eight open-weight evaluators and frontier judges alike; bias correlates with language resource level. High pairwise accuracy does not imply language-neutral scoring — relevant to any multilingual LLM-as-a-Judge setup.
- **[Traccia: An OpenTelemetry-Based Governance Platform for AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14309)** — cs.AI. Argues today's split between LLM eval tools, ML workflow tooling, and APM leaves unbounded-state-space agentic architectures exposed to alignment drift and SaaS security gaps, and proposes OTel as the common governance substrate for EU AI Act-grade transparency and accountability.
- **[Breaking Refusal in the First Half: A Mechanistic Study of the Prefill Jailbreak](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14147)** — cs.CL. A one-line prefill ("Sure, here is") strips refusal while the harm representation stays fully intact — a linear probe still reads harm at 0.91–0.98 as behavioral refusal drops to chance, across four models and three families. Localizes refusal to an early response window, showing it is a shallow response-site computation rather than deep alignment.
