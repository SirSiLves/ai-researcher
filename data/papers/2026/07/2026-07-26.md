## Top picks

**RAGU: A Multi-Step GraphRAG Engine with a Compact Domain-Adapted LLM** — Mikhail Komarov, Ivan Bondarenko, Stanislav Shtuka, Oleg Sedukhin, et al. (Novosibirsk State University + ITMO). _Takeaway:_ Separates KG *extraction* from *consolidation* (two-stage typed extraction → DBSCAN dedup → LLM summarization → Leiden communities) and shows the in-pipeline LLM only needs language skills, not world knowledge — a 7B Meno-Lite-0.1 beats Qwen2.5-32B on KG construction (+12.5% relative harmonic mean), hits 0.84 evidence recall on GraphRAG-Bench Medical (vs ≤0.76), and costs ~$0.001/doc against ~$0.10/doc for API-based GraphRAG. _Category:_ 2607.11683 — MIT-licensed, `pip install graph_ragu`. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11683)

**RESOURCE2SKILL: Distilling Executable Agent Skills from Human-Created Multimodal Resources** — Yijia Fan, Zonglin Di, Zimo Wen, Yifan Yang, et al. (Microsoft Research). _Takeaway:_ Turns tutorial videos, repos, articles and reference artifacts into a hierarchical multimodal "Skill Wiki" (structured text + code + visual examples + provenance) that agents retrieve and compose at inference, with online acquisition when coverage is thin — +11.9pp average over no-skill agents across seven authoring domains (PowerPoint, Excel, Blender, CAD, UI/UX, web gen, music) and beats strong agentic harnesses in 26 of 28 model-domain cells. _Category:_ 2606.29538. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29538)

**Recursive Harness Self-Improvement** — Hyunin Lee, Jinglue Xu, Jeffrey Seely, Donghyun Lee, Matei Zaharia, Yujin Tang (Sakana AI). _Takeaway:_ Treats the harness as a prompt-level specification of the agent loop and refines it via pairwise feedback over its own revision history; a few iterations let *low*-reasoning-effort agents exceed the max-reasoning-effort setting on 30 synthetic ML-research tasks while cutting inference cost up to 60% — and the gains trace to better task-specific context management and inter-agent information flow, not longer reasoning traces. _Category:_ 2607.15524. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15524)

**From Human-Centric to Agentic Code Review: The Impact of Different Generations of Generative AI Technology on Review Quality** — Suzhen Zhong, Shayan Noei, Bram Adams, Ying Zou (Queen's University). _Takeaway:_ 1.02M reviewed pull requests across 207 GitHub projects spanning three eras (human-centric → LLM-assisted → agentic); agent-initiated and multi-agent reviews are associated with materially faster review decisions, but **the efficiency gains do not translate into better review quality** — and human-AI collaboration pattern becomes the single strongest explanatory factor for review efficiency once agents join. _Category:_ 2607.13196 — HF #3 paper of the day (2026-07-20). [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13196)

**When Does Muon Help Agentic Reinforcement Learning?** — Kai Ruan, Jinghao Lin, Zihe Huang, Ziqi Zhou, et al. _Takeaway:_ Applying vanilla Muon only to hidden weight matrices under GiGPO lifts final-window ALFWorld validation success from 0.290 to 0.546 (+88%) on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, but the effect is entangled with the advantage estimator and learning rate (GraphGPO + Muon at 1e-5 reaches 0.901 and hits milestones 30-60 updates earlier) — a caution that optimizer, credit assignment and LR must be tuned jointly. _Category:_ 2607.16169 — single-seed, exploratory; multi-seed validation open. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16169)

**DSWorld: A Data Science World Model for Efficient Autonomous Agents** — Zherui Yang, Fan Liu, Hao Liu (HKUST-GZ). _Takeaway:_ Predicts environment state transitions conditioned on workflow state + candidate operation so a data-science agent can anticipate an operation's effect instead of executing it — cost-aware routing between lightweight real execution and an LLM simulator, trained on an 8K transition-trajectory dataset with error-aware RL, giving ~14x faster RL agent training and 3-6x faster search-based inference at competitive quality (+35.6% over the strongest LLM baseline on transition prediction). _Category:_ 2607.15901. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15901)

**Beyond Entropy: Correctness-Aware Advantage Shaping via Contrastive Policy Optimization** — Weiwen Xu, Jia Liu, Hou Pong Chan, Long Li, Deng Cai, Min Chen, Hao Zhang. _Takeaway:_ Replaces entropy (which cannot separate useful uncertainty from confusion) with token-level contrastive disagreement between reference-guided and vanilla generation distributions as the RLVR correctness signal; on-policy distillation falls out as a special case, the zero-advantage problem disappears, and CPO beats entropy-based RLVR in- and out-of-domain. _Category:_ 2607.14614. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14614)

**Cura 1T: Specialized Model for Agentic Healthcare** — actAVA AI, Haolin Chen, Leon Qi, Steve Brown, Weiran Yao, et al. _Takeaway:_ A healthcare LLM trained by a *human-gated self-evolution loop* — each round a training agent plans a target capability, trains, evaluates benchmark trajectories and refines the data mixture from observed failures — as an answer to the fact that narrow updates for consultation, clinical reasoning, interactive diagnosis or EHR tool use tend to degrade each other; ranks at or near the top of frontier baselines on the healthcare suite while staying competitive out-of-domain. _Category:_ 2607.15314. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15314)

## Other interesting items

- **[Surprisal Theory is Tautological (without Rational Grounding)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21574)** — Ryan Cotterell. Argues that for any non-negative difficulty measure there exists a language model whose surprisal is an affine function of it, so surprisal theory makes no falsifiable predictions without constraining the model — the tautology was masked by the (now empirically undermined) assumption that better corpus fit means better prediction of human behavior.
- **[Artificial Epanorthosis: Why large language models overuse a classical rhetorical figure, and how to mitigate it](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21498)** — Federico Boggia. Traces the "This is not X. It is Y" tic to promotional pretraining data plus RLHF rewarding emphatic phrasing (left-to-right decoding is an amplifier, not the cause); an Epanorthosis Index against genre-specific human baselines shows ~2x overshoot in oratory and undershoot in informal Q&A, and a one-line instruction halves it while a LoRA/SFT adapter nearly removes it.
- **[A Comparative Evaluation of Embeddings and LLMs in a Greek Book Publisher Setting — The CUP Dataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21274)** — Katerina Papantoniou, Panagiotis Papadakos, Theodore Patkos, et al. 868 catalog records + 104 expert-annotated graded-relevance queries; multilingual embeddings beat Greek-specific models, hybrid retrieval wins overall, BM25 still owns named-entity queries, and LLM post-filtering improves early-stage retrieval only at high cost.
- **[news-crawler-LM: A Small Long-Context Model For High-Quality News Crawling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21284)** — Pascal Stolzenburg, Jonas Golde, Max Dallabetta, Alan Akbik. A small long-context model fine-tuned on human-validated Fundus extractions converts raw HTML to plaintext/JSON (+4.8 BLEU HTML→Markdown, +2.2 BLEU HTML→JSON over strong baselines) — but barely edges rule-based parsers on unseen publishers, a useful reality check on "just use an LLM" ingestion pipelines.
