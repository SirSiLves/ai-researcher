## Top picks

**Sample-Efficient Learning from Agent Experience** — Chenhui Gou, Haoqin Tu, Yunhao Fang, et al. _Takeaway:_ "Experience Distillation" folds an agent's in-context trial-and-error into its weights with no extra environment calls, retaining ≥64.8% of the in-context gains (vs 3.8% for plain SFT) and matching RL on 749 software-engineering tasks and six text games with ≥9.6× fewer environment samples. _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.21051. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21051)

**QuantiBias: Benchmarking Quantization-Induced Bias in LLMs** — Emilio Ferrara. _Takeaway:_ Quantized models still refuse harmful prompts and pick the unbiased multiple-choice answer, yet volunteer stereotypes in ~24–27% of open-ended answers across all eight languages probed — a selective bias gap that every standard short-form safety check misses. _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.21063. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21063)

**Anti-Periodic Positional Encoding: Möbius Boundary Conditions Make In-Context Retrieval Reliable** — Ji Ho Bae. _Takeaway:_ Swapping RoPE for an anti-periodic "Möbius" frequency ladder deterministically couples the two ends of the context and lifts needle-in-a-haystack retrieval from 63%±31% to 90%±6% at unchanged perplexity — a one-line, zero-cost hedge against the retrieval "seed lottery." _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.21405. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21405)

**Token Budget Saturation and Mechanistic Early Detection of Reasoning Non-Convergence in CoT Models** — Renuka Oladri, Niveda Jawahar, Abdirisak Mohamed. _Takeaway:_ Converged chain-of-thought runs hit 90.3% on AIME vs 6.6% for budget-exhausting ones; linear probes on layer-20 hidden states flag the doomed runs as early as token 50, opening a path to early-exit inference and adaptive compute allocation. _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.21433. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21433)

**Capital Markets LLM Reliability Score (CM-LRS): From Plausible to Bankable** — Prerit Ahuja. _Takeaway:_ A seven-dimension, workflow-output reliability rubric for regulated finance (traceability, numerical consistency, auditability…) ranks Sonnet 4.6 (4.31), Opus 4.7 (4.30) and GPT-5.5 (4.09) near-tied above Llama 3.3 70B (3.15), with the gap concentrated in retrieval and synthesis rather than extraction. _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.21340. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21340)

**One More Turn, Less Regret: A Regret-Based Multi-Turn Benchmark for LLMs' Clarification Policies** — Minh Ngoc Ta, My Anh Tran Nguyen, Duong D. Nguyen, et al. _Takeaway:_ RegretBench treats clarification as a hidden-intent sequential policy (whether to ask, what to ask, when to stop) and scores regret against a reference policy, exposing that equal-accuracy assistants differ sharply in interaction cost, robustness, and stopping behavior. _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.21143. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21143)

**A Unified Moral-Value Dataset for Instruction Tuning** — Zhaohui Zeng, Florian Mai. _Takeaway:_ Merges existing moral-value corpora into one instruction-response dataset for value alignment; mixing it with general instruction data preserves general-task performance while shifting value-oriented behavior, released openly on HF. _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.21279. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21279)

## Other interesting items

- **[Progressive Cramming: Reliable Token Compression and What It Reveals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21231)** — Dmitrii Tarasov, Timofei Lashukov, Elizaveta Goncharova, et al. Grows the compressed prefix token-by-token to its reconstruction limit and shows "perfect reconstruction" is brittle steering, not transferable semantics — prepending a crammed embedding still collapses generative capability, traced to early-layer attention interactions.
- **[RUMBA: Russian User Memory Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21447)** — Elizaveta Shevtsova, Inna Glebkina, Mark Baushenko, et al. A fine-grained long-term conversational-memory benchmark (timestamped dialogues, cross-session temporal reasoning, aligned English subset) for diagnosing where memory systems and long-context models actually fail.
- **[Adaptive Depth Sparse Framework: Similarity-Driven Resource Allocation for Pre-Trained LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21291)** — Yidu Wu, Xiang Wang, Kejie Zhao, et al. Converts off-the-shelf LLMs into depth-sparse models via per-layer token retention set from input/output cosine similarity (no full retraining), cutting inference FLOPs with less accuracy loss than MoD, D-LLM, and DLO on GPT-NeoX/Qwen2.5.
- **[PrefReward: Learning User Preference Matrix for Personalized Text Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21067)** — Yue Wu, Chengbing Wang, Yimeng Bai, et al. Extracts an explicit per-user style matrix and injects it as a KL-divergence decoding reward, beating non-personalized and retrieval baselines on LongLaMP with more interpretable personalization.
- **[When Trivia Is Not Trivial: Everyday Knowledge Failures in Multilingual LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21445)** — Anna Mosolova, Djamé Seddah. TriviaRoomQA (3,300 parallel MCQs across six European languages, 288 topics) shows 30 open-weight LLMs are strong on history/geography/math but weak on everyday pop culture, with the same question answered differently across languages.
