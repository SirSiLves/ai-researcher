# AI Papers — 2026-07-23

_Collector: ai-papers. Slice: ArXiv (cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.LG, cs.MA, stat.ML, cs.IR), HF Papers, papers.cool._

## Top picks

**Solar Open 2 Technical Report** — Sungrae Park, Sanghoon Kim, Gyoungjin Gim, et al. _Takeaway:_ A 250B-A15B Mixture-of-Experts model built for long-horizon agentic tasks, reaching a 1M-token window via a hybrid attention stack that interleaves a single softmax layer among linear-attention layers so entire agent trajectories fit in one context. Scaled up from Solar Open 1 (100B). _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.20062. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20062)

**SLAI T-Rex: Full-Parameter Post-training of the DeepSeek-V4 Family on Ascend SuperPOD** — Dongfang Li, Xiaodong Luo, Ruoyu Sun, et al. _Takeaway:_ A systems recipe for full-parameter post-training of trillion-parameter-scale MoE models on non-NVIDIA (Ascend) hardware, tackling severe memory pressure, non-overlapped communication overhead, and inefficient kernel execution head-on. Top HF paper today (43 upvotes). _Category:_ cs.CL / cs.AI — 2607.20145. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20145)

**Knowledge-Centric Self-Improvement** — Xuefei Julie Wang, Lauren Hyoseo Yoon, Chengrui Qu, et al. _Takeaway:_ Flips self-improving agents from agent-centric to knowledge-centric — agents stay generic and disposable while a curated shared knowledge base is the persistent object that improves. Raises solve rates while cutting dollar cost, and the distilled knowledge transfers across held-out tasks and LLM families. _Category:_ cs.AI / cs.CL / cs.MA — 2607.19592. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19592)

**NEXUS: Structured Runtime Safety for Tool-Using LLM Agents** — Elias Hossain, Md Mehedi Hasan Nipu, Tasfia Nuzhat Ornee, et al. _Takeaway:_ A structured-plan safety monitor that applies a formal intervention policy across four actions — allow, block, request confirmation, or request revision — to tool-using agents at runtime, instead of screening each prompt in isolation. _Category:_ cs.AI — 2607.19356. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19356)

**Beyond Relevance-Centric Retrieval: Rubric-Oriented Document Set Selection and Ranking** — Kailin Jiang, Lei Liu, Jian Xi, et al. _Takeaway:_ Argues that with LLMs and agents as the primary consumers of search results, document-*set* quality — not per-document relevance — bounds downstream generation; proposes rubric-oriented set selection that models inter-document redundancy and coverage rather than aggregating independent nDCG scores. #3 HF paper today (23 upvotes). _Category:_ cs.CL — 2607.19747. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19747)

**Economic Evaluations of Language Models** — Alexander Wan, Stephane Hatgis-Kessell, Tomás Aguirre, et al. _Takeaway:_ Open-source EconEvals suite that grounds capability measurement in tasks, work activities, and occupations drawn from the US labor economy, pushing evaluation beyond academic benchmarks toward economically valuable work. _Category:_ cs.CY / cs.AI — 2607.19375. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19375)

**Sound Probabilistic Safety Bounds for Large Language Models** — Mahdi Nazeri, Anne-Kathrin Schmuck, Sadegh Soudjani, et al. _Takeaway:_ Applies Clopper-Pearson confidence intervals to produce PAC (probably approximately correct) bounds on the probability that an LLM emits harmful output for a given prompt — a rigorous, distribution-level safety guarantee rather than a spot-check pass rate. _Category:_ cs.CL / cs.AI — 2607.20286. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20286)

**Profile-Graph Memory for LLM Agents: Implicit Cross-Entity Traversal through Narrative Profiles** — Shengtong Zhu. _Takeaway:_ Introduces MemHop (1,000 questions at hop depths 1–5) to measure the multi-hop association that single-hop recall benchmarks miss, and proposes narrative "profile" nodes that let an agent traverse implicitly across entities in long-term memory. _Category:_ cs.AI — 2607.19359. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19359)

## Other interesting items

- **[Scaling Laws for Hypernetwork-Based Knowledge Injection in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19604)** — Nischay Dhankhar, Dos Baha, Abulhair Saparov. Repurposes hypernetworks for train-time (not just test-time) knowledge injection and derives scaling laws for how reliably facts can be written into LLM weights at scale. HF-featured (10 upvotes).
- **[SLPO: Scaling Latent Reasoning via a Surrogate Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19691)** — Runyang You, Zhiyuan Liu, Yongqi Li, et al. Cuts the cost of RL-for-reasoning by carrying intermediate reasoning in latent space through a surrogate policy instead of decoding every step as language tokens. HF-featured.
- **[GraphContainer: A Unified Platform for Comparing and Debugging Graph RAG Methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19362)** — Seonho An, Chaejeong Hyun, Min-Soo Kim. Standardizes heterogeneous graph formats under a Unified Graph Representation layer and adds a Graph Recorder that renders the step-by-step retrieval trace for traceable visual debugging of Graph-RAG.
- **[Stateful Guardrails for Multi-Turn LLM Systems: A Conversational Risk Accumulation Framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19361)** — Sanjay Mishra, Divya Chukkapalli, Ganesh R. Naik. Formalizes "Conversational Risk Accumulation" — harm assembled from individually benign turns via intent drift and fragmented instruction assembly — that per-turn guardrails structurally miss.
- **[JANUS: Foreseeing Latent Risk for Long-Horizon Agent Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19913)** — Yuan Xiong, Linji Hao, Shizhu He, et al. Trains guards to anticipate delayed operational risks from partial agent trajectories, shifting agent safety from content moderation toward foreseeing failures before tools act.
- **[Generative AI floods and dilutes the market for books](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20349)** — Tuhin Chakrabarty, Xinyue Liu, Jane C. Ginsburg, et al. Full-text AI-detection across 14,419 self-published genre-fiction books on Amazon tests — and challenges — the assumption that AI-generated "slop" carries no commercial weight; a data-grounded input to the AI-content policy and copyright debate.
