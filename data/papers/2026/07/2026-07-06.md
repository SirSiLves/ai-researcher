## Top picks

**The Mirage of Optimizing Training Policies: Monotonic Inference Policies as the Real Objective for LLM Reinforcement Learning** — Jing Liang, Hongyao Tang, Yi Ma, Yancheng He, Weixun Wang, et al. _Takeaway:_ Argues the training-inference probability mismatch (separate training vs. generation engines give inconsistent probabilities for the same trajectory even with synchronized weights) is a permanent source of off-policyness that poisons LLM RL post-training, and reframes the real objective around monotonic inference policies rather than the training policy — a diagnosis of why RL fine-tuning is fragile/collapse-prone. _Category:_ cs.LG · 2606.29526. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29526)

**Measuring the Gap Between Human and LLM Research Ideas** — Ziyu Chen, Yilun Zhao, Arman Cohan. _Takeaway:_ Large-scale ideation eval that reverse-engineers, for each high-quality human paper, the small set of prior works that likely inspired its core idea, then prompts LLMs to generate a new idea from those titles/summaries and scores the gap on a two-axis rubric — moves LLM-ideation evaluation past single-idea novelty/feasibility judgments toward a grounded human-vs-model comparison. _Category:_ cs.CL · 2607.01233. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01233)

**Securing the AI Agent: A Unified Framework for Multi-Layer Agent Red Teaming** — Yong Yang, Xing Zheng, Huiyu Wu, Huangsheng Cheng, Xiaorong Shi, et al. _Takeaway:_ AI-Infra-Guard, an open-source red-teaming framework built on the observation that an agent's attack surface is stratified (infrastructure, protocol/tool incl. the MCP ecosystem, agent behavior, model) and no single detection paradigm fits all layers — so it matches a paradigm to each layer, from deterministic rules up. Directly spans the agent-interoperability + safety focus. _Category:_ cs.CR/cs.AI · 2606.31227. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31227)

**AGE: Adaptive-masking for Graph Embedding in Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation** — Bao Long Nguyen Huu, Atsushi Hashimoto. _Takeaway:_ Tackles the core GraphRAG failure mode — misalignment between graph-based and text-based latent features, especially for frozen LLMs — with a Transformer trained via mask-based self-supervised learning to produce graph embeddings the LLM can actually consume. Retrieval/embedding fundamentals for graph-structured external knowledge. _Category:_ cs.CL · 2607.00052. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00052)

**MultAttnAttrib: Training-Free Multimodal Attribution in Long Document Question Answering** — Dang Quang Thien Tran, Quang V. Dang, Vinamra Tyagi, Sai Soorya Rao Veeravalli, Ryan A. Rossi, et al. _Takeaway:_ Training-free method that reuses a model's prefill pass, a selected subset of attention heads, and calibrated thresholds to locate the source evidence for an answer inside a long multimodal document — plus a companion MultAttrEval benchmark. Grounded-QA attribution for user trust / model safety in the under-explored multimodal setting. _Category:_ cs.CL · 2607.01420. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01420)

**Purified OPSD: On-Policy Self-Distillation Without Losing How to Think** — Zhanming Shen, Jintao Tong, Shaotian Yan, Chen Shen, Hao Chen, et al. _Takeaway:_ Shows on-policy self-distillation (privileged teacher supervising a student's own trajectories) consistently *fails* on long chain-of-thought models — marginal gains while destabilizing the reflective reasoning they depend on — then decomposes the teacher's signal to "purify" it and preserve how the model thinks. Practical caution + fix for a popular reasoning-finetuning recipe. _Category:_ cs.AI · 2607.02234. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02234)

**Fast Multi-dimensional Refusal Subspaces via RFM-AGOP** — Thomas Winninger. _Takeaway:_ Refusal behavior lives in a multi-dimensional activation subspace, not a single linear direction, but existing extraction methods are prohibitively expensive on reasoning models with long traces; RFM-AGOP extracts these subspaces cheaply — an efficiency win for activation steering/monitoring in both safety and interpretability. _Category:_ cs.AI · 2607.02396. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02396)

**DRIFTLENS: Measuring Memory-Induced Reasoning Drift in Personalized Language Models** — Xi Fang, Weijie Xu, Yingqiang Ge, Yuhui Xu, Stephanie Eckman, Chandan K. Reddy. _Takeaway:_ Personalization memory (stored user attributes/preferences injected into prompts) can change not just what a model says but the *reasoning trajectory* used to justify it; DRIFTLENS is a ground-truth-free framework that maps and quantifies this drift on open-ended questions — a memory/agent-safety probe as persistent user memory becomes standard in assistants. _Category:_ cs.AI · 2607.02374. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02374)

**A²utoLPBench: An Auto-Generated, Agent-Friendly LP Benchmark via Inverse-KKT Construction** — Shuo Ren, Yaohui Han, Yifan Shi, Libo Shen, Haodong Lu, et al. _Takeaway:_ Instead of hand-written, fixed-size, leak-prone LP word-problem datasets, it constructs problems backward from a chosen feasible primal/dual point (inverse-KKT), so difficulty and size are tunable and the known optimum is baked in — an anti-contamination, auto-scaling eval for LLM-driven optimization agents. _Category:_ cs.AI · 2607.02141. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02141)

**EvoPolicyGym: Evaluating Autonomous Policy Evolution in Interactive Environments** — Zhilin Wang, Han Song, Runzhe Zhan, Jusen Du, Jiacheng Chen, et al. _Takeaway:_ Introduces "Autonomous Policy Evolution" — a controlled setting where a harness-model agent repeatedly edits an executable policy under a fixed interaction budget — instantiated as a benchmark of compact interactive RL environments, so agent self-improvement is measured as a process rather than collapsed into a final score or confounded with open-ended SWE progress. _Category:_ cs.AI · 2607.02440. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02440)

## Other interesting items

- **[Challenges and Recommendations for LLMs-as-a-Judge in Multilingual Settings and Low-Resource Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02235)** — A. Seza Doğruöz, Xixian Liao, Verena Blaschke, David Ifeoluwa Adelani, et al.; surveys current practice and shows LLM-as-a-Judge (validated mostly in English) breaks down in low-resource languages where the judge itself has limited proficiency and human validation is often absent — an eval-methodology caution. (cs.CL · 2607.02235)
- **[Know Your Source: A Public Knowledge Store for Media Background Checks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02383)** — Benjamin Nichols, Michael Schlichtkrull, Nedjma Ousidhoum; challenges the RAG-for-fact-checking assumption that retrieved evidence is reliable, and builds a public store for source background checks so conflicting, outdated, or biased sources can be weighed — retrieval-reliability layer for AFC. (cs.CL · 2607.02383)
- **[Grounded autonomous research: a fault-tolerant LLM pipeline from corpus to manuscript in frontier computational physics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02329)** — Haonan Huang; end-to-end autonomous-research pipeline that forces external-literature calibration at every methodology step instead of citing anchors it never confronts, targeting the hallucination failure mode of unscaffolded research agents in domains without execution feedback. (cs.AI · 2607.02329)
- **[Neuron-Aware Data Selection for Annotation-Free LLM Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02460)** — Zhuowei Chen, Xiang Lorraine Li; improves annotation-free self-evolution (majority-vote pseudo-labels over multiple rollouts) with neuron-aware data selection, addressing the noise/reliability weaknesses of self-supervision in specialized domains where expert labels are costly. (cs.LG · 2607.02460)
- **[Language Models as Measurement Apparatus for Culture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02459)** — Kent K. Chang; argues that when LLMs are used to quantify cultural phenomena the apparatus (model, data, annotation, eval) helps *constitute* the reality it measures rather than passively recording it — a methodological/validity critique for computational-social-science uses of LLMs. (cs.CL · 2607.02459)
- **[Planning over Matrix-Factorization MDPs for Candidate Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02115)** — cs.IR candidate-generation/retrieval work casting matrix-factorization recommendation as an MDP and planning over it — relevant to retrieval/recommendation ranking primitives. (cs.IR · 2607.02115)
