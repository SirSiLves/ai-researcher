## Top picks

- **OmniaBench: Benchmarking General AI Agents Across Diverse Scenarios** — Chengyu Shen et al. _Takeaway:_ A general-agent benchmark of 1,431 executable tasks over a 90/354-domain taxonomy (ToC/ToB/ToE) with a ten-dimensional capability scheme and eight atomic difficulty factors; frontier models struggle badly — Claude-Sonnet-5 and GPT-5.6-Sol reach only ~58.5% and ~57.1% Overall Pass@1, with persistent gaps in planning, constraint maintenance, and adaptive correction. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.14989 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14989)

- **SEED: Self-Evolving On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Reinforcement Learning** — Jinyang Wu et al. _Takeaway:_ Converts completed on-policy trajectories into natural-language "hindsight skills," then distills the skill-induced probability shift back into the policy as a dense token-level signal jointly optimized with outcome-based RL — closing the sparse-reward supervision gap and improving performance and sample efficiency on text- and vision-based agentic tasks (code released). _Category:_ arXiv:2607.14777 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14777)

- **Partition, Prompt, Aggregate: Statistical Self-Consistency in Language Models** — Patrik Wolf, Thomas Kleine Buening, Andreas Krause, Celestine Mendler-Dünner. _Takeaway:_ A reference-free eval criterion: do an LLM's prior-weighted conditional estimates obey the law of total probability across a partition of a population? Frontier models widely violate it, exposing a "macro fallacy" where fine-grained subpopulation estimates align better with human data than direct population-level ones. (ETH Zurich / MPI-IS.) _Category:_ arXiv:2607.15277 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15277)

- **In-Place Tokenizer Expansion for Pre-trained LLMs** — Jimmy T. H. Smith et al. (Liquid AI). _Takeaway:_ An in-place recipe to upgrade a pretrained model's tokenizer by continuing its BPE merges on a multilingual corpus — old tokens carry over unchanged, new rows seed from sub-token embedding means, then a two-stage adaptation recovers quality; applied to an 8B MoE to ship LFM2.5-8B-A1B with a 128K vocab, yielding ~2.2–3.7x per-character decode speedups on Hindi/Vietnamese/Thai. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.15232 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15232)

- **Mask-Aware Policy Gradients for Diffusion Language Models** — Haran Raajesh, Kulin Shah, Adam Klivans, Philipp Krähenbühl. _Takeaway:_ Formalizes masked-diffusion LM generation as a two-stage-action MDP (what token to place, which positions to remask) so the policy gradient cleanly decomposes into a token term and a masking term; optimizing both gives SOTA MDLM results — 87.1% on GSM8K and 53.4% on MBPP. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.15200 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15200)

- **D-Cut: Adaptive Verification Depth Pruning for Batched Speculative Decoding** — Tianyu Liu et al. _Takeaway:_ Cross-request draft-token pruning plus a runtime cost model concentrate the verification budget where acceptance is likely; under high concurrency this lifts average speculative-decoding speedup from 1.26x to 1.65x and reaches up to 3x on MoE models — restoring acceleration in regimes where long-draft baselines are actually slower than autoregressive decoding. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.14647 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14647)

- **Digital Pantheon: Simulating and Auditing Coalition Formation with LLM Agents** — Dylan Van Mulders, Matthias Bogaert, Dirk Van den Poel. _Takeaway:_ A multi-agent framework pairing SFT + DPO (aggressive party-specific personas) with per-party RAG (each agent bounded to its official manifesto) for hub-and-spoke coalition negotiation arbitrated by a formateur; a provenance topology traces every clause of the final agreement to its manifesto origin, and manifesto-anchored lineage reliably predicts real-world outcomes while hallucinated content does not. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.15095 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15095)

- **Rubrics on Trial: Evolving Rubrics from a Single Query via Synthetic Pairwise Evidence** — Haocheng Yang et al. _Takeaway:_ A query-only method that grows an evaluation-rubric set from empty with no human annotations or model training, validating each candidate rubric against synthetic rubric-conditioned response pairs to screen out non-discriminative, over-specific, and style-only rubrics; best average accuracy across five preference-benchmark suites, leading on six of seven sets. _Category:_ arXiv:2607.15092 (cs.CL). [https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15092)

## Other interesting items

- **[Grokipedia vs Wikipedia: An LLM-Based Audit of Political Neutrality along Ideologies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15146)** — cs.CL. Audits 1,394 government-member article pairs across nine ideology dimensions using four LLM judges (Grok, Claude, Mistral, DeepSeek); all judges — including Grok itself — rate Grokipedia less neutral than Wikipedia, with Grokipedia favouring economically right-wing and penalising socially liberal figures.
- **[SciDiagramEdit: Learning to Edit Scientific Diagrams from Paper Revisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15272)** — cs.CL/cs.AI (incl. Jürgen Schmidhuber). Mines before/after figure pairs from arXiv version histories and uses agentic skill-evolution — a proposer refining the agent's skill spec from execution traces — to edit a figure's editable vector source from natural-language instructions.
- **[Latent Trajectory Discrimination for AI-Generated Text Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14967)** — cs.CL. Reframes AI-text detection as distinguishing latent generation trajectories: GTCL segments a document into ordered local units and applies contrastive learning to sequence-level embedding dynamics, consistently beating static-representation baselines across three benchmarks.
- **[The Misclassification of Autistic Writing as AI-Generated](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14729)** — cs.CL. On ~60k Reddit posts, an OpenAI GPT-2 detector flags likely-autistic writing as AI-generated significantly more often than general text (though <2% either way) — empirical evidence of detector bias with ethical implications for academic-integrity use.
- **[T²MLR: Transformer with Temporal Middle-Layer Recurrence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15178)** — cs.CL. Fuses a cached middle-layer representation from the previous token into an earlier layer of the current position so intermediate reasoning persists across decoding steps; recurrence over just ~20% of layers can beat full-layer recurrence, and it retrofits into an existing pretrained 1.7B model to lift math reasoning without training from scratch.
