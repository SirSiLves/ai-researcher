Monday listing — 396 new/cross announcements across the six categories (feeds dated Mon, 03 Aug 2026). Heavy day for **agent safety and agent evaluation**: three independent papers argue that the field's current measurement apparatus (safety benchmarks, component tests, model-centric benchmarking) does not actually validate deployed agentic systems. Persistent agent memory shows up twice as an attack surface and once as a retrieval problem.

## Top picks

**Safety, or Just Capability? A Validity Audit of Agent-Safety Benchmarks** — Youting Wang, Xiao Han, Dingyan Shang et al.
_Takeaway:_ Runs R-Judge, InjecAgent, AgentHarm and AgentDojo under their official scorers on up to 22 models and finds the metrics don't hold — an "always positive" policy scores F1 0.690 on R-Judge, beating five of the 21 models that genuinely discriminate, and the three broad benchmarks rank the same 18 models differently; capability predicts task success (ρ=+0.60) while correlating negatively with the safety scores.
_Category:_ arXiv 2607.28685 (cs.IR → cs.AI). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28685

**Tool Specifications Matter: Uncovering and Mitigating Safety Risks in AI Agents** — Minghui Pan, Jiayuxuan Yang, Yuanyuan Yuan et al.
_Takeaway:_ Pins agent safety degradation on the schema-formatted tool spec itself — white-box representation analysis shows JSON schemas weaken the model's internal refusal signal. SafeKeep decouples the two paths (judge on flattened text specs, execute on the original schema) and lifts average harmful-request refusal from 23.8% to 70.6% across four LLMs.
_Category:_ arXiv 2607.29254 (cs.AI). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29254

**Memory Provenance Laundering in LLM Agents: A Non-Amplification Firewall for Persistent Memory** — Jinghan Xu, Yiyong Xiao, Wanru Shao et al.
_Takeaway:_ Names a concrete failure in agent memory: LLM-based consolidation rewrites an untrusted external observation as apparent user history, preserving the action trigger while erasing the low-trust source. Laundered memories reach 1.000 attack success rate; a provenance-preserving middleware (PPMF) that matches action risk to memory authority blocks every evaluated unauthorized high-risk action.
_Category:_ arXiv 2607.29167 (cs.AI). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29167

**AISPA: User-Centric System Prompt Auditing for Large Language Model Applications** — Xiangning Lin, Shenzhe Zhu, Shu Yang et al. (26 authors)
_Takeaway:_ Audits 3,249 instructions from the system prompts of 88 commercial AI products along eight user-facing dimensions, labelling each instruction protective or problematic — a governance/transparency artifact for a layer that is deployed everywhere but disclosed to neither users nor regulators. Top-5 on HF Papers today (29 upvotes).
_Category:_ arXiv 2607.28617. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28617

**Beyond Component Testing: Validating Agentic AI Systems** — Fabio Orazio Mirto, Luca D'Agati, Giuseppe Tricomi et al.
_Takeaway:_ Synthesises 257 papers across agent evaluation, software assurance, runtime monitoring and regulatory guidance into a five-dimension taxonomy (behavioral, safety, temporal, regulatory, multi-agent). Verdict: behavioral evaluation is comparatively mature; temporal validity, runtime evidence maintenance, regulatory legibility and open-ended multi-agent assurance are not. Survey, but the gap map is the useful part.
_Category:_ arXiv 2607.29405 (cs.MA). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29405

**Benchmarks Are Not Validation: A System-Level View of Financial LLM Applications** — Burak Payzun, İrem Demirtaş, Simona Scala et al.
_Takeaway:_ Position paper drawn from actually validating GenAI applications inside financial institutions: benchmark scores are not production readiness for systems that combine retrieval, proprietary data, tool use, orchestration, monitoring and human escalation. Argues for layered evidence across the stack and lists the controls LLM-as-judge needs (multiple judges, rubrics, agreement, auditability).
_Category:_ arXiv 2607.28840 (cs.CL). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28840

**GoldenRetriever: Non-Interactive Homomorphic Encrypted Retrieval for Privacy-Preserving RAG** — Yang Gao, Gang Quan, Scott Piersall et al.
_Takeaway:_ Drops encrypted top-k ranking in favour of threshold selection, cutting retrieval cost from quadratic to linear in corpus size. CKKS-based, fully non-interactive, and leaks neither the query, the intermediate similarity scores, nor which documents were selected.
_Category:_ arXiv 2607.29019 (cs.CL / cs.IR). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29019

**From RLVR to RLSVR: Task Transformation Induces Self-Verifiable Rewards for Open-Ended LLM Self-Improvement** — Qinsi Wang, Jing Shi, Huazheng Wang et al.
_Takeaway:_ Extends verifiable-reward RL past math and code by transforming open-ended tasks into proxy environments whose internal rules make outcomes self-verifiable — no reward model, no LLM judge, no human preference labels in the loop. Today's most-upvoted paper on HF (69).
_Category:_ arXiv 2607.23802. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23802

## Other interesting items

- **[Mixture-of-Translators: Translating KV Caches Across Heterogeneous Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28979)** — Jin-woo Lee, Minkyung Song, Junghyun Oh et al. Multi-model systems re-prefill the same shared context per model because KV caches are architecture-specific; MoT learns multiple translator modules to map a source model's cache into the target model's cache space, with a context-correction loss for the residual drift. Cross-model cache reuse as an interoperability primitive.
- **[Reproducing LightMem: Naive RAG Is Just as Good for Memory Management](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29104)** — Yongjie Zhou, Shuai Wang, Bevan Koopman et al. (Univ. of Queensland). Swapping only the retriever over a fixed LightMem store moves answer accuracy from 58.1% to 75.5% — retriever choice dominates the memory architecture. Constructed memories don't consistently beat raw-turn retrieval; LightMem wins mainly under tight answering-token budgets.
- **[Bridging the Question-Answer Gap in RAG: Hypothetical Prompt Embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29402)** — Domen Vake, Jernej Vičič, Aleksandar Tošić. HyPE moves HyDE-style hypothetical generation from query time to index time: precompute hypothetical questions per chunk and embed those, turning retrieval into question-question matching with zero added query latency. Six datasets.
- **[Data Turnstile: A Scalable Open Framework for Function-Calling Data Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29250)** — Goutham Ramakrishnan, Megha Sharma. Open-source synthetic-data pipeline that decomposes multi-turn tool use into constrained stepwise generation with validation and error-feedback loops. A Qwen3-0.6B fine-tuned on it hits 75.9% on BFCL single-turn without chain-of-thought, vs 67.4% for the base model with thinking on.
- **[MerchantBench: Benchmarking LLM Agents for Long-Term Coherence in E-Commerce Operations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28956)** — Qiming Shi, Yulong Tao, Linbo Jin et al. A 365-day order-level simulation over 98,843 real product records with 26 tools, built to measure whether agents hold purposeful behavior across long horizons with heterogeneously delayed feedback — the axis most bounded-task agent benchmarks miss.
- **[Safeguards Based on Copyable Context Cannot Provide Reliable Safety for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27951)** — Pingyu Wu, Lingyao Zhu, Weiming Zhang, Nenghai Yu. Derives an exact worst-case floor on attacker assistance whenever the evidence a safeguard relies on is copyable, giving a safety trilemma: useful capability, reliable safety and open access cannot coexist. Points at hard-to-copy trusted credentials as the complement.
- **[QASP: Query-Adaptive Robust Vector Search Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29606)** — Hakan Ferhatosmanoglu, Kushal Kumar, Tal Wagner et al. Predicts the full per-query recall progression curve in one upfront regression, then derives a search policy for any recall target — no iterative model calls during search, no separate predictor per target, and it generalises across index configurations and datasets. Aimed squarely at the per-query recall variance that average-recall evaluation hides.
