## Top picks

- **The MiniMax-M2 Series: Mini Activations Unleashing Max Real-World Intelligence** — MiniMax (institutional release, ~200 authors). _Takeaway:_ Flagship MoE LLM with 229.9B total / only 9.8B activated parameters per token — explicitly designed end-to-end for agentic deployment. Three pillars: agent-driven data pipelines that produce verifiable trajectories, mini-activation MoE architecture, and a training recipe tuned for tool-using / multi-step reasoning workloads. One of the few open releases this month positioning a new frontier-tier weight class as "agent-native" rather than "chat-native" — community traction on HF tracks this as a top trend. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.26494. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26494)

- **VitaBench 2.0: Evaluating Personalized and Proactive Agents in Long-Term User Interactions** — Yuxin Chen, Yi Zhang, Zhengzhou Cai et al. (Meituan + NUS). _Takeaway:_ Successor to VitaBench targeted at the gap most enterprise agent benchmarks ignore: collaboration that requires the agent to infer user intent from *fragmented* interaction history rather than from the current request. Adds personalized-modeling and proactive-interaction dimensions, and pairs them with long-horizon scenarios. Direct counterpart to ContextEcho-style drift work — measures the *constructive* side (does the agent remember and act on user state) rather than the failure side. HF-trending today. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.27141. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27141)

- **MUSE-Autoskill: Self-Evolving Agents via Skill Creation, Memory, Management, and Evaluation** — Huawei Lin, Peng Li, Jie Song, Fuxin Jiang, Tieying Zhang. _Takeaway:_ Skill-centric agent framework that operationalizes skill creation, memory storage, lifecycle management, and per-skill evaluation as first-class loops, rather than treating skills as static one-shot artifacts. Direct response to yesterday's SkillEvolBench finding that "raw-trajectory reuse frequently outperforms distilled skills" — proposes the missing management layer. Important data point for anyone building Anthropic-Skills-style libraries, n8n-style workflow agents, or persistent agent runtimes. HF-trending. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.27366. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27366)

- **Efficient Agentic Reinforcement Learning with On-Policy Intrinsic Knowledge Boundary Enhancement** — Dingwei Chen, Zefang Zong, Zhipeng Ma, Leo Luo, Yang Li, Chengming Li, Peng Chen, Jie Jiang. _Takeaway:_ Identifies a specific pathology of agentic RL training: the policy learns to call tools redundantly and loses the ability to distinguish "tools needed" from "parametric knowledge suffices." Proposes an on-policy intrinsic knowledge-boundary signal as a cleaner alternative to coarse reward shaping. Useful diagnostic for anyone running RLVR / GRPO over tool-using agents — the redundant-tool-call failure mode is showing up in production. HF-trending. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.26952. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26952)

- **Learning to Act under Noise: Enhancing Agent Robustness via Noisy Environments** — Yuxin Chen, Xiaodong Cai, Junfeng Fang, Zhuowen Han et al. _Takeaway:_ Documents the train-vs-deployment gap on tool-using agents: benchmark scores are strong while real deployments degrade because production environments are stochastic and imperfect (latency variance, partial tool failures, format drift). Trains under deliberately noisy environments and shows recovery of robustness without hurting clean-environment scores. Same Meituan/NUS group as VitaBench 2.0 — these are paired contributions (eval + training). HF-trending. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.27209. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27209)

- **UnityMAS-O: A General RL Optimization Framework for LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems** — Yiqun Chen, Wei Yang, Erhan Zhang et al. (Renmin/Tsinghua/Xiaohongshu). _Takeaway:_ Most multi-agent stacks today are still hand-prompted; UnityMAS-O is a unified RL post-training abstraction over user-defined multi-agent workflows with structured interaction and role-specific credit assignment. Argues "the orchestration layer is the optimization target, not the prompts" — directly resonant with this week's "scaling the harness" framing. Important infrastructure piece for anyone running CrewAI / AutoGen / LangGraph at scale. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.26646. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26646)

- **MemFail: Stress-Testing Failure Modes of LLM Memory Systems** — Ishir Garg, Neel Kolhe, Dawn Song, Xuandong Zhao (UC Berkeley). _Takeaway:_ Existing memory benchmarks report aggregate QA accuracy and treat the memory system as a black box, making it impossible to attribute errors to the right design choice. MemFail breaks that down into specific failure-mode taxonomies (retrieval gap, source confusion, write conflict, capacity overflow, summarization drift) and stresses each one independently. Pairs cleanly with yesterday's MemIR "provenance-role collapse" paper — together they form the start of a memory-quality testing suite for persistent-memory agents shipping in production. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.26667. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26667)

- **Rethinking Agentic RAG: Toward LLM-Driven Logical Retrieval Beyond Embeddings** — Yuqi Zeng, Qixiang Deng, Yulei Wan, Ruiquan Jiang, Xiaoqing Zheng, Xuanjing Huang (Fudan). _Takeaway:_ Argues the dominant "embed query, top-k by similarity" primitive is the wrong substrate for agentic retrieval. Proposes a logical-retrieval formulation where the LLM directly drives the retrieval-decision process (what to search, in what order, with what reformulations) rather than relying on the embedding geometry. Sits next to LLM-Wiki, RICE-PO, ICICLE in the rapidly forming "RAG-as-tool-use, not RAG-as-lookup" thesis. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.27123. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27123)

- **The Attribution Blind Spot: Detecting When Language Models Rely on Memory Rather Than Retrieved Context** — Zhe Yu, Wenpeng Xing, Yunzhao Wei, Bo Yang, Chen Ye, Gaolei Li, Meng Han. _Takeaway:_ RAG promises grounded outputs, but the standard test ("does the answer match the retrieved doc?") fails when the retrieved doc overlaps with pretraining data — context-consistency does not imply context-government. Develops detectors that distinguish parametric-recall from retrieval-governed generation. Critical primitive for any high-stakes RAG deployment (financial, legal, medical) where grounding is a compliance requirement, not a quality knob. Companion paper "Detecting Is Not Resolving" (2605.27157) by overlapping authors generalizes this into a monitoring-control gap framing. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.26778. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26778)

- **Lost in the Evidence? Reproducing Document Position and Context Size Effects in RAG** — Jorge Gabín, Anxo Perez, Javier Parapar. _Takeaway:_ Independent reproduction of the "lost-in-the-middle" and context-size effects in RAG — important because prior reports relied on a small set of models and inconsistent context-construction protocols. Confirms position effects persist but quantifies the asymmetry and identifies context-construction choices (chunk ordering, prefix prompting) that materially shift the curve. Reference-grade reproducibility work that production-RAG teams will cite when picking chunk-ordering defaults. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.27105. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27105)

- **Position: AI Safety Requires Effective Controllability** — Yige Li, Yunhao Feng, Jun Sun. _Takeaway:_ Position paper arguing the alignment-first framing of AI safety has reached diminishing returns: aligned behavior on benchmarks doesn't guarantee a deployed agent can be stopped, overridden, or constrained once it operates in open-ended tool-using environments. Reframes safety around effective controllability — stop conditions, override paths, constraint enforcement — as separable from preference alignment. Notable framing shift relevant to the EU AI Act / NIST AI RMF / ISO 42001 governance conversation, where "human oversight" is a binding requirement that controllability operationalizes. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.27117. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27117)

- **Constitutional Arms Races in the Public Goods Game: Co-Evolving LLM Constitutions Under Cooperation-Defection Pressure** — Ujwal Kumar, Arth Singh, Hershraj Niranjani, Machiko Hirota, Takehiro Takayanagi, Alice Saito, Eiji Kamioka, Phan Xuan Tan. _Takeaway:_ Probes whether LLM-guided evolutionary search can discover stable cooperative constitutions when agents are explicitly placed under adversarial pressure (blackmail, sabotage, document-leak temptations). Finds the fitness landscape favors specific constitution motifs and that single-agent alignment training systematically underprovisions for multi-agent goal conflict. Direct extension of the agentic-misalignment-under-goal-conflict thread; useful for anyone designing A2A coordination protocols where defection is a real option. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.26448. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26448)

- **JobBench: Aligning Agent Work With Human Will** — Yuetai Li, Yichen Feng, Zhangchen Xu et al. _Takeaway:_ Pushback on the dominant "GDP-replacement" framing of agent benchmarks. JobBench covers 130 agentic tasks across 35 occupations, scoped not by economic-value-replaceable but by what *experts in the occupation* identify as high-priority-for-delegation work. Each task is packaged as a workspace of heterogeneous artefacts, so the agent is being evaluated on assist-the-human workflows rather than replace-the-human throughput. Useful counterweight benchmark for enterprise-deployment narratives and procurement evaluations. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.26329. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26329)

- **Modeling Agentic Technical Debt and Stochastic Tax: A Standalone Framework for Measurement, Simulation, and Dashboarding** — Muhammad Zia Hydari, Raja Iqbal, Narayan Ramasubbu. _Takeaway:_ Formalizes two separable cost categories every agentic system accrues over time: (a) *agentic technical debt* — a stock of accumulated design and governance liability (skill bloat, untracked memory growth, prompt sprawl), and (b) *stochastic tax* — a recurring flow of operating burden from probabilistic outputs (retries, tool-fail recovery, human-in-the-loop overrides). Provides measurement primitives suitable for an ops dashboard. Pairs with the morning's "MobileExplorer" on-device-agent work and with this week's "scaling the harness" framing — together they sketch the operations layer of the agent platform stack. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.27320. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27320)

- **Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks** — Kevin Kuo, Chhavi Yadav, Virginia Smith (CMU). _Takeaway:_ Recent defenses for open-weight LLMs assume harmful behavior is *learned through* fine-tuning rather than *elicited from* the base model. The authors show this assumption breaks under simple attacks that don't introduce new behavior, just surface it. Direct relevance to the open-weight licensing / ethical-use-clause debate yesterday's "Governance Horizon" paper formalized — together these two argue that downstream restriction mechanisms (license clauses, fine-tune defenses, lineage metadata) all decay sharply and predictably. Companion piece: "Curriculum Learning for Safety Alignment" (2605.26315) from overlapping authors investigates DPO's OOD-generalization brittleness on the training side. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.26526. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26526)

## Other interesting items

- **[Can LLMs Introspect? A Reality Check](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26242)** — Shashwat Singh, Tal Linzen, Shauli Ravfogel (NYU). Argues recent "LLMs can report internal states" claims may be premature — current behavioral evidence doesn't distinguish genuine introspection from pattern-matching on surface cues. Lessons-from-human-metacognition framing. HF-trending; relevant to interpretability-as-safety-tool debates.
- **[ScientistOne: Towards Human-Level Autonomous Research via Chain-of-Evidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26340)** — Rui Meng et al. (Google). Surface-level evaluation misses verifiability failures (fabricated citations, unreproducible scores, method-vs-implementation drift). Proposes a Chain-of-Evidence framework requiring every claim to be traceable to executed code or retrieved artefact. Direct analog of yesterday's QUEST work, focused on auditability rather than capability.
- **[Natural Language Query to Configuration for Retrieval Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27361)** — Melissa Z. Pan, Negar Arabzadeh, Mathew Jacob, Fiodar Kazhamiaka, Esha Choukse, Matei Zaharia (Databricks/UW). Picks RAG agent configurations (LLM, retriever, k, hops, synthesis strategy) per query against an accuracy-or-budget target rather than once-per-workload. Useful primitive for production RAG cost-control.
- **[MobileMoE: Scaling On-Device Mixture of Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27358)** — Yanbei Chen et al. (Meta). Pareto-frontier sub-billion-active MoE LLMs (0.3-0.9B active / 1.3-5.3B total) tuned for on-device deployment. HF-trending; pairs with Apple/Qualcomm on-device-AI roadmap.
- **[Helicase: Uncertainty-Guided Supply Chain Knowledge Graph Construction with Autonomous Multi-Agent LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26835)** — Yunbo Long et al. (Cambridge). Multi-agent KG construction for supply-chain questions that are *structural inference* problems (multi-hop across fragmented web sources), not one-shot QA. Concrete enterprise-LLM use-case template.
- **[Curriculum Learning for Safety Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26315)** — Sandeep Kumar, Virginia Smith, Chhavi Yadav (CMU). Studies why DPO is brittle and exhibits poor OOD generalization for safety alignment, and proposes a curriculum-learning fix. Useful for anyone training open-weight safety classifiers; pairs with the open-weight-defenses-broken paper above.
- **[QUACK: Questioning, Understanding, and Auditing Communicated Knowledge in Multimodal Social Deduction Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27068)** — Ye Yuan et al. Social-deduction-game eval that audits whether an agent's *language* is actually grounded in what it perceived and did, rather than just win-rate scoring. HF-trending; useful diagnostic for multi-agent honesty research.
