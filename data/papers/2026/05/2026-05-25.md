## Top picks

- **Same Model, Different Weakness: How Language and Modality Reshape the Jailbreak Attack Surface in Frontier MLLMs** — Casey Ford, Madison Van Doren, Sicheng Jin, Emily Dix. _Takeaway:_ First systematic cross-lingual + multimodal red-team of Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5, Pixtral Large and Qwen Omni using 363 adversarial prompts in en-US vs es-MX, text-only vs multimodal. Finds alignment failures are not uniform — the same model has different attack surfaces depending on language and modality, with multimodal Spanish being a sharp gap. Direct input for any AI-governance/red-team program. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.23157. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23157)

- **How to Steer Your Multi-Agent System: Human-LLM Collaborative Planning** — Zeyu He, Hannah Kim, Dan Zhang, Estevam Hruschka. _Takeaway:_ Formalizes a design space for human co-planning with orchestrated multi-agent systems along three axes (semantic vs structural, global vs targeted, low vs high-level edits) and ships AMBIPOM, a prototype. The first paper I've seen that treats "how does the human stay in the loop on a multi-agent plan" as a first-class HCI primitive rather than an afterthought — directly relevant to agentic platform UX. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.23023. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23023)

- **Foundation Protocol: A Coordination Layer for Agentic Society** — Bang Liu, Yongfeng Gu, Jiayi Zhang, Zhaoyang Yu, Sirui Hong, Maojia Song, et al. _Takeaway:_ Proposes a general-purpose protocol layer for autonomous-agent coordination — identity, relationships, multi-agent work organization, value exchange, and accountability primitives. Strongly in the MCP/A2A interoperability lineage but aims one layer higher (an "AI economy" stack). Worth reading alongside the MCP/A2A trackers. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.23218. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23218)

- **MemAudit: Post-hoc Auditing of Poisoned Agent Memory via Causal Attribution and Structural Anomaly Detection** — Zhewen Tan, Yilun Yao, Huiyan Jin, Wenhan Yu, Guoan Wang, et al. _Takeaway:_ Memory-poisoning attack class — an adversarial user injects records through ordinary interaction that later steer the agent's reasoning. Existing defences are online; this work targets post-hoc forensic auditing via causal attribution + anomaly detection. Concrete agent-security primitive that pairs naturally with persistent-memory architectures shipping in Claude/ChatGPT today. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.23723. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23723)

- **OpenSkillEval: Automatically Auditing the Open Skill Ecosystem for LLM Agents** — Jiahao Ying, Boxian Ai, Wei Tang, Siyuan Liu, Yixin Cao. _Takeaway:_ As Anthropic's own "Skills" mechanism (and similar in OpenAI, LangChain, etc.) becomes a marketplace, how do you tell which skills are good, which interact well with which model/agent framework, and what the cost-performance trade-off is? OpenSkillEval is the first end-to-end auditor for that ecosystem. Highly aligned with current Claude/Cowork direction. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.23657. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23657)

- **When Planning Fails Despite Correct Execution: On Epistemic Calibration for LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems** — Zehao Wang, Shilong Jin, Zhao Cao, Lanjun Wang. _Takeaway:_ Names a failure mode — "epistemic miscalibration in planning" — where agents misjudge what they know when evaluating plan feasibility, so the plan is self-consistent and executable yet wrong. Latent at planning time, dynamic across new info. Useful concept for anyone debugging real multi-agent reliability. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.23414. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23414)

- **How Far Will They Go? Red-Teaming Online Influence with Large Language Models** — Daniel C. Ruiz, Anna Serbina, Ashwin Rao, Emilio Ferrara, Luca Luceri. _Takeaway:_ Empirical red-teaming framework targeting locally-deployed open-source LLMs (the operationally relevant threat model for privacy-conscious malicious actors) across 30+ models / 10 families / 5 countries of origin. Introduces "Overton Window" measure for political expressivity and quantifies how simple jailbreaks expand it. Tells you which open models are most steerable into influence-operation use. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.22880. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22880)

- **Benchmarking Google Embeddings 2 against Open-Source Models for Multilingual Dense Retrieval and RAG Systems** — Stefano Cirillo, Domenico Desiato, Giuseppe Polese, Giandomenico Solimando. _Takeaway:_ GE2 (Vertex bi-encoder, 2,048-token context, task-type conditioning) vs BGE-M3, E5-large, mE5-L, LaBSE, mMPNet across four BEIR subsets + a synthetic Italian RAG corpus + chunking ablation + per-query CPU latency. Practical buyer's-guide numbers for anyone choosing a multilingual embedding stack right now. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.23618. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23618)

- **Query-Adaptive Semantic Chunking for Retrieval-Augmented Generation (QASC)** — Mudit Rastogi. _Takeaway:_ Most chunking happens at ingest time; QASC dynamically constructs chunks at query time using cosine-similarity seeds + contextual window expansion + chunk-level score aggregation. Reports +18–27% F1 vs fixed chunking and +8–12% vs semantic/agentic chunking across 100 docs / 200 queries. Concrete pattern for production RAG. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.22834. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22834)

- **From Correctness to Preference: A Framework for Personalized Agentic Reinforcement Learning** — Ranxu Zhang, Zeyang Li, Jiacheng Huang, Rui Zhang, Xiaozhou Xu, Sun Zhe, Yanyong Zhang, Chao Wang. _Takeaway:_ Agentic RL today trains on objective-correctness rewards, but real deployments need user-conditioned behavior (same query, different tool-use across users). Proposes a framework that disentangles correctness from preference, handles conformity bias, and adds a personalized memory layer. Bridges Agentic RL with personalization — increasingly the missing piece for assistants. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.23382. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23382)

- **Convex Optimization for Alignment and Preference Learning on a Single GPU** — Miria Feng, Mert Pilanci. _Takeaway:_ Reformulates the preference-learning objective as a convex program, giving a single-GPU alternative to RLHF/DPO with no hyperparameter sweep and consistent ranking accuracy. If the empirical results hold up, this is a substantial democratization of alignment training — relevant to anyone running small-team fine-tuning. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.23244. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23244)

- **The Efficiency Frontier: A Unified Framework for Cost-Performance Optimization in LLM Context Management** — Binqi Shen, Lier Jin, Hanyu Cai, Lan Hu, Yuting Xin. _Takeaway:_ Today retrieval, summarization and memory-compression methods are each evaluated against their own metrics, making it hard to compare or to pick a deployment configuration. This paper introduces a unified Pareto framework over performance × cost and lets you compare context-management strategies on the same axes. Useful for anyone tuning long-context inference budgets. _Category:_ arXiv:2605.23071. [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23071)

## Other interesting items

- **[Graph Alignment Topology as an Inductive Bias for Grounding Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22963)** — Paul Landes, Pranav Herur, Adam Cross, Jimeng Sun. Bipartite alignment graphs between source and LLM output + a GNN over the topology — SOTA on four hallucination/QA benchmarks, beating GPT-4o-based methods. RAG-grounding pattern worth tracking.
- **[When Do LLMs Reason? A Dynamical Systems View via Entropy Phase Transitions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22873)** — Identifies an entropy phase-transition that predicts *when* explicit CoT helps and when it just wastes compute — more principled than the current "use CoT when accuracy is low" heuristic.
- **[The Deterministic Horizon: Impossibility Results as Design Specifications for Trustworthy AI Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23024)** — Argues classical impossibility theorems (Turing, Arrow, NFL) should be treated as design specifications when shipping LLMs into software/legal/clinical workflows. Useful framing paper for governance teams.
